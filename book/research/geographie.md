## 1. Cadre géologique / Geological setting

### 1.1 Laguna de Bay basin: volcanic caldera plus tectonic depression
- ✅ **Macolod Corridor.** A NE-trending belt of Quaternary volcanism, about 40 km wide, cuts across the Luzon arc in SW Luzon from Taal (Batangas) to Laguna de Bay. It includes Taal, Makiling, Banahaw, the San Pablo/Laguna maar field and the Laguna caldera. It is interpreted either as a rift (crustal extension) or as a pull-apart zone between the Philippine Fault and the Sibuyan Sea–Verde Passage fault. No single model is accepted.
  - Förster, H. et al. (1990). "The Macolod Corridor: a rift crossing the Philippine island arc." *Tectonophysics* 183: 265–271. doi:10.1016/0040-1951(90)90420-D
  - Vogel, T.A. et al. (2006). "Geochemistry of silicic magmas in the Macolod Corridor, SW Luzon, Philippines." *Contrib. Mineral. Petrol.* 151: 267–281. doi:10.1007/s00410-005-0050-7. It notes that the pyroclastic flows linked to the **Laguna de Bay Caldera** are chemically distinct from Makiling's domes and lavas.
  - Wikipedia, "Macolod Corridor": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macolod_Corridor
- ✅/⚠️ **Laguna Caldera.** A caldera of about 20 × 10 km forms the central lobe of the lake, between the Jala-Jala peninsula (Mt. Sembrano) and Talim Island. Smithsonian GVP describes it as having formed in **at least two major explosive eruptions, ~1 million and ~27,000–29,000 years ago**. It also lists maars on southern Talim and a solfatara on the flank of Mt. Sembrano.
  - Global Volcanism Program, "Laguna Caldera" (vn 273080): https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=273080 (inaccessible during research; text quoted via Wikipedia)
  - Wikipedia, "Laguna Caldera": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Caldera
  - ⚠️ The dates are disputed. Wikipedia's infobox gives "~1.2 Ma and 29–27 ka", and its text says "unknown when it last erupted". 40Ar/39Ar dates on scoria near Teresa (Rizal), north of the lake, give **1.85 ± 0.01 Ma and 1.83 ± 0.02 Ma** (Sudo et al., Goldschmidt 2007 abstract: https://goldschmidtabstracts.info/2007/984.pdf). The JpGU 1999 abstract suggests volcanism migrated from the Laguna de Bay caldera toward the forearc after 1.6 Ma: https://www2.jpgu.org/meeting/1999/pdf/vc/vc-010_e.pdf. **Recommended wording for the book:** "eruptions between roughly 2 million and 30,000 years ago".
- ✅ **Three lobes, two origins.** The lake has three lobes. The central lobe is volcanic (the caldera). The **West Bay** (Biñan's side) is better read as a tectonic depression bounded on the west by the **Marikina Valley Fault System / West Valley Fault**. Rimando & Knuepfer (2006) show that this fault is mainly right-lateral, with Late Pleistocene–Holocene activity.
  - Rimando, R.E. & Knuepfer, P.L.K. (2006). "Neotectonics of the Marikina Valley fault system (MVFS)…" *Tectonophysics* 415: 17–38.
  - ⚠️ The phrase "the West Bay is a tectonic graben/pull-apart" is a common interpretation (LLDA, textbooks), but I did not find it stated explicitly in a primary paper. Write "largely tectonic in origin" rather than giving a precise mechanism.
- ⚠️ **Popular "Krakatoa-like eruption" story.** LLDA (quoted by Wikipedia) suggests the basin was formed by a cataclysmic eruption. This is speculative.

### 1.2 Tuff plateau (Guadalupe Formation / Diliman Tuff)
- ✅ The **Guadalupe Formation** (Pleistocene) is the regional bedrock of Metro Manila and its southern margins. It is a thick sequence of tuffs (ignimbrites) and tuffaceous sediments, and its upper member is the **Diliman Tuff**. The alluvium of the coastal plain comes from it.
  - ISSMGE, "Geotechnical and geophysical characterization of the Guadalupe Tuff Formation – the regional bedrock of Metro Manila": https://www.issmge.org/publications/publication/geotechnical-and-geophysical-characterization-of-the-guadalupe-tuff-formationthe-regional-bedrock-of-metro-manila-philippines
  - Science Diliman (UP): https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/sciencediliman/article/download/63/14
- ⚠️ **Source of the tuffs.** Arpa (2005, MSU thesis; JVGR 2008) shows that the *upper* Diliman Tuff is chemically distinct from Taal and Laguna caldera deposits, so its source vent is unknown. The ignimbrite sheets of Rizal, Metro Manila, Laguna and Bulacan are, however, attributed to the Laguna caldera (Catane 2005, *Explosive Volcanism in the Philippines*, Tohoku Univ.).
  - Arpa thesis: https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/33512
- ❓ **Biñan specifically.** The western uplands (Langkiwa, Mamplasan, Malamig, Timbao, Ganado…) sit on the gently sloping tuff plateau that rises toward Carmona, Silang and the Tagaytay ridge. That plateau is made of tuffs from the Taal/Tagaytay ignimbrites and the Guadalupe Formation. The geological map of the Biñan sheet (MGB, 1:50,000) should confirm which unit lies under the city.

### 1.3 West Valley Fault: it crosses Biñan
- ✅ The PHIVOLCS **Valley Fault System Atlas** (May 2015) shows the West Valley Fault running for about **100 km** from Bulacan to Carmona/Calamba. It **crosses Biñan**. Barangays named for Biñan: **Biñan (barangay), Malamig, San Francisco (Halang)**. Also named: San Pedro (Calendola, GSIS, Sampaguita Village, San Antonio, San Vicente, Riverside, United Bayanihan), Santa Rosa (Sto. Domingo), Carmona (Bancal, Cabilang Baybay, Lantic, Mabuhay), GMA (San Jose), Calamba (Canlubang) and Cabuyao (Casile).
  - GMA News (18 May 2015), "LIST: Barangays in Metro Manila, nearby provinces near earthquake fault": https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/story/489334/scitech/science/list-barangays-in-metro-manila-nearby-provinces-near-earthquake-fault
  - Inquirer (2015), "Do you live in quake zone? New Phivolcs maps trace 100-km West Valley Fault": https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/692133/do-you-live-in%E2%80%A6t-valley-fault
  - PHIVOLCS FaultFinder / Atlas: https://faultfinder.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/ (check the exact trace there)
  - ⚠️ "Casile" also exists as a Biñan barangay, but the list attributes it to Cabuyao. Check this on FaultFinder.
- ✅ **Hazard.** Maximum magnitude is about **M 7.2**. Recurrence is **400–600 years**, and the last attributed rupture was in **1658**. MMEIRS (JICA–MMDA–PHIVOLCS, 2004) projects up to 34,000 deaths in Metro Manila. PHIVOLCS recommends at least 5 m of setback from the trace.
  - DZRH / PHIVOLCS: https://www.dzrh.com.ph/post/west-valley-fault-nears-ripe-stage-but-no-way-to-tell-when-it-will-move-phivolcs
  - Inquirer, "The Big One could kill 34,000": https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/709030/the-big-one-could-kill-34000
- ⚠️ Total length varies by source: 100 km in the 2015 Atlas, and up to about 129 km in later updates extended to Calamba. Write "about 100 km".
- Visible illustration: the fault plane is exposed in Brgy. Mabuhay, Carmona, a few km from Biñan (see illustrations).

### 1.4 Sols / Soils
- ✅/❓ According to the Biñan Wikipedia article (figures uncited, apparently from the city's ecological profile):
  - **Carmona series**: ~2,577 ha (**59.24 %**), mainly used for rice and sugar.
  - **Lipa series**: 798 ha.
  - **Guadalupe series**: 660 ha.
  - **Gingua fine sandy loam**: 315 ha (lakeshore alluvium, good for vegetables).
  - 85.33 % of the land has a slope of 0–2.5 %.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi%C3%B1an
  - Official source to consult: Alicante et al. (1948), *Soil Survey of Laguna Province*, Bureau of Soils Report No. 10 (catalogue: https://tuklas.up.edu.ph/Record/UP-99796217604356273). BSWM maps: https://www.bswm.da.gov.ph/
- ✅ **Why fertile.** These soils form on volcanic tuff and ash (Guadalupe, Carmona) or on volcanic ash (Lipa). They are deep, clay-rich and mineral-rich, and with flat land and abundant water (rain, streams, the lake) they suit wet-rice farming and sugarcane. PhilRice, Guadalupe series profile (parent material: volcanic tuff): https://dbmp.philrice.gov.ph/soils/soilseries/Guadalupe
  - ⚠️ "Guadalupe clay = excellent rice soil" is common in Cavite literature (Cavite blog, secondary). Phrase it carefully.

## 2. Laguna de Bay

- ✅ **Size.** Largest lake in the Philippines. Area about **911 km²** at normal level (figures range from 900 to 949 km² depending on level). About 47 km E–W and 40 km N–S. Shoreline about 220 km.
  - Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_Bay
  - NASA EO uses 891 km².
- ✅ **Depth.** Very shallow: **average ~2.5–2.8 m**, maximum ~20 m at Diablo Pass (near Talim). Surface about 1–2 m above sea level.
- ✅/⚠️ **Watershed.** About **3,820 km²**. That is the LLDA figure quoted by Philstar (2009); Wikipedia's "45,000 km²" is clearly a mistake. **21 major tributaries.** The Pagsanjan River supplies about 35 % of the water and the Santa Cruz River about 15 %.
  - Philstar (10 Oct 2009): https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2009/10/10/512548/laguna-de-bay-flooding-recede-5-months
- ✅ **Outlet.** The only natural outlet is the **Pasig River** (~25 km to Manila Bay) through the **Napindan Channel**. The Pasig flows both ways: in the dry season, when the lake is lower than Manila Bay, the current reverses and **seawater enters** the lake.
  - The **Napindan Hydraulic Control Structure (NHCS, 1983)** regulates that backflow.
  - The **Manggahan Floodway (1986)** diverts the Marikina River's floods into the lake.
  - Santiago (1991), SEAFDEC, "Turbidity and seawater intrusion in Laguna de Bay": https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/1351
  - Copernicus PIAHS 2024 (hydrodynamic modelling): https://piahs.copernicus.org/articles/386/95/2024/
- ✅ **Salt and clarity.** Seawater intrusion makes suspended clays settle, which clears the water and helps the lake's productivity. After the 1997 backflow, clearing took 2–3 months. Blocking this exchange is debated.
- ✅ **Lake level and seasons** (LLDA gauge, local datum). About **10.5 m** in the dry season (April–May), about **12.5 m** at the end of the rainy season. Recent peak: **13.85–14.0 m with Ondoy (2009)**. Record: **14.6 m in 1919**, with 1972 second.
  - Inquirer: https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=246985
  - Philstar (2009), link above.
  - ⚠️ These are gauge readings, not altitudes above sea level (LLDA datum: 10 m below mean lower low water, ❓ check).
- ✅ **Ondoy (Ketsana), 26 Sept 2009, then Pepeng.**
  - 192 lakeshore barangays were under water.
  - Lake volume rose from 3.1 to 5.7 billion m³.
  - Authorities expected **at least 144 days** (about 5 months) for the water to drain. In practice, lakeshore towns stayed flooded for **weeks to months**.
  - Biñan and San Pedro were among the worst-hit towns. In Biñan, **De La Paz and Malaban** were cut off by knee-to-waist-deep water.
  - Sources: Philstar 2009 (above); Inquirer, "Flooding eases but villagers still suffer": https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/260306/flooding-eases-but-villagers-still-suffer
  - ⚠️ The De La Paz/Malaban detail comes from a 2018 article (2018 floods, not 2009). ❓ Find a Biñan-specific source for 2009 (city CDRRMO, Inquirer Southern Luzon archives).
- ✅ **Fisheries.**
  - Early 1960s: the catch (about 83,000 t/yr) was **95 % ayungin** (*Leiopotherapon/Therapon plumbeus*, endemic to the Philippines), **biyang puti** (*Glossogobius giuris*) and **kanduli** (*Arius/Neoarius manillensis*, endemic).
  - Twenty years later, native species had collapsed. Introduced or farmed species took over: **tilapia** and **bangus** (milkfish, a marine fish raised in pens), plus **knifefish** (*Chitala ornata*, invasive since about 2008) and janitor fish.
  - ~~SEAFDEC/AQD, LakeCon2003 paper: https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/6120~~ (erreur : ce handle concerne le lac Manguao, à Palawan). Bonne source pour l'histoire des prises : Bagarinao, « The decline of native fishes and fisheries and the rise of aquaculture in lakes and rivers in the Philippines », SEAFDEC, 2001, https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/834 (résumé : « ayungin », « biyang puti » et « kanduli » = 95 % des 83 000 t annuelles au début des années 1960 ; effondrement vers 1970).
  - Fishers' perceptions study (2022), all native species in decline: https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/fishers-perspectives-the-drivers-behind-the-decline-in-fish-catch/
  - BFAR, "disappearing endemic fishes": https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/290234/bfar-raises-alarm-over-disappearing-endemic-fishes
- ✅ **Fish pens and cages.**
  - Introduced in the 1970s (bangus pens about 1970–71, ❓ check the date). They spread uncontrolled in the 1980s.
  - Current zoning: **LLDA Board Resolution 540 (2018)** caps aquaculture at **9,200 ha** (about 10 % of the lake). The rest is reserved for open fishing by small fishers.
  - LLDA BR 540-2018: https://law.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/LLDA-BR-No-540-Series-of-2018.pdf
  - Inquirer, "Fish pens strangle Laguna de Bay": https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/858767/fish-pens-strangle-laguna-de-bay
- ✅ **Pollution.** About 70 % of the organic load (BOD) comes from households, 19 % from industry and 11 % from runoff (LLDA 2005, via Wikipedia). The **Biñan River is LLDA monitoring station 7** (since 2011) and regularly fails the dissolved-oxygen standard.
  - LLDA Annual Water Quality Report 2009–2012 (Scribd copy): https://www.scribd.com/document/464167016/Laguna-Lake-AWQR-2009-2012
  - lbtimes.ph (LLDA data): https://lbtimes.ph/whole-of-community-approach-stricter-policy-implementation-sought-water-quality-of-laguna-lakes-tributaries-continue-to-deteriorate-%e2%88%92-llda-data/
- ✅ **Transport and trade.** In the Spanish period and up to the 20th century, the lake and the Pasig formed the highway to Manila. **Cascos** (flat-bottomed barges) carried rice, sugar, coconuts and other goods, later towed in trains by steamboats. Passenger boats linked the lake towns.
  - Wikipedia, "Casco (barge)": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casco_(barge)
  - ❓ For Biñan, check the role of its landing (lakeshore pier/port) in the history chapters already written.
- ✅ **Name.** "Laguna de Bay" means "lagoon/lake of Bay", after the town of **Bay**, the first provincial capital. It is often wrongly read as "Laguna Bay". Older names: *Pulilan* (1613, ❓) and possibly *Puliran*. The Laguna Copperplate Inscription (900 CE) mentions **Puliran**; the reading "Puliran Kasumuran" is contested.
  - Wikipedia (link above). ⚠️ For the Copperplate reading, cite Postma (1992), "The Laguna Copper-Plate Inscription", *Philippine Studies* 40(2).

## 3. Paysage de Biñan / Biñan's landscape

- ✅ **Area.** **43.50 km²** (PSA), 2.27 % of Laguna province. PhilAtlas computes 40.27 km² from geospatial data. Use the PSA figure.
- ✅ **Boundaries.**
  - North: San Pedro.
  - South: Santa Rosa.
  - West: Carmona, General Mariano Alvarez (GMA) and Silang (Cavite).
  - East: Laguna de Bay.
  - Wikipedia Biñan: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi%C3%B1an
- ⚠️ **Elevation.** Lowest point **2 m** (lakeshore), average 68 m, **highest point 343 m** (Wikipedia, uncited). The city centre (Poblacion) sits at **11.8 m** (PhilAtlas). ❓ 343 m looks high for the Carmona/GMA border: check against SRTM or NAMRIA topo maps. The western uplands of Biñan probably rise to about 100–200 m.
  - PhilAtlas: https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/r04a/laguna/binan.html
- ✅ **Waterways.**
  - The **Biñan River** rises in the Carmona/GMA/Silang uplands and flows through the Poblacion to the lake. PhilAtlas lists it as Biñan & Carmona → Laguna Lake.
  - **Tibagan stream** (with a small waterfall) is named on the city tourism site.
  - A **Biñan Creek** is listed in San Pedro.
  - PhilAtlas, rivers of CALABARZON: https://www.philatlas.com/lists/physical/rivers-r04a.html
  - Experience Biñan (city Culture, History, Arts & Tourism Office): https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/
  - ⚠️ "San Isidro River" and "Mabuhay River" are not confirmed as Biñan streams. The "Tubigan River" in PhilAtlas is in Batangas, unrelated. The barangay name **Tubigan** comes from *Patubigan*, the irrigation system (Experience Biñan).
- ✅ **Lakeshore vs upland barangays.**
  - Lakeshore: **Malaban** ("waterfront barangay") and **De La Paz** (view of the lake, swimming). ❓ Check whether San Antonio, Santo Tomas or Platero also reach the shore.
  - Upland, west of SLEX, industrial and residential: **Langkiwa, Mamplasan, Malamig, Timbao, Ganado, Loma, Biñan (barangay)**. This is where the industrial parks are: **Laguna Technopark** (partly in Biñan, partly in Santa Rosa) and **Laguna International Industrial Park (LIIP)**.
  - Largest barangay: San Francisco (Halang), 732 ha. Smallest: Casile, 12 ha.
  - Experience Biñan, barangay table with areas and land uses (link above).
- ✅ **Land-use change.** Biñan went from rice fields and sugarcane (old Dominican estate, *Hacienda de Biñan*) to a city that is now classified **100 % urban** (all 24 barangays urban per PSA). The changes came with SLEX (1970s), the industrial parks (1990s) and subdivisions (Greenfield City, Southwoods, etc.).
  - ❓ No quantified CLUP figures found online. Ask the City Planning and Development Office for the city's CLUP / Ecological Profile, which would give hectares by land use.
- ✅ **Barangay names linked to nature** (Experience Biñan):
  - **Soro-soro**: from the *sorosoro* medicinal plant (*Euphorbia neriifolia*, ❓ identification to check).
  - **Bungahan**: fruit trees along the river.
  - **Tubigan**: from *patubigan* (irrigation).
  - **Malamig**: "cool".
  - **Langkiwa**: ❓ possibly a plant name.

## 4. Climat / Climate

- ✅ **Climate type.** PAGASA Modified Coronas **Type I**: two pronounced seasons, **dry from November to April, wet from May/June to October/November**. Rainfall peaks in July–September with the SW monsoon (*habagat*).
  - cropsreview (from Kintanar 1984): https://www.cropsreview.com/climate-types/
  - Wikipedia, "Climate of the Philippines": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_Philippines
- ⚠️ **Köppen.** Probably **Aw** (tropical savanna, like Manila) or borderline **Am**. No source specific to Biñan.
- ✅ **Rainfall (nearest PAGASA station: Sangley Point, Cavite, ~25 km NW).** Normal annual total about **2,078 mm**:
  - Jan 16.9, Feb 11.1, Mar 9.4, Apr 18.5, May 139.1, Jun 264.5, Jul 422.4, Aug 457.5, Sep 341.8, Oct 224.3, Nov 110.5, Dec 62.7 mm.
  - About 90 % of the total falls between May and October.
  - PAGASA Climatological Normals: https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/climate/climatological-normals (PDF Sangley Point: https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/cds/Sangley_Point.pdf)
  - ⚠️ The Biñan Wikipedia table (Meteoblue "Bay", 1,094 mm/yr) is a model, not measured data, and its text says "200 mm". **Do not use it.**
  - Alternative station: UPLB/Los Baños (agrometeorological, ~25 km SE). Its data are not online (FOI).
- ⚠️ **Temperature.** Mean maximum about 29–34 °C (April–May hottest). Mean minimum about 21–24 °C (January–February coolest). Annual mean about 27–28 °C. These are model values (Meteoblue); ❓ confirm with the PAGASA Sangley/NAIA normals.
- ✅ **Typhoons.** Laguna lies in the path of Pacific typhoons (June–December). Notable events:
  - **Ondoy/Ketsana (2009)**: record rainfall, lake flood.
  - **Milenyo/Xangsane (2006)**: winds.
  - **Glenda/Rammasun (2014)**.
  - **Ulysses/Vamco (2020)**: rising lake, "like Ondoy all over again".
  - The **habagat** (SW monsoon) floods of 2012 and 2013 also flooded the lakeshore.
  - Inquirer, "Like 'Ondoy' all over again": https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1360021/like-ondoy-all-over-again

## 5. Environnement naturel / Natural surroundings

- ✅ **Mount Makiling.** 1,090 m, a Pleistocene volcano (stratovolcano with domes) about 25 km SE of Biñan, visible across the West Bay. It is the setting of the legend of **Maria Makiling**. ⚠️ PHIVOLCS status: inactive (some lists say "potentially active").
  - Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Makiling
- ✅ **Mount Banahaw.** About 2,158–2,170 m (GVP / Wikipedia), an active volcano (Quezon/Laguna) about 70 km ESE, visible in clear weather. Sacred mountain.
- ✅ **Tagaytay ridge and Taal.** The rim of the Taal caldera (Tagaytay ridge, ~600–700 m) closes the horizon to the SW. ⚠️ Taal Volcano itself is hidden behind the ridge, so it cannot be seen from Biñan. Its **January 2020 ash** did reach Laguna/Cavite (NASA ISS photo).
- ⚠️ **Birds.** The lake hosts resident and migratory waterbirds (October–March): little egret and great egret, herons (Ardeidae), whiskered tern, Philippine duck (*Anas luzonica*, endemic), plus migrant ducks and shorebirds. They are counted each January in the **Asian Waterbird Census** (DENR-BMB/Wetlands International). ❓ No Laguna de Bay-specific published synthesis found. Contact the Wild Bird Club of the Philippines or DENR-BMB.
  - BMB AWC: https://bmb.gov.ph/?p=3953
  - WBCP: https://birdwatch.ph/2023/01/31/the-wbcp-participates-in-the-2023-asian-waterbird-census/
- ✅/⚠️ **Etymology of "Biñan"** (for consistency with the book): **uncertain** according to Wikipedia.
  - Traditional theory: **binyagan**, "place of baptism", after the 1571 baptisms associated with the arrival of Juan de Salcedo (the friars named are Alonso de Alvarado and Diego Espinar).
  - The root *binyag* would come from a **Brunei Malay** word meaning "to pour water from above", per the *Vocabulario de la lengua tagala*.
  - Other theories: a plant that grew there (BusinessMirror, 2023), or a merchant's term (weak).
  - Historical spellings: *Viñan* (Mallat 1846), Biniang, Binyang, Binang, Biñang.
  - Wikipedia Biñan (above).
  - City government: https://www.binan.gov.ph/history/
  - Experience Biñan: https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/binans-beginnings/
  - BusinessMirror (2 Feb 2023): https://www.pressreader.com/philippines/businessmirror/20230202/281977496765405
  - ⚠️ The **"binyagan" plant** mentioned in the brief is not confirmed by any source.

## 7. Plan proposé du chapitre / Proposed chapter outline (6 paragraphs)

1. **Between lake and plateau (overview).** Biñan in one image: a narrow strip about 43.5 km² that slopes gently from the shore of Laguna de Bay (~2 m) west toward the tuff plateau of Carmona and Silang. Coordinates, neighbours, and the view of Makiling and Banahaw. Hook: the view from the ISS (NASA photo).
2. **Born of fire and faults (geology).** The Macolod Corridor and the Laguna caldera (eruptions between ~2 Ma and ~30 ka). The ignimbrite plateau (Guadalupe Formation). The West Valley Fault crossing Biñan (Malamig, San Francisco, barangay Biñan): M 7.2, last rupture 1658, recurrence 400–600 years. Present the risk soberly.
3. **Land that feeds (soils and waters).** Carmona, Lipa and Guadalupe soils from volcanic tuff and ash; flat land (85 % under 2.5 % slope). The Biñan River and the streams that come down from the plateau, the *patubigan*. Why rice and sugarcane flourished. Link to the hacienda chapters.
4. **The lake, Biñan's other street (Laguna de Bay).** Area, shallow depth, the two-way Pasig outlet and its salt. Historical fisheries (ayungin, kanduli, biya), then fish pens of tilapia and bangus. Cascos to Manila. The name "Laguna de Bay".
5. **Sun, monsoon and floods (climate).** Type I climate: dry November–April, wet May–October. About 2,000 mm of rain a year, 90 % of it in the *habagat*. Typhoons. Ondoy 2009: the lake reached ~14 m and the lakeshore barangays (De La Paz, Malaban) stayed flooded for weeks.
6. **From rice fields to city (population and land use).** From 283,000 inhabitants (2010) to about 584,000 (2024). SLEX, CALAX, PNR, Laguna Technopark. The landscape transformed, and the environmental challenges (Biñan River pollution, building in a flood zone and near the fault). Transition to the history chapters.

## 8. « Alam mo ba? » / "Did you know?" ideas

1. **The lake that drinks the sea.** In the dry season the Pasig River reverses and Manila Bay seawater enters Laguna de Bay. The salt makes the mud settle, and the water becomes clear for a few months (SEAFDEC, Santiago 1991).
2. **A fault under our feet.** The West Valley Fault, which last moved in 1658, passes through barangays Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) and Biñan. Its scarp is visible in Mabuhay, Carmona, just next door (PHIVOLCS 2015).
3. **Ayungin, the fish of the past.** In the 1960s, 95 % of the lake's catch was three native fish: ayungin, biyang puti and kanduli. Today tilapia and bangus dominate, and the native fish have become rare.
   - Alternative: **"Laguna de *Bay*" is not a "bay"**. The name honours the town of Bay, the first capital of the province.

## A. Tectonique des plaques / Plate tectonics

- ✅ **Philippine Mobile Belt.** The archipelago (except Palawan and Mindoro's continental blocks) is a belt of island-arc blocks squeezed between two plates converging obliquely:
  - the **Philippine Sea Plate** to the east;
  - the **Sunda (Eurasian) Plate** to the west.
  - Total convergence is about **8–9 cm/yr**. Figures in the literature: 83–94 mm/yr (Sunda–PSP), and more than 100 mm/yr in the north decreasing to under 20 mm/yr in the Mindoro collision zone, depending on latitude and model.
  - Rangin, C. et al. (1999). "Plate convergence measured by GPS across the Sundaland/Philippine Sea Plate deformed boundary: the Philippines and eastern Indonesia." *Geophys. J. Int.* 139: 296–316. PDF: https://www.geologie.ens.fr/~rooke/NCRpdf4web/Rangin&al-1999.pdf
  - Galgana et al. (2007), block model of the Philippine Mobile Belt (AGU abstract): https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007AGUFM.G21C0670G
  - ⚠️ Rates vary by method (GPS vs geology), segment and period. Write "8 to 10 cm per year, about the speed at which fingernails grow… twice over" only if the author wants an image; otherwise "several centimetres per year".
- ✅ **Two opposite-facing subduction zones.**
  - **Manila Trench** (west of Luzon): the Sunda Plate (South China Sea) dives east under Luzon. Its convergence rate is **~65–100 mm/yr**, decreasing southward. It feeds the western Luzon arc: Pinatubo, Taal, and the volcanoes of the Macolod Corridor.
  - **East Luzon Trough / Philippine Trench** (east): the Philippine Sea Plate dives west. Convergence across the Philippine Trench is about 5 cm/yr, oblique.
  - Sources: Rangin et al. 1999 (above); thesis NCU (Taiwan) on the Manila subduction zone: https://www.gep.ncu.edu.tw/storage/thesis/2017/thesis_1497918236.pdf
- ✅ **Philippine Fault Zone** (~1,200 km, Luzon to Mindanao). A left-lateral strike-slip fault that takes up the sideways part of the oblique convergence ("shear partitioning"). Slip rates are about **2–4 cm/yr** depending on segment (~20–40 mm/yr in Luzon). It produced the 1990 Luzon earthquake. Nearest point to Biñan: the Infanta/Guinayangan segment (Quezon), about 60–80 km east (❓ estimate).
  - Aurelio, M.A. (2000). "Shear partitioning in the Philippines: constraints from Philippine Fault and GPS data." *The Island Arc* 9(4): 584–597.
  - Wikipedia, "Philippine fault system": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_fault_system
- ✅ **Macolod Corridor in this frame.** The corridor (Taal → Laguna de Bay, ~40 km wide, NE trend) cuts across the Manila Trench arc. It is interpreted as either:
  - a **rift** (Förster et al. 1990), or
  - a **pull-apart / extensional zone** opened between the Philippine Fault and the Sibuyan Sea–Verde Passage faults, with the Manila Trench slab beneath (Sudo et al.; Vogel et al. 2006).
  - Extension thins the crust and lets magma rise, which explains the density of volcanoes (Taal, Makiling, Banahaw, Malepunyo, San Pablo maars, the Laguna caldera).
  - The **Valley Fault System** (West Valley Fault, which crosses Biñan) is a right-lateral fault at the northern end of this system. It bounds the west side of Laguna de Bay (Rimando & Knuepfer 2006).
  - JpGU 1999 abstract (migration of volcanism after 1.6 Ma): https://www2.jpgu.org/meeting/1999/pdf/vc/vc-010_e.pdf
- ✅ **Ring of Fire.** The Philippines lies on the western edge of the Pacific Ring of Fire. PHIVOLCS counts **24 active volcanoes** (❓ check the latest count: 24 active per PHIVOLCS volcano list; the number has varied, e.g. 23 or 24) and many "potentially active" ones.
  - PHIVOLCS volcano list: https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/ (Volcanology → List of Active Volcanoes)
- ✅/⚠️ **Volcanoes near Biñan.**

  | Volcano | Distance | Notes |

## C. Climat sur des millions d'années / Deep-time climate

- ✅ **Pleistocene (2.58 Ma – 11.7 ka).** About a hundred-thousand-year cycle of glaciations and interglacials. The calderas of the Macolod Corridor and the tuff plateau formed during this period, while sea level oscillated by more than 100 m.
- ✅ **Last Glacial Maximum (~21–19 ka).** Global sea level about **−120 to −134 m**.
  - The **Sunda Shelf** emerged: **Sundaland** joined Borneo, Sumatra, Java and the Malay peninsula.
  - **Greater Palawan** formed. Palawan's link to Borneo is debated, since it needs more than 135 m of lowering.
  - Robles et al. 2015: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/handle/1885/14352
  - Ochoa et al. 2022, *Antiquity*: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/tropical-island-adaptations-in-southeast-asia-during-the-last-glacial-maximum-evidence-from-palawan/A55AABA43B6E6F9CD4C66662B73D55A8
- ✅/⚠️ **Luzon stayed an island.** It was enlarged into "Greater Luzon" (with Mindoro? ⚠️ no: Mindoro stayed separate) but never joined Sundaland. Deep straits remained (Huxley's line), which explains Luzon's very high endemism.
  - Heaney, L.R. (1985). "Zoogeographic evidence for Middle and Late Pleistocene land bridges to the Philippine Islands." *Modern Quaternary Research in SE Asia* 9: 127–144. (❓ to cite after checking)
- ⚠️ **Manila Bay at the LGM.** *Inference*, not found stated in a study: Manila Bay is shallow (average depth ~17 m, ❓ check). At −120 m it was therefore **dry land**, a plain crossed by the ancestral Pampanga and Pasig rivers flowing toward a coast far to the west. The Laguna de Bay basin (bottom ~0 to −2 m) may have been a freshwater lake or marsh draining toward the Pasig. **No core found for the LGM.**
- ✅ **Holocene: the lake was an arm of the sea.** The best local finding.
  - A **10.5 m core from the West Bay** (the lobe beside Biñan) shows **marine sediments deposited ~6,600–4,700 cal BP**, during the mid-Holocene highstand when sea level stood **above** today's.
  - Molluscs and diatoms show a **lagoon >10 m deep about 5,700 years ago**. It shallowed to intertidal by ~3,700–4,700 BP, became **brackish by ~4,100 BP**, and **fresh by ~3,100–3,000 BP** (freshwater snail *Vivipara burroughiana*).
  - The separation from Manila Bay came from **falling sea level combined with uplift on the West Marikina Valley Fault**. That uplift raised a sill: the Muntinlupa–Parañaque land strip.
  - Salinity pulses roughly every 250 years are attributed to tectonics, i.e. **movements of the same fault that crosses Biñan**.
  - Since the 1960s, salinity has been rising again: dry years, low lake levels and subsidence of the Pasig delta.
  - Jaraula, C.M.B., Siringan, F.P., Klingel, R., Sato, Y., Yokoyama, Y. (2014). "Records and causes of Holocene salinity shifts in Laguna de Bay, Philippines." *Quaternary International* 349: 207–220. doi:10.1016/j.quaint.2014.08.048. Record: https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/30255
  - Jaraula & Siringan (2005). "Transformation of Laguna Lake from a marine arm of Manila Bay to a freshwater system." *Proc. 1st National Congress on Philippine Lakes* (SEARCA), p. 124: https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/6127
  - UP thesis, "Changes in salinity and sediment supply in Laguna de Bay during the past 6,000 years": https://tuklas.up.edu.ph/Record/UP-99796217607611819
  - ⚠️ Preprint (not peer-reviewed): three marine phases between 6,510 and 4,590 years ago; a "hypersaline, anoxic" phase; uplift of the "Parañaque Land Strip". https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.21203/rs.3.rs-6897953/v1
  - ⚠️ **Highstand height in Manila Bay.** Usually given as +1 to +3 m around 6–4 ka in the Philippines; ❓ figure not confirmed for Manila Bay. Biñan's lakeshore (2 m) was therefore probably **under a sea lagoon** 6,000 years ago. Phrase it carefully ("the lake's shore was then an arm of the sea").
- ✅/⚠️ **Philippine paleoclimate and monsoon.**
  - **Palawan stalagmites** (Puerto Princesa Underground River caves): a **Younger Dryas** drier episode (~12.9–11.7 ka) and a record since ~13.7 ka (Nature Communications 2015). Rainfall rose through the Holocene (maritime monsoon).
    - Partin et al. 2015, *Nat. Commun.* 6: 8061: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9061
    - AGU 2012 abstract: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012AGUFMPP31C2044P
  - Palawan record vs **NE Luzon** (Ari-0 cave): anti-correlated on millennial scales (SW vs NE monsoon). GSA 2025 abstract, preliminary: https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2025SE/webprogram/Paper409192.html
  - ❓ **Lake Paoay** (Ilocos Norte) and Luzon pollen cores: not found in this pass.
  - Main message for the book: Biñan's monsoon has varied over millennia. Today's two seasons are a recent state.

## E. Changement climatique actuel / Current climate change (local data)

*ERA5/Open-Meteo series for Biñan's coordinates: computed separately by the main session, not here.*

- ✅ **Observed national warming (PAGASA).**
  - **+0.648 °C over 1951–2010** (≈ 0.11 °C per decade).
  - **+0.68 °C over 1951–2015**.
  - About +0.6 °C over 1991–2020 (2024 CMIP6 update).
  - **2024 was the hottest year on record in the Philippines** (PAGASA).

- ✅ **Projections.**
  - **2011 report** (A1B, vs 1971–2000): **+0.9 to 1.1 °C in the 2020s**, **+1.8 to 2.2 °C in the 2050s**. Includes **province-level** tables, Laguna among them: ❓ extract the Laguna rows from the PDF (Annex tables, "Region IV-A"). Typical signal for Luzon: a drier March–May and a wetter June–November.
  - **RCP** (CLIMPAT 2018, "Observed and Projected Climate Change in the Philippines"): mid-century **+0.9 to 1.9 °C (RCP4.5)** and **+1.2 to 2.3 °C (RCP8.5)**.
  - **CMIP6 2024 update** (SSP): up to **+4.4 °C** by end of century under SSP5-8.5 (vs 1981–2010).
  - Municipal data: **PAGASA CLIMAP / CliMap V2.0** (❓ URL to find on pagasa.dost.gov.ph, section Climate → Climate Change).
- ✅/⚠️ **Sea level and subsidence, Manila Bay.**
  - Manila tide gauge: about **2 mm/yr from 1902 to the early 1960s** (≈ global rate). After that, relative rise of about **1 m between 1964 and 2002** (~25 mm/yr), attributed mainly to **subsidence from groundwater pumping** (Siringan, quoted by Philstar 2006).
    - Rodolfo, K.S. & Siringan, F.P. (2006). "Global sea-level rise is recognised, but flooding from anthropogenic land subsidence is ignored around northern Manila Bay, Philippines." *Disasters* 30(1): 118–139. PDF: https://research.fit.edu/media/site-specific/researchfitedu/coast-climate-adaptation-library/asia-amp-indian-ocean/southeast-asia-amp-philippines/Rodolfo--Siringan.--2006.--Global-SLR--Flooding-in-the-Northern-Philippines.pdf
    - Philstar (15 Oct 2006): https://philstar.com/agriculture/2006/10/15/363094/up-experts-reinforce-findings-pampanga-delta-plain-camanava-sinking
  - 2023 figure: Manila Bay sea level "accelerating at **13.13 mm/yr**" (Philippine Engineering Journal): https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/pej/article/download/9137/8067. ⚠️ Period and method unclear.
  - InSAR: Manila subsided **>2 cm/yr in 2015–2020**, about 7× global sea-level rise (Tay et al. 2022, *Geophys. Res. Lett.*; news coverage: https://www.preventionweb.net/news/asia-coastal-cities-sinking-faster-sea-level-rise).
  - ⚠️ **Laguna lakeshore (Biñan/Santa Rosa): no published InSAR figure found.** The lakeshore towns (Muntinlupa to Calamba) pump heavily from wells for industry and subdivisions, so local subsidence is plausible. ❓ Look for: "InSAR Laguna Lake subsidence", "Calabarzon groundwater subsidence", NWRB groundwater studies, Metro Manila preprint 2026 (https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202603.0666).
  - For Biñan the relevant sea level is the **lake level**, not the sea itself: subsidence of the shore plus higher lake levels in floods equals longer flooding. The lake's salinity link to Manila Bay (part 2) also makes sea-level rise relevant: stronger backflow in dry years.
- ✅ **Heat index.** PAGASA categories: 42–51 °C "danger", ≥52 °C "extreme danger".
  - **NAS-UPLB, Los Baños** (~27 km SE, the nearest synoptic/agromet station) reached **50 °C on 15 April 2025**, the hottest in the country that day.
  - Los Baños moved schools online on 29–30 April 2024 because of the heat.
  - National record 2024: **53 °C at Iba, Zambales (28 April 2024)**. NCR: 46 °C at NAIA (24 April 2024).

### Ideas « Alam mo ba? » (additions)
4. **6,000 years ago, Laguna de Bay was an arm of the sea.** Marine shells lie under the West Bay's mud. The lake turned fresh only about 3,000 years ago, when the West Valley Fault, the very fault that crosses Biñan, raised the land between Muntinlupa and Parañaque (Jaraula et al. 2014).
5. **In 1880 an earthquake brought down Biñan's church, tribunal and every brick house** (Masó 1910 catalogue, ⚠️ check the wording).
6. **Biñan hosted the national earthquake drill on 28 September 2016**, at Juana Village, Brgy. San Francisco. That barangay is one of those the West Valley Fault passes through.
