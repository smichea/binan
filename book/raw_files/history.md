# History of Biñan City / Kasaysayan ng Lungsod ng Biñan

## Geography / Heograpiya

Biñán occupies a narrow strip of land of about **43.5 square kilometres** on the western shore of **Laguna de Bay**, some 35 kilometres south of Manila. From the lakeshore, barely 2 metres above sea level, the ground rises gently westward toward the tuff plateau of Carmona and Silang in Cavite; the old town centre, the Poblacion, stands at about 12 metres ([Biñan | PhilAtlas](https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/r04a/laguna/binan.html)). The city is bounded by San Pedro to the north, Santa Rosa to the south, Carmona, General Mariano Alvarez and Silang to the west, and the lake to the east. On a clear day its horizon is closed by volcanoes: **Mount Makiling** (1,090 m) about 27 km to the southeast, **Mount Banahaw** beyond it, and to the southwest the Tagaytay ridge, the rim of the caldera that hides **Taal Volcano**, only 39 km away ([Mount Makiling | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Makiling)).

This landscape was made by one of the most restless places on Earth. The Philippine islands are a mosaic of crustal blocks – geologists call it the **Philippine Mobile Belt** – caught between two great tectonic plates: the **Philippine Sea Plate** to the east and the **Sunda (Eurasian) Plate** to the west, which close in on each other by several centimetres a year, about 8 to 9 centimetres across the whole system ([Rangin et al. 1999, Geophysical Journal International](https://www.geologie.ens.fr/~rooke/NCRpdf4web/Rangin&al-1999.pdf)). Each plate dives beneath the islands along its own deep-sea trench: the **Manila Trench** west of Luzon and the **Philippine Trench** in the east, while the sideways part of the movement is absorbed by the 1,200-kilometre **Philippine Fault** ([Philippine fault system | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_fault_system)). This collision puts the country on the Pacific **“Ring of Fire”**, with some two dozen active volcanoes.

Biñán lies at the northern end of the **Macolod Corridor**, a belt of volcanoes about 40 km wide that runs from Taal to Laguna de Bay, where the crust is being stretched and magma rises easily ([Macolod Corridor | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macolod_Corridor)). The central part of the lake is itself a volcanic **caldera**, some 20 by 10 kilometres, between Talim Island and the Jala-Jala peninsula, left by great explosive eruptions whose dates are still debated – somewhere between about two million and thirty thousand years ago ([Laguna Caldera | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Caldera)). Ash and glowing avalanches from such eruptions built the layers of volcanic tuff on which western Biñán stands. The fire has not gone out: hot springs still bubble at Los Baños and Calamba, and when Taal erupted on **12 January 2020**, its ash fell on Biñán and classes were suspended across the province ([Heavy ashfall in Batangas, Cavite, Laguna | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=1211958)).

Beneath the city also runs a fault. The **West Valley Fault**, about 100 km long from Bulacan to Carmona, crosses Biñán through the barangays of Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) and Biñán ([LIST: Barangays near earthquake fault | GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/story/489334/scitech/science/list-barangays-in-metro-manila-nearby-provinces-near-earthquake-fault)). It can produce an earthquake of magnitude 7.2 and moves roughly every 400 to 600 years; its last great rupture is dated to **1658** ([West Valley Fault nears ‘ripe stage’ | DZRH](https://www.dzrh.com.ph/post/west-valley-fault-nears-ripe-stage-but-no-way-to-tell-when-it-will-move-phivolcs)). The region has been shaken many times since: the old Spanish catalogue of earthquakes records that on **20 July 1880** a shock “of destructive violence” struck “the towns surrounding Lake Bay, especially those south and west of the lake”, damaging churches, convents and courthouses ([Masó, Catalogue of Violent and Destructive Earthquakes in the Philippines, 1910 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18556)). Today most tremors felt in Biñán are weak, but the city prepares for the “Big One”: on 28 September 2016 it hosted the nationwide earthquake drill in Barangay San Francisco, one of the barangays the fault passes through ([Biñan quake drill | Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/moveph/147635-binan-pagyanig-quake-drill/)).

**Laguna de Bay** is the largest lake in the Philippines – about 911 km² – but also a very shallow one, only 2.5 to 2.8 metres deep on average. Twenty-one rivers feed it from a basin of some 3,820 km², and it has a single outlet, the **Pasig River**, which carries its water to Manila Bay ([Laguna de Bay | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_Bay)). In the dry season, when the lake falls below the level of the sea, the Pasig runs backward and **seawater flows into the lake**; the salt makes the mud settle and the water turns clear for a few months ([Santiago 1991, Turbidity and seawater intrusion in Laguna de Bay | SEAFDEC](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/1351)). For centuries the lake was Biñán’s other street, the road of the boats that carried rice and sugar to Manila, and its pantry: in the early 1960s, 95 per cent of the catch was made of three native fish – *ayungin*, *biyang puti* and *kanduli* – which have since given way to tilapia and bangus raised in pens and cages ([SEAFDEC, LakeCon 2003](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/6120)). The **Biñán River**, which rises in the Carmona uplands and crosses the Poblacion before reaching the lake, is one of the lake’s monitored tributaries – and one that often lacks oxygen because of the wastewater it carries ([Water quality of Laguna Lake’s tributaries | LB Times](https://lbtimes.ph/whole-of-community-approach-stricter-policy-implementation-sought-water-quality-of-laguna-lakes-tributaries-continue-to-deteriorate-%e2%88%92-llda-data/)).

The shape of this shore has changed over the ages. During the ice ages of the last two million years, the sea rose and fell by more than a hundred metres. Some 20,000 years ago it stood about 120 metres lower than today: the shallow seas of Southeast Asia were dry land, joining Borneo, Sumatra and Java to the continent, but Luzon always remained an island ([Robles et al. 2015 | ANU Open Research](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/handle/1885/14352)). When the ice melted, the sea came back – and went further than today. A sediment core drilled in the West Bay, the part of the lake in front of Biñán, shows that **between about 6,600 and 4,700 years ago the lake was an arm of the sea**, a salt lagoon more than ten metres deep connected to Manila Bay. It became brackish around 4,100 years ago and fresh around 3,000 years ago, as the sea fell back and the land between Muntinlupa and Parañaque was lifted by movements of the same West Valley Fault ([Jaraula et al. 2014, Records and causes of Holocene salinity shifts in Laguna de Bay | Quaternary International](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/30255)).

Today Biñán has a tropical climate with two clear seasons: a **dry season from November to April** and a **wet season from May to October**, when the southwest monsoon, the *habagat*, brings about 90 per cent of the year’s rain. At Sangley Point, the nearest weather station of PAGASA, the national weather service, the normal yearly rainfall is about 2,080 mm, with more than 400 mm in each of July and August ([Climatological Normals | PAGASA](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/climate/climatological-normals)). Typhoons cross the region between June and December. The worst in living memory was **Ondoy** (Ketsana) in September 2009, which raised the lake to about 14 metres on its gauge – close to the record of 1919 – and left 192 lakeshore barangays under water for weeks, with an expected decline of at least 144 days ([Laguna de Bay flooding to recede in 5 months | Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2009/10/10/512548/laguna-de-bay-flooding-recede-5-months)). Lakeshore towns lived it again in 2020 with Ulysses ([Like ‘Ondoy’ all over again | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1360021/like-ondoy-all-over-again)) and in October 2024 with Kristine ([A month after Kristine, Laguna barangay still flooded | ABS-CBN](https://www.abs-cbn.com/regions/2024/11/19/in-photos-a-month-after-kristine-laguna-barangay-still-flooded-1531)).

The climate is also changing. PAGASA measured a warming of about **0.65 °C across the Philippines between 1951 and 2010**, and **2024 was the hottest year ever recorded** in the country ([Climate Change in the Philippines, 2011 | PAGASA](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/iaas/ClimateChange_in_the_Philippines_MDGF_Report_2011.pdf)) ([2024 is hottest year in Philippines | ABS-CBN](https://abs-cbn.com/news/weather-traffic/2025/1/23/2024-is-hottest-year-in-philippines-pagasa-1717)). NASA’s climate reconstruction for Biñán’s own coordinates tells the same story: the average temperature of the years 2015–2024 was about 0.4 °C higher than that of 1981–1990, and 2024 was the warmest year of the series ([NASA POWER](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/)). PAGASA projects a further warming of about 2 °C by the middle of the century. The heat is already felt: on 15 April 2025, the heat index reached **50 °C** at Los Baños, 27 km away ([Los Baños sizzles with 50°C heat index | Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/4/15/los-banos-laguna-sizzles-with-50-c-heat-index-on-april-15)). And the water is rising: around Manila Bay, the relative sea level rose by about a metre between 1964 and 2002, mostly because the ground is sinking as groundwater is pumped out – a threat that also lengthens the floods of the lakeshore ([Rodolfo & Siringan 2006, Disasters](https://research.fit.edu/media/site-specific/researchfitedu/coast-climate-adaptation-library/asia-amp-indian-ocean/southeast-asia-amp-philippines/Rodolfo--Siringan.--2006.--Global-SLR--Flooding-in-the-Northern-Philippines.pdf)).

Volcanic ash, river silt and flat land made Biñán fertile: its soils, formed on volcanic tuff and ash, are deep and rich in clay, and most of its land is almost level ([Biñan | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi%C3%B1an)). For centuries this ground fed rice fields and sugarcane, irrigated by canals – the *patubigan* that gave its name to Barangay Tubigan ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)). In the last fifty years the expressways, the railway and the industrial parks have turned these fields into a city where all 24 barangays are now classified as urban. The chapters that follow tell how people came to live on this shore, between the fire, the fault and the lake.

Ang Biñán ay sumasakop sa isang makitid na lupain na humigit-kumulang **43.5 kilometro kuwadrado** sa kanlurang pampang ng **Lawa ng Laguna**, mga 35 kilometro sa timog ng Maynila. Mula sa baybayin ng lawa, na halos 2 metro lamang ang taas sa ibabaw ng dagat, dahan-dahang tumataas ang lupa pakanluran patungo sa talampas ng Carmona at Silang sa Cavite; ang lumang sentro ng bayan, ang Poblacion, ay nasa mga 12 metro ang taas. Hangganan nito ang San Pedro sa hilaga, ang Santa Rosa sa timog, ang Carmona, General Mariano Alvarez at Silang sa kanluran, at ang lawa sa silangan. Sa maaliwalas na araw, mga bulkan ang nakikita sa abot-tanaw: ang **Bundok Makiling** (1,090 metro) mga 27 kilometro sa timog-silangan, ang **Bundok Banahaw** sa likod nito, at sa timog-kanluran ang tagaytay ng Tagaytay, ang gilid ng kaldera na nagtatago sa **Bulkang Taal**, na 39 kilometro lamang ang layo.

Ang tanawing ito ay hinubog ng isa sa pinakamagulong bahagi ng mundo. Ang kapuluan ng Pilipinas ay binubuo ng magkakadikit na piraso ng crust ng daigdig – tinatawag ito ng mga heologo na **Philippine Mobile Belt** – na naiipit sa pagitan ng dalawang malalaking tectonic plate: ang **Philippine Sea Plate** sa silangan at ang **Sunda (Eurasian) Plate** sa kanluran, na nagkakalapit nang ilang sentimetro bawat taon, mga 8 hanggang 9 na sentimetro sa kabuuan. Bawat plate ay sumusuot sa ilalim ng mga pulo sa kani-kaniyang malalim na trench sa dagat: ang **Manila Trench** sa kanluran ng Luzon at ang **Philippine Trench** sa silangan, habang ang pahalang na bahagi ng paggalaw ay sinasalo ng 1,200-kilometrong **Philippine Fault**. Dahil sa banggaang ito, nasa **“Ring of Fire”** ng Pasipiko ang bansa, na may mga dalawampu’t apat na aktibong bulkan.

Nasa hilagang dulo ng **Macolod Corridor** ang Biñán, isang sinturon ng mga bulkan na mga 40 kilometro ang lapad mula Taal hanggang Lawa ng Laguna, kung saan nababanat ang crust at madaling umaakyat ang magma. Ang gitnang bahagi mismo ng lawa ay isang **kaldera** ng bulkan, mga 20 por 10 kilometro, sa pagitan ng Pulo ng Talim at ng tangway ng Jala-Jala, na iniwan ng malalakas na pagsabog na hindi pa tiyak ang petsa – sa pagitan ng mga dalawang milyon at tatlumpung libong taon na ang nakararaan. Ang abo at ang nagbabagang agos ng mga pagsabog na iyon ang bumuo sa mga patong ng batong tuff na kinatatayuan ng kanlurang Biñán. Hindi pa namamatay ang apoy: may mga bukal pa ring mainit sa Los Baños at Calamba, at nang pumutok ang Taal noong **Enero 12, 2020**, umulan ng abo sa Biñán at sinuspinde ang mga klase sa buong lalawigan.

May fault din sa ilalim ng lungsod. Ang **West Valley Fault**, na mga 100 kilometro ang haba mula Bulacan hanggang Carmona, ay dumaraan sa Biñán sa mga barangay ng Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) at Biñán. Maaari itong magdulot ng lindol na may lakas na 7.2 at gumagalaw nang humigit-kumulang bawat 400 hanggang 600 taon; noong **1658** ang huling malakas na paggalaw nito. Maraming ulit nang niyanig ang rehiyon mula noon: ayon sa lumang katalogo ng mga lindol, noong **Hulyo 20, 1880** ay isang lindol na “mapanira ang lakas” ang tumama sa “mga bayang nakapalibot sa Lawa ng Bay, lalo na sa timog at kanluran nito”, at nasira ang mga simbahan, kumbento at tribunal. Sa ngayon, mahina ang karamihan sa mga lindol na nararamdaman sa Biñán, ngunit naghahanda ang lungsod para sa “Big One”: noong Setyembre 28, 2016, dito ginanap ang pambansang earthquake drill, sa Barangay San Francisco, isa sa mga barangay na dinaraanan ng fault.

Ang **Lawa ng Laguna** ang pinakamalaking lawa sa Pilipinas – mga 911 kilometro kuwadrado – ngunit napakababaw, 2.5 hanggang 2.8 metro lamang ang karaniwang lalim. Dalawampu’t isang ilog ang nagpapadaloy ng tubig dito mula sa isang palanggana na mga 3,820 kilometro kuwadrado, at iisa lamang ang labasan nito, ang **Ilog Pasig**, na nagdadala ng tubig nito sa Look ng Maynila. Sa tag-araw, kapag bumaba ang lawa sa antas ng dagat, pabalik ang agos ng Pasig at **pumapasok ang tubig-dagat sa lawa**; pinalulubog ng asin ang putik kaya luminaw ang tubig sa loob ng ilang buwan. Sa loob ng maraming siglo, ang lawa ang naging isa pang lansangan ng Biñán, ang daan ng mga bangkang nagdadala ng bigas at asukal sa Maynila, at ang hapag-kainan nito: noong unang bahagi ng dekada 1960, 95 bahagdan ng huli ay tatlong katutubong isda – ayungin, biyang puti at kanduli – na napalitan na ngayon ng tilapia at bangus na inaalagaan sa mga baklad at kulungan. Ang **Ilog Biñán**, na nagmumula sa kabundukan ng Carmona at dumaraan sa Poblacion bago umabot sa lawa, ay isa sa mga binabantayang sangang-ilog ng lawa – at madalas itong kulang sa oxygen dahil sa maruming tubig na dala nito.

Nagbago ang hugis ng baybaying ito sa paglipas ng panahon. Sa mga panahon ng yelo sa nakalipas na dalawang milyong taon, tumaas at bumaba ang dagat nang mahigit isandaang metro. Mga 20,000 taon na ang nakararaan, mga 120 metro itong mas mababa kaysa ngayon: naging tuyong lupa ang mababababaw na dagat ng Timog-Silangang Asya at nadugtong ang Borneo, Sumatra at Java sa kontinente, ngunit nanatiling pulo ang Luzon. Nang matunaw ang yelo, bumalik ang dagat – at lumampas pa sa kinaroroonan nito ngayon. Ipinakikita ng isang core ng putik na hinukay sa West Bay, ang bahagi ng lawa sa harap ng Biñán, na **mga 6,600 hanggang 4,700 taon na ang nakararaan ay bahagi ng dagat ang lawa**, isang maalat na lagunang mahigit sampung metro ang lalim na karugtong ng Look ng Maynila. Naging bahagyang maalat ito mga 4,100 taon na ang nakararaan at naging tubig-tabang mga 3,000 taon na ang nakararaan, nang umatras ang dagat at iniangat ang lupa sa pagitan ng Muntinlupa at Parañaque ng paggalaw ng mismong West Valley Fault.

Sa ngayon, tropikal ang klima ng Biñán na may dalawang malinaw na panahon: ang **tag-araw mula Nobyembre hanggang Abril** at ang **tag-ulan mula Mayo hanggang Oktubre**, kapag dinadala ng habagat ang humigit-kumulang 90 bahagdan ng ulan sa buong taon. Sa Sangley Point, ang pinakamalapit na istasyon ng PAGASA, ang karaniwang dami ng ulan sa isang taon ay mga 2,080 milimetro, at mahigit 400 milimetro bawat isa sa Hulyo at Agosto. Dumaraan ang mga bagyo sa rehiyon mula Hunyo hanggang Disyembre. Ang pinakamatindi sa alaala ng mga tao ay ang **Ondoy** noong Setyembre 2009, na nagpaangat sa lawa sa mga 14 na metro sa panukat nito – malapit sa rekord noong 1919 – at nagpalubog sa 192 barangay sa baybayin nang ilang linggo, na inaasahang aabutin nang hindi bababa sa 144 na araw bago humupa. Naranasan itong muli ng mga bayan sa baybayin noong 2020 dahil sa Ulysses at noong Oktubre 2024 dahil sa Kristine.

Nagbabago rin ang klima. Sinukat ng PAGASA ang pag-init na mga **0.65 °C sa buong Pilipinas mula 1951 hanggang 2010**, at ang **2024 ang pinakamainit na taon** na naitala sa bansa. Ganito rin ang ipinakikita ng rekonstruksiyon ng klima ng NASA para sa mismong kinaroroonan ng Biñán: ang karaniwang temperatura noong 2015–2024 ay mga 0.4 °C na mas mataas kaysa noong 1981–1990, at ang 2024 ang pinakamainit na taon sa serye. Inaasahan ng PAGASA ang karagdagang pag-init na mga 2 °C pagsapit ng kalagitnaan ng siglo. Ramdam na ang init: noong Abril 15, 2025, umabot sa **50 °C** ang heat index sa Los Baños, 27 kilometro ang layo. At tumataas ang tubig: sa paligid ng Look ng Maynila, tumaas nang mga isang metro ang antas ng dagat kaugnay ng lupa mula 1964 hanggang 2002, pangunahin dahil lumulubog ang lupa sa labis na pagsipsip ng tubig sa ilalim nito – isang panganib na nagpapatagal din sa mga baha sa baybayin ng lawa.

Pinataba ng abo ng bulkan, ng banlik ng ilog at ng patag na lupa ang Biñán: ang mga lupa nito, na nabuo sa batong tuff at abo ng bulkan, ay malalim at mayaman sa luad, at halos patag ang karamihan ng lupain nito. Sa loob ng maraming siglo, binuhay ng lupang ito ang mga palayan at tubuhan na pinatutubigan ng mga kanal – ang *patubigan* na pinagmulan ng pangalan ng Barangay Tubigan. Sa nakalipas na limampung taon, ginawang lungsod ng mga expressway, ng riles at ng mga industrial park ang mga bukid na ito, at urban na ngayon ang lahat ng 24 na barangay. Isinasalaysay ng mga susunod na kabanata kung paano nanirahan ang mga tao sa baybaying ito, sa pagitan ng apoy, ng fault at ng lawa.

**Did You Know?** **Laguna de Bay is not a “bay”.** The name means “the lake of Bay”, after the lakeshore town of Bay, the first capital of the province. In the dry season the lake even drinks from the sea: the Pasig River flows backward and brings in salt water from Manila Bay ([Laguna de Bay | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_de_Bay)).

**Did You Know?** **One of the world’s most active volcanoes is Biñán’s neighbour.** Taal Volcano lies only about 39 km from the Poblacion, hidden behind the Tagaytay ridge – barely farther than Manila. The ash of its January 2020 eruption reached the city ([Taal Volcano | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taal_Volcano)).

## Prehistory / Sinaunang Panahon

The story of the first people on the shores of Laguna de Bay begins far away, in Africa, where the human family was born. Some 1.8 million years ago, early humans had already left Africa: their bones and tools have been found at Dmanisi, in Georgia, between Europe and Asia ([Ferring et al. 2011, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3127884)). *Homo erectus* reached the island of Java around 1.5 to 1.3 million years ago ([Matsu’ura et al. 2020, Science | EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/766424)). Java, Sumatra and Borneo could then be reached on foot, because the low seas of the ice ages joined them to the mainland. But further east, beyond an invisible frontier later called the **Wallace Line**, the islands – Sulawesi, Flores and the Philippines – were always separated from the continent by deep water. **Luzon has never been connected to any continent** ([Field Museum, 2016](https://www.fieldmuseum.org/about/press/worlds-greatest-concentration-unique-mammal-species-philippine-island)). To reach it, early humans had to cross the sea – by accident on rafts of vegetation, or on purpose; nobody knows.

And yet they came. In the Cagayan Valley of northern Luzon, in the town of Rizal, Kalinga (not to be confused with the province of Rizal near our lake), archaeologists found 57 stone tools and the skeleton of an extinct rhinoceros, *Rhinoceros philippinensis*, whose bones bear cut marks and were broken to reach the marrow. They are about **709,000 years old** – the oldest known trace of humans in the Philippines, left by people whose species we do not know ([Ingicco et al. 2018, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0072-8)). Much later, in Callao Cave, near Peñablanca in Cagayan, researchers found the teeth and bones of a small human with curved finger and toe bones. In 2019 they named it a new species, ***Homo luzonensis***, the human of Luzon. It lived at least 50,000 years ago, and perhaps more than 130,000 years ago according to a new dating published in 2023 ([Détroit et al. 2019, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1067-9)) ([Grün & Stringer 2023, Quaternary Science Reviews](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/direct-dating-of-human-fossils-and-the-ever-changing-story-of-hum/)). Like the tiny *Homo floresiensis* of Flores, it evolved alone on an island for a very long time.

Our own species, ***Homo sapiens***, left Africa much later, perhaps 70,000 to 50,000 years ago, and followed the coasts of Asia ([Bergström et al. 2021, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03244-5)). On Sulawesi, people painted a wild pig on a cave wall at least 45,500 years ago ([Brumm et al. 2021, Science Advances](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7806210)). In the Philippines, the oldest bone of a modern human is a shin bone from **Tabon Cave** in Palawan, dated to about 47,000 years ago, with a wide margin of error; the famous skull fragment known as “Tabon Man” is younger, about 16,500 years old ([Détroit et al. 2004, Comptes Rendus Palevol](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/en/periodiques/comptes-rendus-palevol/3/fasc8/upper-pleistocene-homo-sapiens-tabon-cave-palawan-philippines-description-and-dating-new-discoveries)). The newcomers met older humans on the way: the **Ayta Magbukon** of Bataan carry more DNA from the archaic **Denisovans** than any other people in the world. Genetics shows that the Philippines was peopled by **at least five waves** of migration over the last 50,000 years, beginning with the ancestors of today’s Agta, Ayta and Dumagat peoples ([Larena et al. 2021, Current Biology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8596304)) ([Larena et al. 2021, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2026132118)). Their descendants, the Dumagat and Remontado, still live in the Sierra Madre, east of the lake.

The land these people knew was not the one we see. During the last ice age, some 20,000 years ago, the sea stood about 120 metres lower than today and the coasts of Luzon lay far out; when the ice melted, the sea returned, and between about 6,600 and 4,700 years ago the part of the lake in front of Biñán was an arm of the sea, a salt lagoon connected to Manila Bay. It became the freshwater lake we know only about 3,000 years ago (see the chapter on geography) ([Jaraula et al. 2014, Quaternary International](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/30255)).

Around the time the lake turned fresh, new people arrived by sea. Between about 4,500 and 3,500 years ago, seafarers speaking **Austronesian** languages came down from Taiwan through the Batanes islands into northern Luzon ([Bellwood 2011, Current Anthropology](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/658181)). In the Cagayan Valley they left red-slipped pottery like that of eastern Taiwan and ornaments of Taiwanese jade ([Hung et al. 2007, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0707304104)). They knew the outrigger canoe and perhaps rice: the oldest grain of rice found in the Philippines, at Andarayan in Cagayan, is about 3,400 years old, although we do not know whether it was grown or gathered (Snow et al. 1986). Some scholars, following Wilhelm Solheim, prefer to see not one migration but a network of sea trade in which ideas travelled in every direction. Either way, **Tagalog is an Austronesian language**, a distant cousin of Malay, Javanese, Malagasy and Hawaiian: *mata* (eye), *lima* (five) and *bato* (stone) have relatives on islands from Taiwan to Hawaii.

Traces of these ancient times have been found around the lake. In the valleys of Baras, in Rizal province, stone tools collected in the early 20th century are thought, from their shape, to be between 50,000 and 20,000 years old, and small blades of obsidian and chert come from Baras, Tanay and Pililla ([Vitales 2013, Archaeological research in the Laguna de Bay area | Hukay](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/asp/article/view/3961)). On a cliff of volcanic tuff in Binangonan, the painter Carlos “Botong” Francisco reported in 1965 more than a hundred figures carved into the rock: the **Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs**, declared a National Cultural Treasure in 1973 and probably several thousand years old, although their exact age is unknown ([Angono Petroglyphs | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angono_Petroglyphs)). In 2013, in Barangay Puypuy in the town of Bay, on the southern shore, a family digging in their garden found hundreds of pieces of earthenware jars; archaeologists of the University of the Philippines identified them as burial jars and estimated, from their shape, that they could be some 2,800 years old ([Ancient jars found in Bay, Laguna | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/435837)). Far away in Palawan, the **Manunggul Jar**, made around 800 BCE, shows on its lid two figures in a boat carrying a soul to the afterlife.

And in Biñán? **No prehistoric site has yet been found or excavated in Biñán**, nor anywhere on the western shore of the lake, from San Pedro to Calamba. This does not mean that no one lived here: the old shore lies under metres of river silt and lake mud, part of it was under the sea six thousand years ago, and the city grew quickly over the fields before anyone looked. Any old pottery, stone tool or bone found while digging should be reported to the National Museum, as the National Cultural Heritage Act (Republic Act No. 10066) asks.

We can only imagine the life of the first people of the lakeshore, using what archaeology has found nearby and what the first Spanish chroniclers saw a few centuries later: fishing in the lake, gardens of taro, yam and banana, and later rice; houses raised on stilts; boats of planks sewn and pegged together, like the *balangay* of Butuan, the oldest of which is about 1,700 years old – the boat whose name became *barangay*, the village community ([The Butuan archaeological finds | UNESCO Silk Roads](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/the_butuan_archaeological_finds_profound_implications_for_philippines_and_southeast_asian_prehistory.pdf)); spirits and ancestors, the *anito*, honoured in rituals; the dead buried in jars. During the Metal Age, from about 500 BCE, iron tools, glass beads and gold came through trade. By around 900 CE, the lake was already part of a wider world of trade and writing – as a copper plate found at Lumban would one day reveal.

Ang kuwento ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng Lawa ng Laguna ay nagsisimula sa malayong Aprika, kung saan isinilang ang pamilya ng tao. Mga 1.8 milyong taon na ang nakararaan, nakaalis na sa Aprika ang mga sinaunang tao: natagpuan ang kanilang mga buto at kasangkapan sa Dmanisi, sa Georgia, sa pagitan ng Europa at Asya. Nakarating ang *Homo erectus* sa pulo ng Java mga 1.5 hanggang 1.3 milyong taon na ang nakararaan. Noon, maaaring lakarin ang Java, Sumatra at Borneo, dahil nadudugtong ang mga ito sa kontinente kapag mababa ang dagat sa panahon ng yelo. Ngunit sa dakong silangan, lampas sa isang di-nakikitang hangganan na tinawag kalaunang **Wallace Line**, laging nakahiwalay sa kontinente ang mga pulo – ang Sulawesi, Flores at ang Pilipinas – dahil sa malalim na dagat. **Kailanman ay hindi nadugtong ang Luzon sa anumang kontinente.** Upang marating ito, kinailangang tawirin ng mga sinaunang tao ang dagat – nang di-sinasadya sakay ng mga balsa ng halaman, o nang sinasadya; walang nakaaalam.

Ngunit dumating sila. Sa Lambak ng Cagayan sa hilagang Luzon, sa bayan ng Rizal, Kalinga (hindi ang lalawigan ng Rizal malapit sa ating lawa), nakatagpo ang mga arkeologo ng 57 kasangkapang bato at ng kalansay ng isang nawalang uri ng rhinoceros, ang *Rhinoceros philippinensis*, na may mga bakas ng hiwa sa buto at binasag upang makuha ang utak nito. Mga **709,000 taon na** ang tanda ng mga ito – ang pinakamatandang kilalang bakas ng tao sa Pilipinas, iniwan ng mga taong hindi natin alam ang uri. Pagkaraan ng mahabang panahon, sa Kuweba ng Callao malapit sa Peñablanca, Cagayan, natagpuan ng mga mananaliksik ang mga ngipin at buto ng isang maliit na tao na may mga baluktot na buto ng daliri sa kamay at paa. Noong 2019, pinangalanan nila itong bagong uri, ang ***Homo luzonensis***, ang tao ng Luzon. Nabuhay ito nang hindi bababa sa 50,000 taon na ang nakararaan, at baka mahigit pa sa 130,000 taon ayon sa isang bagong pagtataya noong 2023. Tulad ng maliit na *Homo floresiensis* ng Flores, nag-iisa itong umunlad sa isang pulo sa napakahabang panahon.

Ang sarili nating uri, ang ***Homo sapiens***, ay umalis sa Aprika nang mas huli, marahil 70,000 hanggang 50,000 taon na ang nakararaan, at sinundan ang mga baybayin ng Asya. Sa Sulawesi, nagpinta ang mga tao ng isang baboy-ramo sa dingding ng kuweba nang hindi bababa sa 45,500 taon na ang nakararaan. Sa Pilipinas, ang pinakamatandang buto ng modernong tao ay isang buto ng binti mula sa **Kuweba ng Tabon** sa Palawan, na tinatayang mga 47,000 taon na, bagaman malaki ang palugit; mas bata ang tanyag na piraso ng bungo na kilala bilang “Taong Tabon”, mga 16,500 taon lamang. Nakatagpo ng mga bagong dating ang mas sinaunang mga tao sa daan: ang mga **Ayta Magbukon** ng Bataan ang may pinakamaraming DNA ng sinaunang **Denisovan** sa buong mundo. Ipinakikita ng henetika na ang Pilipinas ay pinanirahan ng **hindi bababa sa limang alon** ng pandarayuhan sa nakalipas na 50,000 taon, simula sa mga ninuno ng mga Agta, Ayta at Dumagat ngayon. Ang kanilang mga inapo, ang mga Dumagat at Remontado, ay naninirahan pa rin sa Sierra Madre, sa silangan ng lawa.

Hindi ganito ang lupaing nakilala ng mga taong ito. Noong huling panahon ng yelo, mga 20,000 taon na ang nakararaan, mga 120 metrong mas mababa ang dagat kaysa ngayon at malayo ang mga baybayin ng Luzon; nang matunaw ang yelo, bumalik ang dagat, at mula mga 6,600 hanggang 4,700 taon na ang nakararaan, bahagi ng dagat ang lawa sa harap ng Biñán, isang maalat na lagunang karugtong ng Look ng Maynila. Naging lawang tubig-tabang ito na kilala natin mga 3,000 taon pa lamang na ang nakararaan (tingnan ang kabanata tungkol sa heograpiya).

Halos kasabay ng pagtabang ng lawa, may mga bagong taong dumating mula sa dagat. Mula mga 4,500 hanggang 3,500 taon na ang nakararaan, bumaba mula Taiwan, sa pamamagitan ng mga pulo ng Batanes, patungong hilagang Luzon ang mga manlalayag na nagsasalita ng mga wikang **Austronesyo**. Sa Lambak ng Cagayan, nag-iwan sila ng mapulang palayok na kahawig ng sa silangang Taiwan at ng mga palamuting yari sa jade ng Taiwan. Kilala nila ang bangkang may katig at marahil ang palay: ang pinakamatandang butil ng bigas na natagpuan sa Pilipinas, sa Andarayan, Cagayan, ay mga 3,400 taon na, bagaman hindi natin alam kung itinanim ito o tinipon lamang. May mga iskolar, kasunod ni Wilhelm Solheim, na mas nakikita hindi ang iisang pandarayuhan kundi isang lambat ng kalakalang pandagat kung saan naglakbay ang mga ideya sa lahat ng direksiyon. Alinman dito, **wikang Austronesyo ang Tagalog**, malayong pinsan ng Malay, Javanese, Malagasy at Hawaiian: may mga kamag-anak na salita ang *mata*, *lima* at *bato* sa mga pulo mula Taiwan hanggang Hawaii.

May mga bakas ng sinaunang panahong ito na natagpuan sa paligid ng lawa. Sa mga lambak ng Baras, sa lalawigan ng Rizal, may mga kasangkapang bato na tinipon noong unang bahagi ng ika-20 siglo na, batay sa hugis, ay tinatayang 50,000 hanggang 20,000 taon na, at may maliliit na talim na obsidiyana at chert mula sa Baras, Tanay at Pililla. Sa isang bangin ng batong tuff sa Binangonan, iniulat ng pintor na si Carlos “Botong” Francisco noong 1965 ang mahigit isandaang larawang inukit sa bato: ang **Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs**, na idineklarang Pambansang Yamang Pangkultura noong 1973 at marahil ilang libong taon na ang tanda, bagaman hindi tiyak ang eksaktong edad. Noong 2013, sa Barangay Puypuy sa bayan ng Bay, sa timog na baybayin, nakahukay ang isang pamilya ng daan-daang piraso ng tapayang luwad sa kanilang bakuran; kinilala ng mga arkeologo ng Unibersidad ng Pilipinas na mga tapayang libingan ang mga ito at tinantiya, batay sa hugis, na maaaring mga 2,800 taon na ang tanda. Sa malayong Palawan, ang **Banga ng Manunggul**, na ginawa noong mga 800 BCE, ay may dalawang pigura sa takip na nakasakay sa bangkang nagdadala ng kaluluwa sa kabilang buhay.

At sa Biñán? **Wala pang natatagpuan o nahuhukay na sinaunang pook sa Biñán**, ni saanman sa kanlurang baybayin ng lawa, mula San Pedro hanggang Calamba. Hindi ito nangangahulugang walang nanirahan dito: nasa ilalim ng ilang metrong banlik ng ilog at putik ng lawa ang lumang baybayin, bahagi nito ay nasa ilalim ng dagat anim na libong taon na ang nakararaan, at mabilis na lumaki ang lungsod sa ibabaw ng mga bukid bago pa may nakapaghanap. Anumang lumang palayok, kasangkapang bato o buto na matagpuan sa paghuhukay ay dapat iulat sa Pambansang Museo, ayon sa hinihingi ng National Cultural Heritage Act (Batas Republika Blg. 10066).

Maaari lamang nating isipin ang buhay ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng lawa, gamit ang natagpuan ng arkeolohiya sa malapit at ang nakita ng mga unang tagapagtalang Espanyol pagkaraan ng ilang siglo: pangingisda sa lawa, mga taniman ng gabi, ube at saging, at kalaunan ay palay; mga bahay na nakataas sa mga haligi; mga bangkang yari sa tablang tinahi at pinakuan ng tarugo, gaya ng *balangay* ng Butuan, na ang pinakamatanda ay mga 1,700 taon na – ang bangkang pinagmulan ng salitang *barangay*, ang pamayanan; mga espiritu at ninuno, ang mga *anito*, na pinararangalan sa mga ritwal; at ang mga patay na inililibing sa mga tapayan. Sa Panahon ng Metal, mula mga 500 BCE, dumating sa pamamagitan ng kalakalan ang mga kasangkapang bakal, mga butil na salamin at ginto. Pagsapit ng mga taong 900, bahagi na ang lawa ng isang mas malawak na mundo ng kalakalan at pagsulat – gaya ng ibubunyag balang araw ng isang platong tanso na natagpuan sa Lumban.

**Did You Know?** **The Philippines had its own human species.** *Homo luzonensis*, named in 2019 after the island of Luzon, lived in Callao Cave at least 50,000 years ago – perhaps more than 130,000 years ago. Its tiny teeth and curved finger bones show that it evolved apart from every other human ([Détroit et al. 2019, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1067-9)).

**Did You Know?** **The word *barangay* comes from a boat.** The *balangay* was a plank boat that carried a whole community; its name passed to the village it founded. The oldest balangay found at Butuan, in Mindanao, is about 1,700 years old ([The Butuan archaeological finds | UNESCO Silk Roads](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/the_butuan_archaeological_finds_profound_implications_for_philippines_and_southeast_asian_prehistory.pdf)).

## Pre-Colonial Era / Panahong Pre-Kolonyal

For the six centuries before the Spaniards came, no document names Biñán and no excavation has yet been made on its soil. What we know of life on this shore comes from its neighbours – **Tabuco** (today Cabuyao), Bay, Pila, Lumban – from Chinese and Spanish writers, and from the customs the Tagalog communities of the region shared. The land of present-day Biñán most likely belonged to Tabuco ([The evolution of Biñan from pueblo to city | Manila Standard](https://www.manilastandard.net/?p=314204584)). Its people lived in small settlements scattered along the lake and the rivers coming down from the hills: they fished in Laguna de Bay, planted rice and gardens, gathered fruits in the forest and hunted wild pig, and they were grouped in **barangays** led by chiefs, the **datu**.

The oldest written document ever found in the Philippines comes from this lake. In the late 1980s, a worker dredging sand near the mouth of the Lumbang River, at Lumban, found a thin copper plate covered with writing. It is dated to the year 822 of the Śaka era – **21 April 900** ([Santos 1996, The Date of the Laguna Copperplate Inscription | Philippine Studies](https://www.jstor.org/stable/42634198)). Written mostly in Old Malay, in the Kawi script of Java, with Sanskrit and Old Javanese or Old Tagalog words, the **Laguna Copperplate Inscription** is a receipt: on behalf of the chief of **Tundun** (Tondo), it frees the descendants of a man named Namwaran from a debt of one *kati* and eight *suwarna* of gold – about 865 grams – before witnesses from places called Pailah, Puliran and Binuangan ([Laguna Copperplate Inscription, after Postma 1992 | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Copperplate_Inscription)). Scholars still debate where these places were: Pailah may be Pila, on our lake, or Paila in Bulacan; none of them is Biñán. But the plate shows that by the year 900 the lake belonged to a world of writing, gold, debts and titles linked to Java and Sumatra. It is kept today at the National Museum in Manila.

The lake also belonged to the trade routes of Asia. Chinese annals mention merchants from a country called **Ma-i** as early as 971. In 1225 the Chinese official Zhao Rugua described Ma-i: big villages on both banks of a river, Chinese ships anchored before the “official place” where trade took place, local chiefs who received **white umbrellas** as gifts, and goods exchanged on credit – cotton, yellow wax, tortoiseshell, betel nuts and cloth against porcelain, gold, iron pots, lead, glass beads and needles. Ma-i is usually placed on Mindoro, but the historian Go Bon Juan has argued that it may have been **Ba-i**, the town of Bay that gave its name to our lake – an attractive but unproven idea ([Go 2005, Ma’i in Chinese Records – Mindoro or Bai? | Philippine Studies](https://web.archive.org/web/20131021221348/http://www.philippinestudies.net/ojs/index.php/ps/article/download/216/223)). What is certain is that at Pinagbayanan, in Pila, archaeologists excavated in 1967–68 some 241 graves of the 12th to 14th centuries, with Chinese porcelain of the Song and Yuan dynasties, cremations, net weights, spindle whorls and traces of wet-rice farming, cotton weaving and ironworking ([Vitales 2013, Archaeological research in the Laguna de Bay area | Hukay](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/asp/article/view/3961)).

In the 16th century the strongest ties of the Tagalog world ran toward **Brunei** and Islam. When the Spaniards reached Manila Bay in 1570–71, they found Maynila ruled by **Rajah Sulayman** and Tondo by **Lakandula**. A Spanish report of 1572 notes that the people of the coast did not eat pork “because they have had dealings with the Moros of Burney, who have preached to them a little of the teaching of Mahoma”, and that some who had been to Brunei could read a few words of the Koran ([Relation of the Conquest of the Island of Luzon, 1572, in Blair & Robertson vol. 3 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13616)). The influence reached the language itself: according to the great Tagalog dictionary of 1754, the word *binyag*, “to baptize”, “es de Borney” – it came from Brunei, where it meant to pour water from above in a Muslim ablution, before it was used for the Christian baptism ([Noceda & Sanlúcar, Vocabulario de la lengua tagala, 1754 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/vocabulariodelal00noce)).

The best description of Tagalog society was written on the shores of this lake: the Franciscan **Juan de Plasencia** signed his *Customs of the Tagalogs* at Nagcarlán, Laguna, on 21 October 1589. “This people always had chiefs, called by them *datos*,” he wrote; each ruled “sometimes as many as a hundred houses, sometimes even less than thirty”, a group “called in Tagalo a *barangay*”. Below the datu were the free **maharlika**, who followed him to war and rowed for him, the **aliping namamahay**, who had their own houses and gave him part of their harvest, and the **aliping sa gigilid**, who lived in his house and could be sold; one could fall into servitude through war or debt and buy one’s freedom back in gold. Some chiefs owned “fisheries, with established limits, and sections of the rivers for markets”; at Pila, the free men paid their datu a hundred measures of rice a year. Women kept their own property, received a dowry, the *bigaycaya*, and could divorce ([Plasencia, Customs of the Tagalogs, 1589, in Blair & Robertson vol. 7 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13701)). Settlements could be fortified: in 1571 Cainta, at the head of the lake, had about a thousand inhabitants behind thick bamboo, a wall and small cannons.

The Tagalogs worshipped **Bathala**, “maker of all things”, honoured the sun, the moon and the stars that marked the farming year – *Tala*, the morning star, and *Mapolon*, the Pleiades – and the spirits of their ancestors, the *anito*, represented by images called *lic-ha*. There were no temples: a house was prepared for each feast, led by a *catalonan*, often a woman of rank. The dead were mourned for four days and laid in a boat as a coffin. Men and women wore gold bracelets and anklets; around 1590 the illustrated *Boxer Codex* shows Tagalog couples in their *baro*, *saya* and *putong* ([Boxer Codex | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Codex)). Many could read and write in **baybayin**, the Tagalog script, which appears beside the Latin alphabet in the *Doctrina Christiana* of 1593, the first book printed in the Philippines ([Doctrina Christiana, 1593 | Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/48031307/)).

This world changed in 1571. Miguel López de Legazpi settled in Manila in May. On **15 August 1571**, his young captain **Juan de Salcedo** left with a galley, a hundred soldiers and three pieces of artillery against Cainta, which he took after a short siege; about four hundred of its defenders, men and women, were killed. The same report then states that “the villages about this lake, containing about twenty-four or twenty-six thousand men, were pacified by the captain Juan de Sauzedo” ([Relation of the Conquest of the Island of Luzon, 1572 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13616)). The lake towns were soon given as *encomiendas* to Spanish settlers: Pila in November 1571, and in 1591 **Tabuco** was held by Don Luis Enríquez with a thousand tributes, about four thousand people – Biñán was probably among them, though it is not named ([Account of the encomiendas, 1591, in Blair & Robertson vol. 8 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13742)). From 1578, the Franciscans of Juan de Plasencia founded missions all around the lake. A local tradition says that Salcedo and missionaries stopped on the site of Biñán, planted a cross and baptized its people, and that the place was called *Binyagan*, the “place of baptism”; the documents of the time confirm Salcedo’s journey around the lake in the second half of 1571, but they do not mention Biñán.

Sa loob ng anim na siglo bago dumating ang mga Espanyol, walang dokumentong bumabanggit sa Biñán at wala pang paghuhukay na ginagawa sa lupa nito. Ang alam natin tungkol sa buhay sa baybaying ito ay nagmumula sa mga kapitbahay nito – ang **Tabuco** (Cabuyao ngayon), Bay, Pila at Lumban – sa mga manunulat na Tsino at Espanyol, at sa mga kaugaliang pinagsasaluhan ng mga pamayanang Tagalog sa rehiyon. Malamang na bahagi ng Tabuco ang lupain ng Biñán ngayon. Nanirahan ang mga tao nito sa maliliit na pamayanang nakakalat sa tabi ng lawa at ng mga ilog na bumababa mula sa kabundukan: nangingisda sila sa Lawa ng Laguna, nagtatanim ng palay at gulay, nangunguha ng prutas sa gubat at nangangaso ng baboy-ramo, at nakapangkat sila sa mga **barangay** na pinamumunuan ng mga **datu**.

Mula sa lawang ito ang pinakamatandang nakasulat na dokumentong natagpuan sa Pilipinas. Sa huling bahagi ng dekada 1980, isang manggagawang humuhukay ng buhangin malapit sa bunganga ng Ilog Lumbang, sa Lumban, ang nakatagpo ng isang manipis na platong tanso na puno ng sulat. Ito ay may petsang taong 822 ng panahong Śaka – **Abril 21, 900**. Nakasulat ito pangunahin sa Lumang Malay, sa titik Kawi ng Java, na may mga salitang Sanskrit at Lumang Javanese o Lumang Tagalog. Ang **Inskripsiyon sa Binatbat na Tanso ng Laguna** ay isang katibayan ng pagbabayad: sa ngalan ng pinuno ng **Tundun** (Tondo), pinalalaya nito ang mga inapo ng isang lalaking nagngangalang Namwaran mula sa utang na isang *kati* at walong *suwarna* ng ginto – mga 865 gramo – sa harap ng mga saksi mula sa mga lugar na tinatawag na Pailah, Puliran at Binuangan. Pinagtatalunan pa ng mga iskolar kung nasaan ang mga lugar na ito: maaaring ang Pailah ay ang Pila, sa ating lawa, o ang Paila sa Bulacan; wala sa mga ito ang Biñán. Ngunit ipinakikita ng plato na pagsapit ng taong 900, bahagi na ang lawa ng isang mundo ng pagsulat, ginto, utang at mga titulong kaugnay ng Java at Sumatra. Iniingatan ito ngayon sa Pambansang Museo sa Maynila.

Bahagi rin ang lawa ng mga ruta ng kalakalan ng Asya. Binabanggit ng mga talaang Tsino ang mga mangangalakal mula sa bansang tinatawag na **Ma-i** noon pang 971. Noong 1225, inilarawan ng opisyal na Tsinong si Zhao Rugua ang Ma-i: malalaking nayon sa magkabilang pampang ng isang ilog, mga barkong Tsino na nakadaong sa harap ng “opisyal na lugar” kung saan nagaganap ang kalakalan, mga lokal na pinunong binibigyan ng **puting payong** bilang regalo, at mga kalakal na ipinagpapalit nang pautang – bulak, dilaw na pagkit, kabibi ng pagong, bunga ng nganga at tela kapalit ng porselana, ginto, kaldero ng bakal, tingga, butil na salamin at karayom. Karaniwang inilalagay ang Ma-i sa Mindoro, ngunit iminungkahi ng mananalaysay na si Go Bon Juan na maaaring ito ang **Ba-i**, ang bayan ng Bay na nagbigay ng pangalan sa ating lawa – isang kaakit-akit ngunit hindi pa napatutunayang ideya. Ang tiyak ay sa Pinagbayanan, sa Pila, nahukay ng mga arkeologo noong 1967–68 ang mga 241 libingan mula ika-12 hanggang ika-14 na siglo, na may porselanang Tsino ng mga dinastiyang Song at Yuan, mga labi ng pagsusunog ng bangkay, mga pabigat ng lambat, mga ikiran ng sinulid at mga bakas ng pagsasaka ng palay sa basang bukid, paghahabi ng bulak at paggawa ng bakal.

Noong ika-16 na siglo, patungong **Brunei** at Islam ang pinakamalakas na ugnayan ng mundong Tagalog. Nang dumating ang mga Espanyol sa Look ng Maynila noong 1570–71, nadatnan nilang pinamumunuan ni **Rajah Sulayman** ang Maynila at ni **Lakandula** ang Tondo. Ayon sa isang ulat Espanyol noong 1572, hindi kumakain ng baboy ang mga tao sa baybayin “dahil nakipag-ugnayan sila sa mga Moro ng Burney, na nangaral sa kanila nang kaunti tungkol sa aral ni Mahoma”, at may ilang nakarating sa Brunei na nakababasa ng ilang salita ng Koran. Umabot ang impluwensiyang ito sa wika mismo: ayon sa malaking diksiyonaryong Tagalog noong 1754, ang salitang *binyag* ay “es de Borney” – galing ito sa Brunei, kung saan ang ibig sabihin ay pagbubuhos ng tubig mula sa itaas sa isang paglilinis na Muslim, bago ito ginamit para sa binyag na Kristiyano.

Isinulat sa baybayin ng lawang ito ang pinakamahusay na paglalarawan ng lipunang Tagalog: nilagdaan ng Pransiskanong si **Juan de Plasencia** ang kaniyang *Mga Kaugalian ng mga Tagalog* sa Nagcarlán, Laguna, noong Oktubre 21, 1589. “Laging may mga pinuno ang bayang ito, na tinatawag nilang *datos*,” isinulat niya; ang bawat isa ay namumuno “kung minsan sa hanggang isandaang bahay, kung minsan ay kulang pa sa tatlumpu”, isang pangkat na “tinatawag sa Tagalog na *barangay*”. Sa ilalim ng datu ay ang malalayang **maharlika**, na sumasama sa kaniya sa digmaan at sumasagwan para sa kaniya; ang mga **aliping namamahay**, na may sariling bahay at nagbibigay sa kaniya ng bahagi ng kanilang ani; at ang mga **aliping sa gigilid**, na nakatira sa kaniyang bahay at maaaring ipagbili. Maaaring maging alipin dahil sa digmaan o utang, at maaaring tubusin ang sariling kalayaan sa pamamagitan ng ginto. May mga pinunong nagmamay-ari ng “mga palaisdaan na may takdang hangganan, at mga bahagi ng ilog para sa pamilihan”; sa Pila, nagbabayad ang mga malayang tao sa kanilang datu ng isandaang takal ng palay bawat taon. Nananatili sa kababaihan ang sarili nilang ari-arian, tumatanggap sila ng bigay-kaya, at maaari silang makipaghiwalay. Maaaring may kuta ang mga pamayanan: noong 1571, ang Cainta, sa ulunan ng lawa, ay may mga isanlibong naninirahan sa likod ng makapal na kawayan, isang pader at maliliit na kanyon.

Sinasamba ng mga Tagalog si **Bathala**, “ang lumikha ng lahat ng bagay”, iginagalang ang araw, ang buwan at ang mga bituing nagtatakda ng taon ng pagsasaka – ang *Tala*, ang bituin sa umaga, at ang *Mapolon*, ang Pleyades – at ang mga espiritu ng kanilang mga ninuno, ang mga *anito*, na kinakatawan ng mga larawang tinatawag na *lic-ha*. Walang templo: inihahanda ang isang bahay para sa bawat pagdiriwang, na pinangungunahan ng isang *catalonan*, na kadalasang babaeng may mataas na katayuan. Ipinagluluksa ang mga patay nang apat na araw at inihihimlay sa isang bangka bilang kabaong. Nagsusuot ang mga lalaki at babae ng mga pulseras at kadena sa bukung-bukong na ginto; noong mga 1590, ipinakikita ng may larawang *Boxer Codex* ang mga mag-asawang Tagalog na nakasuot ng *baro*, *saya* at *putong*. Marami ang marunong bumasa at sumulat sa **baybayin**, ang sulat Tagalog, na makikita sa tabi ng alpabetong Latin sa *Doctrina Christiana* ng 1593, ang unang aklat na inilimbag sa Pilipinas.

Nagbago ang mundong ito noong 1571. Nanirahan si Miguel López de Legazpi sa Maynila noong Mayo. Noong **Agosto 15, 1571**, umalis ang kaniyang batang kapitang si **Juan de Salcedo** sakay ng isang galera, kasama ang isandaang sundalo at tatlong kanyon, laban sa Cainta, na nasakop niya pagkaraan ng maikling pagkubkob; mga apat na raan sa mga tagapagtanggol nito, lalaki at babae, ang napatay. Sinasabi pa ng parehong ulat na “ang mga nayon sa paligid ng lawang ito, na may mga dalawampu’t apat o dalawampu’t anim na libong lalaki, ay pinatahimik ni Kapitan Juan de Sauzedo”. Di nagtagal, ipinagkaloob ang mga bayan sa lawa bilang *encomienda* sa mga Espanyol: ang Pila noong Nobyembre 1571, at noong 1591 ay hawak ni Don Luis Enríquez ang **Tabuco** na may isanlibong tributo, mga apat na libong katao – malamang na kasama rito ang Biñán, bagaman hindi ito binabanggit. Mula 1578, nagtatag ang mga Pransiskano ni Juan de Plasencia ng mga misyon sa buong paligid ng lawa. Ayon sa isang lokal na tradisyon, huminto si Salcedo at ang mga misyonero sa pook ng Biñán, nagtirik ng krus at nagbinyag sa mga tao nito, kaya tinawag ang lugar na *Binyagan*, ang “lugar ng binyag”; pinatutunayan ng mga dokumento noong panahong iyon ang paglalakbay ni Salcedo sa paligid ng lawa sa ikalawang hati ng 1571, ngunit hindi nila binabanggit ang Biñán.

**Did You Know?** **The oldest document of the Philippines is about a debt of gold.** In the year 900, on behalf of the chief of Tondo, a copper plate cleared the descendants of Namwaran of a debt of about 865 grams of gold. It was found by chance, more than a thousand years later, by a worker dredging sand at Lumban, on the other shore of our lake ([Laguna Copperplate Inscription | Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Copperplate_Inscription)).

**Did You Know?** **Chinese merchants gave white umbrellas to local chiefs.** According to Zhao Rugua, writing in 1225, traders arriving in Ma-i offered white umbrellas to the chiefs before doing business – and then left their goods on credit until the next season.

**Did You Know?** **The word *binyag* came from Brunei.** The Tagalog dictionary of 1754 notes that *binyag* “es de Borney”: it first named a Muslim ablution of pouring water from above. Local tradition derives the name of Biñán from *binyagan*, “the place of baptism” – a beautiful story that the documents of 1571 do not record ([Noceda & Sanlúcar 1754 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/vocabulariodelal00noce)).

## Spanish Colonial Period / Panahon ng Kolonyalismong Espanyol

After Juan de Salcedo’s campaign around the lake in 1571, the lakeshore towns were given as *encomiendas* to Spanish settlers, and missionaries began to work among them. The land of Biñán was then part of **Tabuco** (Cabuyao). Spanish documents write its name in many ways – *Biñan*, *Biñang*, *Viñan* – and a local tradition, recalled in the previous chapter, links it to *binyagan*, “the place of baptism”.

From the 1640s, the Dominican friars of the **College of Santo Tomás** in Manila – the future University of Santo Tomás – assembled a great estate in the territory of Tabuco, in three stages between 1641 and 1677 (the date usually given is 1644). Its income supported the college. The **Hacienda de Biñán** stretched from the shore of the lake to the hills of Cavite and from San Pedro Tunasan to the south of Santa Rosa, which made up more than half of it ([Santiago 2005, The Last Aspirants: Biñán and Santa Rosa | Philippine Quarterly of Culture and Society](https://philippinevillas.blogspot.com/2007/06/epilogue-last-aspirants-bin-and-santa.html)). To farm it, the Dominicans also settled Christian Chinese from the Parián of Manila. One of them, **Domingo Lam-co**, cleared the land of what is now Barangay Tubigan; his son **Francisco Mercado** headed the town in 1783, and his descendants would include José Rizal ([Craig 1913, Lineage, Life and Labors of José Rizal | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6867)).

The estate also brought conflict. In 1743, when the lands of “**the estate of Biñan, which is owned by the religious of St. Dominic**” were surveyed, a court clerk named Juan Monroy measured them with false units that gave the friars more than half of the lands of the neighbouring town of **Silang**. The quarrel set the countryside on fire: in 1745 the people of Silang attacked the lands of the hacienda, and revolts spread to Taguig, Parañaque, Bacoor, Imus, San Mateo and much of Bulacan – the largest uprising of Hispanicized Filipinos before 1896. The judge **Pedro Calderón Enríquez**, sent to investigate, punished the clerk, declared the usurped lands to belong to the Crown and returned them to the villages; in 1751 the king approved his decisions ([Royal decree of 7 November 1751, in Blair & Robertson vol. 48 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54740)) ([Lynch 1988, Land Rights, Land Laws and Land Usurpation | Philippine Law Journal](https://philippinelawjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/63PLJ082_LYNCH.pdf)). But the friar estates themselves remained.

Meanwhile Biñán became a town in its own right. In **1688**, when the province of La Laguna was reorganized, it was separated from Tabuco, and on **4 February 1747** it became a full *pueblo*, with its own town centre (*población*) and barrios – the date the city still celebrates as its founding anniversary ([Biñan: ‘The City of Life’ returns | BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2023/02/20/binan-the-city-of-life-returns-with-a-brighter-future-yet-to-unfold/)). Tradition names Father José Monroy, in 1757, as its first parish priest and Antonio de Santa Rosa as its first *capitán*. In 1791–92 the southern barrio of **Santa Rosa** was in turn separated to form a new town.

The wealth of the hacienda was **rice**. Around 1850, the geographers Buzeta and Bravo wrote that the Dominicans owned “all the land of Biñan” and that “it is rice that makes its wealth”, harvests yielding fifty or sixty times the seed thanks to the river that comes down from Mount Sungay and its many tributaries; sugar cane, fruit trees and vegetables grew in the house gardens. Tenants (*inquilinos*) cleared and farmed the land rent-free for three or four years, then paid five *cavans* of rice for each *cabalita* of irrigated land – hardly a tenth of the harvest – and could not be evicted unless they failed to pay for two years running. Many tenants were rich men who worked their land through sharecroppers, the *kasamá* ([Buzeta & Bravo, Diccionario geográfico… de las Islas Filipinas, 1850, art. “Biñan”, quoted on Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi%C3%B1%C3%A1n)). The estate is said to have had a dozen dams; two survive, at **Timbao** and **Soro-soro**, declared local heritage in 2023 ([Biñan leads the way to heritage conservation efforts | Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)).

By the 19th century Biñán was a town of **mestizos**, prosperous Chinese-Filipino families who owned the best houses, “not a few of stone and tile, a luxury found in few towns”, with gardens of fruit trees. It counted **10,948 souls** around 1850. Its church, “of good construction”, was served by Filipino secular priests, while the house of the Dominican hacienda stood against it, joined to it through the choir loft. Fine roads (*calzadas*) led to the neighbouring towns, and the surplus of rice and the cloth woven by the women went to Manila on the lake, by *casco* barges and later by steamboats. In 1858–59, the British governor of Hong Kong, **Sir John Bowring**, stayed with **Don José Alberto**, Rizal’s uncle, and noted “an active trade between Biñan and Manila” ([Bowring 1859, A Visit to the Philippine Islands | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46695)). The town’s families included the Mercado, Alonso and Alberto, who served as its heads, and it was the birthplace of the priest **Modesto de Castro** (1819), the father of Tagalog prose and author of *Urbana at Felisa*, of **Francisco Mercado** (1818), Rizal’s father, who moved to Calamba ([Francisco Mercado | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/francisco-mercado-1818-1898/)), and of the lawyer **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista** (1830).

In 1870 a nine-year-old boy arrived from Calamba to study Latin: **José Rizal**. In his memoirs he describes the aunt over seventy who lodged him, his teacher **Justiniano Aquino Cruz**, “a tall, thin man with a long neck and a sharp nose”, his fight with the teacher’s son, the old painter Juancho who let him help with his pictures, and his days: Mass at four in the morning, class until ten and again in the afternoon, rice and two dried sardines for breakfast. He left Biñán by the steamer *Talim* on **Saturday, 17 December 1870** ([Rizal’s Own Story of His Life, 1918 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/48438)). The marker set up in 1948 at the site of his school gives 1871, and textbooks often say 1869. In his novel *Noli me tangere*, Rizal later recalled that “in the year 70, when lightning struck in Biñán”, it fell on the church tower and destroyed the clock and an altar.

Spanish rule ended in revolution. In 1896–97, Biñán and Santa Rosa served as supply bases and field hospitals for the Spanish troops fighting the revolutionaries in Cavite, and on 19 January 1898 the provincial junta even proposed to make them *villas* with the motto “**Siempre fiel y leal**” – “ever faithful and loyal” ([Santiago 2005](https://philippinevillas.blogspot.com/2007/06/epilogue-last-aspirants-bin-and-santa.html)). The request was shelved when Emilio Aguinaldo returned. A few months later, on 12 June 1898, a son of Biñán, **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista**, wrote and read the Act of the Proclamation of Philippine Independence at Kawit ([Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/ambrosio-rianzares-bautista-1830-1903-2/)), and by the end of August the whole province of La Laguna was in Filipino hands. The fate of the friar lands would be settled under the Americans.

Matapos ang paglalakbay ni Juan de Salcedo sa paligid ng lawa noong 1571, ipinagkaloob bilang mga *encomienda* sa mga Espanyol ang mga bayan sa baybayin, at nagsimulang mangaral ang mga misyonero sa kanila. Bahagi noon ng **Tabuco** (Cabuyao) ang lupain ng Biñán. Iba-iba ang pagsulat ng pangalan nito sa mga dokumentong Espanyol – *Biñan*, *Biñang*, *Viñan* – at iniuugnay ito ng isang lokal na tradisyon, na binanggit sa nakaraang kabanata, sa *binyagan*, “ang lugar ng binyag”.

Mula dekada 1640, unti-unting binuo ng mga paring Dominiko ng **Kolehiyo ng Santo Tomás** sa Maynila – ang magiging Unibersidad ng Santo Tomás – ang isang malawak na asyenda sa nasasakupan ng Tabuco, sa tatlong yugto mula 1641 hanggang 1677 (karaniwang ibinibigay ang taong 1644). Ang kita nito ang tumutustos sa kolehiyo. Umaabot ang **Asyenda ng Biñán** mula sa baybayin ng lawa hanggang sa kaburulan ng Cavite at mula San Pedro Tunasan hanggang sa timog ng Santa Rosa, na mahigit kalahati ng kabuuan nito. Upang sakahin ito, pinanirahan din ng mga Dominiko rito ang mga Tsinong Kristiyano mula sa Parián ng Maynila. Isa sa kanila, si **Domingo Lam-co**, ang naglinis ng lupain ng tinatawag ngayong Barangay Tubigan; ang kaniyang anak na si **Francisco Mercado** ang namuno sa bayan noong 1783, at kabilang sa kaniyang mga inapo si José Rizal.

Nagdala rin ng alitan ang asyenda. Noong 1743, nang sukatin ang mga lupain ng “asyenda ng Biñán, na pag-aari ng mga paring Dominiko”, gumamit ang isang kawani ng hukuman na si Juan Monroy ng huwad na sukat na nagbigay sa mga prayle ng mahigit kalahati ng mga lupain ng karatig-bayang **Silang**. Nag-alab ang kanayunan dahil sa alitang ito: noong 1745, sinalakay ng mga taga-Silang ang mga lupain ng asyenda, at kumalat ang mga pag-aalsa sa Taguig, Parañaque, Bacoor, Imus, San Mateo at malaking bahagi ng Bulacan – ang pinakamalaking pag-aalsa ng mga Pilipinong Kristiyano bago ang 1896. Ang hukom na si **Pedro Calderón Enríquez**, na ipinadala upang magsiyasat, ay nagparusa sa kawani, ipinahayag na pag-aari ng Korona ang mga lupaing inagaw at ibinalik ang mga ito sa mga nayon; noong 1751, sinang-ayunan ng hari ang kaniyang mga pasya. Ngunit nanatili ang mga asyenda ng mga prayle.

Samantala, naging ganap na bayan ang Biñán. Noong **1688**, nang muling isaayos ang lalawigan ng La Laguna, inihiwalay ito sa Tabuco, at noong **Pebrero 4, 1747** ay naging ganap itong *pueblo*, na may sariling kabayanan (*población*) at mga baryo – ang petsang ipinagdiriwang pa rin ng lungsod bilang anibersaryo ng pagkakatatag nito. Ayon sa tradisyon, si Padre José Monroy, noong 1757, ang unang kura paroko nito at si Antonio de Santa Rosa ang unang *kapitan*. Noong 1791–92, inihiwalay naman ang timog na baryo ng **Santa Rosa** upang maging bagong bayan.

**Palay** ang yaman ng asyenda. Noong mga 1850, isinulat ng mga heograpong sina Buzeta at Bravo na pag-aari ng mga Dominiko “ang buong lupain ng Biñán” at na “palay ang bumubuo sa yaman nito”: limampu o animnapung ulit ng binhi ang ani dahil sa ilog na bumababa mula sa Bundok Sungay at sa maraming sanga nito; sa mga bakuran naman tumutubo ang tubo, mga punong-kahoy na namumunga at mga gulay. Ang mga umuupa (*inquilino*) ay naglilinis at nagsasaka ng lupa nang walang upa sa loob ng tatlo o apat na taon, pagkatapos ay nagbabayad ng limang kaban ng palay sa bawat *kabalita* ng lupang may patubig – halos ikasampu lamang ng ani – at hindi sila maaaring paalisin malibang hindi sila makabayad nang dalawang magkasunod na taon. Marami sa mga umuupa ay mayayamang nagpapasaka ng kanilang lupa sa pamamagitan ng mga **kasamá**. Sinasabing may isang dosenang prinsa ang asyenda; dalawa ang natitira, sa **Timbao** at **Soro-soro**, na idineklarang lokal na pamana noong 2023.

Pagsapit ng ika-19 na siglo, bayan na ng mga **mestizo** ang Biñán, mga maunlad na pamilyang Tsino-Pilipino na nagmamay-ari ng pinakamagagandang bahay, “hindi iilan ang yari sa bato at tisa, isang karangyaang matatagpuan sa iilang bayan lamang”, na may mga halamanan ng punong namumunga. Mayroon itong **10,948 kaluluwa** noong mga 1850. Ang simbahan nito, “na mahusay ang pagkakagawa”, ay pinaglilingkuran ng mga paring sekular na Pilipino, samantalang nakadikit dito ang bahay ng asyenda ng mga Dominiko, na nakakonekta rito sa pamamagitan ng koro. May magagandang daan (*calzada*) patungo sa mga karatig-bayan, at dinadala sa Maynila sa pamamagitan ng lawa ang sobrang palay at ang telang hinabi ng kababaihan, sakay ng mga *casco* at kalaunan ng mga bapor. Noong 1858–59, tumuloy ang gobernador ng Hong Kong na si **Sir John Bowring** sa bahay ni **Don José Alberto**, tiyuhin ni Rizal, at napansin niya ang “masiglang kalakalan sa pagitan ng Biñán at Maynila”. Kabilang sa mga pamilya ng bayan ang mga Mercado, Alonso at Alberto, na naglingkod bilang mga pinuno nito, at dito isinilang ang paring si **Modesto de Castro** (1819), ang ama ng tuluyang Tagalog at may-akda ng *Urbana at Felisa*, si **Francisco Mercado** (1818), ama ni Rizal, na lumipat sa Calamba, at ang abogadong si **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista** (1830).

Noong 1870, dumating mula Calamba ang isang siyam na taong gulang na batang lalaki upang mag-aral ng Latin: si **José Rizal**. Sa kaniyang mga alaala, inilalarawan niya ang kaniyang tiyahing mahigit pitumpung taong gulang na nagpatuloy sa kaniya, ang kaniyang gurong si **Justiniano Aquino Cruz**, “isang matangkad at payat na lalaki na mahaba ang leeg at matulis ang ilong”, ang pakikipag-away niya sa anak ng guro, ang matandang pintor na si Juancho na nagpatulong sa kaniya sa mga larawan nito, at ang kaniyang mga araw: misa sa ikaapat ng umaga, klase hanggang ikasampu at muli sa hapon, kanin at dalawang tuyo sa almusal. Umalis siya sa Biñán sakay ng bapor na *Talim* noong **Sabado, Disyembre 17, 1870**. Ang panandang itinayo noong 1948 sa pook ng kaniyang paaralan ay nagsasabing 1871, at madalas sabihin ng mga aklat-aralin na 1869. Sa kaniyang nobelang *Noli me tangere*, ginunita ni Rizal na “noong taong ’70, nang tamaan ng kidlat ang Biñán”, tumama ito sa kampanaryo ng simbahan at sinira ang orasan at isang altar.

Nagwakas sa rebolusyon ang pamamahala ng Espanya. Noong 1896–97, nagsilbing himpilan ng panustos at pansamantalang ospital ang Biñán at Santa Rosa para sa mga sundalong Espanyol na lumalaban sa mga rebolusyonaryo sa Cavite, at noong Enero 19, 1898, iminungkahi pa ng junta ng lalawigan na gawin silang mga *villa* na may sawikaing “**Siempre fiel y leal**” – “laging tapat at matapat”. Isinantabi ang kahilingan nang bumalik si Emilio Aguinaldo. Pagkaraan ng ilang buwan, noong Hunyo 12, 1898, isang anak ng Biñán, si **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista**, ang sumulat at bumasa ng Acta de la Proclamación de la Independencia sa Kawit, at pagsapit ng katapusan ng Agosto ay nasa kamay na ng mga Pilipino ang buong lalawigan ng La Laguna. Pagpapasyahan sa ilalim ng mga Amerikano ang kapalaran ng mga lupain ng prayle.

([File:Alberto Mansion Biñan City, Laguna.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alberto_Mansion_Bi%C3%B1an_City,_Laguna.jpg)) *The historic Alberto Mansion in Biñán, originally built in the early 19th century, was the ancestral home of José Rizal’s mother, **Teodora Alonso** ([History Reborn: The Alberto Mansion in Biñan ](https://mikeegungon.wixsite.com/welcome/post/history-reborn-the-alberto-mansion-in-bi%C3%B1an#:~:text=The%20Alberto%20Mansion%20is%20a,Jose%20Rizal%27s%20mother%2C%20Teodora%20Alonso)). Restored in recent years at its original site, this bahay-na-bato stands as a proud reminder of the city’s Spanish-era heritage.*

**Did You Know?** **Biñán was almost made an “ever faithful and loyal” town of Spain.** In January 1898 the province proposed to give Biñán and Santa Rosa the rank of *villa* with the motto “Siempre fiel y leal”. Five months later, a son of Biñán, Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista, read the Act of the Proclamation of Philippine Independence ([Santiago 2005](https://philippinevillas.blogspot.com/2007/06/epilogue-last-aspirants-bin-and-santa.html)).

**Did You Know?** **The people of Biñán welcomed the governor of Hong Kong with a hot-air balloon.** When Sir John Bowring stayed with Don José Alberto in 1858–59, the townspeople released a balloon bearing the words “The people of Biñan to their illustrious visitors” ([Bowring 1859 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46695)).

**Did You Know?** **Rizal put Biñán’s church in his novel.** In *Noli me tangere* (1887), the old philosopher Tasio asks why, “in the year 70, when lightning struck in Biñán”, it fell precisely on the church tower, destroying the clock and an altar. Rizal was then nine years old and a pupil in the town.

## American Period / Panahon ng Amerikano

The end of the Spanish era in 1898 and the onset of American rule ushered in new political and social changes in Biñán. After the Philippines was ceded to the United States, Biñán—like other towns—was reorganized under the American civil government. In **1903**, the colonial government briefly **merged Biñán with the neighboring town of San Pedro Tunasan (and even Muntinlupa)** as part of a municipal streamlining. Biñán became the center of an enlarged municipality, with its government administering those areas. This arrangement was short-lived: **Muntinlupa** was returned to Rizal Province by 1905, and **San Pedro Tunasan** was separated and restored as an independent municipality by **1907**. Thereafter, Biñán stood once again as a distinct municipality in the province of Laguna.

Under American rule, Biñán saw advancements in infrastructure, education, and local governance. The Americans introduced a public school system—establishing Biñán’s first public elementary schools—building upon the town’s tradition of learning (Biñán’s educated class, including José Rizal’s relatives, had long valued education). New civic institutions were put in place: a **municipal president** (the American-era title for town executive) was elected, replacing the Spanish *capitán*. Notably, one of Biñán’s pre-war municipal presidents, **Marcial Alintanahin**, would later play a heroic role in World War II as a guerrilla leader. 

Economically, Biñán remained a primarily **agricultural and trading community** in the early American period. Farmers continued to plant rice, sugar cane, and coconuts in its fertile fields. The town’s public market – already important in the Spanish era – grew even more active as a regional trading center, thanks to improvements in transportation. In the 1910s, the construction of the **Manila-South railway** brought a train station to Biñán, linking it directly to Manila and the southern provinces. This railway connection made it easier to bring Biñán’s local products (like sugar, rice, and fruits) to the markets of Manila. Biñán also became known for cottage industries: by the 1920s–1930s, the town’s artisans were producing shoes, slippers, and woven hats. (In fact, two barangays, *Dela Paz* and *Malaban*, later became noted for their footwear-making, and *Platero* for hat weaving.) Town life during the American period was relatively peaceful and prosperous – local families engaged in farming and trade, children attended the new public schools, and American-introduced sports like baseball were played in the plaza. 

As the decades progressed, Biñán’s population steadily grew. By the 1930s, it was one of the larger towns in Laguna outside the provincial capital, supported by its robust agriculture and commerce. In 1941, on the eve of World War II, Biñán was a first-class municipality with a well-established local government, a bustling marketplace, and a community proud of both its traditional heritage and the modern ideas introduced by the Americans.

Sa pagdating ng mga Amerikano pagkatapos ng 1898, nagkaroon ng mga bagong sistema at pagbabago sa Biñán. Noong **1903**, bilang bahagi ng programa ng mga Amerikano na bawasan ang bilang ng mga bayan, pansamantalang **isinanib ang San Pedro Tunasan (at sandaling idinugtong ang Muntinlupa) sa munisipalidad ng Biñán**. Naging mas malawak ang nasasakupan ng Biñán, subalit di nagtagal ay ibinalik din sa dati: naibalik ang **Muntinlupa** sa Lalawigan ng Rizal pagsapit ng 1905, at muling ginawang bukod na bayan ang **San Pedro** noong 1907. Mula noon, muling naging hiwalay at sarili ang pamahalaang lokal ng Biñán sa ilalim ng pamahalaang Amerikano.

Naging **maunlad at mapayapa** ang Biñán noong panahon ng mga Amerikano. Pinairal ang makabagong edukasyon – nagtatag ng mga pampublikong paaralan kung saan ang mga kabataan ay natutong magbasa at sumulat sa wikang Ingles kasabay ng pagpapanatili ng kanilang wikang Tagalog. Masigla ang kalakalan sa **pamilihang bayan ng Biñán**, na lalo pang lumaki at dinarayo ng mga kalapit-bayan dahil sa mas pinahusay na transportasyon. Nagkaroon ng mga bagong kalsada at linya ng tren na nagdugtong sa Biñán patungo sa Maynila, kaya’t napadali ang pagluluwas ng ani tulad ng bigas, asukal, at prutas. Dahil dito, nakilala ang Biñán bilang isang mahalagang **sentro ng bentahan ng ani at kalakal** sa timog ng Maynila. 

Sa pamumuno, pinalitan ng titulong **Municipal President** ang dating *Capitán Municipal*. Ang mga lokal na halalan ay nagbigay-daan upang mailuklok ang mga Biñense sa posisyon sa munisipyo – tanda ng pagtanggap sa demokratikong sistema. Sa tahanan naman, unti-unting nakaimpluwensiya ang kulturang Amerikano sa araw-araw na buhay: natutunan ng mga tao ang ilang kaugaliang kanluranin, mula sa pananamit (barong at *American suit*) hanggang sa paglilibang (pagsali sa mga palaro gaya ng baseball at pagsine sa bayan). Gayunman, **pinanatili ng mga taga-Biñán ang kanilang tradisyon at kaugaliang Pilipino**. Patuloy nilang ipinagdiwang ang mga pistang panrelihiyon at iginagalang ang nakatatanda sa pamamagitan ng *pagmamano* at *bayanihan*. Sa kabuuan, sa ilalim ng pananakop ng Amerikano, naihanda ang Biñán sa makabagong panahon nang hindi isinasantabi ang sariling pagkakakilanlan at kultura.

**Did You Know?** Despite its growth under American rule, **Biñán never lost its identity as a hub of traditional local industries**. Even in the early 20th century, Biñan was already known for its distinctive **rice cake delicacy called “Puto Biñán,”** a baked rice-flour cake topped with cheese or butter. To this day, the best Puto Biñán makers are found in Barangay San Vicente, and the city proudly calls itself “The Home of the Famous Puto Biñán in Laguna”. So when you enjoy a bite of Puto Biñán, you’re tasting a piece of the town’s cultural history that has been passed down through generations.

## World War II and Japanese Occupation / Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig

The outbreak of World War II in the Pacific in 1941 brought Biñán into a period of turmoil and hardship. Japanese forces occupied Laguna, including Biñán, in 1942 as they took control of the Philippines. During the **Japanese Occupation (1942–1945)**, Biñán and its people endured severe difficulties. The occupying forces imposed strict rules and curfews; food production was commandeered to support the Japanese war effort, leading to shortages for local families. Many Biñenses joined or aided the resistance in whatever ways they could. A clandestine network of Filipino guerrillas operated in Laguna, and Biñán was no exception – local men and women provided food, intelligence, and shelter to guerrilla fighters in the surrounding areas. **Life under occupation** was perilous: residents lived in fear of reprisals, and some were forced into labor or worse. The old Biñán Central School was repurposed by the Japanese military, and the town plaza saw Japanese troops patrolling where children once played.

As Allied forces began liberating the Philippines in late 1944, Biñán became a strategic target. On **February 3, 1945**, Biñán was **liberated from Japanese control by Filipino guerrilla forces**. In a swift and coordinated assault, the famed **Hunters ROTC guerrillas**, led by Commander **Marcial Alintanahin** (a former municipal president of Biñán), attacked Japanese garrisons from three directions: from Carmona to the west, San Pedro to the north, and Santa Rosa to the south ([Liberation of Biñán | EL FILIPINISMO](https://pepealas.wordpress.com/2023/02/03/liberation-of-binan/)). At that time, roughly 30 Japanese soldiers and 50 Filipino collaborators were holding Biñán. The guerrillas overwhelmed them in a day-long battle, freeing the town from occupation. Accounts differ on the details: the memoir of Hunters ROTC veteran Proculo Mojica credits the attack to guerrillas under Col. Emmanuel de Ocampo and puts the Japanese garrison at 80 men ([History comes alive at 80th Biñan Liberation Day | OpinYon](https://opinyon.net/national/history-comes-alive-at-80th-binan-liberation-day)). This local victory was part of the larger campaign by combined Filipino and American forces that liberated Laguna province in early 1945.

Biñán emerged from WWII with comparatively **minimal physical damage**, especially when contrasted with other towns in Laguna that were heavily bombarded. Fortunately, Biñán **was spared from the widespread destruction** that the closing months of the war brought to many places. While the provincial capital Santa Cruz and nearby Calamba suffered bombing runs, Biñán’s town center and its beloved church of San Isidro survived intact. This meant that many of Biñán’s historic structures – including ancestral houses dating back to the Spanish era – **remained standing** after the war. However, the toll on human life and community welfare was heavy. Dozens of Biñenses lost their lives during the occupation and the fighting. Families who had fled to safer rural areas slowly returned to rebuild their homes and livelihoods in mid-1945. When the war officially ended in September 1945, Biñán, like the rest of the country, faced the enormous challenge of **post-war reconstruction**.

Ang pananakop ng mga Hapones noong Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig ay nagdulot ng matinding hirap at pighati sa mga taga-Biñán. Pinasok ng **Imperyal na Hukbong Hapones** ang Laguna noong 1942 at kontrolado nila ang Biñán sa loob ng tatlong taon. Sa panahong ito, ang karaniwang araw-araw na buhay ay napalitan ng takot at kakulangan. **Nagkulang ang suplay ng pagkain** dahil kinukuha ng mga mananakop ang ani para sa kanilang hukbo, kaya’t kinailangang magtipid at magtago ng mga pamilya. Maraming kabataan ang hindi nakapag-aral at ang pamahalaang bayan ay napasailalim sa mahigpit na utos ng mga Hapones. Subalit sa kabila ng panganib, ang diwa ng paglaban ay buhay sa puso ng mga Biñense. Palihim na sumuporta ang mga residente sa mga **gerilya** – ang ilan ay sumapi mismo sa mga pangkat, at ang iba naman ay nagbigay ng pagkain, taguan, o impormasyon sa mga mandirigma sa kabundukan. Naging bahagi ang Biñán ng malawakang kilusang paglaban sa okupasyon.

Nang papalapit na ang kalayaan, naging saksi ang Biñán sa mga huling kabanata ng digmaan. **Pebrero 3, 1945** nang tuluyang **napalaya ang Biñán mula sa pananakop**. Sa araw na ito, isang sama-samang pagsalakay ang isinagawa ng mga gerilyang Pilipino – ang kilalang **Hunters ROTC** – sa pangunguna ni **Komandante Marcial Alintanahin**. Mula sa iba’t ibang direksyon sila nagmula: may dumating mula sa Carmona sa kanluran, mula sa San Pedro sa hilaga, at mula sa Santa Rosa sa timog. Nagulat at di nakaagapay ang mga natitirang kawal na Hapones sa Biñán, kaya’t sa loob lamang ng isang araw ay napasuko sila at ang mga kakamping lokal na kolaborasyonistang kasama nila. Matapos ang labanan, **nagwagayway ang watawat ng Pilipinas sa Biñán**, hudyat ng katapusan ng mapait na yugto ng digmaan para sa bayan.

Bagama’t napakasaklap ng sinapit ng marami, mapalad ang Biñán dahil **hindi ito lubusang nawasak** noong digmaan. Maraming bayan sa Laguna ang naabo ang mga gusali at simbahan sa mga pambobomba, subalit ang simbahan at kabayanan ng Biñán ay nakaligtas sa matinding pinsala. Dahil dito, **napanatili ang maraming lumang bahay at istruktura** sa Biñán na itinayo pa noong panahon ng Espanyol – na isang bihirang pangyayari sa ibang lugar na lubhang nasira ang mga makasaysayang gusali. Gayunpaman, ang sugat sa pamayanan ay hindi biro: may mga naulila, may mga nasugatan at namatayan, at halos lahat ay dumanas ng gutom at takot. Matapos ang liberasyon, nagsimula ang mahaba at mahirap na **pagbangon**. Nagbalik unti-unti ang mga pamilya mula sa mga pinagtaguan nilang baryo at bundok, upang muling itayo ang mga bahay, linisin ang mga lansangan, at buhayin ang naantalang mga kabuhayan. Ang **diwa ng bayanihan** ang nagsilbing sandigan ng mga Biñense sa panahong ito – nagtulungan ang bawat isa upang ibangon ang Biñán mula sa pagkakadapa. Nang makamit ng Pilipinas ang kasarinlan noong Hulyo 4, 1946, ang Biñán ay humakbang na rin patungo sa bagong yugto bilang bahagi ng isang malayang bansa, bitbit ang mga aral at alaala ng digmaan.

**Did You Know?** At the heart of Biñán’s town plaza stands a **monument of Dr. José Rizal, erected in 1918**, which is one of the oldest Rizal monuments in the Philippines. Local lore holds that it was sculpted according to a design by National Artist Guillermo Tolentino. In an uncanny incident, this Rizal statue was **struck by lightning in September 2015**, which **decapitated** the figure of the national hero. The people of Biñán saw this as a call to further honor their heritage. The damaged statue was carefully restored by 2017 and is now displayed at City Hall, while a replica stands proud at the plaza where Rizal has stood for over a century. This story highlights Biñán’s reverence for history – even nature’s fury could not remove Rizal from the town he once called home.

## Post-War Reconstruction and Contemporary Era / Pagbangon at Makabagong Panahon

In the decades after World War II, Biñán underwent rapid recovery and development. During the post-war **Reconstruction period (late 1940s–1950s)**, national and local government programs helped rebuild infrastructure: roads were repaired, a new municipal hall was constructed to replace the old **casa tribunal**, and public schools were re-opened and expanded. The post-war population boom was evident – families grew and migrants from neighboring areas moved to Biñán, drawn by its economic opportunities and proximity to Metro Manila. By 1960, Biñán’s population had rebounded robustly from the war, and the town continued to rank among Laguna’s most important municipalities.

The latter half of the 20th century saw Biñán transform from a primarily agricultural town into an **industrial and suburban economic center**. One pivotal moment was the late 1980s: under Mayor **Bayani Alonte** (who served from 1988 to 1997), Biñán aggressively pursued industrialization and investment ([How Biñán became a city | EL FILIPINISMO](https://pepealas.wordpress.com/2023/02/02/how-binan-became-a-city/)). The city government partnered with private investors to establish large-scale industrial estates that would provide jobs and boost local revenue. In **1990**, the **Laguna Technopark, Inc. (LTI)** was launched in barangays Malamig (Biñán) and Don José (shared with Santa Rosa) – a 224-hectare industrial zone hosting light and medium-scale industries. This was followed in **1992** by the **Laguna International Industrial Park (LIIP)**, a 117-hectare high-tech industrial park located entirely in Biñán’s Barangay Mamplasan. These two pioneering industrial parks attracted numerous multinational and local companies (eventually housing over 200 firms) and **generated thousands of jobs** for Biñenses and people from surrounding towns. With manufacturing, electronics, automotive, and other industries flourishing, Biñán’s economy diversified beyond agriculture. The municipality’s income skyrocketed, funding further improvements in infrastructure such as better roads, bridges, and public facilities.

By the early 2000s, Biñán had become a thriving urban center. **Subdivisions and residential communities** were developed to accommodate its growing population, turning Biñán into a suburban enclave for people working in Metro Manila. At the same time, its own local economy remained robust – the **Biñán Public Market** grew to be one of the largest in CALABARZON, and new shopping centers and banks opened in town. As a testament to its progress, **Biñán was recorded as the richest municipality in the Philippines in 2007**, with an annual gross income of ₱677 million and a net income of ₱250 million. It also far exceeded the population requirement for cityhood, having over 262,000 residents by that year. All these achievements set the stage for Biñán’s next political milestone.

On **February 2, 2010**, Biñán **achieved cityhood**. By virtue of **Republic Act No. 9740**, enacted by Congress and approved by President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo, the Municipality of Biñán was converted into the **City of Biñán**. In a plebiscite, the people of Biñán overwhelmingly ratified this conversion, making Biñán the fourth city in Laguna Province and the 139th city in the Philippines. Cityhood ushered in a new era of self-governance and fiscal autonomy, allowing Biñán to retain more of its revenues for local development. In the years that followed, Biñán continued to advance. In **2015**, through **Republic Act No. 10658**, Biñán was granted its own lone Congressional District, separating it from Laguna’s first district to give the city direct representation in Congress. This change reflected Biñán’s significant population and influence.

Today, Biñán is a dynamic component city that balances **economic growth with cultural preservation**. The latest census (2020) recorded a population of **407,437**, making Biñán the third most populous locality in Laguna (after Calamba and Santa Rosa). The city spans 43.50 square kilometers, characterized by a mix of industrial parks, commercial centers, and residential subdivisions. Biñán has become both a **suburban residential community of Metro Manila and an industrial hub** in its own right. Everyday, thousands of residents commute to and from Metro Manila via the South Luzon Expressway, while many work in Biñán’s own factories, business process outsourcing offices, and trading companies. The city’s slogan as the “Trading and Commerce Center of the South” is well-earned – its central public market and modern malls draw shoppers and traders from across southern Luzon. 

Importantly, Biñán has made a name for itself in **heritage conservation and culture-led governance** in recent years. In 2016, the city government launched the **“Balik-Biñán” project**, an ambitious program to revive and celebrate the city’s rich history ([PIA - Biñan embraces tradition with economic growth](https://mirror.pia.gov.ph/features/2024/02/08/binan-embraces-tradition-with-economic-growth)). Through this initiative, the old Spanish-era municipal hall was transformed into the **Sentrong Pangkultura ng Biñán** (Biñán Center for Culture and the Arts). The historic Alberto Mansion – once dismantled and taken to a heritage park in Bataan – was reconstructed on its original site and fully restored as a **heritage museum**. The city also established a Heritage District and conducted a comprehensive **Cultural Mapping Project** to document historical sites, traditions, and artifacts. These efforts have instilled pride among Biñenses and attracted cultural tourism. In recognition of its innovative approach, Biñán won the prestigious **Galing Pook Award** in 2022 for its “Balik-Biñán: Tourism Development through Heritage Conservation” program. This award highlighted how Biñán successfully marries economic progress with honoring its past. As Mayor Walfredo “Arman” Dimaguila Jr. put it: *“The past lives in our city. The past gives character to our city and people.”* ([Past forward: Araw ng Biñan channeling history, heritage and culture for sustainable progress | BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/02/19/past-forward-araw-ng-binan-channeling-history-heritage-and-culture-for-sustainable-progress/))

Looking at Biñán today, one can see a city that embraces modernity while **cherishing its heritage**. Bustling industrial estates operate a short drive from centuries-old bahay-na-bato houses. The feast of San Isidro and other local fiestas are celebrated with the same fervor as the city’s founding anniversary and cityhood day. The local government actively promotes cultural education, ensuring that the next generation knows the stories of Biñán – from the legend of “Binyagan” to the heroism of local guerrillas and the legacy of Rizal’s schooling. In governance, Biñán continues to innovate, focusing on sustainable development, quality education, and inclusive growth for its people. As of 2025, Biñán City is a **first-class city** and a key player in the CALABARZON region’s economy, boasting low unemployment and a high literacy rate. It remains a city of entrepreneurs, from the vendors in the grand public market to the tech engineers in its industrial parks.

Pagkatapos ng digmaan, mabilis na nakabangon ang bayan ng Biñán at pumasok sa isang panahon ng masiglang pagbabago. Noong huling bahagi ng dekada 1940 at dekada ’50, sama-samang ibinangon muli ng pamahalaan at mamamayan ang bayan – inayos ang mga nasirang kalsada, muling itinayo ang mga tulay at gusali, at sinigurong makabalik ang mga bata sa paaralan. Muling sumigla ang agrikultura at kalakalan: nagbalik ang **palengke ng Biñán** bilang pangunahing bagsakan ng mga ani sa rehiyon, at unti-unti ring nagtayuan ang maliliit na pabrika at pagawaan. Sa mga sumunod na dekada, lalo pang lumago ang Biñán. Pagsapit ng dekada 1980, mas naging **industrial** ang karakter ng bayan. Sinikap ng mga lokal na lider na akitin ang malalaking mamumuhunan upang lumikha ng trabaho para sa mga Biñense. Nagbunga ito nang itatag ang mga **industrial park** sa Biñán – ang Laguna Technopark at Laguna International Industrial Park – na nagdala ng daan-daang kompanya at libu-libong trabaho sa lungsod. Dahil dito, ang dating tahimik na lupang sakahan ay naging lunsaran ng modernong pagawaan at teknolohiya. Maraming mga magsasaka ang naging **manggagawa sa pabrika**, at ang iba’y nagtayo ng maliliit na negosyo upang suportahan ang lumalaking populasyon ng Biñán.

Sa pag-unlad na ito, hindi nawala ang puso ng mga Biñense sa kanilang **nakaraan at kultura**. Noong 2010, nang maging ganap na lungsod ang Biñán sa bisa ng RA 9740, ipinagdiwang hindi lamang ang bagong katayuan kundi pati ang mayamang kasaysayan ng pamayanan. Itinakda ng lungsod ang **Araw ng Biñán** tuwing unang linggo ng Pebrero bilang tatlong araw na pagdiriwang – ang Pebrero 2 ay ang anibersaryo ng pagiging lungsod, Pebrero 3 ang Araw ng Liberasyon mula sa mga Hapones, at Pebrero 4 ang anibersaryo ng pagkakatatag ng bayan noong 1747. Taun-taon, isinasadula ang makulay na kasaysayan sa mga programa at parada, na tila binabalik-tanaw ang bawat yugto mula sa pagka-“Binyagan” ng Biñán hanggang sa modernong panahon.

Sa kasalukuyan, ang Biñán ay isang lungsod na **pinagsasama ang kaunlaran at tradisyon**. Ayon sa pinakahuling senso, mahigit **400,000** na ang populasyon nito, patuloy na dumarami dahil sa lokasyon nitong malapit sa Kamaynilaan at sa pagdami ng hanapbuhay dito. Maraming residente ang dito na naninirahan subalit sa Makati o Maynila nagtatrabaho; marami rin naman ang nakahanap ng ikabubuhay sa mga **pabrika, malls, at opisina** mismo sa Biñán. Sa kabila ng pagiging abala at modernisado, hindi nalimot ng lungsod ang kanyang pinagmulan. **Pinangangalagaan ng Biñán ang mga pamanang gusali at sining** – katunayan, inilunsad ng pamahalaang lokal ang “Balik-Biñán” project noong 2016 upang ibalik ang sigla ng downtown heritage district. Sa proyektong ito, ang lumang munisipyo ay ginawang **Sentrong Pangkultura**, ang makasaysayang Alberto Mansion ay naibalik at naisaayos, at ang buong Poblacion ay idineklara bilang **Heritage District** kung saan bawal ang paggiba sa mga makalumang istruktura. Pinangunahan din ng lungsod ang paggawa ng **Cultural Mapping** – pagtitipon ng mga kuwento, larawan, at kaalaman tungkol sa mga tradisyon at kagamitan ng Biñán. 

Ang mga pagsisikap na ito ay nagbunga ng karangalan: kinilala ang Biñán sa pambansang antas at binigyan ng **Galing Pook Award** noong 2022 dahil sa matagumpay na pagsasama ng turismo at konserbasyon ng pamana sa kaunlaran ng lungsod. Ipinakita ng karangalang ito na posible ang pag-unlad na hindi sinasakripisyo ang kaluluwa ng lungsod. Ayon nga kay Mayor Walfredo “Arman” Dimaguila Jr., ang pagbabalik sa kasaysayan ang susi sa pagharap sa kinabukasan: *“Isang Biñan tayong nagpapahalaga sa ating nakaraan kaya’t Isang Biñan tayong sasalubong sa ating magandang bukas,”* aniya noong Araw ng Biñán 2025 ([Honoring Biñan’s legacy: a triple celebration of history and progress | Journal News](https://journalnews.com.ph/honoring-binans-legacy-a-triple-celebration-of-history-and-progress)). Sa panahong abala ang karamihan sa paghabol sa modernisasyon, ang Biñán ay tumitingin din sa pinanggalingan nito upang gumuhit ng tuwid na landas patungo sa hinaharap.

Sa kabuuan, ang **Kasalukuyang Biñán** ay larawan ng isang lungsod na yumayabong – umuunlad ang ekonomiya, mataas ang kita at antas ng pamumuhay – subalit matibay ang koneksyon sa ugat ng kasaysayan. Ang mga kabataan ng Biñán ay lumalaki na may pagmamalaki sa kanilang lungsod: alam nila ang kuwento ng Puto Biñán, ang mga alamat ng *baryo*, at ang kontribusyon ng Biñán sa buhay ni Rizal. Ang mga **kaugaliang pampamilya** at paggalang sa nakatatanda ay buhay pa rin sa bawat barangay, kahit sabay-sabay na rin silang nakakagamit ng mga smartphone at internet. Tunay na ang Biñán ay **“City of Life”** ([Biñan: ‘The City of Life’ returns with a brighter future yet to unfold | BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2023/02/20/binan-the-city-of-life-returns-with-a-brighter-future-yet-to-unfold/)) para sa mga naninirahan dito – buhay na buhay ang kalakalan tuwing umaga sa palengke, ang pananampalataya tuwing pista, at ang pag-asa sa bawat proyektong pangkaunlaran ng pamahalaan. Habang patuloy na isinusulong ng Biñán ang pagiging makabago at kompetitibo, sinisiguro rin nitong hindi mawawala ang puso at diwa na nagbibigay ng natatanging kulay sa Lungsod ng Biñán.

**Did You Know?** **Even before becoming a city, Biñán was already known for its economic might.** It was officially the **richest municipality in the Philippines in 2007**, topping over 1,500 other towns in terms of income. This financial strength – built on its thriving industries and commerce – paved the way for its cityhood in 2010. Today, that robust economy continues to fund improved public services, from modern healthcare facilities to quality education programs, benefiting every Biñense.

**Did You Know?** **Biñán’s commitment to heritage has made it a model city for cultural preservation.** The city’s transformation of its old municipal hall into a Cultural Center and the reconstruction of the 200-year-old Alberto Mansion earned nationwide praise. In fact, Biñán is one of the few cities where you can walk along a Heritage District with restored Spanish-colonial homes, then a few blocks away find a bustling technohub. This unique blend of old and new is a source of pride – as the local saying goes, *“Ang hindi marunong lumingon sa pinanggalingan ay hindi makararating sa paroroonan,”* and Biñán has taken this to heart by honoring its past while marching forward.

## Culture, Daily Life and Commerce / Kultura, Pang-araw-araw na Buhay at Kalakalan

Biñán has always been a town of traders. Its **public market**, near the plaza and the old municipal hall, is where daily life begins: from early morning the stalls fill with fruit, vegetables, fish and flowers, while jeepneys and tricycles crowd the streets of the Poblacion. Small family businesses grew around it. For generations, households in barangays such as *Dela Paz* and *Malaban* made **slippers and shoes** – a trade so tied to the town’s identity that its bandstand was built in the shape of a slipper ([Biñan leads the way to heritage conservation efforts in Laguna | Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)). The market also nourished the town’s food traditions. In the mornings, vendors sold **pospas**, a yellow rice porridge with chicken and innards coloured with *kasubha* (safflower); the dish is said to have been pioneered in Biñán by Merta Silva during the Japanese occupation, and it is still served in the Poblacion by Mang Tony’s ([Pospas | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/pospas)).

The city’s most famous product is **Puto Biñán**, a steamed rice cake “as big as a pizza”, fluffy yet moist, topped with grated cheese, bits of salted egg and a drizzle of condensed milk. According to local tradition it was created almost a century ago by a housewife, **Petronila “Aling Nila” Samaniego**. She experimented with a batter (*galapong*) of rice flour, brown sugar and baking powder, poured it onto a steamer (*lansungan*) covered with muslin (*katsa*), and steamed it in an oven fired with rice husks (*ipa*). Her puto was first sold to travellers at the old Biñán railway station in Barangay Santo Niño; other makers followed, and her recipe is still made in the original family bakery, now run by her heir ([Puto Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/puto-binan)) ([Biñan pospas and puto | Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/starweek-magazine/2015/12/05/1529383/bian-pospas-and-puto)).

Since 2011, the city has celebrated its rice cake and its dance together in the **Puto Latik Festival** ([Biñan brings back face-to-face revelries at 12th Puto Latik Festival 2022 | Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2022/05/16/binan-brings-back-face-to-face-revelries-at-12th-puto-latik-festival-2022/)). Its name joins *puto* and **maglalatik**, a folk dance said to have been first performed by the men of Loma and Zapote as an offering to San Isidro Labrador during his procession. Dancers with halved coconut shells strapped to their chests, hips and thighs stage a mock battle over *latik*, the sweet curd of slow-cooked coconut milk: Moros in red trousers against Christians in blue, in a fight that ends in reconciliation ([Maglalatik | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/fee-binan/maglalatik)). First held in February, the festival was moved to the feast of San Isidro on 15 May and now lasts nine days, from 15 to 23 May. It opens with a procession of the image of *Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje*, continues with inter-barangay street dancing, floats and a Puto Biñán cook-off, and closes with the *Pistang Intsik* and the *Santacruzan* ([Puto Latik Festival | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/feel-binan/puto-latik-festival/)). It won the Department of Tourism–ATOP Pearl Awards in 2018, 2019 and 2022, and was named Best Cultural Festival in the city category in 2025 ([Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)).

The year in Biñán is marked by these celebrations – the **Araw ng Biñán** in early February and the **fiesta of San Isidro** in May – and each day has its own rhythm: Mass at the church of San Isidro Labrador, the morning market, and evening strolls on the plaza.

Mula pa noon, bayan na ng mga mangangalakal ang Biñán. Ang **pampublikong pamilihan** nito, malapit sa plasa at sa lumang munisipyo, ang pinagmumulan ng araw-araw na buhay: madaling-araw pa lamang ay napupuno na ang mga puwesto ng prutas, gulay, isda at bulaklak, habang siksikan ang mga dyip at traysikel sa mga kalye ng Poblacion. Sa paligid nito umusbong ang maliliit na negosyong pampamilya. Sa loob ng maraming henerasyon, gumawa ng **tsinelas at sapatos** ang mga sambahayan sa mga barangay tulad ng *Dela Paz* at *Malaban* – isang hanapbuhay na lubhang nakaugat sa pagkakakilanlan ng bayan kaya hugis-tsinelas pa ang itinayong entablado ng banda nito. Pinayaman din ng pamilihan ang mga tradisyon sa pagkain. Tuwing umaga, may nagtitinda roon ng **pospas**, isang dilaw na lugaw na may manok at lamang-loob na kinukulayan ng kasubha; sinasabing si Merta Silva ang nagpasimula nito sa Biñán noong panahon ng Hapon, at hanggang ngayon ay inihahain pa ito ng Mang Tony’s sa Poblacion.

Ang pinakatanyag na produkto ng lungsod ay ang **Puto Biñán**, isang pinasingawang kakanin na kasinlaki ng pizza, malambot at basa-basa, at nilalagyan ng ginadgad na keso, piraso ng itlog na maalat at kaunting gatas na kondensada. Ayon sa salaysay ng bayan, nilikha ito halos isang siglo na ang nakararaan ng isang maybahay, si **Petronila “Aling Nila” Samaniego**. Sinubukan niya ang isang galapong na yari sa harina ng bigas, pulang asukal at baking powder, ibinuhos ito sa lansungan na tinakpan ng katsa, at pinasingawan sa pugon na pinaaapoy ng ipa. Unang inilako ang kaniyang puto sa mga pasahero sa lumang estasyon ng tren ng Biñán sa Barangay Santo Niño; sumunod ang iba pang gumagawa ng puto, at hanggang ngayon ay niluluto pa rin ang kaniyang resipe sa orihinal na pagawaan ng pamilya, na pinamamahalaan na ngayon ng kaniyang tagapagmana.

Mula noong 2011, sabay na ipinagdiriwang ng lungsod ang kakanin at ang sayaw nito sa **Puto Latik Festival**. Pinagsama sa pangalan nito ang puto at ang **maglalatik**, isang katutubong sayaw na sinasabing unang isinayaw ng mga kalalakihan ng Loma at Zapote bilang handog kay San Isidro Labrador sa kaniyang prusisyon. Sa sayaw na ito, ang mga lalaking may nakataling kalahating bao ng niyog sa dibdib, balakang at hita ay nagtatanghal ng kunwaring labanan para sa latik, ang matamis na latak ng pinakuluang gata: mga Moro na nakapulang pantalon laban sa mga Kristiyanong nakaasul, sa isang labanang nagtatapos sa pagkakasundo. Dating idinaraos tuwing Pebrero, inilipat ang pista sa kapistahan ni San Isidro tuwing Mayo 15, at ngayon ay tumatagal ito nang siyam na araw, mula Mayo 15 hanggang 23. Nagsisimula ito sa prusisyon ng imahen ng *Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje*, sinusundan ng paligsahan sa sayaw-kalye ng mga barangay, parada ng mga karosa at paligsahan sa pagluluto ng Puto Biñán, at nagtatapos sa *Pistang Intsik* at *Santacruzan*. Ilang ulit na itong ginawaran ng Pearl Awards ng Kagawaran ng Turismo at ng mga opisyal ng turismo – noong 2018, 2019 at 2022 – at hinirang na pinakamahusay na pistang pangkultura sa kategorya ng mga lungsod noong 2025.

Sa ganitong paraan, ang taon sa Biñán ay minamarkahan ng mga pagdiriwang – ang **Araw ng Biñán** sa mga unang araw ng Pebrero at ang **pista ni San Isidro** sa Mayo – at may sariling indayog ang bawat araw: ang misa sa simbahan ni San Isidro Labrador, ang pamilihan sa umaga, at ang pamamasyal sa plasa sa gabi.
