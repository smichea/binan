# The Book of Biñan / Kasaysayan ng Lungsod ng Biñan

# Part One · History / Unang Bahagi · Kasaysayan

## Geography / Heograpiya

### 1. Between the lake and the plateau

Biñan occupies a narrow strip of land of some **40 to 44 square kilometres** – 43.5 in the city’s own figures, 40.27 in the national statistics of 2013 – on the western shore of **Laguna de Bay**, some 35 kilometres south of Manila. From the lakeshore, barely 2 metres above sea level, the ground rises gently westward toward the tuff plateau of Carmona and Silang in Cavite; the old town centre, the Poblacion, stands at about 12 metres ([Biñan City, Laguna Profile | PhilAtlas](https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/r04a/laguna/binan.html)) ([Laguna de Bay | World Lake Database, ILEC](https://wldb.ilec.or.jp/Display/html/3530)). The city is bounded by San Pedro to the north, Santa Rosa to the south, Carmona, General Mariano Alvarez and Silang to the west, and the lake to the east. On a clear day its horizon is closed by volcanoes: **Mount Makiling** (1,090 m) about 26 km to the southeast, **Mount Banahaw** beyond it, and to the southwest the Tagaytay ridge, the rim of the caldera that hides **Taal Volcano**, only 38 km away ([Mount Makiling Forest Reserve | ASEAN Clearing House Mechanism](https://asean.chm-cbd.net/node/538)) ([Taal | Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=273070)).

### 2. Two plates

This landscape was made by one of the most restless places on Earth. The Philippine islands are a mosaic of crustal blocks – geologists call it the **Philippine Mobile Belt** – caught between two great tectonic plates: the **Philippine Sea Plate** to the east and the **Sunda (Eurasian) Plate** to the west, which close in on each other by several centimetres a year, about 8 to 9 centimetres across the whole system ([Rangin et al. 1999, Geophysical Journal International](https://www.geologie.ens.fr/~rooke/NCRpdf4web/Rangin&al-1999.pdf)). Each plate dives beneath the islands along its own deep-sea trench – the **Manila Trench** west of Luzon, and the **East Luzon Trench** and the **Philippine Trench** in the east – while the sideways part of the movement is absorbed by the 1,200-kilometre **Philippine Fault** ([Large-scale digital mapping of the Philippine fault zone | PHIVOLCS](https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/philippine-fault-zone-maps/)) ([MMEIRS Final Report, ch. 2, 2004 | JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11763745_02.pdf)). This collision puts the country on the Pacific **“Ring of Fire”**, with some two dozen active volcanoes.

### 3. Born of fire

Biñan lies at the northern edge of the **Macolod Corridor**, a belt of young volcanoes that runs from Taal to Laguna de Bay across the volcanic arc of Luzon; geologists see it as a rift, where the crust is being stretched and magma rises easily ([Förster et al., The Macolod Corridor: a rift crossing the Philippine island arc, 1990 | Tectonophysics](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-1951%2890%2990420-D)) ([Sudo et al., New ages from the Macolod Corridor, 2007 | Goldschmidt Conference Abstracts](https://goldschmidtabstracts.info/2007/984.pdf)). The central part of the lake is itself a volcanic **caldera**, some 24 by 12 kilometres, between Talim Island and the Jala-Jala peninsula. It was left by great explosive eruptions whose dates are still debated: about one million and 27,000 to 29,000 years ago according to the Smithsonian’s Global Volcanism Program, while volcanic deposits north of the lake have been dated to about 1.8 to 1.9 million years ([Laguna Caldera | Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=273080)) ([Sudo et al. 2007 | Goldschmidt Conference Abstracts](https://goldschmidtabstracts.info/2007/984.pdf)). Ash and glowing avalanches from such eruptions built the layers of volcanic tuff on which western Biñan stands. The fire has not gone out: hot springs still bubble at Los Baños and Calamba, and when Taal erupted on **12 January 2020**, its ash fell on Biñan and classes were suspended across the province ([Heavy ashfall in Batangas, Cavite, Laguna | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=1211958)).

### 4. The fault

Beneath the city also runs a fault.<span id="ref-fault"></span> The **West Valley Fault**, about 100 km long from Bulacan to Carmona, crosses Biñan through the barangays of Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) and Biñan ([LIST: Barangays near earthquake fault | GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/story/489334/scitech/science/list-barangays-in-metro-manila-nearby-provinces-near-earthquake-fault)). It can produce an earthquake of magnitude 7.2 and moves roughly every 400 to 600 years; its last great rupture is dated to **1658** ([West Valley Fault nears ‘ripe stage’ | DZRH](https://www.dzrh.com.ph/post/west-valley-fault-nears-ripe-stage-but-no-way-to-tell-when-it-will-move-phivolcs)) ([MMEIRS Final Report, ch. 2, 2004 | JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11763745_02.pdf)). The region has been shaken many times since: the old Spanish catalogue of earthquakes records that on **20 July 1880** a shock “of destructive violence” struck “the towns surrounding Lake Bay, especially those south and west of the lake”, damaging churches, convents and courthouses ([Masó, Catalogue of Violent and Destructive Earthquakes in the Philippines, 1910 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18556)). Today most tremors felt in Biñan are weak, but the city prepares for the “Big One”: on 28 September 2016 it hosted the nationwide earthquake drill in Barangay San Francisco, one of the barangays the fault passes through ([Biñan quake drill | Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/moveph/147635-binan-pagyanig-quake-drill/)).

### 5. The lake

**Laguna de Bay** is the largest lake in the Philippines – about 900 km² – but also a very shallow one, only 2.5 to 2.8 metres deep on average. Twenty-one major rivers and more than a hundred streams feed it from a basin of some 3,820 km², and it has a single outlet, the **Pasig River**, which carries its water to Manila Bay ([Santos-Borja & Nepomuceno, Laguna de Bay: Experience and Lessons Learned Brief, 2006 | ILEC](https://wldb.ilec.or.jp/itembyid/10202)) ([Laguna de Bay | World Lake Database, ILEC](https://wldb.ilec.or.jp/Display/html/3530)) ([Environmental tax, Laguna de Bay | Convention on Biological Diversity](https://www.cbd.int/financial/fiscalenviron/philippines-fiscaltax.pdf)). In the dry season, when the lake falls below the level of the sea, the Pasig runs backward and **seawater flows into the lake**; the salt makes the mud settle and the water turns clear for a few months ([Santiago 1991, Turbidity and seawater intrusion in Laguna de Bay | SEAFDEC](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/1351)). For centuries the lake was Biñan’s other street, the road of the boats that carried rice and sugar to Manila, and its pantry: in the early 1960s, 95 per cent of the catch was made of three native fish – *ayungin*, *biyang puti* and *kanduli* – which have since given way to tilapia and bangus raised in pens and cages ([Bagarinao, The decline of native fishes and fisheries and the rise of aquaculture in lakes and rivers in the Philippines, 2001 | SEAFDEC](https://repository.seafdec.org.ph/handle/10862/834)). The **Biñan River**, which rises in the Carmona uplands and crosses the Poblacion before reaching the lake, is one of the lake’s monitored tributaries – and one that often lacks oxygen because of the wastewater it carries ([Water quality of Laguna Lake’s tributaries | LB Times](https://lbtimes.ph/whole-of-community-approach-stricter-policy-implementation-sought-water-quality-of-laguna-lakes-tributaries-continue-to-deteriorate-%e2%88%92-llda-data/)).

### 6. When the lake was the sea

The shape of this shore has changed over the ages. During the ice ages of the last two million years, the sea rose and fell by more than a hundred metres. Some 20,000 years ago it stood about 120 metres lower than today: the shallow seas of Southeast Asia were dry land, joining Borneo, Sumatra and Java to the continent, but Luzon always remained an island ([Robles et al. 2015 | ANU Open Research](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/handle/1885/14352)). When the ice melted, the sea came back – and went further than today. A sediment core drilled in the West Bay, the part of the lake in front of Biñan, shows that **between about 6,600 and 4,700 years ago the lake was an arm of the sea**, a salt lagoon more than ten metres deep connected to Manila Bay. It became brackish around 4,100 years ago and fresh around 3,000 years ago, as the sea fell back and the land between Muntinlupa and Parañaque was lifted by movements of the same West Valley Fault ([Jaraula et al. 2014, Records and causes of Holocene salinity shifts in Laguna de Bay | Quaternary International](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/30255)).

### 7. Seasons and typhoons

Today Biñan has a tropical climate with two clear seasons: a **dry season from November to April** and a **wet season from May to October**, when the southwest monsoon, the *habagat*, brings about 90 per cent of the year’s rain. At Sangley Point, the nearest weather station of PAGASA, the national weather service, the normal yearly rainfall is about 2,080 mm, with more than 400 mm in each of July and August ([Climatological Normals | PAGASA](https://www.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/climate/climatological-normals)). Typhoons cross the region between June and December. The worst in living memory was **Ondoy** (Ketsana) in September 2009, which raised the lake to about 14 metres on its gauge – close to the record of 1919 – and left 192 lakeshore barangays under water for weeks, with an expected decline of at least 144 days ([Laguna de Bay flooding to recede in 5 months | Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2009/10/10/512548/laguna-de-bay-flooding-recede-5-months)). Lakeshore towns lived it again in 2020 with Ulysses ([Like ‘Ondoy’ all over again | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1360021/like-ondoy-all-over-again)) and in October 2024 with Kristine ([A month after Kristine, Laguna barangay still flooded | ABS-CBN](https://www.abs-cbn.com/regions/2024/11/19/in-photos-a-month-after-kristine-laguna-barangay-still-flooded-1531)).

### 8. A changing climate

The climate is also changing. PAGASA measured a warming of about **0.65 °C across the Philippines between 1951 and 2010**, and **2024 was the hottest year ever recorded** in the country ([Climate Change in the Philippines, 2011 | PAGASA](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/iaas/ClimateChange_in_the_Philippines_MDGF_Report_2011.pdf)) ([2024 is hottest year in Philippines | ABS-CBN](https://abs-cbn.com/news/weather-traffic/2025/1/23/2024-is-hottest-year-in-philippines-pagasa-1717)). NASA’s climate reconstruction for Biñan’s own coordinates tells the same story: the average temperature of the years 2015–2024 was about 0.4 °C higher than that of 1981–1990, and 2024 was the warmest year of the series ([NASA POWER](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/)). PAGASA projects a further warming of about 2 °C by the middle of the century. The heat is already felt: on 15 April 2025, the heat index reached **50 °C** at Los Baños, 27 km away ([Los Baños sizzles with 50°C heat index | Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/4/15/los-banos-laguna-sizzles-with-50-c-heat-index-on-april-15)). And the water is rising: around Manila Bay, the relative sea level rose by about a metre between 1964 and 2002, mostly because the ground is sinking as groundwater is pumped out – a threat that also lengthens the floods of the lakeshore ([Rodolfo & Siringan 2006, Disasters](https://research.fit.edu/media/site-specific/researchfitedu/coast-climate-adaptation-library/asia-amp-indian-ocean/southeast-asia-amp-philippines/Rodolfo--Siringan.--2006.--Global-SLR--Flooding-in-the-Northern-Philippines.pdf)).

### 9. Fertile land, growing city

Volcanic ash, river silt and flat land made Biñan fertile: the soils of this part of the Cavite–Laguna plateau, formed on volcanic tuff, are mostly deep clays and clay loams, and the plain near the lake is almost level ([CALA East–West National Road Project, EIS, ch. 3, 2006 | JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11844016_05.pdf)). For centuries this ground fed rice fields and sugarcane, irrigated by canals – the *patubigan* that gave its name to Barangay Tubigan ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)). In the last fifty years the expressways, the railway and the industrial parks have turned these fields into a city where all 24 barangays are now classified as urban. The chapters that follow tell how people came to live on this shore, between the fire, the fault and the lake.

### 1. Sa pagitan ng lawa at ng talampas

Ang Biñan ay sumasakop sa isang makitid na lupain na mga **40 hanggang 44 kilometro kuwadrado** – 43.5 ayon sa lungsod, 40.27 ayon sa pambansang estadistika ng 2013 – sa kanlurang pampang ng **Lawa ng Laguna**, mga 35 kilometro sa timog ng Maynila. Mula sa baybayin ng lawa, na halos 2 metro lamang ang taas sa ibabaw ng dagat, dahan-dahang tumataas ang lupa pakanluran patungo sa talampas ng Carmona at Silang sa Cavite; ang lumang sentro ng bayan, ang Poblacion, ay nasa mga 12 metro ang taas. Hangganan nito ang San Pedro sa hilaga, ang Santa Rosa sa timog, ang Carmona, General Mariano Alvarez at Silang sa kanluran, at ang lawa sa silangan. Sa maaliwalas na araw, mga bulkan ang nakikita sa abot-tanaw: ang **Bundok Makiling** (1,090 metro) mga 26 na kilometro sa timog-silangan, ang **Bundok Banahaw** sa likod nito, at sa timog-kanluran ang tagaytay ng Tagaytay, ang gilid ng kaldera na nagtatago sa **Bulkang Taal**, na 38 kilometro lamang ang layo.

### 2. Dalawang tectonic plate

Ang tanawing ito ay hinubog ng isa sa pinakamagulong bahagi ng mundo. Ang kapuluan ng Pilipinas ay binubuo ng magkakadikit na piraso ng crust ng daigdig – tinatawag ito ng mga heologo na **Philippine Mobile Belt** – na naiipit sa pagitan ng dalawang malalaking tectonic plate: ang **Philippine Sea Plate** sa silangan at ang **Sunda (Eurasian) Plate** sa kanluran, na nagkakalapit nang ilang sentimetro bawat taon, mga 8 hanggang 9 na sentimetro sa kabuuan. Bawat plate ay sumusuot sa ilalim ng mga pulo sa kani-kaniyang malalim na trench sa dagat: ang **Manila Trench** sa kanluran ng Luzon, at ang **East Luzon Trench** at **Philippine Trench** sa silangan, habang ang pahalang na bahagi ng paggalaw ay sinasalo ng 1,200-kilometrong **Philippine Fault**. Dahil sa banggaang ito, nasa **“Ring of Fire”** ng Pasipiko ang bansa, na may mga dalawampu’t apat na aktibong bulkan.

### 3. Isinilang sa apoy

Nasa hilagang gilid ng **Macolod Corridor** ang Biñan, isang sinturon ng mga batang bulkan mula Taal hanggang Lawa ng Laguna na tumatawid sa arko ng mga bulkan ng Luzon; itinuturing ito ng mga heologo na isang *rift*, kung saan nababanat ang crust at madaling umaakyat ang magma. Ang gitnang bahagi mismo ng lawa ay isang **kaldera** ng bulkan, mga 24 por 12 kilometro, sa pagitan ng Pulo ng Talim at ng tangway ng Jala-Jala. Iniwan ito ng malalakas na pagsabog na pinagtatalunan pa ang petsa: mga isang milyon at 27,000 hanggang 29,000 taon na ang nakararaan ayon sa Global Volcanism Program ng Smithsonian, habang napetsahan sa mga 1.8 hanggang 1.9 milyong taon ang mga deposito ng bulkan sa hilaga ng lawa. Ang abo at ang nagbabagang agos ng mga pagsabog na iyon ang bumuo sa mga patong ng batong tuff na kinatatayuan ng kanlurang Biñan. Hindi pa namamatay ang apoy: may mga bukal pa ring mainit sa Los Baños at Calamba, at nang pumutok ang Taal noong **Enero 12, 2020**, umulan ng abo sa Biñan at sinuspinde ang mga klase sa buong lalawigan.

### 4. Ang fault

May fault din sa ilalim ng lungsod.<span id="ref-fault"></span> Ang **West Valley Fault**, na mga 100 kilometro ang haba mula Bulacan hanggang Carmona, ay dumaraan sa Biñan sa mga barangay ng Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) at Biñan. Maaari itong magdulot ng lindol na may lakas na 7.2 at gumagalaw nang humigit-kumulang bawat 400 hanggang 600 taon; noong **1658** ang huling malakas na paggalaw nito. Maraming ulit nang niyanig ang rehiyon mula noon: ayon sa lumang katalogo ng mga lindol, noong **Hulyo 20, 1880** ay isang lindol na “mapanira ang lakas” ang tumama sa “mga bayang nakapalibot sa Lawa ng Bay, lalo na sa timog at kanluran nito”, at nasira ang mga simbahan, kumbento at tribunal. Sa ngayon, mahina ang karamihan sa mga lindol na nararamdaman sa Biñan, ngunit naghahanda ang lungsod para sa “Big One”: noong Setyembre 28, 2016, dito ginanap ang pambansang earthquake drill, sa Barangay San Francisco, isa sa mga barangay na dinaraanan ng fault.

### 5. Ang lawa

Ang **Lawa ng Laguna** ang pinakamalaking lawa sa Pilipinas – mga 900 kilometro kuwadrado – ngunit napakababaw, 2.5 hanggang 2.8 metro lamang ang karaniwang lalim. Dalawampu’t isang malalaking ilog at mahigit isandaang sapa ang nagpapadaloy ng tubig dito mula sa isang palanggana na mga 3,820 kilometro kuwadrado, at iisa lamang ang labasan nito, ang **Ilog Pasig**, na nagdadala ng tubig nito sa Look ng Maynila. Sa tag-araw, kapag bumaba ang lawa sa antas ng dagat, pabalik ang agos ng Pasig at **pumapasok ang tubig-dagat sa lawa**; pinalulubog ng asin ang putik kaya luminaw ang tubig sa loob ng ilang buwan. Sa loob ng maraming siglo, ang lawa ang naging isa pang lansangan ng Biñan, ang daan ng mga bangkang nagdadala ng bigas at asukal sa Maynila, at ang hapag-kainan nito: noong unang bahagi ng dekada 1960, 95 bahagdan ng huli ay tatlong katutubong isda – ayungin, biyang puti at kanduli – na napalitan na ngayon ng tilapia at bangus na inaalagaan sa mga baklad at kulungan. Ang **Ilog Biñan**, na nagmumula sa kabundukan ng Carmona at dumaraan sa Poblacion bago umabot sa lawa, ay isa sa mga binabantayang sangang-ilog ng lawa – at madalas itong kulang sa oxygen dahil sa maruming tubig na dala nito.

### 6. Noong dagat pa ang lawa

Nagbago ang hugis ng baybaying ito sa paglipas ng panahon. Sa mga panahon ng yelo sa nakalipas na dalawang milyong taon, tumaas at bumaba ang dagat nang mahigit isandaang metro. Mga 20,000 taon na ang nakararaan, mga 120 metro itong mas mababa kaysa ngayon: naging tuyong lupa ang mababababaw na dagat ng Timog-Silangang Asya at nadugtong ang Borneo, Sumatra at Java sa kontinente, ngunit nanatiling pulo ang Luzon. Nang matunaw ang yelo, bumalik ang dagat – at lumampas pa sa kinaroroonan nito ngayon. Ipinakikita ng isang core ng putik na hinukay sa West Bay, ang bahagi ng lawa sa harap ng Biñan, na **mga 6,600 hanggang 4,700 taon na ang nakararaan ay bahagi ng dagat ang lawa**, isang maalat na lagunang mahigit sampung metro ang lalim na karugtong ng Look ng Maynila. Naging bahagyang maalat ito mga 4,100 taon na ang nakararaan at naging tubig-tabang mga 3,000 taon na ang nakararaan, nang umatras ang dagat at iniangat ang lupa sa pagitan ng Muntinlupa at Parañaque ng paggalaw ng mismong West Valley Fault.

### 7. Mga panahon at bagyo

Sa ngayon, tropikal ang klima ng Biñan na may dalawang malinaw na panahon: ang **tag-araw mula Nobyembre hanggang Abril** at ang **tag-ulan mula Mayo hanggang Oktubre**, kapag dinadala ng habagat ang humigit-kumulang 90 bahagdan ng ulan sa buong taon. Sa Sangley Point, ang pinakamalapit na istasyon ng PAGASA, ang karaniwang dami ng ulan sa isang taon ay mga 2,080 milimetro, at mahigit 400 milimetro bawat isa sa Hulyo at Agosto. Dumaraan ang mga bagyo sa rehiyon mula Hunyo hanggang Disyembre. Ang pinakamatindi sa alaala ng mga tao ay ang **Ondoy** noong Setyembre 2009, na nagpaangat sa lawa sa mga 14 na metro sa panukat nito – malapit sa rekord noong 1919 – at nagpalubog sa 192 barangay sa baybayin nang ilang linggo, na inaasahang aabutin nang hindi bababa sa 144 na araw bago humupa. Naranasan itong muli ng mga bayan sa baybayin noong 2020 dahil sa Ulysses at noong Oktubre 2024 dahil sa Kristine.

### 8. Nagbabagong klima

Nagbabago rin ang klima. Sinukat ng PAGASA ang pag-init na mga **0.65 °C sa buong Pilipinas mula 1951 hanggang 2010**, at ang **2024 ang pinakamainit na taon** na naitala sa bansa. Ganito rin ang ipinakikita ng rekonstruksiyon ng klima ng NASA para sa mismong kinaroroonan ng Biñan: ang karaniwang temperatura noong 2015–2024 ay mga 0.4 °C na mas mataas kaysa noong 1981–1990, at ang 2024 ang pinakamainit na taon sa serye. Inaasahan ng PAGASA ang karagdagang pag-init na mga 2 °C pagsapit ng kalagitnaan ng siglo. Ramdam na ang init: noong Abril 15, 2025, umabot sa **50 °C** ang heat index sa Los Baños, 27 kilometro ang layo. At tumataas ang tubig: sa paligid ng Look ng Maynila, tumaas nang mga isang metro ang antas ng dagat kaugnay ng lupa mula 1964 hanggang 2002, pangunahin dahil lumulubog ang lupa sa labis na pagsipsip ng tubig sa ilalim nito – isang panganib na nagpapatagal din sa mga baha sa baybayin ng lawa.

### 9. Matabang lupa, lumalaking lungsod

Pinataba ng abo ng bulkan, ng banlik ng ilog at ng patag na lupa ang Biñan: ang mga lupa ng bahaging ito ng talampas ng Cavite at Laguna, na nabuo sa batong tuff, ay kadalasang malalalim na luad at *clay loam*, at halos patag ang kapatagan malapit sa lawa. Sa loob ng maraming siglo, binuhay ng lupang ito ang mga palayan at tubuhan na pinatutubigan ng mga kanal – ang *patubigan* na pinagmulan ng pangalan ng Barangay Tubigan. Sa nakalipas na limampung taon, ginawang lungsod ng mga expressway, ng riles at ng mga industrial park ang mga bukid na ito, at urban na ngayon ang lahat ng 24 na barangay. Isinasalaysay ng mga susunod na kabanata kung paano nanirahan ang mga tao sa baybaying ito, sa pagitan ng apoy, ng fault at ng lawa.

**Did You Know?** **Laguna de Bay is not a “bay”.** The name means “the lake of Bay”, after the lakeshore town of Bay: in 1591 the whole province was still “the alcalde-mayoralty styled by itself Bay”. In the dry season the lake even drinks from the sea: when its level falls below that of Manila Bay, the Pasig River flows backward and brings in salt water ([Account of the encomiendas, 1591, in Blair & Robertson vol. 8 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13742)) ([Santos-Borja & Nepomuceno 2006 | ILEC](https://wldb.ilec.or.jp/itembyid/10202)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Hindi “look” ang Laguna de Bay.** Ang ibig sabihin ng pangalan ay “ang lawa ng Bay”, mula sa bayan ng Bay sa baybayin nito: noong 1591, “alcaldía mayor na tinatawag na Bay” pa ang buong lalawigan. Sa tag-araw, umiinom pa nga ang lawa mula sa dagat: kapag bumaba ito sa antas ng Look ng Maynila, bumabaligtad ang agos ng Ilog Pasig at nagdadala ito ng tubig-alat.

**Did You Know?** **One of the most active volcanoes of the Philippines is Biñan’s neighbour.** Taal, the second most frequently erupting of the country’s 24 active volcanoes, lies only about 38 km from the Poblacion, hidden behind the Tagaytay ridge. The ash of its January 2020 eruption fell across Laguna and Metro Manila, on either side of Biñan ([Delos Reyes et al., A synthesis and review of historical eruptions at Taal Volcano, 2018 | PHIVOLCS](https://www.phivolcs.dost.gov.ph/tag/taal-volcanoeruption-historytephra-fallpyroclastic-density-currentvolcanic-tsunami/)) ([Impacts of Taal Volcano phreatic eruption, 2020 | Manila Observatory](https://www.observatory.ph/2020/04/20/impacts-of-taal-volcano-phreatic-eruption-12-january-2020-on-the-environment-and-population-satellite-based-observations-compared-with-historical-records/)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Kapitbahay ng Biñan ang isa sa pinakaaktibong bulkan sa Pilipinas.** Ang Taal, ang ikalawa sa pinakamadalas pumutok sa 24 na aktibong bulkan ng bansa, ay mga 38 kilometro lamang mula sa Poblacion, nakatago sa likod ng tagaytay ng Tagaytay. Bumagsak ang abo ng pagsabog nito noong Enero 2020 sa Laguna at sa Kamaynilaan, sa magkabilang panig ng Biñan.

## Prehistory / Sinaunang Panahon

### 1. A long walk

The story of the first people on the shores of Laguna de Bay begins far away, in Africa, where the human family was born. Some 1.8 million years ago, early humans had already left Africa: their bones and tools have been found at Dmanisi, in Georgia, between Europe and Asia ([Ferring et al. 2011, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3127884)). *Homo erectus* reached the island of Java about 1.3 million years ago, and no earlier than 1.5 million years ago ([Matsu’ura et al., Age control of the first appearance datum for Javanese Homo erectus, 2020 | Science (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919224/)). Java, Sumatra and Borneo could then be reached on foot, because the low seas of the ice ages joined them to the mainland. But further east, beyond an invisible frontier later called the **Wallace Line**, the islands – Sulawesi, Flores and the Philippines – were always separated from the continent by deep water. **Luzon has never been connected to any continent** ([Field Museum, 2016](https://www.fieldmuseum.org/about/press/worlds-greatest-concentration-unique-mammal-species-philippine-island)). To reach it, early humans had to cross the sea – by accident on rafts of vegetation, or on purpose; nobody knows.

### 2. The first visitors of Luzon

And yet they came. In the Cagayan Valley of northern Luzon, in the town of Rizal, Kalinga (not to be confused with the province of Rizal near our lake), archaeologists found 57 stone tools and the skeleton of an extinct rhinoceros, *Rhinoceros philippinensis*, whose bones bear cut marks and were broken to reach the marrow. They are about **709,000 years old** – the oldest known trace of humans in the Philippines, left by people whose species we do not know ([Ingicco et al. 2018, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0072-8)). Much later, in Callao Cave, near Peñablanca in Cagayan, researchers found the teeth and bones of a small human with curved finger and toe bones. In 2019 they named it a new species, ***Homo luzonensis***, the human of Luzon. It lived at least 50,000 years ago, and perhaps more than 130,000 years ago according to a new dating published in 2023 ([Détroit et al. 2019, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1067-9)) ([Grün & Stringer 2023, Quaternary Science Reviews](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/direct-dating-of-human-fossils-and-the-ever-changing-story-of-hum/)). Like the tiny *Homo floresiensis* of Flores, it evolved alone on an island for a very long time.

### 3. Our species arrives

Our own species, ***Homo sapiens***, left Africa much later, perhaps 70,000 to 50,000 years ago, and followed the coasts of Asia ([Bergström et al. 2021, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03244-5)). On Sulawesi, people painted a wild pig on a cave wall at least 45,500 years ago ([Brumm et al. 2021, Science Advances](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7806210)). In the Philippines, the oldest bone of a modern human is a shin bone from **Tabon Cave** in Palawan, dated to about 47,000 years ago, with a wide margin of error; the famous skull fragment known as “Tabon Man” is younger, about 16,500 years old ([Détroit et al. 2004, Comptes Rendus Palevol](https://sciencepress.mnhn.fr/en/periodiques/comptes-rendus-palevol/3/fasc8/upper-pleistocene-homo-sapiens-tabon-cave-palawan-philippines-description-and-dating-new-discoveries)). The newcomers met older humans on the way: the **Ayta Magbukon** of Bataan carry more DNA from the archaic **Denisovans** than any other people in the world. Genetics shows that the Philippines was peopled by **at least five waves** of migration over the last 50,000 years, beginning with the ancestors of today’s Agta, Ayta and Dumagat peoples ([Larena et al. 2021, Current Biology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8596304)) ([Larena et al. 2021, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2026132118)). Their descendants, the Dumagat and Remontado, still live in the Sierra Madre, east of the lake.

### 4. A different land

The land these people knew was not the one we see. During the last ice age, some 20,000 years ago, the sea stood about 120 metres lower than today and the coasts of Luzon lay far out; when the ice melted, the sea returned, and between about 6,600 and 4,700 years ago the part of the lake in front of Biñan was an arm of the sea, a salt lagoon connected to Manila Bay. It became the freshwater lake we know only about 3,000 years ago (see the chapter on geography) ([Jaraula et al. 2014, Quaternary International](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/30255)).

### 5. The seafarers

Around the time the lake turned fresh, new people arrived by sea. Between about 4,500 and 3,500 years ago, seafarers speaking **Austronesian** languages came down from Taiwan through the Batanes islands into northern Luzon ([Bellwood 2011, Current Anthropology](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/658181)). In the Cagayan Valley they left red-slipped pottery like that of eastern Taiwan and ornaments of Taiwanese jade ([Hung et al. 2007, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0707304104)). They knew the outrigger canoe and perhaps rice: the oldest grain of rice found in the Philippines, at Andarayan in Cagayan, is about 3,400 years old, although we do not know whether it was grown or gathered (Snow et al. 1986). Some scholars, following Wilhelm Solheim, prefer to see not one migration but a network of sea trade in which ideas travelled in every direction. Either way, **Tagalog is an Austronesian language**, a distant cousin of Malay, Javanese, Malagasy and Hawaiian: *mata* (eye), *lima* (five) and *bato* (stone) have relatives on islands from Taiwan to Hawaii.

### 6. Traces around the lake

Traces of these ancient times have been found around the lake. In the valleys of Baras, in Rizal province, stone tools collected in the early 20th century are thought, from their shape, to be between 50,000 and 20,000 years old, and small blades of obsidian and chert come from Baras, Tanay and Pililla ([Vitales 2013, Archaeological research in the Laguna de Bay area | Hukay](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/asp/article/view/3961)). On a cliff of volcanic tuff at the border of Angono and Binangonan, the painter Carlos “Botong” Francisco documented in 1965 more than a hundred figures carved into the rock – the National Museum now counts 179: the **Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs**, declared a National Cultural Treasure in 1973. Museum scientists have suggested a date around 3000 BCE, but their age cannot be established ([Presidential Decree No. 260, 1973 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1973/pd_260_1973.html)) ([Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs | National Museum of the Philippines](https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/regional-museums/angono-binangonan-petroglyphs/)) ([Philippines’ oldest artworks in danger of disappearing (AFP), 2014 | Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/389513/philippines-oldest-artworks-in-danger-of-disappearing)). In 2013, in Barangay Puypuy in the town of Bay, on the southern shore, a family digging in their garden found hundreds of pieces of earthenware jars; archaeologists of the University of the Philippines identified them as burial jars and estimated, from their shape, that they could be some 2,800 years old ([Ancient jars found in Bay, Laguna | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/435837)). Far away in Palawan, the **Manunggul Jar**, made around 800 BCE, shows on its lid two figures in a boat carrying a soul to the afterlife.

### 7. And in Biñan?

And in Biñan? **No prehistoric site has yet been found or excavated in Biñan**, nor anywhere on the western shore of the lake, from San Pedro to Calamba. This does not mean that no one lived here: the old shore lies under metres of river silt and lake mud, part of it was under the sea six thousand years ago, and the city grew quickly over the fields before anyone looked. Any old pottery, stone tool or bone found while digging should be reported to the National Museum, as the National Cultural Heritage Act (Republic Act No. 10066) asks.

### 8. Life on the lakeshore before writing

We can only imagine the life of the first people of the lakeshore, using what archaeology has found nearby and what the first Spanish chroniclers saw a few centuries later: fishing in the lake, gardens of taro, yam and banana, and later rice; houses raised on stilts; boats of planks sewn and pegged together, like the *balangay* of Butuan, the oldest of which is about 1,700 years old – the boat whose name became *barangay*, the village community ([The Butuan archaeological finds | UNESCO Silk Roads](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/the_butuan_archaeological_finds_profound_implications_for_philippines_and_southeast_asian_prehistory.pdf)); spirits and ancestors, the *anito*, honoured in rituals; the dead buried in jars. During the Metal Age, from about 500 BCE, iron tools, glass beads and gold came through trade. By around 900 CE, the lake was already part of a wider world of trade and writing – as a copper plate found at Lumban would one day reveal.

### 1. Isang mahabang paglalakbay

Ang kuwento ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng Lawa ng Laguna ay nagsisimula sa malayong Aprika, kung saan isinilang ang pamilya ng tao. Mga 1.8 milyong taon na ang nakararaan, nakaalis na sa Aprika ang mga sinaunang tao: natagpuan ang kanilang mga buto at kasangkapan sa Dmanisi, sa Georgia, sa pagitan ng Europa at Asya. Nakarating ang *Homo erectus* sa pulo ng Java mga 1.3 milyong taon na ang nakararaan, at hindi bago ang 1.5 milyong taon. Noon, maaaring lakarin ang Java, Sumatra at Borneo, dahil nadudugtong ang mga ito sa kontinente kapag mababa ang dagat sa panahon ng yelo. Ngunit sa dakong silangan, lampas sa isang di-nakikitang hangganan na tinawag kalaunang **Wallace Line**, laging nakahiwalay sa kontinente ang mga pulo – ang Sulawesi, Flores at ang Pilipinas – dahil sa malalim na dagat. **Kailanman ay hindi nadugtong ang Luzon sa anumang kontinente.** Upang marating ito, kinailangang tawirin ng mga sinaunang tao ang dagat – nang di-sinasadya sakay ng mga balsa ng halaman, o nang sinasadya; walang nakaaalam.

### 2. Ang mga unang dumalaw sa Luzon

Ngunit dumating sila. Sa Lambak ng Cagayan sa hilagang Luzon, sa bayan ng Rizal, Kalinga (hindi ang lalawigan ng Rizal malapit sa ating lawa), nakatagpo ang mga arkeologo ng 57 kasangkapang bato at ng kalansay ng isang nawalang uri ng rhinoceros, ang *Rhinoceros philippinensis*, na may mga bakas ng hiwa sa buto at binasag upang makuha ang utak nito. Mga **709,000 taon na** ang tanda ng mga ito – ang pinakamatandang kilalang bakas ng tao sa Pilipinas, iniwan ng mga taong hindi natin alam ang uri. Pagkaraan ng mahabang panahon, sa Kuweba ng Callao malapit sa Peñablanca, Cagayan, natagpuan ng mga mananaliksik ang mga ngipin at buto ng isang maliit na tao na may mga baluktot na buto ng daliri sa kamay at paa. Noong 2019, pinangalanan nila itong bagong uri, ang ***Homo luzonensis***, ang tao ng Luzon. Nabuhay ito nang hindi bababa sa 50,000 taon na ang nakararaan, at baka mahigit pa sa 130,000 taon ayon sa isang bagong pagtataya noong 2023. Tulad ng maliit na *Homo floresiensis* ng Flores, nag-iisa itong umunlad sa isang pulo sa napakahabang panahon.

### 3. Dumating ang ating uri

Ang sarili nating uri, ang ***Homo sapiens***, ay umalis sa Aprika nang mas huli, marahil 70,000 hanggang 50,000 taon na ang nakararaan, at sinundan ang mga baybayin ng Asya. Sa Sulawesi, nagpinta ang mga tao ng isang baboy-ramo sa dingding ng kuweba nang hindi bababa sa 45,500 taon na ang nakararaan. Sa Pilipinas, ang pinakamatandang buto ng modernong tao ay isang buto ng binti mula sa **Kuweba ng Tabon** sa Palawan, na tinatayang mga 47,000 taon na, bagaman malaki ang palugit; mas bata ang tanyag na piraso ng bungo na kilala bilang “Taong Tabon”, mga 16,500 taon lamang. Nakatagpo ng mga bagong dating ang mas sinaunang mga tao sa daan: ang mga **Ayta Magbukon** ng Bataan ang may pinakamaraming DNA ng sinaunang **Denisovan** sa buong mundo. Ipinakikita ng henetika na ang Pilipinas ay pinanirahan ng **hindi bababa sa limang alon** ng pandarayuhan sa nakalipas na 50,000 taon, simula sa mga ninuno ng mga Agta, Ayta at Dumagat ngayon. Ang kanilang mga inapo, ang mga Dumagat at Remontado, ay naninirahan pa rin sa Sierra Madre, sa silangan ng lawa.

### 4. Isang ibang lupain

Hindi ganito ang lupaing nakilala ng mga taong ito. Noong huling panahon ng yelo, mga 20,000 taon na ang nakararaan, mga 120 metrong mas mababa ang dagat kaysa ngayon at malayo ang mga baybayin ng Luzon; nang matunaw ang yelo, bumalik ang dagat, at mula mga 6,600 hanggang 4,700 taon na ang nakararaan, bahagi ng dagat ang lawa sa harap ng Biñan, isang maalat na lagunang karugtong ng Look ng Maynila. Naging lawang tubig-tabang ito na kilala natin mga 3,000 taon pa lamang na ang nakararaan (tingnan ang kabanata tungkol sa heograpiya).

### 5. Ang mga manlalayag

Halos kasabay ng pagtabang ng lawa, may mga bagong taong dumating mula sa dagat. Mula mga 4,500 hanggang 3,500 taon na ang nakararaan, bumaba mula Taiwan, sa pamamagitan ng mga pulo ng Batanes, patungong hilagang Luzon ang mga manlalayag na nagsasalita ng mga wikang **Austronesyo**. Sa Lambak ng Cagayan, nag-iwan sila ng mapulang palayok na kahawig ng sa silangang Taiwan at ng mga palamuting yari sa jade ng Taiwan. Kilala nila ang bangkang may katig at marahil ang palay: ang pinakamatandang butil ng bigas na natagpuan sa Pilipinas, sa Andarayan, Cagayan, ay mga 3,400 taon na, bagaman hindi natin alam kung itinanim ito o tinipon lamang. May mga iskolar, kasunod ni Wilhelm Solheim, na mas nakikita hindi ang iisang pandarayuhan kundi isang lambat ng kalakalang pandagat kung saan naglakbay ang mga ideya sa lahat ng direksiyon. Alinman dito, **wikang Austronesyo ang Tagalog**, malayong pinsan ng Malay, Javanese, Malagasy at Hawaiian: may mga kamag-anak na salita ang *mata*, *lima* at *bato* sa mga pulo mula Taiwan hanggang Hawaii.

### 6. Mga bakas sa paligid ng lawa

May mga bakas ng sinaunang panahong ito na natagpuan sa paligid ng lawa. Sa mga lambak ng Baras, sa lalawigan ng Rizal, may mga kasangkapang bato na tinipon noong unang bahagi ng ika-20 siglo na, batay sa hugis, ay tinatayang 50,000 hanggang 20,000 taon na, at may maliliit na talim na obsidiyana at chert mula sa Baras, Tanay at Pililla. Sa isang bangin ng batong tuff sa hangganan ng Angono at Binangonan, itinala ng pintor na si Carlos “Botong” Francisco noong 1965 ang mahigit isandaang larawang inukit sa bato – 179 na ang bilang ngayon ng Pambansang Museo: ang **Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs**, na idineklarang Pambansang Yamang Pangkultura noong 1973. Iminungkahi ng mga siyentipiko ng museo na mula ang mga ito noong mga 3000 BCE, ngunit hindi matiyak ang kanilang edad. Noong 2013, sa Barangay Puypuy sa bayan ng Bay, sa timog na baybayin, nakahukay ang isang pamilya ng daan-daang piraso ng tapayang luwad sa kanilang bakuran; kinilala ng mga arkeologo ng Unibersidad ng Pilipinas na mga tapayang libingan ang mga ito at tinantiya, batay sa hugis, na maaaring mga 2,800 taon na ang tanda. Sa malayong Palawan, ang **Banga ng Manunggul**, na ginawa noong mga 800 BCE, ay may dalawang pigura sa takip na nakasakay sa bangkang nagdadala ng kaluluwa sa kabilang buhay.

### 7. At sa Biñan?

At sa Biñan? **Wala pang natatagpuan o nahuhukay na sinaunang pook sa Biñan**, ni saanman sa kanlurang baybayin ng lawa, mula San Pedro hanggang Calamba. Hindi ito nangangahulugang walang nanirahan dito: nasa ilalim ng ilang metrong banlik ng ilog at putik ng lawa ang lumang baybayin, bahagi nito ay nasa ilalim ng dagat anim na libong taon na ang nakararaan, at mabilis na lumaki ang lungsod sa ibabaw ng mga bukid bago pa may nakapaghanap. Anumang lumang palayok, kasangkapang bato o buto na matagpuan sa paghuhukay ay dapat iulat sa Pambansang Museo, ayon sa hinihingi ng National Cultural Heritage Act (Batas Republika Blg. 10066).

### 8. Buhay sa baybayin ng lawa bago ang pagsulat

Maaari lamang nating isipin ang buhay ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng lawa, gamit ang natagpuan ng arkeolohiya sa malapit at ang nakita ng mga unang tagapagtalang Espanyol pagkaraan ng ilang siglo: pangingisda sa lawa, mga taniman ng gabi, ube at saging, at kalaunan ay palay; mga bahay na nakataas sa mga haligi; mga bangkang yari sa tablang tinahi at pinakuan ng tarugo, gaya ng *balangay* ng Butuan, na ang pinakamatanda ay mga 1,700 taon na – ang bangkang pinagmulan ng salitang *barangay*, ang pamayanan; mga espiritu at ninuno, ang mga *anito*, na pinararangalan sa mga ritwal; at ang mga patay na inililibing sa mga tapayan. Sa Panahon ng Metal, mula mga 500 BCE, dumating sa pamamagitan ng kalakalan ang mga kasangkapang bakal, mga butil na salamin at ginto. Pagsapit ng mga taong 900, bahagi na ang lawa ng isang mas malawak na mundo ng kalakalan at pagsulat – gaya ng ibubunyag balang araw ng isang platong tanso na natagpuan sa Lumban.

**Did You Know?** **The Philippines had its own human species.** *Homo luzonensis*, named in 2019 after the island of Luzon, lived in Callao Cave at least 50,000 years ago – perhaps more than 130,000 years ago. Its tiny teeth and curved finger bones show that it evolved apart from every other human ([Détroit et al. 2019, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1067-9)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **May sariling uri ng tao ang Pilipinas.** Ang *Homo luzonensis*, na pinangalanan noong 2019 mula sa pulo ng Luzon, ay nanirahan sa Kuweba ng Callao hindi bababa sa 50,000 taon na ang nakararaan – marahil higit pa sa 130,000 taon. Ipinakikita ng maliliit nitong ngipin at ng mga kurbadong buto ng daliri nito na umunlad ito nang hiwalay sa lahat ng ibang uri ng tao.

**Did You Know?** **The word *barangay* comes from a boat.** The *balangay* was a plank boat that carried a whole community; its name passed to the village it founded. The oldest balangay found at Butuan, in Mindanao, is about 1,700 years old ([The Butuan archaeological finds | UNESCO Silk Roads](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/the_butuan_archaeological_finds_profound_implications_for_philippines_and_southeast_asian_prehistory.pdf)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Galing sa isang bangka ang salitang *barangay*.** Ang *balangay* ay isang bangkang yari sa tabla na nagdadala ng isang buong pamayanan; napunta ang pangalan nito sa nayong itinatag nito. Mga 1,700 taon na ang pinakamatandang balangay na natagpuan sa Butuan, sa Mindanao.

## Pre-Colonial Era / Panahong Pre-Kolonyal

### 1. What we know, and how

For the six centuries before the Spaniards came, no document names Biñan and no excavation has yet been made on its soil. What we know of life on this shore comes from its neighbours – **Tabuco** (today Cabuyao), Bay, Pila, Lumban – from Chinese and Spanish writers, and from the customs the Tagalog communities of the region shared. The land of present-day Biñan most likely belonged to Tabuco ([The evolution of Biñan from pueblo to city | Manila Standard](https://www.manilastandard.net/?p=314204584)). Its people lived in small settlements scattered along the lake and the rivers coming down from the hills: they fished in Laguna de Bay, planted rice and gardens, gathered fruits in the forest and hunted wild pig, and they were grouped in **barangays** led by chiefs, the **datu**.

### 2. The copper plate of 900

The oldest written document ever found in the Philippines comes from this lake. In the late 1980s, a worker dredging sand near the mouth of the Lumbang River, at Lumban, found a thin copper plate covered with writing. It is dated to the year 822 of the Śaka era – **21 April 900** ([Santos 1996, The Date of the Laguna Copperplate Inscription | Philippine Studies](https://www.jstor.org/stable/42634198)). Written mostly in Old Malay, in the Kawi script of Java, with Sanskrit and Old Javanese or Old Tagalog words, the **Laguna Copperplate Inscription** is a receipt: on behalf of the chief of **Tundun** (Tondo), it frees the descendants of a man named Namwaran from a debt of one *kati* and eight *suwarna* – weights of gold – before witnesses from places called Pailah, Puliran and Binuangan ([Postma, The Laguna Copper-Plate Inscription: A Valuable Philippine Document, 1991 | Bulletin of the Indo-Pacific Prehistory Association](https://journals.lib.washington.edu/index.php/BIPPA/article/view/11381)). Scholars still debate where these places were: Pailah may be Pila, on our lake, or Paila in Bulacan; none of them is Biñan. But the plate shows that by the year 900 the lake belonged to a world of writing, gold, debts and titles linked to Java and Sumatra. It is kept today at the National Museum in Manila.

### 3. Ma-i and the porcelain trade

The lake also belonged to the trade routes of Asia. Chinese annals mention merchants from a country called **Ma-i** as early as 982 ([Hirth & Rockhill, Chau Ju-kua, 1911, p. 160 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/chaujukuahiswork00chauuoft/page/159/mode/2up)). In 1225 the Chinese official Zhao Rugua described Ma-i: big villages on both banks of a river, Chinese ships anchored before the “official place” where trade took place, local chiefs who received **white umbrellas** as gifts, and goods exchanged on credit – cotton, yellow wax, tortoiseshell, betel nuts and cloth against porcelain, gold, iron pots, lead, glass beads and needles. Ma-i is usually placed on Mindoro, but the historian Go Bon Juan has argued that it may have been **Ba-i**, the town of Bay that gave its name to our lake – an attractive but unproven idea ([Go 2005, Ma’i in Chinese Records – Mindoro or Bai? | Philippine Studies](https://web.archive.org/web/20131021221348/http://www.philippinestudies.net/ojs/index.php/ps/article/download/216/223)). What is certain is that at Pinagbayanan, in Pila, archaeologists excavated in 1967–68 some 241 graves of the 12th to 14th centuries, with Chinese porcelain of the Song and Yuan dynasties, cremations, net weights, spindle whorls and traces of wet-rice farming, cotton weaving and ironworking ([Vitales 2013, Archaeological research in the Laguna de Bay area | Hukay](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/asp/article/view/3961)).

### 4. Brunei and Islam

In the 16th century the strongest ties of the Tagalog world ran toward **Brunei** and Islam. When the Spaniards reached Manila Bay in 1570–71, they found Maynila ruled by **Rajah Sulayman** and Tondo by **Lakandula**. A Spanish report of 1572 notes that the people of the coast did not eat pork “because they have had dealings with the Moros of Burney, who have preached to them a little of the teaching of Mahoma”, and that some who had been to Brunei could read a few words of the Koran ([Relation of the Conquest of the Island of Luzon, 1572, in Blair & Robertson vol. 3 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13616)). The influence reached the language itself: according to the great Tagalog dictionary of 1754, the word *binyag*, “to baptize”, “es de Borney” – it came from Brunei, where it meant to pour water from above in a Muslim ablution, before it was used for the Christian baptism ([Noceda & Sanlúcar, Vocabulario de la lengua tagala, 1754 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/vocabulariodelal00noce)).

### 5. The society of the barangay

The best description of Tagalog society was written on the shores of this lake: the Franciscan **Juan de Plasencia** signed his *Customs of the Tagalogs* at Nagcarlán, Laguna, on 21 October 1589. “This people always had chiefs, called by them *datos*,” he wrote; each ruled “sometimes as many as a hundred houses, sometimes even less than thirty”, a group “called in Tagalo a *barangay*”. Below the datu were the free **maharlika**, who followed him to war and rowed for him, the **aliping namamahay**, who had their own houses and gave him part of their harvest, and the **aliping sa gigilid**, who lived in his house and could be sold; one could fall into servitude through war or debt and buy one’s freedom back in gold. Some chiefs owned “fisheries, with established limits, and sections of the rivers for markets”; at Pila, the free men paid their datu a hundred measures of rice a year. Women kept their own property, received a dowry, the *bigaycaya*, and could divorce ([Plasencia, Customs of the Tagalogs, 1589, in Blair & Robertson vol. 7 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13701)). Settlements could be fortified: in 1571 Cainta, at the head of the lake, had about a thousand inhabitants behind thick bamboo, a wall and small cannons.

### 6. Beliefs and the baybayin

The Tagalogs worshipped **Bathala**, “maker of all things”, honoured the sun, the moon and the stars that marked the farming year – *Tala*, the morning star, and *Mapolon*, the Pleiades – and the spirits of their ancestors, the *anito*, represented by images called *lic-ha*. There were no temples: a house was prepared for each feast, led by a *catalonan*, often a woman of rank. The dead were mourned for four days and laid in a boat as a coffin. Men and women wore gold bracelets and anklets; around 1590 the illustrated *Boxer Codex* shows Tagalog couples in their *baro*, *saya* and *putong* ([Boxer Codex Illustrations | CCP Encyclopedia of Philippine Art](https://epa.culturalcenter.gov.ph/3/82/2152)) ([Boxer Codex, ca. 1590, digital copy | Lilly Library, Indiana University](https://purl.dlib.indiana.edu/iudl/general/VAB8326)). Many could read and write in **baybayin**, the Tagalog script, which appears beside the Latin alphabet in the *Doctrina Christiana* of 1593, the first book printed in the Philippines ([Doctrina Christiana, 1593 | Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/48031307/)).

### 7. The year 1571

This world changed in 1571. Miguel López de Legazpi settled in Manila in May. On **15 August 1571**, his young captain **Juan de Salcedo** left with a galley, a hundred soldiers and three pieces of artillery against Cainta, which he took after a short siege; about four hundred of its defenders, men and women, were killed. The same report then states that “the villages about this lake, containing about twenty-four or twenty-six thousand men, were pacified by the captain Juan de Sauzedo” ([Relation of the Conquest of the Island of Luzon, 1572 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13616)). The lake towns were soon given as *encomiendas* to Spanish settlers: Pila in November 1571, and in 1591 **Tabuco** was held by Don Luis Enríquez with a thousand tributes, about four thousand people – Biñan was probably among them, though it is not named ([Account of the encomiendas, 1591, in Blair & Robertson vol. 8 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13742)). From 1578, the Franciscans of Juan de Plasencia founded missions all around the lake. A local tradition says that Salcedo and missionaries stopped on the site of Biñan, planted a cross and baptized its people, and that the place was called *Binyagan*, the “place of baptism”; the documents of the time confirm Salcedo’s journey around the lake in the second half of 1571, but they do not mention Biñan.

### 1. Ang ating nalalaman, at kung paano

Sa loob ng anim na siglo bago dumating ang mga Espanyol, walang dokumentong bumabanggit sa Biñan at wala pang paghuhukay na ginagawa sa lupa nito. Ang alam natin tungkol sa buhay sa baybaying ito ay nagmumula sa mga kapitbahay nito – ang **Tabuco** (Cabuyao ngayon), Bay, Pila at Lumban – sa mga manunulat na Tsino at Espanyol, at sa mga kaugaliang pinagsasaluhan ng mga pamayanang Tagalog sa rehiyon. Malamang na bahagi ng Tabuco ang lupain ng Biñan ngayon. Nanirahan ang mga tao nito sa maliliit na pamayanang nakakalat sa tabi ng lawa at ng mga ilog na bumababa mula sa kabundukan: nangingisda sila sa Lawa ng Laguna, nagtatanim ng palay at gulay, nangunguha ng prutas sa gubat at nangangaso ng baboy-ramo, at nakapangkat sila sa mga **barangay** na pinamumunuan ng mga **datu**.

### 2. Ang platong tanso ng 900

Mula sa lawang ito ang pinakamatandang nakasulat na dokumentong natagpuan sa Pilipinas. Sa huling bahagi ng dekada 1980, isang manggagawang humuhukay ng buhangin malapit sa bunganga ng Ilog Lumbang, sa Lumban, ang nakatagpo ng isang manipis na platong tanso na puno ng sulat. Ito ay may petsang taong 822 ng panahong Śaka – **Abril 21, 900**. Nakasulat ito pangunahin sa Lumang Malay, sa titik Kawi ng Java, na may mga salitang Sanskrit at Lumang Javanese o Lumang Tagalog. Ang **Inskripsiyon sa Binatbat na Tanso ng Laguna** ay isang katibayan ng pagbabayad: sa ngalan ng pinuno ng **Tundun** (Tondo), pinalalaya nito ang mga inapo ng isang lalaking nagngangalang Namwaran mula sa utang na isang *kati* at walong *suwarna* – mga timbang ng ginto – sa harap ng mga saksi mula sa mga lugar na tinatawag na Pailah, Puliran at Binuangan. Pinagtatalunan pa ng mga iskolar kung nasaan ang mga lugar na ito: maaaring ang Pailah ay ang Pila, sa ating lawa, o ang Paila sa Bulacan; wala sa mga ito ang Biñan. Ngunit ipinakikita ng plato na pagsapit ng taong 900, bahagi na ang lawa ng isang mundo ng pagsulat, ginto, utang at mga titulong kaugnay ng Java at Sumatra. Iniingatan ito ngayon sa Pambansang Museo sa Maynila.

### 3. Ang Ma-i at ang kalakalan ng porselana

Bahagi rin ang lawa ng mga ruta ng kalakalan ng Asya. Binabanggit ng mga talaang Tsino ang mga mangangalakal mula sa bansang tinatawag na **Ma-i** noon pang 982. Noong 1225, inilarawan ng opisyal na Tsinong si Zhao Rugua ang Ma-i: malalaking nayon sa magkabilang pampang ng isang ilog, mga barkong Tsino na nakadaong sa harap ng “opisyal na lugar” kung saan nagaganap ang kalakalan, mga lokal na pinunong binibigyan ng **puting payong** bilang regalo, at mga kalakal na ipinagpapalit nang pautang – bulak, dilaw na pagkit, kabibi ng pagong, bunga ng nganga at tela kapalit ng porselana, ginto, kaldero ng bakal, tingga, butil na salamin at karayom. Karaniwang inilalagay ang Ma-i sa Mindoro, ngunit iminungkahi ng mananalaysay na si Go Bon Juan na maaaring ito ang **Ba-i**, ang bayan ng Bay na nagbigay ng pangalan sa ating lawa – isang kaakit-akit ngunit hindi pa napatutunayang ideya. Ang tiyak ay sa Pinagbayanan, sa Pila, nahukay ng mga arkeologo noong 1967–68 ang mga 241 libingan mula ika-12 hanggang ika-14 na siglo, na may porselanang Tsino ng mga dinastiyang Song at Yuan, mga labi ng pagsusunog ng bangkay, mga pabigat ng lambat, mga ikiran ng sinulid at mga bakas ng pagsasaka ng palay sa basang bukid, paghahabi ng bulak at paggawa ng bakal.

### 4. Ang Brunei at ang Islam

Noong ika-16 na siglo, patungong **Brunei** at Islam ang pinakamalakas na ugnayan ng mundong Tagalog. Nang dumating ang mga Espanyol sa Look ng Maynila noong 1570–71, nadatnan nilang pinamumunuan ni **Rajah Sulayman** ang Maynila at ni **Lakandula** ang Tondo. Ayon sa isang ulat Espanyol noong 1572, hindi kumakain ng baboy ang mga tao sa baybayin “dahil nakipag-ugnayan sila sa mga Moro ng Burney, na nangaral sa kanila nang kaunti tungkol sa aral ni Mahoma”, at may ilang nakarating sa Brunei na nakababasa ng ilang salita ng Koran. Umabot ang impluwensiyang ito sa wika mismo: ayon sa malaking diksiyonaryong Tagalog noong 1754, ang salitang *binyag* ay “es de Borney” – galing ito sa Brunei, kung saan ang ibig sabihin ay pagbubuhos ng tubig mula sa itaas sa isang paglilinis na Muslim, bago ito ginamit para sa binyag na Kristiyano.

### 5. Ang lipunan ng barangay

Isinulat sa baybayin ng lawang ito ang pinakamahusay na paglalarawan ng lipunang Tagalog: nilagdaan ng Pransiskanong si **Juan de Plasencia** ang kaniyang *Mga Kaugalian ng mga Tagalog* sa Nagcarlán, Laguna, noong Oktubre 21, 1589. “Laging may mga pinuno ang bayang ito, na tinatawag nilang *datos*,” isinulat niya; ang bawat isa ay namumuno “kung minsan sa hanggang isandaang bahay, kung minsan ay kulang pa sa tatlumpu”, isang pangkat na “tinatawag sa Tagalog na *barangay*”. Sa ilalim ng datu ay ang malalayang **maharlika**, na sumasama sa kaniya sa digmaan at sumasagwan para sa kaniya; ang mga **aliping namamahay**, na may sariling bahay at nagbibigay sa kaniya ng bahagi ng kanilang ani; at ang mga **aliping sa gigilid**, na nakatira sa kaniyang bahay at maaaring ipagbili. Maaaring maging alipin dahil sa digmaan o utang, at maaaring tubusin ang sariling kalayaan sa pamamagitan ng ginto. May mga pinunong nagmamay-ari ng “mga palaisdaan na may takdang hangganan, at mga bahagi ng ilog para sa pamilihan”; sa Pila, nagbabayad ang mga malayang tao sa kanilang datu ng isandaang takal ng palay bawat taon. Nananatili sa kababaihan ang sarili nilang ari-arian, tumatanggap sila ng bigay-kaya, at maaari silang makipaghiwalay. Maaaring may kuta ang mga pamayanan: noong 1571, ang Cainta, sa ulunan ng lawa, ay may mga isanlibong naninirahan sa likod ng makapal na kawayan, isang pader at maliliit na kanyon.

### 6. Mga paniniwala at ang baybayin

Sinasamba ng mga Tagalog si **Bathala**, “ang lumikha ng lahat ng bagay”, iginagalang ang araw, ang buwan at ang mga bituing nagtatakda ng taon ng pagsasaka – ang *Tala*, ang bituin sa umaga, at ang *Mapolon*, ang Pleyades – at ang mga espiritu ng kanilang mga ninuno, ang mga *anito*, na kinakatawan ng mga larawang tinatawag na *lic-ha*. Walang templo: inihahanda ang isang bahay para sa bawat pagdiriwang, na pinangungunahan ng isang *catalonan*, na kadalasang babaeng may mataas na katayuan. Ipinagluluksa ang mga patay nang apat na araw at inihihimlay sa isang bangka bilang kabaong. Nagsusuot ang mga lalaki at babae ng mga pulseras at kadena sa bukung-bukong na ginto; noong mga 1590, ipinakikita ng may larawang *Boxer Codex* ang mga mag-asawang Tagalog na nakasuot ng *baro*, *saya* at *putong*. Marami ang marunong bumasa at sumulat sa **baybayin**, ang sulat Tagalog, na makikita sa tabi ng alpabetong Latin sa *Doctrina Christiana* ng 1593, ang unang aklat na inilimbag sa Pilipinas.

### 7. Ang taong 1571

Nagbago ang mundong ito noong 1571. Nanirahan si Miguel López de Legazpi sa Maynila noong Mayo. Noong **Agosto 15, 1571**, umalis ang kaniyang batang kapitang si **Juan de Salcedo** sakay ng isang galera, kasama ang isandaang sundalo at tatlong kanyon, laban sa Cainta, na nasakop niya pagkaraan ng maikling pagkubkob; mga apat na raan sa mga tagapagtanggol nito, lalaki at babae, ang napatay. Sinasabi pa ng parehong ulat na “ang mga nayon sa paligid ng lawang ito, na may mga dalawampu’t apat o dalawampu’t anim na libong lalaki, ay pinatahimik ni Kapitan Juan de Sauzedo”. Di nagtagal, ipinagkaloob ang mga bayan sa lawa bilang *encomienda* sa mga Espanyol: ang Pila noong Nobyembre 1571, at noong 1591 ay hawak ni Don Luis Enríquez ang **Tabuco** na may isanlibong tributo, mga apat na libong katao – malamang na kasama rito ang Biñan, bagaman hindi ito binabanggit. Mula 1578, nagtatag ang mga Pransiskano ni Juan de Plasencia ng mga misyon sa buong paligid ng lawa. Ayon sa isang lokal na tradisyon, huminto si Salcedo at ang mga misyonero sa pook ng Biñan, nagtirik ng krus at nagbinyag sa mga tao nito, kaya tinawag ang lugar na *Binyagan*, ang “lugar ng binyag”; pinatutunayan ng mga dokumento noong panahong iyon ang paglalakbay ni Salcedo sa paligid ng lawa sa ikalawang hati ng 1571, ngunit hindi nila binabanggit ang Biñan.

**Did You Know?** **The oldest document of the Philippines is about a debt of gold.** In the year 900, on behalf of the chief of Tondo, a copper plate cleared the descendants of Namwaran of a debt of one *kati* and eight *suwarna* of gold. It was found by chance, more than a thousand years later, while sand was being dredged at the mouth of the Lumbang River, on the other shore of our lake ([Postma 1991 | Bulletin of the Indo-Pacific Prehistory Association](https://journals.lib.washington.edu/index.php/BIPPA/article/view/11381)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Tungkol sa utang na ginto ang pinakamatandang dokumento ng Pilipinas.** Noong taong 900, sa ngalan ng pinuno ng Tondo, pinalaya ng isang platong tanso ang mga inapo ni Namwaran mula sa utang na isang *kati* at walong *suwarna* ng ginto. Natagpuan ito nang di-sinasadya, mahigit isang libong taon pagkaraan, habang hinuhukay ang buhangin sa bukana ng Ilog Lumbang, sa kabilang pampang ng ating lawa.

**Did You Know?** **Chinese merchants gave white umbrellas to local chiefs.** According to Zhao Rugua, writing in 1225, the chiefs of Ma-i liked to use white umbrellas, so the Chinese traders offered them as gifts – and the local traders then carried the goods away on credit, paying for them eight or nine months later ([Hirth & Rockhill, Chau Ju-kua, 1911, ch. 40 “Ma-i”, p. 159–160 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/chaujukuahiswork00chauuoft/page/159/mode/2up)) ([A Chinese Gazetteer of Foreign Lands (Zhufan zhi) | Global Middle Ages Project](https://globalmiddleages.org/node/109)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Nagbigay ng mga puting payong sa mga pinuno ang mga mangangalakal na Tsino.** Ayon kay Zhao Rugua, na sumulat noong 1225, mahilig gumamit ng puting payong ang mga pinuno ng Ma-i, kaya iniaalay ito sa kanila ng mga mangangalakal na Tsino bilang regalo – at dinadala naman ng mga lokal na mangangalakal ang mga kalakal nang pautang, at binabayaran nila ito makalipas ang walo o siyam na buwan.

**Did You Know?** **The word *binyag* came from Brunei.** The Tagalog dictionary of 1754 notes that *binyag* “es de Borney”: it first named a Muslim ablution of pouring water from above. Local tradition derives the name of Biñan from *binyagan*, “the place of baptism” – a beautiful story that the documents of 1571 do not record ([Noceda & Sanlúcar 1754 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/vocabulariodelal00noce)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Galing sa Brunei ang salitang *binyag*.** Itinala ng diksiyonaryong Tagalog ng 1754 na ang *binyag* ay “es de Borney”: una nitong tinukoy ang isang paghuhugas ng mga Muslim sa pamamagitan ng pagbubuhos ng tubig mula sa itaas. Ayon sa tradisyon ng bayan, mula sa *binyagan*, “ang lugar ng binyag”, ang pangalan ng Biñan – isang magandang kuwento na hindi itinala ng mga dokumento ng 1571.

## Spanish Colonial Period / Panahon ng Kolonyalismong Espanyol

### 1. From encomienda to town

After Juan de Salcedo’s campaign around the lake in 1571, the lakeshore towns were given as *encomiendas* to Spanish settlers, and missionaries began to work among them. The land of Biñan was then part of **Tabuco** (Cabuyao). Spanish documents write its name in many ways – *Biñan*, *Biñang*, *Viñan* – and a local tradition, recalled in the previous chapter, links it to *binyagan*, “the place of baptism”.

### 2. An estate for the college

From the 1640s, the Dominican friars of the **College of Santo Tomás** in Manila – the future University of Santo Tomás – assembled a great estate in the territory of Tabuco, in three stages between 1641 and 1677 (the date usually given is 1644). Its income supported the college.<span id="ref-estate"></span> The **Hacienda de Biñan** ran from the shore of the lake to the border of Cavite and from San Pedro Tunasan southward; the lands of Santa Rosa made up more than half of it ([Santiago, Pomp, Pageantry and Gold: The Eight Spanish Villas in the Philippines (1565–1887), 2005 | Philippine Quarterly of Culture and Society](https://www.jstor.org/stable/29792568)) ([Buzeta & Bravo, Diccionario geográfico, estadístico, histórico de las Islas Filipinas, t. I, 1850, art. “Biñan”, p. 388–390 | Bayerische Staatsbibliothek](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10433378?page=414)). To farm it, the Dominicans also settled Christian Chinese from the Parián of Manila. One of them, **Domingo Lam-co**, cleared the land of what is now Barangay Tubigan; his son **Francisco Mercado** headed the town in 1783, and his descendants would include José Rizal ([Craig 1913, Lineage, Life and Labors of José Rizal | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6867)).

### 3. The fraud of 1743 and the revolts of 1745

The estate also brought conflict. In 1743, when the lands of “**the estate of Biñan, which is owned by the religious of St. Dominic**” were surveyed, a court clerk named Juan Monroy measured them with false units that gave the friars more than half of the lands of the neighbouring town of **Silang**. The quarrel set the countryside on fire: in 1745 the people of Silang attacked the lands of the hacienda, and revolts spread to Taguig, Parañaque, Bacoor, Imus, San Mateo and much of Bulacan – the largest uprising of Hispanicized Filipinos before 1896. The judge **Pedro Calderón Enríquez**, sent to investigate, punished the clerk, declared the usurped lands to belong to the Crown and returned them to the villages; in 1751 the king approved his decisions ([Royal decree of 7 November 1751, in Blair & Robertson vol. 48 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/54740)) ([Lynch 1988, Land Rights, Land Laws and Land Usurpation | Philippine Law Journal](https://philippinelawjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/63PLJ082_LYNCH.pdf)). But the friar estates themselves remained.

### 4. A town of its own

Meanwhile Biñan became a town in its own right. In **1688**, when the province of La Laguna was reorganized, it was separated from Tabuco, and on **4 February 1747** it became a full *pueblo*, with its own town centre (*población*) and barrios – the date the city still celebrates as its founding anniversary ([Biñan: ‘The City of Life’ returns | BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2023/02/20/binan-the-city-of-life-returns-with-a-brighter-future-yet-to-unfold/)). Tradition names Father José Monroy, in 1757, as its first parish priest and Antonio de Santa Rosa as its first *capitán*. In 1791–92 the southern barrio of **Santa Rosa** was in turn separated to form a new town.

### 5. The rice of the hacienda

The wealth of the hacienda was **rice**. Around 1850, the geographers Buzeta and Bravo wrote that the Dominicans owned “all the land of Biñan” and that “it is rice that makes its wealth”, harvests yielding fifty or sixty times the seed thanks to the river that comes down from Mount Sungay and its many tributaries; sugar cane, fruit trees and vegetables grew in the house gardens. Tenants (*inquilinos*) cleared and farmed the land rent-free for three or four years, then paid five *cavans* of rice for each *cabalita* of irrigated land – hardly a tenth of the harvest – and could not be evicted unless they failed to pay for two years running. Many tenants were rich men who worked their land through sharecroppers, the *kasamá* ([Buzeta & Bravo, Diccionario geográfico, estadístico, histórico de las Islas Filipinas, t. I, 1850, art. “Biñan”, p. 388–390 | Bayerische Staatsbibliothek](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10433378?page=414)). The estate is said to have had a dozen dams; two survive, at **Timbao** and **Soro-soro**, declared local heritage in 2023 ([Biñan leads the way to heritage conservation efforts | Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)).

### 6. A town of mestizos

By the 19th century Biñan was a town of **mestizos**, prosperous Chinese-Filipino families who owned the best houses, “not a few of stone and tile, a luxury found in few towns”, with gardens of fruit trees. It counted **10,948 souls** around 1850 ([Buzeta & Bravo, 1850, p. 388–390 | Bayerische Staatsbibliothek](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10433378?page=414)). Its church, “of good construction”, was served by Filipino secular priests, while the house of the Dominican hacienda stood against it, joined to it through the choir loft. Fine roads (*calzadas*) led to the neighbouring towns, and the surplus of rice and the cloth woven by the women went to Manila on the lake, by *casco* barges and later by steamboats. In 1858–59, the British governor of Hong Kong, **Sir John Bowring**, stayed with **Don José Alberto**, Rizal’s uncle, and noted “an active trade between Biñan and Manila” ([Bowring 1859, A Visit to the Philippine Islands | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46695)). The town’s families included the Mercado, Alonso and Alberto, who served as its heads, and it was the birthplace of the priest **Modesto de Castro** (1819), the father of Tagalog prose and author of *Urbana at Felisa*, of **Francisco Mercado** (1818), Rizal’s father, who moved to Calamba ([Francisco Mercado | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/francisco-mercado-1818-1898/)), and of the lawyer **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista** (1830).

### 7. The schoolboy of 1870

In 1870 a nine-year-old boy arrived from Calamba to study Latin: **José Rizal**. In his memoirs he describes the aunt over seventy who lodged him, his teacher **Justiniano Aquino Cruz**, “a tall, thin man with a long neck and a sharp nose”, his fight with the teacher’s son, the old painter Juancho who let him help with his pictures, and his days: Mass at four in the morning, class until ten and again in the afternoon, rice and two dried sardines for breakfast. He left Biñan by the steamer *Talim* on **Saturday, 17 December 1870** ([Rizal’s Own Story of His Life, 1918 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/48438)). The marker set up in 1948 at the site of his school gives 1871, and textbooks often say 1869. In his novel *Noli me tangere*, Rizal later recalled that “in the year 70, when lightning struck in Biñan”, it fell on the church tower and destroyed the clock and an altar ([Rizal, Noli me tángere, 1887, ch. XIV | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47584)).

### 8. The end of Spanish rule

Spanish rule ended in revolution. In 1896–97, Biñan and Santa Rosa served as supply bases and field hospitals for the Spanish troops fighting the revolutionaries in Cavite, and on 19 January 1898 the provincial junta even proposed to make them *villas* with the title “**Siempre fiel y leal**” – “ever faithful and loyal” ([Santiago, Pomp, Pageantry and Gold: The Eight Spanish Villas in the Philippines (1565–1887), 2005 | Philippine Quarterly of Culture and Society](https://www.jstor.org/stable/29792568)). The request was shelved when Emilio Aguinaldo returned. A few months later, on 12 June 1898, a son of Biñan, **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista**, wrote and read the Act of the Proclamation of Philippine Independence at Kawit ([Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/ambrosio-rianzares-bautista-1830-1903-2/)). Biñan had already gone over to the revolution: it rose before Calamba, and its friars, warned in time by parishioners, had escaped to Manila. On 6 June the Spanish friars and soldiers who had capitulated at Calamba were brought to Biñan by boat – a town “once so Spanish and so friendly to us”, one of the friars recalled, “and now swarming with people who received us as declared enemies”. The chief of its revolutionaries and new *presidente*, Pedro de León, known by the nickname “Palañac”, at first refused them the boat they needed to reach Manila: “let the friars stay here: they are the ones to blame for everything that is happening” ([Herrero, Nuestra prisión en poder de los revolucionarios filipinos, 1900, pp. 106–116 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/nuestraprisionen00herr)). By the end of August the whole province of La Laguna was in Filipino hands. The fate of the friar lands would be settled under the Americans.

### 1. Mula encomienda hanggang bayan

Matapos ang paglalakbay ni Juan de Salcedo sa paligid ng lawa noong 1571, ipinagkaloob bilang mga *encomienda* sa mga Espanyol ang mga bayan sa baybayin, at nagsimulang mangaral ang mga misyonero sa kanila. Bahagi noon ng **Tabuco** (Cabuyao) ang lupain ng Biñan. Iba-iba ang pagsulat ng pangalan nito sa mga dokumentong Espanyol – *Biñan*, *Biñang*, *Viñan* – at iniuugnay ito ng isang lokal na tradisyon, na binanggit sa nakaraang kabanata, sa *binyagan*, “ang lugar ng binyag”.

### 2. Isang asyenda para sa kolehiyo

Mula dekada 1640, unti-unting binuo ng mga paring Dominiko ng **Kolehiyo ng Santo Tomás** sa Maynila – ang magiging Unibersidad ng Santo Tomás – ang isang malawak na asyenda sa nasasakupan ng Tabuco, sa tatlong yugto mula 1641 hanggang 1677 (karaniwang ibinibigay ang taong 1644).<span id="ref-estate"></span> Ang kita nito ang tumutustos sa kolehiyo. Umaabot ang **Asyenda ng Biñan** mula sa baybayin ng lawa hanggang sa hangganan ng Cavite at mula San Pedro Tunasan patimog; mahigit kalahati nito ang mga lupain ng Santa Rosa. Upang sakahin ito, pinanirahan din ng mga Dominiko rito ang mga Tsinong Kristiyano mula sa Parián ng Maynila. Isa sa kanila, si **Domingo Lam-co**, ang naglinis ng lupain ng tinatawag ngayong Barangay Tubigan; ang kaniyang anak na si **Francisco Mercado** ang namuno sa bayan noong 1783, at kabilang sa kaniyang mga inapo si José Rizal.

### 3. Ang pandaraya ng 1743 at ang mga pag-aalsa ng 1745

Nagdala rin ng alitan ang asyenda. Noong 1743, nang sukatin ang mga lupain ng “asyenda ng Biñan, na pag-aari ng mga paring Dominiko”, gumamit ang isang kawani ng hukuman na si Juan Monroy ng huwad na sukat na nagbigay sa mga prayle ng mahigit kalahati ng mga lupain ng karatig-bayang **Silang**. Nag-alab ang kanayunan dahil sa alitang ito: noong 1745, sinalakay ng mga taga-Silang ang mga lupain ng asyenda, at kumalat ang mga pag-aalsa sa Taguig, Parañaque, Bacoor, Imus, San Mateo at malaking bahagi ng Bulacan – ang pinakamalaking pag-aalsa ng mga Pilipinong Kristiyano bago ang 1896. Ang hukom na si **Pedro Calderón Enríquez**, na ipinadala upang magsiyasat, ay nagparusa sa kawani, ipinahayag na pag-aari ng Korona ang mga lupaing inagaw at ibinalik ang mga ito sa mga nayon; noong 1751, sinang-ayunan ng hari ang kaniyang mga pasya. Ngunit nanatili ang mga asyenda ng mga prayle.

### 4. Isang sariling bayan

Samantala, naging ganap na bayan ang Biñan. Noong **1688**, nang muling isaayos ang lalawigan ng La Laguna, inihiwalay ito sa Tabuco, at noong **Pebrero 4, 1747** ay naging ganap itong *pueblo*, na may sariling kabayanan (*población*) at mga baryo – ang petsang ipinagdiriwang pa rin ng lungsod bilang anibersaryo ng pagkakatatag nito. Ayon sa tradisyon, si Padre José Monroy, noong 1757, ang unang kura paroko nito at si Antonio de Santa Rosa ang unang *kapitan*. Noong 1791–92, inihiwalay naman ang timog na baryo ng **Santa Rosa** upang maging bagong bayan.

### 5. Ang palay ng asyenda

**Palay** ang yaman ng asyenda. Noong mga 1850, isinulat ng mga heograpong sina Buzeta at Bravo na pag-aari ng mga Dominiko “ang buong lupain ng Biñan” at na “palay ang bumubuo sa yaman nito”: limampu o animnapung ulit ng binhi ang ani dahil sa ilog na bumababa mula sa Bundok Sungay at sa maraming sanga nito; sa mga bakuran naman tumutubo ang tubo, mga punong-kahoy na namumunga at mga gulay. Ang mga umuupa (*inquilino*) ay naglilinis at nagsasaka ng lupa nang walang upa sa loob ng tatlo o apat na taon, pagkatapos ay nagbabayad ng limang kaban ng palay sa bawat *kabalita* ng lupang may patubig – halos ikasampu lamang ng ani – at hindi sila maaaring paalisin malibang hindi sila makabayad nang dalawang magkasunod na taon. Marami sa mga umuupa ay mayayamang nagpapasaka ng kanilang lupa sa pamamagitan ng mga **kasamá**. Sinasabing may isang dosenang prinsa ang asyenda; dalawa ang natitira, sa **Timbao** at **Soro-soro**, na idineklarang lokal na pamana noong 2023.

### 6. Isang bayan ng mga mestiso

Pagsapit ng ika-19 na siglo, bayan na ng mga **mestizo** ang Biñan, mga maunlad na pamilyang Tsino-Pilipino na nagmamay-ari ng pinakamagagandang bahay, “hindi iilan ang yari sa bato at tisa, isang karangyaang matatagpuan sa iilang bayan lamang”, na may mga halamanan ng punong namumunga. Mayroon itong **10,948 kaluluwa** noong mga 1850. Ang simbahan nito, “na mahusay ang pagkakagawa”, ay pinaglilingkuran ng mga paring sekular na Pilipino, samantalang nakadikit dito ang bahay ng asyenda ng mga Dominiko, na nakakonekta rito sa pamamagitan ng koro. May magagandang daan (*calzada*) patungo sa mga karatig-bayan, at dinadala sa Maynila sa pamamagitan ng lawa ang sobrang palay at ang telang hinabi ng kababaihan, sakay ng mga *casco* at kalaunan ng mga bapor. Noong 1858–59, tumuloy ang gobernador ng Hong Kong na si **Sir John Bowring** sa bahay ni **Don José Alberto**, tiyuhin ni Rizal, at napansin niya ang “masiglang kalakalan sa pagitan ng Biñan at Maynila”. Kabilang sa mga pamilya ng bayan ang mga Mercado, Alonso at Alberto, na naglingkod bilang mga pinuno nito, at dito isinilang ang paring si **Modesto de Castro** (1819), ang ama ng tuluyang Tagalog at may-akda ng *Urbana at Felisa*, si **Francisco Mercado** (1818), ama ni Rizal, na lumipat sa Calamba, at ang abogadong si **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista** (1830).

### 7. Ang batang mag-aaral ng 1870

Noong 1870, dumating mula Calamba ang isang siyam na taong gulang na batang lalaki upang mag-aral ng Latin: si **José Rizal**. Sa kaniyang mga alaala, inilalarawan niya ang kaniyang tiyahing mahigit pitumpung taong gulang na nagpatuloy sa kaniya, ang kaniyang gurong si **Justiniano Aquino Cruz**, “isang matangkad at payat na lalaki na mahaba ang leeg at matulis ang ilong”, ang pakikipag-away niya sa anak ng guro, ang matandang pintor na si Juancho na nagpatulong sa kaniya sa mga larawan nito, at ang kaniyang mga araw: misa sa ikaapat ng umaga, klase hanggang ikasampu at muli sa hapon, kanin at dalawang tuyo sa almusal. Umalis siya sa Biñan sakay ng bapor na *Talim* noong **Sabado, Disyembre 17, 1870**. Ang panandang itinayo noong 1948 sa pook ng kaniyang paaralan ay nagsasabing 1871, at madalas sabihin ng mga aklat-aralin na 1869. Sa kaniyang nobelang *Noli me tangere*, ginunita ni Rizal na “noong taong ’70, nang tamaan ng kidlat ang Biñan”, tumama ito sa kampanaryo ng simbahan at sinira ang orasan at isang altar.

### 8. Ang wakas ng pamamahala ng Espanya

Nagwakas sa rebolusyon ang pamamahala ng Espanya. Noong 1896–97, nagsilbing himpilan ng panustos at pansamantalang ospital ang Biñan at Santa Rosa para sa mga sundalong Espanyol na lumalaban sa mga rebolusyonaryo sa Cavite, at noong Enero 19, 1898, iminungkahi pa ng junta ng lalawigan na gawin silang mga *villa* na may titulong “**Siempre fiel y leal**” – “laging tapat at matapat”. Isinantabi ang kahilingan nang bumalik si Emilio Aguinaldo. Pagkaraan ng ilang buwan, noong Hunyo 12, 1898, isang anak ng Biñan, si **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista**, ang sumulat at bumasa ng Acta de la Proclamación de la Independencia sa Kawit. Pumanig na noon sa rebolusyon ang Biñan: nauna pa itong nag-alsa kaysa sa Calamba, at nakatakas na sa Maynila ang mga prayle nito, na binalaan sa tamang oras ng kanilang mga parokyano. Noong Hunyo 6, dinala sakay ng bangka sa Biñan ang mga prayle at sundalong Espanyol na sumuko sa Calamba – isang bayang “dati’y napaka-Espanyol at kaibigan namin,” paggunita ng isa sa mga prayle, “at ngayo’y puno ng mga taong tumanggap sa amin bilang hayagang mga kaaway.” Tumanggi noong una ang pinuno ng mga rebolusyonaryo nito at bagong *presidente*, si Pedro de León, na kilala sa palayaw na “Palañac”, na ibigay sa kanila ang bangkang kailangan nila upang makarating sa Maynila: “maiwan dito ang mga prayle: sila ang may kasalanan ng lahat ng nangyayari.” Pagsapit ng katapusan ng Agosto, nasa kamay na ng mga Pilipino ang buong lalawigan ng La Laguna. Pagpapasyahan sa ilalim ng mga Amerikano ang kapalaran ng mga lupain ng prayle.

**Did You Know?** **Biñan was almost made an “ever faithful and loyal” town of Spain.** In January 1898 the province proposed to give Biñan and Santa Rosa the rank of *villa* with the title “Siempre fiel y leal”. Five months later, a son of Biñan, Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista, read the Act of the Proclamation of Philippine Independence ([Santiago, Pomp, Pageantry and Gold: The Eight Spanish Villas in the Philippines (1565–1887), 2005 | Philippine Quarterly of Culture and Society](https://www.jstor.org/stable/29792568)) ([Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista (1830–1903) | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/ambrosio-rianzares-bautista-1830-1903-2/)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Muntik nang gawing bayang “laging tapat at matapat” sa Espanya ang Biñan.** Noong Enero 1898, iminungkahi ng lalawigan na bigyan ang Biñan at Santa Rosa ng ranggong *villa* na may titulong “Siempre fiel y leal”. Pagkaraan ng limang buwan, binasa ng isang anak ng Biñan, si Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista, ang Akta ng Pagpapahayag ng Kasarinlan ng Pilipinas.

**Did You Know?** **The people of Biñan welcomed the governor of Hong Kong with a hot-air balloon.** When Sir John Bowring stayed with Don José Alberto in 1858–59, the townspeople released a balloon bearing the words “The people of Biñan to their illustrious visitors” ([Bowring 1859 | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/46695)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Sinalubong ng mga taga-Biñan ang gobernador ng Hong Kong ng isang lobong pinalilipad ng mainit na hangin.** Nang tumuloy si Sir John Bowring kay Don José Alberto noong 1858–59, nagpalipad ang mga taga-bayan ng isang lobo na may nakasulat na “Ang mga taga-Biñan sa kanilang mga bantog na panauhin”.

**Did You Know?** **Rizal put Biñan’s church in his novel.** In *Noli me tangere* (1887), the old philosopher Tasio asks why, “in the year 70, when lightning struck in Biñan”, it fell precisely on the church tower, destroying the clock and an altar. Rizal was then nine years old and a pupil in the town ([Rizal, Noli me tángere, 1887, ch. XIV | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47584)) ([Rizal, The Social Cancer, trans. Derbyshire, 1912, ch. XIV | Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6737)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Isinama ni Rizal sa kanyang nobela ang simbahan ng Biñan.** Sa *Noli me tangere* (1887), itinanong ng matandang pilosopong si Tasio kung bakit, “noong taong ’70, nang tamaan ng kidlat ang Biñan”, sa tore mismo ng simbahan ito bumagsak, at nasira ang orasan at isang altar. Siyam na taong gulang noon si Rizal at nag-aaral sa bayan.

## American Period / Panahon ng Amerikano

### 1. A country sold

The Philippines changed masters without being asked. On 13 August 1898, Spanish and American commanders staged a **“mock battle”** for Manila so that the city would surrender to the Americans rather than to the Filipino army besieging it. On 10 December 1898, by the **Treaty of Paris**, Spain ceded the islands to the United States, which paid it “the sum of **twenty million dollars**” ([Treaty of Peace between the United States and Spain, 1898, art. III | Avalon Project, Yale Law School](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/sp1898.asp)). The Philippine Republic proclaimed at Malolos in January 1899 – whose independence had been declared the year before in words written by Biñan’s Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista – was not recognized. On 4 February 1899, fighting broke out in Manila: the **Philippine–American War** had begun ([The Philippine-American War, 1899–1902 | U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1899-1913/war)).

### 2. The cost of the war

The war was long and brutal. After the Filipino army turned to guerrilla warfare, American forces burned villages and crops, tortured prisoners with the “water cure”, and herded the rural population into guarded “zones of protection”. After the killing of 48 American soldiers at Balangiga in 1901, General Jacob Smith ordered Samar turned into a “howling wilderness”. Officially the war ended on 4 July 1902, but resistance continued, notably under **Macario Sakay** in the hills of Laguna, Cavite and Rizal until 1906. The U.S. State Department today counts more than 4,200 American and over 20,000 Filipino combatants killed, and “as many as 200,000 Filipino civilians” dead from violence, famine and disease; some historians give far higher figures ([Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1899-1913/war)).

### 3. Biñan on the front line

Biñan was on the front line. In 1899 it was the base of the Filipino brigade of **General Mariano Noriel**, whose trenches ran from Calamba toward Santa Rosa. On **2 January 1900**, American volunteers of the 39th Infantry under Colonel Robert Bullard advanced from Santa Rosa: “Battalion deployed and drove enemy into city of Binan. Company M had sharp engagement at bridge leading into city… City occupied at 10 a.m., engagement lasting about one hour.” In the quarters of the town’s *presidente* they seized Filipino flags and military records – but they were ordered back to Calamba the next day. On **6 January**, General Theodore Schwan’s brigade marched in from Muntinlupa and San Pedro across the rice fields, routed Noriel’s rearguard in a wood 800 yards north of the town – the Americans reported 18 Filipinos killed and 25 taken prisoner – and landed its rations from barges, the soldiers wading ashore through rough water. On 7 January a battalion of the 46th Infantry was left “to garrison Binan, establish civil government, and scour the neighboring country” ([Annual Reports of the War Department, 1900 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/annualreportswa50deptgoog)) ([idem, Schwan’s report | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/annualreportswa83deptgoog)). Half a century later, Canlalay still remembered that 275 revolutionaries under one Eligio Masangkay had fought the Americans there and that only 30 of them escaped; the barrio’s history of 1953 dates the fight to 1898, but it most probably belongs to these days of January 1900 ([Historical Data Papers: Canlalay, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b1/home.htm)).

### 4. Zones and cholera

For two years Biñan was a garrison town. In December 1901, General J. Franklin Bell ordered the people of “all towns… in the provinces of Batangas and Laguna” to move into guarded zones with their rice and animals; whatever was left outside could be confiscated or destroyed, and men could not leave without a pass ([Bell’s circulars, in Ramsey, A Masterpiece of Counterguerrilla Warfare | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA474917)). Across the two provinces at least 100,000 people were concentrated, and thousands died of hunger and disease ([Twomey 2022, Reconcentration and the Camp System | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/robert-cribb-et-al.-2022-detention-camps-in-asia-the-conditions-of-confinement-i)). On 18 February 1902 a battalion of Filipino “Tiradores” surrendered to the American commander of Biñan, and in May and June 1902 the post went through what an officer called “a terrible cholera epidemic” ([Cullum, Biographical Register, vol. V | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/biographicalreg01gradgoog)). The town remembered where it began: on 23 May 1902, its history of 1953 says, in the house of Vicente Almeda, the *hermano mayor* in charge of the festivities of the ninth day of the patron saint’s feast ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)). When civil government returned to La Laguna on 1 July 1902, its people were exempted from the land tax of that year “in view of their distressed condition” ([Act No. 424, 1902 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/acts/act1902/act_424_1902.html)).

### 5. A reshaped town

The Americans then reshaped the town. A municipal code replaced the Spanish *capitán* with an elected *presidente municipal*, chosen by a narrow electorate of property owners and literate men. To save money, the colonial government merged **San Pedro Tunasan** into Biñan in October 1903 and briefly attached **Muntinlupa** as well; Muntinlupa went back to Rizal province in 1905, and San Pedro became a town again in January 1907 ([Act No. 939, 1903 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/acts/act1903/act_939_1903.html)) ([Act No. 1553, 1906 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/acts/act1906/act_1553_1906.html)). The census of 1903 counted **9,563** people in Biñan, the fourth town of Laguna; in 1918 there were **10,692**, spread over the Poblacion and sixteen barrios from Canlalay and Malaban to Tubigan and Timbao ([Census of the Philippine Islands, 1903 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/ajb5834.0002.001.umich.edu)) ([Census of the Philippine Islands, 1918 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/ajb5835.0001.002.umich.edu)).

### 6. The friar lands

The great question was the land. On 8 August 1898, as American troops prepared to take Manila, the Dominicans had quietly sold the **Hacienda de Biñan** – 3,474 hectares, “for the greater part irrigation paddy land with, generally, two crops a year, part sugar-cane land” – to a British businessman; the colonial government’s lawyers judged the sale void ([Report of the Philippine Commission, 1904 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/1904-vol-xi-p-1-report-of-the-united-states-philippine-c)). On 22 December 1903 the government bought all the friar estates of the islands for about 7.2 million dollars, and the Friar Lands Act of 1904 provided for their resale to the people who farmed them. The sale of Biñan opened on 9 May 1910. “The office is full of people, as is also the street and stairway,” the land agent wrote, “and everything, even the money I am taking in, smells of betel nut”; in eight days 2,500 of the 3,200 parcels were sold, to be paid over twenty-five years ([Administration of Philippine Lands, House Committee on Insular Affairs, 1911 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/afr0955.0002.001.umich.edu)). The same year the town bought the old Dominican *casa hacienda* on the plaza for ₱29,000 “for municipal and school purposes” ([Journal of the Philippine Commission, 1910 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/aag2698.0004.001.umich.edu)).

### 7. School, railway and monument

Schools came early. By 1904 most towns of Laguna had American teachers and all had Filipino teachers, who also taught English to adults at night ([Report of the Philippine Commission, 1904](https://archive.org/details/1904-vol-xi-p-1-report-of-the-united-states-philippine-c)); in Biñan the public school moved into the old hacienda house, which engineers in 1914 found “large and full of rooms” but too dark for classes ([Quarterly Bulletin of the Bureau of Public Works, 1914 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/acw7643.0003.003.umich.edu)). One pupil of this generation, **Encarnación Alzona**, born in Biñan in 1895, became the first Filipino woman to earn a doctorate in history, a leader of the campaign for women’s suffrage, and a National Scientist. On 2 September 1908 the Manila Railroad opened its line from San Pedro to Biñan, which got its first railway station. A great fire in 1904 destroyed a part of the town. And on **14 May 1918** the people of Biñan completed the **Rizal monument** on the plaza, inscribed “Al Dr. José Rizal el pueblo de Biñang en cumplimiento de su deber”, in front of the Alberto house – by then a cinema.

### 8. Toward the Commonwealth

In the 1920s and 1930s Biñan grew slowly, a town of rice fields, shoemakers, hat weavers and *puto* makers on the road and the railway to Manila. Its population reached 16,238 in 1939 ([Population Enumerated in Various Censuses, 1903–2007: Laguna | NSO, via Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/PhilippinesCensusofPopulationLGUs19032007)). The town also knew violence: on 9 April 1938 Mayor Julio A. Antiporda was attacked with knives inside the municipal building and died on the way to the hospital, and President Quezon suspended the whole police force of the town ([Philippine Magazine, 1938, reprinted in Messages of the President, Book 3, vol. 1 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/MOP-Vol1-Manuel-L-Quezon)). In 1935 the **Commonwealth of the Philippines** was inaugurated as a ten-year transition toward independence, and in 1937 Filipino women, Encarnación Alzona among their leaders, voted for the first time in a plebiscite on their own suffrage. Four years later, war came again.

### 1. Isang bansang ipinagbili

Hindi tinanong ang mga Pilipino nang magpalit ng panginoon ang kanilang bayan. Noong Agosto 13, 1898, nagsagawa ang mga pinunong Espanyol at Amerikano ng isang **“kunwaring labanan”** sa Maynila upang sa mga Amerikano sumuko ang lungsod at hindi sa hukbong Pilipinong kumukubkob dito. Noong Disyembre 10, 1898, sa bisa ng **Kasunduan sa Paris**, ibinigay ng Espanya ang kapuluan sa Estados Unidos, na nagbayad dito ng “halagang **dalawampung milyong dolyar**”. Hindi kinilala ang Republikang ipinahayag sa Malolos noong Enero 1899 – na ang kalayaan ay ipinahayag noong nakaraang taon sa mga salitang isinulat ng taga-Biñan na si Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista. Noong Pebrero 4, 1899, sumiklab ang labanan sa Maynila: nagsimula na ang **Digmaang Pilipino–Amerikano**.

### 2. Ang halaga ng digmaan

Mahaba at malupit ang digmaan. Nang bumaling sa pakikidigmang gerilya ang hukbong Pilipino, sinunog ng mga Amerikano ang mga nayon at pananim, pinahirapan ang mga bilanggo sa pamamagitan ng “water cure”, at tinipon ang mga taga-nayon sa mga binabantayang “sona”. Matapos mapatay ang 48 sundalong Amerikano sa Balangiga noong 1901, iniutos ni Heneral Jacob Smith na gawing “umaatungal na ilang” ang Samar. Opisyal na nagwakas ang digmaan noong Hulyo 4, 1902, ngunit nagpatuloy ang paglaban, lalo na sa ilalim ni **Macario Sakay** sa kabundukan ng Laguna, Cavite at Rizal hanggang 1906. Ayon ngayon sa Kagawaran ng Estado ng Estados Unidos, mahigit 4,200 Amerikano at mahigit 20,000 Pilipinong mandirigma ang napatay, at “hanggang 200,000 sibilyang Pilipino” ang namatay dahil sa karahasan, gutom at sakit; may mga mananalaysay na nagbibigay ng mas malalaking bilang.

### 3. Ang Biñan sa unahan ng labanan

Nasa unahan ng labanan ang Biñan. Noong 1899, ito ang himpilan ng brigadang Pilipino ni **Heneral Mariano Noriel**, na ang mga trinsera ay umaabot mula Calamba hanggang Santa Rosa. Noong **Enero 2, 1900**, sumulong mula Santa Rosa ang mga boluntaryong Amerikano ng 39th Infantry sa ilalim ni Koronel Robert Bullard: nagkaroon ng mainit na sagupaan sa tulay papasok sa bayan, at nasakop nila ang Biñan pagkaraan ng isang oras. Sa tanggapan ng *presidente* ng bayan, nasamsam nila ang mga watawat ng Pilipinas at mga dokumentong militar – ngunit inutusan silang bumalik sa Calamba kinabukasan. Noong **Enero 6**, dumating mula Muntinlupa at San Pedro ang brigada ni Heneral Theodore Schwan sa gitna ng mga palayan, tinalo ang huling hanay ni Noriel sa isang kakahuyan 800 yarda sa hilaga ng bayan – 18 Pilipino ang iniulat na napatay at 25 ang nabihag – at ibinaba ang kanilang rasyon mula sa mga casco, habang lumulusong ang mga sundalo sa maalong tubig. Noong Enero 7, iniwan ang isang batalyon ng 46th Infantry upang “bantayan ang Biñan, magtatag ng pamahalaang sibil at suyurin ang paligid”. Makalipas ang kalahating siglo, naaalala pa ng Canlalay na 275 rebolusyonaryo sa pamumuno ng isang Eligio Masangkay ang lumaban doon sa mga Amerikano, at 30 lamang sa kanila ang nakatakas; 1898 ang petsang nakasulat sa kasaysayan ng baryo noong 1953, ngunit malamang na sa mga araw na ito ng Enero 1900 nangyari ang labanan.

### 4. Mga sona at kolera

Sa loob ng dalawang taon, naging bayang may garison ang Biñan. Noong Disyembre 1901, iniutos ni Heneral J. Franklin Bell na lumipat ang mga taga-“lahat ng bayan sa mga lalawigan ng Batangas at Laguna” sa mga binabantayang sona dala ang kanilang palay at mga hayop; maaaring kumpiskahin o wasakin ang anumang maiwan sa labas, at hindi makaaalis ang mga lalaki nang walang pases. Sa dalawang lalawigan, hindi bababa sa 100,000 katao ang tinipon, at libu-libo ang namatay sa gutom at sakit. Noong Pebrero 18, 1902, sumuko sa komandanteng Amerikano ng Biñan ang isang batalyon ng mga “Tiradores”, at noong Mayo at Hunyo 1902, dinanas ng himpilan ang tinawag ng isang opisyal na “isang kakila-kilabot na epidemya ng kolera”. Naaalala ng bayan kung saan ito nagsimula: noong Mayo 23, 1902, ayon sa kasaysayan nito na isinulat noong 1953, sa bahay ni Vicente Almeda, ang *hermano mayor* na nangangasiwa sa pagdiriwang ng ikasiyam na araw ng kapistahan ng patron. Nang bumalik ang pamahalaang sibil sa La Laguna noong Hulyo 1, 1902, hindi na pinagbayad ng buwis sa lupa para sa taong iyon ang mga mamamayan nito “dahil sa kanilang kahabag-habag na kalagayan”.

### 5. Isang binagong bayan

Pagkatapos ay binago ng mga Amerikano ang bayan. Pinalitan ng isang kodigo munisipal ang *kapitan* ng panahon ng Espanyol ng isang inihahalal na *presidente municipal*, na pinipili ng iilang botante – mga may-ari ng ari-arian at marunong bumasa. Upang makatipid, isinanib ng pamahalaang kolonyal ang **San Pedro Tunasan** sa Biñan noong Oktubre 1903 at sandaling idinugtong din ang **Muntinlupa**; ibinalik ang Muntinlupa sa lalawigan ng Rizal noong 1905, at muling naging bayan ang San Pedro noong Enero 1907. Binilang ng senso ng 1903 ang **9,563** katao sa Biñan, ang ikaapat na bayan ng Laguna; noong 1918 ay **10,692** na sila, nakakalat sa Poblacion at sa labing-anim na baryo mula Canlalay at Malaban hanggang Tubigan at Timbao.

### 6. Ang mga lupain ng prayle

Ang lupa ang malaking tanong. Noong Agosto 8, 1898, habang naghahanda ang mga sundalong Amerikano na sakupin ang Maynila, palihim na ipinagbili ng mga Dominiko ang **Asyenda ng Biñan** – 3,474 ektarya, “karamihan ay palayang may patubig na karaniwang dalawang beses inaani sa isang taon, at bahagi ay tubuhan” – sa isang negosyanteng Briton; itinuring na walang bisa ng mga abogado ng pamahalaang kolonyal ang bentang ito. Noong Disyembre 22, 1903, binili ng pamahalaan ang lahat ng asyenda ng mga prayle sa kapuluan sa halagang mga 7.2 milyong dolyar, at itinakda ng Friar Lands Act ng 1904 ang muling pagbebenta ng mga ito sa mga taong nagsasaka rito. Binuksan ang bentahan sa Biñan noong Mayo 9, 1910. “Puno ng tao ang opisina, pati ang kalye at ang hagdan,” isinulat ng ahente ng lupa, “at amoy-nganga ang lahat, pati ang perang tinatanggap ko”; sa loob ng walong araw, 2,500 sa 3,200 lote ang naipagbili, na babayaran sa loob ng dalawampu’t limang taon. Nang taon ding iyon, binili ng bayan ang lumang *casa hacienda* ng mga Dominiko sa plasa sa halagang ₱29,000 “para sa gamit ng munisipyo at ng paaralan”.

### 7. Paaralan, riles at bantayog

Maagang dumating ang mga paaralan. Pagsapit ng 1904, may mga gurong Amerikano ang karamihan ng bayan sa Laguna at may mga gurong Pilipino ang lahat, na nagtuturo rin ng Ingles sa mga nakatatanda sa gabi; sa Biñan, lumipat ang pampublikong paaralan sa lumang bahay ng asyenda, na natagpuan ng mga inhinyero noong 1914 na “malaki at maraming silid” ngunit masyadong madilim para sa mga klase. Isang mag-aaral ng henerasyong ito, si **Encarnación Alzona**, na isinilang sa Biñan noong 1895, ang naging unang Pilipinang nagkamit ng doktorado sa kasaysayan, isang pinuno ng kilusan para sa karapatang bumoto ng kababaihan, at isang Pambansang Siyentipiko. Noong Setyembre 2, 1908, binuksan ng Manila Railroad ang linya nito mula San Pedro hanggang Biñan, na nagkaroon ng unang estasyon ng tren. Isang malaking sunog noong 1904 ang tumupok sa isang bahagi ng bayan. At noong **Mayo 14, 1918**, natapos ng mga taga-Biñan ang **bantayog ni Rizal** sa plasa, na may nakaukit na “Al Dr. José Rizal el pueblo de Biñang en cumplimiento de su deber”, sa harap ng bahay ng mga Alberto – na noon ay isa nang sinehan.

### 8. Patungo sa Komonwelt

Noong dekada 1920 at 1930, dahan-dahang lumaki ang Biñan, isang bayan ng mga palayan, mga gumagawa ng sapatos, mga manghahabi ng sumbrero at mga gumagawa ng puto sa tabi ng daan at ng riles patungong Maynila. Umabot sa 16,238 ang populasyon nito noong 1939. Hindi rin nakaligtas ang bayan sa karahasan: noong Abril 9, 1938, sinaksak sa loob mismo ng munisipyo si Alkalde Julio A. Antiporda, at namatay siya habang dinadala sa ospital; sinuspinde ni Pangulong Quezon ang buong pulisya ng bayan. Noong 1935, pinasinayaan ang **Komonwelt ng Pilipinas** bilang sampung taong paghahanda tungo sa kalayaan, at noong 1937, unang bumoto ang kababaihang Pilipino, na kabilang sa mga pinuno si Encarnación Alzona, sa isang plebisito tungkol sa sarili nilang karapatang bumoto. Pagkaraan ng apat na taon, muling dumating ang digmaan.

**Did You Know?** **Biñan was captured twice in five days.** On 2 January 1900 American volunteers took the town after an hour’s fight at the bridge and found Filipino flags in the *presidente*’s quarters – then were ordered back to Calamba. On 6 January another American brigade marched in from San Pedro, and this time it stayed ([Annual Reports of the War Department, 1900](https://archive.org/details/annualreportswa50deptgoog)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Dalawang beses nasakop ang Biñan sa loob ng limang araw.** Noong Enero 2, 1900, nasakop ng mga boluntaryong Amerikano ang bayan matapos ang isang oras na labanan sa tulay, at nakakita sila ng mga watawat ng Pilipinas sa tanggapan ng *presidente* – saka sila inutusang bumalik sa Calamba. Noong Enero 6, pumasok mula San Pedro ang isa pang brigadang Amerikano, at sa pagkakataong ito ay nanatili na.

**Did You Know?** **“Everything smells of betel nut.”** When the former Dominican estate was put on sale to its farmers in May 1910, the crowd filled the office, the stairway and the street; the land agent wrote that everything, “even the money I am taking in”, smelled of betel nut ([House Committee on Insular Affairs, 1911](https://archive.org/details/afr0955.0002.001.umich.edu)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **“Amoy-nganga ang lahat.”** Nang ipagbili sa mga magsasaka nito ang dating asyenda ng mga Dominiko noong Mayo 1910, napuno ng tao ang tanggapan, ang hagdanan at ang kalye; isinulat ng ahente ng lupa na ang lahat, “pati ang perang tinatanggap ko”, ay amoy-nganga.

**Did You Know?** **The friars’ house became a school.** In 1910 the town bought the Dominican *casa hacienda* on the plaza, with its stone storehouse and 16,663 square metres of land, for ₱29,000. By 1914 it was the Biñan schoolhouse ([Quarterly Bulletin of the Bureau of Public Works, 1914](https://archive.org/details/acw7643.0003.003.umich.edu)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Naging paaralan ang bahay ng mga prayle.** Noong 1910, binili ng bayan ang *casa hacienda* ng mga Dominiko sa plaza, kasama ang kamalig nitong bato at 16,663 metro kuwadradong lupa, sa halagang ₱29,000. Pagsapit ng 1914, ito na ang paaralan ng Biñan.

**Did You Know?** **Shopping by boat.** In 1919 the lakeshore barrios of De La Paz and Malaban stayed under water for more than two months, and rice rose to 75 centavos a *ganta*. In August 1931 the flood reached the Poblacion itself: “Bancas were used by vendors to sell their goods to the people right in their homes” ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Pamimili sakay ng bangka.** Noong 1919, mahigit dalawang buwang lubog sa tubig ang mga baryo sa baybayin, ang De La Paz at Malaban, at umakyat sa 75 sentimo ang isang *ganta* ng bigas. Noong Agosto 1931, umabot ang baha hanggang sa Poblacion: sakay ng bangka ang mga tindero habang ipinagbibili nila ang kanilang paninda hanggang sa mismong mga bahay.

## World War II and Japanese Occupation / Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig

### 1. The invasion

War came to Biñan from the south-east. On 8 December 1941, hours after Pearl Harbor, Japanese aircraft struck Luzon. On 24 December the Japanese 16th Division landed at **Lamon Bay**, on the Pacific coast of Tayabas, with orders to “move west across the mountains along Route 1, then north along the shore of Laguna de Bay and on to Manila” ([Morton, The Fall of the Philippines, 1953, ch. VIII | HyperWar](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-P-PI/)). The Filipino and American troops of the South Luzon Force fell back around the lake toward Bataan. By 1 January 1942 the Japanese vanguard was on Manila Bay, and on 2 January it entered the capital from the south. The road it followed – Route 1, through Calamba, Biñan and Muntinlupa – crossed the Poblacion: Biñan was occupied without a fight in the first days of 1942. On Bataan, Brigadier General **Mateo Capinpin**, whose name a street of Biñan now bears, commanded the Philippine Army’s 21st Division until he was captured by the Japanese on 5 April 1942, four days before the surrender and the Death March that followed ([Morton, The Fall of the Philippines, 1953, ch. XXIV | HyperWar](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-P-PI/USA-P-PI-24.html)) ([Rizal in Biñan (marker) | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/rizal-in-binan/)).

### 2. Life under occupation

A Japanese military administration took over in January 1942. It was followed by a single party, the **KALIBAPI** (December 1942), and on 14 October 1943 by a Republic under José P. Laurel that was independent in name only. Schools reopened under Japanese control and taught *Nippongo*, but fewer children attended. Oral histories gathered in Laguna describe the first two years, in towns with small garrisons, as “fairly routine”; life became truly hard in 1944 ([Gotiangco, Laguna during the Japanese Occupation, 2007 | ejournals.ph](https://ejournals.ph/article.php?id=5341)). Food was the great problem. The army bought or seized rice, and the Laurel government replaced the pre-war rice agency with the **BIBA** (*Bigasang Bayan*), yet “almost all the plans failed”, undone by insecurity, the lack of fuel and transport, and the needs of the Japanese army ([Jose, The Problem of Food and Inflation, 1998 | NAST](https://transactions.nast.ph/?p=5823)). Prices soared, and the occupation peso – nicknamed “Mickey Mouse money” – lost so much value that notes of 500 and 1,000 pesos had to be printed.

### 3. Fear

Behind the routine lay fear. The **Kempeitai**, the Japanese military police, arrested suspected guerrillas, and torture was common. Gustavo Ingles, a Hunters-ROTC officer arrested in June 1943, was tortured at Fort Santiago and then held at **New Bilibid Prison** in Muntinlupa, a few kilometres north of Biñan; on 24 June 1944 a guerrilla raid set him free ([Henderson, Rescue at Los Baños, 2015 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/rescue-at-los-banos-the-most-daring-prison-camp-raid-of-world-war-2)). As the Americans drew near, the occupiers grew harsher. In December 1944 the **Makapili** was formed, a pro-Japanese militia whose members fought beside the Japanese in Rizal, Laguna and Bulacan.

In Biñan itself the Japanese kept a garrison, where suspects were held. The teachers who wrote the barrio histories in 1953 remembered that in August 1942 an underground group, the “Biñan Unit”, was formed in De La Paz and attached a month later to the McKinley Brigade of the **Fil-American Irregular Troops**, under the American colonel Hugh Straughn. In July 1943 the Kempeitai arrested one of its captains, **Enrique Alcabasa**, and took him to the garrison of San Pedro, while many of its members were held in the garrison of Biñan and released for lack of evidence. In August Alcabasa was seized again in his house in De La Paz, the unit’s headquarters; he was “badly tortured”, and the next day he was executed ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio of De La Paz, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b2/home.htm)). The young mayor, the lawyer **Rogelio Limaco**, elected in 1940, saved men of Platero who had been taken to the garrison. At dawn on **24 October 1944** the Japanese seized him in turn, and he was never seen again: “he became a hero and was killed by the Japanese in his line of duty”, the history of Platero says ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio Platero, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b4/home.htm)) ([Rogelio Limaco panel, Biñan City Museum | Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:6312Bi%C3%B1an_City,_Laguna_Museum_Old_Town_Hall_Landmarks_08.jpg)). The city remembers him every 16 September, the day he was born in 1913 ([Biñan commemorates Limaco Day | City of Biñan, 2019](https://www.binan.gov.ph/binan-commemorates-limaco-day/)).

### 4. The resistance

Resistance grew all the same. In Laguna four great guerrilla networks operated largely on their own: the **Hunters-ROTC**, founded by cadets of the Philippine Military Academy and the ROTC; **Marking’s Fil-Americans**; the **President Quezon’s Own Guerrillas** (PQOG) around San Pablo; and the **Fil-American Irregular Troops** (FAIT). Beside them fought the communist-led **Hukbalahap** and the Chinese **Wha-Chi** ([Gotiangco 2007](https://ejournals.ph/article.php?id=5341)) ([Guerrilla unit files, Philippine Archives Collection | NARA](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/ww2/philippine/guerrilla-list-2.html)). In December 1944 the Hunters-ROTC placed its 47th Division in Manila, Cavite and Batangas, with its **48th Regiment** in Cavite, just across the hills from Biñan. Its 45th Regiment, in Laguna, counted only 80 armed fighters – but some 5,000 “home guards”, civilians who supplied food, messages and information ([Adevoso, Commander’s report on the Hunters-ROTC, December 1944, RG 407, Entry 1087, Box 246 | U.S. National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/ww2/philippine/guerrilla-list-2.html)) ([Transcription of the report | Batangas History](https://www.batangashistory.date/2020/04/commanders-report-on-strength-and-composition-of-hunters-rotc-dec-44.html)). One of the region’s best-known leaders came from next door: **Abelardo “Capitan Remo” Remoquillo** of San Pedro, killed in 1945 while attacking a Japanese garrison at Bay.

### 5. The liberation of 3 February 1945

In January 1945 the Americans returned to Luzon. On 31 January the 11th Airborne Division landed at Nasugbu, in Batangas, and on **3 February** its paratroopers dropped on Tagaytay Ridge ([Smith, Triumph in the Philippines, 1963, ch. XII | HyperWar](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-P-Triumph/)). That same day, before any American soldier reached it, **Biñan was freed by Filipinos**. In the words of the Hunters-ROTC veteran and historian Proculo Mojica: “Coming down from Carmona, Ocampo decided to take and secure Biñan. On February 3, he marched on Biñan in three columns.” One column attacked the garrison – about 30 Japanese soldiers “bolstered by some 50 Makapilis” – head on; a second sealed the roads from San Pedro to the north; a third entered from the south and blocked any escape toward Santa Rosa. “In one day, the entire garrison was annihilated and Biñan fell to the Hunters” (Mojica, *Terry’s Hunters*, 1965, quoted on the Hunters-ROTC memorial at Biñan City Hall). The attackers were the **48th Regiment, 47th Division** of the Hunters-ROTC under **Colonel Emmanuel de Ocampo** ([Republic Act No. 9427, 2007 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9427_2007.html)). A local account names Marcial Alintanahin as the commander who led the attack ([Liberation of Biñan | EL FILIPINISMO](https://pepealas.wordpress.com/2023/02/03/liberation-of-binan/)). Plaques now in the city museum confirm that he was a major of the Hunters-ROTC, commander of the “Blue Lake Battalion” of that same 48th Regiment and a former chief of police, and that he became Biñan’s “military mayor” after the liberation; they do not say what he did on 3 February ([Hunters-ROTC Association plaque for “Hunter Marcial Alintanahin”, 1971, Biñan City Museum | Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:6361Bi%C3%B1an_City,_Laguna_Museum_Old_Town_Hall_Landmarks_08.jpg)) ([Biñan Lions Club plaque for Marcial A. Alintanahin, 1982, Biñan City Museum | Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:6263Bi%C3%B1an_City,_Laguna_Museum_Old_Town_Hall_Landmarks_28.jpg)). On 5 February the guerrillas came back from San Pedro to “Free Biñan” before moving against Santa Rosa ([History comes alive at 80th Biñan Liberation Day | OpinYon, 2025](https://opinyon.net/national/history-comes-alive-at-80th-binan-liberation-day)) – which is why the town long celebrated its liberation on that date: a certificate now in the city museum was presented “during the 37th Anniversary of Liberation Day of Biñan this 5th day of February” 1982 ([Certificate of 5 February 1982, Biñan City Museum | Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:6263Bi%C3%B1an_City,_Laguna_Museum_Old_Town_Hall_Landmarks_06.jpg)).

### 6. The Los Baños raid

Biñan then became a rear base for the most famous rescue of the war. At the College of Agriculture of the University of the Philippines in Los Baños, across the lake, the Japanese held **2,147 civilian internees**, most of them Americans and many of them weak with hunger. On 20 February four American engineers crossed Laguna de Bay **by banca from Biñan** to choose a landing beach ([Rottman, The Los Baños Prison Camp Raid, 2010 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/the-los-banos-prison-camp-raid-the-philippines-1945)). Gustavo Ingles – the man freed from New Bilibid – also sailed from Biñan to coordinate the attack with the guerrilla chiefs on the far shore ([Henderson 2015](https://archive.org/details/rescue-at-los-banos-the-most-daring-prison-camp-raid-of-world-war-2)). On 21 February a battalion of paratroopers marched south along Highway 1, the road through Biñan, to Mamatid in Cabuyao, where amphibious tractors were waiting. At dawn on **23 February** paratroopers dropped beside the camp, guerrillas struck the guards, and the amtracs ferried the internees across the lake to safety. The Japanese took their revenge on those who remained. In the barrios of Los Baños, soldiers killed more than two hundred civilians in the weeks after the raid. At Calamba the toll was heavier still: U.S. war crimes investigators estimated that some 1,500 people were massacred between 12 and 15 February 1945, and the evidence heard at General Yamashita’s trial put the dead of 12 February alone at more than 2,500 ([Whitney, The Case of General Yamashita: A Memorandum, c. 1950, app. C | Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/llmlp/Yamashita/Yamashita.pdf)) ([Henderson, Rescue at Los Baños, 2015 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/rescue-at-los-banos-the-most-daring-prison-camp-raid-of-world-war-2)).

### 7. A town spared

By mid-February Biñan, on the north-western shore, lay behind the front. The fighting moved south and east, toward Lipa, Tanauan and the southern shore of the lake, which the American XIV Corps and Filipino guerrillas cleared in March and April ([Smith, ch. XXIII | HyperWar](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-P-Triumph/)). Elsewhere the cost was terrible: Lipa was “battered beyond recognition”, the cathedral of San Pablo was destroyed, and the town of Mabitac was completely demolished. No battle or massacre on that scale is recorded in Biñan. “Only a handful of the townpeople were killed during the Occupation,” its teachers wrote in 1953; “the Japs did not have the time to burn the houses or kill the people” ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)). Yet the arrests, the tortures and the deaths of Captain Alcabasa and Mayor Limaco show that the town, too, paid its price. Its old houses of stone and wood around the plaza – the Alberto, Ocampo, Gonzales and other family homes – came through the war, which is why Biñan can still speak of its heritage houses today ([Biñan leads the way to heritage conservation | Daily Tribune, 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/biñan-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)). Life resumed quickly. On 15 May 1945, while the war still raged in northern Luzon, Manila’s *Free Philippines* carried a small notice: “BIÑAN FIESTA TODAY” ([Free Philippines, 15 May 1945 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/ManilaFreePhilippinesPart2)).

### 8. From liberation to independence

The war ended in September 1945. Japan signed its surrender in Tokyo Bay on 2 September, and the next day General Tomoyuki Yamashita, commander of the Japanese army in the Philippines, surrendered at Baguio. Tried in Manila, he was hanged at Los Baños on 23 February 1946 – a year to the day after the raid. On **4 July 1946** the Philippines became independent, and the Philippine War Damage Commission began to pay claims for houses, crops and public buildings destroyed in the war. The memory of those years lives on in Biñan. Every 3 February the city celebrates **Biñan Liberation Day** at the guerrilla memorial, and in 2025, for the eightieth anniversary, the guerrillas’ march from Carmona was re-enacted ([OpinYon, 2025](https://opinyon.net/national/history-comes-alive-at-80th-binan-liberation-day)).

### 1. Ang paglusob

Mula sa timog-silangan dumating ang digmaan sa Biñan. Noong Disyembre 8, 1941, ilang oras matapos salakayin ang Pearl Harbor, binomba ng mga eroplanong Hapones ang Luzon. Noong Disyembre 24, lumunsad ang 16th Division ng Hapon sa **Lamon Bay**, sa baybaying Pasipiko ng Tayabas, na may utos na tumawid pakanluran sa kabundukan sa Route 1, saka pahilaga sa baybayin ng Laguna de Bay hanggang Maynila. Umatras paikot sa lawa patungong Bataan ang mga sundalong Pilipino at Amerikano ng South Luzon Force. Pagsapit ng Enero 1, 1942, nasa Look ng Maynila na ang unahan ng hukbong Hapones, at noong Enero 2, pumasok ito sa kabisera mula sa timog. Ang dinaanan nitong daan – ang Route 1, sa Calamba, Biñan at Muntinlupa – ay tumatawid sa Poblacion: nasakop ang Biñan nang walang labanan sa mga unang araw ng 1942. Sa Bataan, pinamunuan ni Brigadier General **Mateo Capinpin**, na nakapangalan ngayon sa isang kalye ng Biñan, ang 21st Division ng Hukbong Pilipino hanggang sa mabihag siya ng mga Hapones noong Abril 5, 1942, apat na araw bago ang pagsuko at ang Martsa ng Kamatayan na sumunod dito.

### 2. Ang buhay sa ilalim ng pananakop

Isang administrasyong militar ng Hapon ang namahala mula Enero 1942. Sinundan ito ng nag-iisang partido, ang **KALIBAPI** (Disyembre 1942), at noong Oktubre 14, 1943, ng isang Republika sa ilalim ni José P. Laurel na malaya sa pangalan lamang. Muling binuksan ang mga paaralan sa ilalim ng kontrol ng Hapon at itinuro ang *Nippongo*, ngunit kumaunti ang mga batang pumapasok. Ayon sa mga salaysay na tinipon sa Laguna, “halos karaniwan” ang unang dalawang taon sa mga bayang kaunti ang sundalong Hapones; noong 1944 tunay na humirap ang buhay. Pagkain ang pinakamabigat na suliranin. Binibili o kinukuha ng hukbo ang bigas, at pinalitan ng pamahalaan ni Laurel ang dating ahensiya ng bigas ng **BIBA** (*Bigasang Bayan*), ngunit “halos lahat ng plano ay nabigo” dahil sa kaguluhan, sa kakulangan ng gasolina at sasakyan, at sa mga pangangailangan ng hukbong Hapones. Tumaas nang husto ang mga presyo, at labis na bumagsak ang halaga ng salaping pang-okupasyon – ang tinaguriang “Mickey Mouse money” – kaya kinailangang maglimbag ng mga perang papel na 500 at 1,000 piso.

### 3. Ang takot

Sa likod ng karaniwang buhay ay may takot. Inaaresto ng **Kempeitai**, ang pulisyang militar ng Hapon, ang mga pinaghihinalaang gerilya, at karaniwan ang pagpapahirap. Si Gustavo Ingles, isang opisyal ng Hunters-ROTC na dinakip noong Hunyo 1943, ay pinahirapan sa Fort Santiago at saka ikinulong sa **Bilangguang New Bilibid** sa Muntinlupa, ilang kilometro lamang sa hilaga ng Biñan; noong Hunyo 24, 1944, pinalaya siya ng isang pagsalakay ng mga gerilya. Habang papalapit ang mga Amerikano, lalong naging malupit ang mga mananakop. Noong Disyembre 1944, itinatag ang **Makapili**, isang milisyang maka-Hapon na ang mga kasapi ay lumaban kasama ng mga Hapones sa Rizal, Laguna at Bulacan.

Sa Biñan mismo, may garison ang mga Hapones, kung saan ikinukulong ang mga pinaghihinalaan. Naalala ng mga gurong sumulat ng kasaysayan ng mga baryo noong 1953 na noong Agosto 1942, itinatag sa De La Paz ang isang lihim na samahan, ang “Biñan Unit”, na makalipas ang isang buwan ay isinama sa McKinley Brigade ng **Fil-American Irregular Troops**, sa ilalim ng Amerikanong koronel na si Hugh Straughn. Noong Hulyo 1943, dinakip ng Kempeitai ang isa sa mga kapitan nito, si **Enrique Alcabasa**, at dinala siya sa garison ng San Pedro, habang marami sa mga kasapi nito ang ikinulong sa garison ng Biñan at pinalaya dahil walang ebidensiya. Noong Agosto, muling dinakip si Alcabasa sa kanyang bahay sa De La Paz, ang punong-himpilan ng samahan; pinahirapan siya nang husto, at kinabukasan ay pinatay siya. Ang batang alkalde, ang abogadong si **Rogelio Limaco**, na nahalal noong 1940, ang nagligtas sa mga lalaking taga-Platero na dinala sa garison. Sa madaling-araw ng **Oktubre 24, 1944**, siya naman ang dinakip ng mga Hapones, at hindi na siya muling nakita: “naging bayani siya at pinatay ng mga Hapones habang ginagampanan ang kanyang tungkulin,” ayon sa kasaysayan ng Platero. Inaalala siya ng lungsod tuwing Setyembre 16, ang araw ng kanyang kapanganakan noong 1913.

### 4. Ang paglaban

Gayunman, lumakas ang paglaban. Sa Laguna, apat na malalaking samahan ng gerilya ang kumilos nang halos magkakahiwalay: ang **Hunters-ROTC**, na itinatag ng mga kadete ng Philippine Military Academy at ng ROTC; ang **Marking’s Fil-Americans**; ang **President Quezon’s Own Guerrillas** (PQOG) sa paligid ng San Pablo; at ang **Fil-American Irregular Troops** (FAIT). Kasama nilang lumaban ang **Hukbalahap**, na pinamumunuan ng mga komunista, at ang **Wha-Chi** ng mga Tsino. Noong Disyembre 1944, nasa Maynila, Cavite at Batangas ang 47th Division ng Hunters-ROTC, at nasa Cavite ang **48th Regiment** nito, sa kabila lamang ng mga burol mula sa Biñan. Ang 45th Regiment naman nito sa Laguna ay may 80 armadong mandirigma lamang – ngunit may humigit-kumulang 5,000 “home guard”, mga sibilyang nagbibigay ng pagkain, mensahe at impormasyon. Taga-kalapit na bayan ang isa sa mga pinakakilalang pinuno sa rehiyon: si **Abelardo “Kapitan Remo” Remoquillo** ng San Pedro, na napatay noong 1945 habang sinasalakay ang isang garison ng Hapon sa Bay.

### 5. Ang paglaya noong Pebrero 3, 1945

Noong Enero 1945, bumalik ang mga Amerikano sa Luzon. Noong Enero 31, lumunsad sa Nasugbu, Batangas, ang 11th Airborne Division, at noong **Pebrero 3**, bumaba sa Tagaytay Ridge ang mga paratrooper nito. Nang araw ding iyon, bago pa makarating ang sinumang sundalong Amerikano, **pinalaya ng mga Pilipino ang Biñan**. Ayon sa beterano at mananalaysay ng Hunters-ROTC na si Proculo Mojica, bumaba mula Carmona si de Ocampo at nagpasyang kunin ang Biñan, at noong Pebrero 3, nagmartsa siya patungo sa bayan sa tatlong hanay. Sinalakay nang harapan ng unang hanay ang garison – mga 30 sundalong Hapones na dinagdagan ng mga 50 Makapili. Isinara ng ikalawang hanay ang mga daan mula San Pedro sa hilaga, at pumasok ang ikatlo mula sa timog upang hadlangan ang anumang pagtakas patungong Santa Rosa. “Sa loob ng isang araw, nalipol ang buong garison at bumagsak ang Biñan sa mga Hunters.” Ang mga sumalakay ay ang **48th Regiment, 47th Division** ng Hunters-ROTC sa ilalim ni **Koronel Emmanuel de Ocampo**. Sa isang lokal na salaysay, binanggit si Marcial Alintanahin bilang pinunong namuno sa pagsalakay. Pinatutunayan ng mga plakeng nasa museo na ngayon ng lungsod na isa siyang medyor ng Hunters-ROTC, pinuno ng “Blue Lake Battalion” ng 48th Regiment ding iyon at dating hepe ng pulisya, at na naging “military mayor” siya ng Biñan pagkatapos ng paglaya; hindi nila sinasabi kung ano ang ginawa niya noong Pebrero 3. Noong Pebrero 5, bumalik ang mga gerilya mula San Pedro sa “Malayang Biñan” bago sumalakay sa Santa Rosa – kaya matagal na ipinagdiwang ng bayan ang paglaya nito sa petsang iyon: iginawad ang isang sertipikong nasa museo na ngayon ng lungsod “sa ika-37 Anibersaryo ng Araw ng Paglaya ng Biñan, ngayong ika-5 ng Pebrero” 1982.

### 6. Ang pagsalakay sa Los Baños

Pagkatapos, naging himpilan sa likuran ang Biñan ng pinakatanyag na pagliligtas sa digmaan. Sa Kolehiyo ng Agrikultura ng Unibersidad ng Pilipinas sa Los Baños, sa kabilang pampang ng lawa, nakakulong ang **2,147 sibilyan**, karamihan ay Amerikano at marami ang nanghihina na sa gutom. Noong Pebrero 20, tumawid sa Laguna de Bay ang apat na inhinyerong Amerikano **sakay ng bangka mula sa Biñan** upang pumili ng lalapagan. Mula rin sa Biñan naglayag si Gustavo Ingles – ang mismong pinalaya mula sa New Bilibid – upang makipag-ugnayan sa mga pinuno ng gerilya sa kabilang pampang. Noong Pebrero 21, nagmartsa patimog ang isang batalyon ng mga paratrooper sa Highway 1, ang daang dumaraan sa Biñan, hanggang Mamatid sa Cabuyao, kung saan naghihintay ang mga *amphibious tractor*. Sa madaling-araw ng **Pebrero 23**, bumaba ang mga paratrooper sa tabi ng kampo, sinalakay ng mga gerilya ang mga bantay, at itinawid ng mga amtrac ang mga bilanggo sa lawa tungo sa kaligtasan. Sa mga naiwan ibinunton ng mga Hapones ang kanilang paghihiganti. Sa mga baryo ng Los Baños, mahigit dalawandaang sibilyan ang pinatay ng mga sundalo sa mga linggong sumunod sa pagsalakay. Mas mabigat pa ang sinapit ng Calamba: tinaya ng mga imbestigador ng Amerika sa mga krimen sa digmaan na mga 1,500 katao ang minasaker mula Pebrero 12 hanggang 15, 1945, at ayon sa mga ebidensiyang dininig sa paglilitis kay Heneral Yamashita, mahigit 2,500 ang namatay noong Pebrero 12 pa lamang.

### 7. Isang bayang nakaligtas

Pagsapit ng kalagitnaan ng Pebrero, nasa likod na ng labanan ang Biñan, sa hilagang-kanlurang pampang ng lawa. Lumipat ang labanan patimog at pasilangan, sa Lipa, Tanauan at sa timog na pampang ng lawa, na nilinis ng XIV Corps ng Amerika at ng mga gerilyang Pilipino noong Marso at Abril. Napakabigat ng sinapit ng ibang lugar: halos hindi na makilala ang Lipa sa pagkawasak, nawasak ang katedral ng San Pablo, at lubusang nagiba ang bayan ng Mabitac. Walang naitalang labanan o masaker na ganoon kalaki sa Biñan. “Iilan lamang sa mga taga-bayan ang napatay noong Okupasyon,” isinulat ng mga guro nito noong 1953; “hindi na nagkaroon ng panahon ang mga Hapon na sunugin ang mga bahay o patayin ang mga tao.” Gayunman, ipinakikita ng mga pag-aresto, ng mga pagpapahirap at ng pagkamatay ni Kapitan Alcabasa at ni Alkalde Limaco na nagbayad din ng sariling halaga ang bayan. Nakaligtas sa digmaan ang mga lumang bahay na bato at kahoy sa paligid ng plaza – ang mga tahanan ng mga Alberto, Ocampo, Gonzales at iba pang angkan – kaya may maipagmamalaking mga bahay-pamana pa rin ang Biñan ngayon. Mabilis na nanumbalik ang buhay. Noong Mayo 15, 1945, habang nagpapatuloy pa ang digmaan sa hilagang Luzon, naglathala ang *Free Philippines* ng Maynila ng isang maikling paunawa: “BIÑAN FIESTA TODAY”.

### 8. Mula sa paglaya hanggang sa kasarinlan

Natapos ang digmaan noong Setyembre 1945. Nilagdaan ng Hapon ang pagsuko nito sa Look ng Tokyo noong Setyembre 2, at kinabukasan ay sumuko sa Baguio si Heneral Tomoyuki Yamashita, ang pinuno ng hukbong Hapones sa Pilipinas. Nilitis siya sa Maynila at binitay sa Los Baños noong Pebrero 23, 1946 – eksaktong isang taon matapos ang pagsalakay. Noong **Hulyo 4, 1946**, naging malaya ang Pilipinas, at nagsimulang magbayad ang Philippine War Damage Commission para sa mga bahay, pananim at gusaling pampublikong nawasak sa digmaan. Buhay pa rin sa Biñan ang alaala ng mga taong iyon. Tuwing Pebrero 3, ipinagdiriwang ng lungsod ang **Araw ng Paglaya ng Biñan** sa bantayog ng mga gerilya, at noong 2025, sa ikawalumpung anibersaryo, muling isinadula ang martsa ng mga gerilya mula Carmona.

**Did You Know?** **The date that changed by law.** For decades Biñan celebrated its liberation on 5 February – the city museum keeps a certificate from the “37th Anniversary of Liberation Day” in 1982 – and Republic Act No. 9427 of 2007 made that date official. In 2016 the National Historical Commission, relying on Proculo Mojica’s history of the Hunters-ROTC, confirmed **3 February 1945**, and in 2019 Republic Act No. 11442 moved Biñan Liberation Day to that date ([RA 9427 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9427_2007.html)) ([RA 11442 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2019/ra_11442_2019.html)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Ang petsang binago ng batas.** Sa loob ng ilang dekada, ipinagdiwang ng Biñan ang paglaya nito tuwing Pebrero 5 – may iniingatang sertipiko ang museo ng lungsod mula sa “ika-37 Anibersaryo ng Araw ng Paglaya” noong 1982 – at ginawang opisyal ng Batas Republika Blg. 9427 ng 2007 ang petsang iyon. Noong 2016, batay sa kasaysayan ng Hunters-ROTC na isinulat ni Proculo Mojica, pinagtibay ng Pambansang Komisyong Pangkasaysayan ang **Pebrero 3, 1945**, at noong 2019, inilipat ng Batas Republika Blg. 11442 ang Araw ng Paglaya ng Biñan sa petsang iyon.

**Did You Know?** **A famous rescue was prepared from Biñan.** Before the raid of 23 February 1945 that freed 2,147 internees at Los Baños, American engineers and a guerrilla officer crossed Laguna de Bay by *banca* from Biñan to scout the landing beach and meet the guerrilla chiefs ([Rottman 2010](https://archive.org/details/the-los-banos-prison-camp-raid-the-philippines-1945)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Inihanda mula sa Biñan ang isang tanyag na pagliligtas.** Bago ang pagsalakay noong Pebrero 23, 1945 na nagpalaya sa 2,147 bilanggo sa Los Baños, tumawid sa Laguna de Bay sakay ng *bangka* mula sa Biñan ang mga inhinyerong Amerikano at isang opisyal ng gerilya upang suriin ang lalapagan at makipagkita sa mga pinuno ng gerilya.

**Did You Know?** **A Bataan general is remembered in Biñan.** Brigadier General Mateo Capinpin, who commanded the 21st Division on Bataan in 1942, gave his name to a street of the old town, General M. Capinpin Street ([Rizal in Biñan (marker) | NHCP](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/rizal-in-binan/)) ([Morton, The Fall of the Philippines, 1953, ch. XXIV | HyperWar](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-P-PI/USA-P-PI-24.html)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Inaalala sa Biñan ang isang heneral ng Bataan.** Si Brigadier General Mateo Capinpin, na namuno sa 21st Division sa Bataan noong 1942, ay nakapangalan sa isang kalye ng lumang bayan, ang General M. Capinpin Street.

## Post-War Reconstruction and Contemporary Era / Pagbangon at Makabagong Panahon

### 1. Rebuilding

The first years of peace were spent rebuilding. Bridges on the national road were rebuilt under a Philippine–American programme, the War Damage Commission paid claims for destroyed property, and the census of 1948 counted **20,794** people in Biñan ([Population Enumerated in Various Censuses, 1903–2007: Laguna | NSO, via Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/PhilippinesCensusofPopulationLGUs19032007)). That same year a steel bridge, the Evangelista Bridge, named after its benefactor, replaced the old hanging bridge of the Poblacion, and on 30 December a marker to Rizal was unveiled at the site of his school; two colleges, Grinhar and Santa Catalina, had opened since 1946 ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)). In the 1950s the old Dominican *casa hacienda* on the plaza, which the town had bought in 1910 to house its public school, was repaired, given a portico and turned into the **municipal hall**, once the school had moved to a new building in Santo Domingo ([Sentrong Pangkultura ng Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/histo-cultural/sentrong-pangkultura-ng-binan/)). The friars’ granary of the eighteenth century thus became the seat of local government for more than half a century.

### 2. The Huk years

Peace in the countryside was fragile. The **Hukbalahap**, which had fought the Japanese, took up arms against the new Republic, and after the arrest of the communist party’s leaders in Manila on 18 October 1950 its units multiplied raids and hold-ups. A U.S. Army history of the insurrection records that “in Binan, Huks robbed the local bank of $76,000” late in 1950 ([Greenberg, The Hukbalahap Insurrection, 1987 | U.S. Army Center of Military History](https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/93-8-1.pdf)); the history of barrio San Vicente, written a few years later, remembers that on 31 October 1950 the pay train of the Manila Railroad Company was held up at the San Vicente station by men “believed to be Huks” ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio San Vicente, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b5/home.htm)). Around the town, life remained rural. The lots of the old friar estate – still described in land titles as the “Biñan Estate” – were farmed in rice and sugarcane, and the Poblacion lived from its market, its shoemakers and its *puto* makers on the national road and the railway to Manila. In 1953 the town had more than a hundred factories turning out thousands of pairs of shoes, wooden clogs and slippers, and its *Puto Biñan* had made it famous; Malaban alone had five shoe and slipper factories, three hat factories and some fifty duck raisers ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)) ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio of Malaban, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b3/home.htm)).

### 3. Fire and growth

The town grew quickly in the 1960s, from **33,309** people in 1960 to **58,290** in 1970. In **1968** fire destroyed the church of San Isidro Labrador and its convent; the present church was built in its place around **1970** ([Biñan City’s San Isidro Church to be declared a Diocesan Shrine | OpinYon, 2022](https://opinyon.net/laguna/binan-city-s-san-isidro-church-to-be-declared-a-diocesan-shrine)), and in December 1972 the Los Maduros club honoured the former mayor Jesus M. Garcia (1952–1959), chairman of the parish construction committee, for “the completion of the San Isidro Parish Church” ([Los Maduros plaque for Jesus M. Garcia, 1972, Biñan City Museum | Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:6263Bi%C3%B1an_City,_Laguna_Museum_Old_Town_Hall_Landmarks_24.jpg)). The building that faces the plaza today is therefore younger than many of the houses around it. Biñan also kept its station on the railway south: on the night of 16 March 1972, robbers boarded the Bicol Express as it left Biñan station, killed a railway employee and took more than ₱32,000 ([People v. Ong, 1981 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1981/feb1981/gr_43957_1981.html)).

### 4. Martial law and the expressway

Six months later, on 21 September 1972, President Ferdinand Marcos placed the country under **martial law**. Little has yet been written about Biñan in those years, and the town’s own memories of them remain to be collected. What changed the town most was a road. In the late 1970s the **South Luzon Expressway** was extended from Alabang toward Calamba, cutting through Biñan and bringing it within easy reach of Manila. The population rose to **83,684** in 1980. Miguelito Limaco was mayor from 1980 to 1986; after the People Power revolution of February 1986, elected local officials were replaced by officers-in-charge until the elections of January 1988 ([BCHATO accords pillars of good governance | City of Biñan, 2018](https://www.binan.gov.ph/bchato-accords-pillars-good-governance/)).

### 5. From cane fields to factories

Those elections brought in **Bayani “Arthur” Alonte**, mayor from 1988 to 1998, and with him the factories. In **1990** the **Laguna Technopark**, a venture of Ayala Land with Japanese partners, opened on former sugarcane land straddling Santa Rosa and Biñan; by 2020 it covered 471 hectares and housed more than 270 companies employing over 100,000 people ([Laguna Technopark celebrates 30 years | Ayala Land Logistics, 2020](https://www.ayalalandlogistics.com/all-news/laguna-technopark-celebrates-30-years/)). Its extensions reached barangay Biñan in 1997 and Loma and Timbao in 1998 ([Proclamation No. 1052, 1997 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc1997/proc_1052_1997.html)) ([Proclamation No. 1147, 1998 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc1998/proc_1147_1998.html)), and in 1992 Samsung and Solid Corporation opened the **Laguna International Industrial Park** in Mamplasan and Ganado. Subdivisions followed for workers and for families leaving Metro Manila: Biñan counted **134,553** people in 1990 and **201,186** in 2000. Politics had its scandal too: in December 1997 a Manila court convicted Mayor Alonte of rape, but in March 1998 the Supreme Court annulled the conviction for lack of due process and sent the case back for a new trial ([Alonte v. Savellano, 1998 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/mar1998/gr_131652_1998.html)). Under his successor, Hermis Perez (1998–2007), Biñan became one of the wealthiest towns in the country: by the end of his terms it was, in the city’s own words, “the richest municipality in the Philippines”, and government statistics for 2008 ranked it first among all Philippine municipalities by income ([Most top-earning towns derive income from IRA (NSCB data), BusinessMirror, 2010, reprinted | NTUC Philippines](https://ntucphl.org/2010/10/most-top-earning-towns-derive-income-from-ira/)) ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)).

### 6. A city

In 2007 **Len Alonte**, the former mayor’s daughter, became the town’s first woman mayor. Two years later, on 26 September 2009, Typhoon **Ondoy** poured record rains on the region; Laguna de Bay rose to nearly 14 metres and the lakeshore barangays of De La Paz and Malaban stayed under water for weeks ([Flooding eases but villagers still suffer | Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/260306/flooding-eases-but-villagers-still-suffer)). On 30 October 2009 the President signed **Republic Act No. 9740**, the charter of the City of Biñan ([RA 9740 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2009/ra_9740_2009.html)); the voters ratified it in a plebiscite on **2 February 2010** ([Proclamation No. 1984, 2010 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2010/proc_1984_2010.html)), and Biñan became the fourth city of Laguna, after San Pablo, Calamba and Santa Rosa. In 2015 Republic Act No. 10658 made it a congressional district of its own, and in 2016 it elected its first representative, Len Alonte ([RA 10658 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2015/ra_10658_2015.html)).

### 7. Back to the past: Balik-Biñan

Under Mayor **Arman Dimaguila** (2016–2025) the city turned back toward its past. The **Balik-Biñan** programme, launched in 2016, converted the old municipal hall into the **Sentrong Pangkultura ng Biñan**, with a museum, a library and the city’s culture and tourism office, revived the heritage district around Plaza Rizal and mapped the city’s cultural assets; in 2022 it won a **Galing Pook** award for good local governance ([Balik-Biñan Project | Galing Pook, 2022](https://galingpook.org/what-we-do/awards/awardees/balik-bi%C3%B1an-project-tourism-development-through-heritage-conservation/)). Its hardest fight was for the **Alberto house**, where Rizal’s mother grew up. Sold around 2010 to a resort owner who began moving its interiors to Bagac, in Bataan, the house lost its roof on 18 October 2012 ([Roof of the ancestral house of Rizal’s mother caves in | GMA News, 2012](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/regions/278933/roof-of-the-ancestral-house-of-rizal-s-mother-caves-in/story)). The city sued to expropriate it in 2017, rebuilt it, and reopened it on 6 December 2019 – a careful reconstruction that keeps only about a tenth of the original fabric ([Historic Alberto Mansion | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/histo-cultural/historic-alberto-mansion/)). Each February the city now celebrates **Araw ng Biñan** over three days: its cityhood on the 2nd, its liberation in 1945 on the 3rd and its founding as a *pueblo* in 1747 on the 4th. As Mayor Dimaguila put it: *“The past lives in our city. The past gives character to our city and people”* ([Past forward: Araw ng Biñan | BusinessMirror, 2024](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/02/19/past-forward-araw-ng-binan-channeling-history-heritage-and-culture-for-sustainable-progress/)).

### 8. Biñan today

The census of 2024 counted **584,479** people in Biñan – more than Calamba – making it, for the first time, the most populous city in Laguna; in 2020 it had been third, with 407,437 ([Proclamation No. 973, 2025 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_973_2025.html)) ([Biñan population | City Population](https://citypopulation.de/en/philippines/luzon/admin/laguna/043403__bi%C3%B1an/)). On its forty-odd square kilometres, the city of the friars’ hacienda is now a dense suburb of the capital, with industrial parks, malls and gated villages. Growth has its costs. The Biñan River regularly fails the lake authority’s water-quality standards ([LLDA data | Los Baños Times](https://lbtimes.ph/whole-of-community-approach-stricter-policy-implementation-sought-water-quality-of-laguna-lakes-tributaries-continue-to-deteriorate-%e2%88%92-llda-data/)), and in October 2024 Typhoon Kristine flooded 20 of the city’s 24 barangays. In 2025 the city elected a new mayor, Gel Alonte, while Arman Dimaguila went to Congress. Between the expressway and the lake, Biñan still has to reconcile the growth that made it rich with the heritage that makes it Biñan.

### 1. Muling pagtatayo

Pagkatapos ng digmaan, ginugol ang mga unang taon ng kapayapaan sa muling pagtatayo. Muling itinayo ang mga tulay sa pambansang daan sa ilalim ng isang programang Pilipino-Amerikano, nagbayad ang War Damage Commission para sa mga nasirang ari-arian, at nabilang sa senso ng 1948 ang **20,794** katao sa Biñan. Nang taon ding iyon, pinalitan ng isang tulay na bakal, ang Evangelista Bridge, na ipinangalan sa nag-abuloy nito, ang lumang tulay na *colgante* ng Poblacion, at noong Disyembre 30, inilantad ang isang panandang alay kay Rizal sa kinatayuan ng kanyang paaralan; mula 1946, nagbukas na rin ang dalawang kolehiyo, ang Grinhar at ang Santa Catalina. Noong dekada 1950, ang lumang *casa hacienda* ng mga Dominiko sa plaza, na binili ng bayan noong 1910 para sa pampublikong paaralan nito, ay kinumpuni, nilagyan ng portiko at ginawang **munisipyo**, nang makalipat na ang paaralan sa isang bagong gusali sa Santo Domingo. Sa gayon, ang kamalig ng mga prayle noong ikalabingwalong siglo ay naging luklukan ng pamahalaang bayan nang mahigit kalahating siglo.

### 2. Ang mga taon ng Huk

Marupok ang kapayapaan sa kanayunan. Nag-armas laban sa bagong Republika ang **Hukbalahap**, na lumaban noon sa mga Hapones, at matapos dakpin ang mga pinuno ng partido komunista sa Maynila noong Oktubre 18, 1950, dumami ang kanilang mga pagsalakay at panghoholdap. Ayon sa isang kasaysayan ng hukbong Amerikano tungkol sa pag-aalsa, sa huling bahagi ng 1950, “ninakawan ng mga Huk ang bangko ng Biñan ng $76,000”; naaalala naman ng kasaysayan ng baryo San Vicente, na isinulat makalipas ang ilang taon, na noong Oktubre 31, 1950, hinoldap sa istasyon ng San Vicente ang tren na nagdadala ng sahod ng Manila Railroad Company, ng mga lalaking “pinaniniwalaang mga Huk”. Sa paligid ng bayan, nanatiling makabukid ang buhay. Tinamnan ng palay at tubo ang mga lote ng dating asyenda ng mga prayle – na tinatawag pa ring “Biñan Estate” sa mga titulo ng lupa – at nabubuhay ang Poblacion sa palengke nito, sa mga sapatero at sa mga gumagawa ng puto sa tabi ng pambansang daan at ng riles patungong Maynila. Noong 1953, mahigit sandaang pagawaan sa bayan ang gumagawa ng libu-libong pares ng sapatos, bakya at tsinelas, at pinatanyag na ito ng *Puto Biñan*; sa Malaban lamang, may limang pagawaan ng sapatos at tsinelas, tatlong pagawaan ng sumbrero at mga limampung nag-aalaga ng itik.

### 3. Sunog at paglago

Mabilis na lumaki ang bayan noong dekada 1960, mula **33,309** katao noong 1960 hanggang **58,290** noong 1970. Noong **1968**, tinupok ng apoy ang simbahan ni San Isidro Labrador at ang kumbento nito; itinayo sa lugar nito ang kasalukuyang simbahan noong bandang **1970**, at noong Disyembre 1972, pinarangalan ng samahang Los Maduros ang dating alkaldeng si Jesus M. Garcia (1952–1959), tagapangulo ng lupon sa pagpapatayo ng parokya, dahil sa “pagkakatapos ng Simbahang Parokya ni San Isidro”. Kaya mas bata pa ang gusaling nakaharap sa plaza ngayon kaysa sa maraming bahay sa paligid nito. Napanatili rin ng Biñan ang istasyon nito sa riles patimog: noong gabi ng Marso 16, 1972, sumakay ang mga tulisan sa Bicol Express habang umaalis ito sa istasyon ng Biñan, pinatay ang isang kawani ng riles at tinangay ang mahigit ₱32,000.

### 4. Ang batas militar at ang expressway

Makalipas ang anim na buwan, noong Setyembre 21, 1972, isinailalim ni Pangulong Ferdinand Marcos ang bansa sa **batas militar**. Kakaunti pa ang naisusulat tungkol sa Biñan sa mga taong iyon, at kailangan pang tipunin ang sariling alaala ng bayan tungkol dito. Isang daan ang pinakanagpabago sa bayan. Noong huling bahagi ng dekada 1970, pinahaba ang **South Luzon Expressway** mula Alabang patungong Calamba; tinawid nito ang Biñan at inilapit ito sa Maynila. Umakyat sa **83,684** ang populasyon noong 1980. Si Miguelito Limaco ang alkalde mula 1980 hanggang 1986; matapos ang rebolusyong People Power noong Pebrero 1986, pinalitan ng mga *officer-in-charge* ang mga halal na opisyal ng bayan hanggang sa halalan noong Enero 1988.

### 5. Mula sa tubuhan hanggang sa pabrika

Dinala ng halalang iyon si **Bayani “Arthur” Alonte**, alkalde mula 1988 hanggang 1998, at kasama niya ang mga pabrika. Noong **1990**, binuksan ang **Laguna Technopark**, isang proyekto ng Ayala Land at mga katuwang na Hapones, sa dating tubuhan na sakop ng Santa Rosa at Biñan; pagsapit ng 2020, may lawak itong 471 ektarya at mahigit 270 kompanya na may higit sa 100,000 manggagawa. Umabot ang mga karugtong nito sa barangay Biñan noong 1997 at sa Loma at Timbao noong 1998, at noong 1992, binuksan ng Samsung at ng Solid Corporation ang **Laguna International Industrial Park** sa Mamplasan at Ganado. Sumunod ang mga subdibisyon para sa mga manggagawa at sa mga pamilyang umaalis sa Kamaynilaan: **134,553** katao ang Biñan noong 1990 at **201,186** noong 2000. Nagkaroon din ng iskandalo sa pulitika: noong Disyembre 1997, hinatulan ng isang hukuman sa Maynila si Alkalde Alonte sa kasong panggagahasa, ngunit noong Marso 1998, pinawalang-bisa ng Korte Suprema ang hatol dahil hindi nasunod ang wastong proseso, at ibinalik ang kaso para sa bagong paglilitis. Sa ilalim ng humalili sa kanya, si Hermis Perez (1998–2007), naging isa ang Biñan sa pinakamayayamang bayan sa bansa: pagsapit ng katapusan ng kanyang mga termino, ito, sa sariling salita ng lungsod, ang “pinakamayamang munisipalidad sa Pilipinas”, at ayon sa estadistika ng pamahalaan para sa 2008, ito ang nanguna sa kita sa lahat ng munisipalidad ng bansa.

### 6. Isang lungsod

Noong 2007, naging unang babaeng alkalde ng bayan si **Len Alonte**, anak ng dating alkalde. Makalipas ang dalawang taon, noong Setyembre 26, 2009, nagbuhos ng di-pangkaraniwang dami ng ulan ang Bagyong **Ondoy**; tumaas ang Laguna de Bay nang halos 14 metro, at nanatiling lubog sa tubig nang ilang linggo ang mga barangay sa baybayin na De La Paz at Malaban. Noong Oktubre 30, 2009, nilagdaan ng Pangulo ang **Batas Republika Blg. 9740**, ang karta ng Lungsod ng Biñan; pinagtibay ito ng mga botante sa isang plebisito noong **Pebrero 2, 2010**, at naging ikaapat na lungsod ng Laguna ang Biñan, kasunod ng San Pablo, Calamba at Santa Rosa. Noong 2015, ginawa itong sariling distritong pangkongreso ng Batas Republika Blg. 10658, at noong 2016, inihalal nito ang una nitong kinatawan, si Len Alonte.

### 7. Pagbabalik sa nakaraan: Balik-Biñan

Sa ilalim ni Alkalde **Arman Dimaguila** (2016–2025), muling lumingon ang lungsod sa nakaraan nito. Sa programang **Balik-Biñan**, na sinimulan noong 2016, ginawang **Sentrong Pangkultura ng Biñan** ang lumang munisipyo, na may museo, aklatan at tanggapan ng kultura at turismo ng lungsod; muling binuhay ang pook-pamana sa paligid ng Plaza Rizal at itinala ang mga yamang pangkultura ng lungsod. Noong 2022, nagkamit ito ng gantimpalang **Galing Pook** para sa mahusay na pamamahalang lokal. Ang pinakamahirap na laban ay para sa **bahay ng mga Alberto**, kung saan lumaki ang ina ni Rizal. Ipinagbili ito noong mga 2010 sa isang may-ari ng resort na nagsimulang maglipat ng mga bahagi nito sa Bagac, Bataan, at bumagsak ang bubong nito noong Oktubre 18, 2012. Nagsampa ang lungsod ng kaso upang ito’y makuha noong 2017, muli itong itinayo, at binuksan noong Disyembre 6, 2019 – isang maingat na muling pagtatayo na halos ikasampung bahagi lamang ng orihinal na materyal ang napanatili. Tuwing Pebrero, ipinagdiriwang na ngayon ng lungsod ang **Araw ng Biñan** sa loob ng tatlong araw: ang pagiging lungsod nito sa ika-2, ang paglaya nito noong 1945 sa ika-3, at ang pagkakatatag nito bilang *pueblo* noong 1747 sa ika-4. Ayon kay Alkalde Dimaguila: *“Isang Biñan tayong nagpapahalaga sa ating nakaraan kaya’t Isang Biñan tayong sasalubong sa ating magandang bukas”* ([Honoring Biñan’s legacy | Journal News, 2025](https://journalnews.com.ph/honoring-binans-legacy-a-triple-celebration-of-history-and-progress)).

### 8. Ang Biñan ngayon

Nabilang sa senso ng 2024 ang **584,479** katao sa Biñan – higit pa sa Calamba – kaya sa unang pagkakataon, ito ang naging pinakamataong lungsod sa Laguna; noong 2020, ikatlo lamang ito, na may 407,437. Sa mahigit apatnapung kilometro kuwadrado nito, ang lungsod ng asyenda ng mga prayle ay isa nang siksik na karugtong ng kabisera, na may mga industrial park, mall at saradong subdibisyon. May kapalit ang paglago. Madalas na bumabagsak ang Ilog Biñan sa pamantayan ng kalidad ng tubig ng awtoridad ng lawa, at noong Oktubre 2024, binaha ng Bagyong Kristine ang 20 sa 24 na barangay ng lungsod. Noong 2025, naghalal ang lungsod ng bagong alkalde, si Gel Alonte, habang napunta sa Kongreso si Arman Dimaguila. Sa pagitan ng expressway at ng lawa, kailangan pa ring pagtugmain ng Biñan ang paglagong nagpayaman dito at ang pamanang nagbibigay rito ng sariling pagkakakilanlan.

**Did You Know?** **One building, four lives.** The old municipal hall on the plaza was built by the Dominicans as the house and granary of their hacienda. The town bought it in 1910 for its school; after the war it became the municipal hall; and since 2016 it has been the Sentrong Pangkultura ng Biñan, home of the city museum ([Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/histo-cultural/sentrong-pangkultura-ng-binan/)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Isang gusali, apat na buhay.** Itinayo ng mga Dominiko ang lumang munisipyo sa plaza bilang bahay at kamalig ng kanilang asyenda. Binili ito ng bayan noong 1910 para sa paaralan nito; pagkatapos ng digmaan, naging munisipyo ito; at mula noong 2016, ito na ang Sentrong Pangkultura ng Biñan, ang tahanan ng museo ng lungsod.

**Did You Know?** **From third to first.** In 2020 Biñan was the third-largest city of Laguna. Four years later the census counted 584,479 people, ahead of Calamba (575,046): Biñan had become the most populous place in the province ([City Population, after PSA](https://citypopulation.de/en/philippines/luzon/admin/0434__laguna/)). In 1948 it had just 20,794 inhabitants ([Population Enumerated in Various Censuses, 1903–2007: Laguna | NSO, via Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/PhilippinesCensusofPopulationLGUs19032007)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Mula ikatlo hanggang una.** Noong 2020, ikatlong pinakamalaking lungsod ng Laguna ang Biñan. Makalipas ang apat na taon, nabilang ng senso ang 584,479 katao, higit pa sa Calamba (575,046): naging pinakamataong lugar sa lalawigan ang Biñan. Noong 1948, 20,794 lamang ang naninirahan dito.

**Did You Know?** **Rizal lost his head – and got it back.** The monument to José Rizal on Biñan’s plaza, completed on 14 May 1918 and believed to have been sculpted by the future National Artist Guillermo Tolentino, is more than a century old. On 22 September 2015 lightning struck it during a heavy downpour and decapitated the statue. A replica by Frederic Caedo took its place on the plaza in 2016, and the restored original was unveiled at City Hall on 18 December 2017 ([Century-old Rizal monument in Laguna ‘beheaded’ by lightning, 2015 | Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/lifestyle/arts-and-culture/2015/09/24/1503488/century-old-rizal-monument-laguna-beheaded-lightning)) ([Rizal Monument | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/heritage-sites/rizal-monument)) ([Unveiling Biñan’s Gallant History, 2017 | City of Biñan](https://www.binan.gov.ph/unveiling-binans-gallant-history/)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Naputulan ng ulo si Rizal – at naibalik ito.** Mahigit isang siglo na ang bantayog ni José Rizal sa plaza ng Biñan, na natapos noong Mayo 14, 1918 at pinaniniwalaang nililok ng magiging Pambansang Alagad ng Sining na si Guillermo Tolentino. Noong Setyembre 22, 2015, sa gitna ng malakas na buhos ng ulan, tinamaan ito ng kidlat at naputol ang ulo ng estatwa. Isang replika na gawa ni Frederic Caedo ang pumalit dito sa plaza noong 2016, at noong Disyembre 18, 2017, inilantad sa Bulwagang Panlungsod ang naibalik na orihinal.

# Part Two · Biñan Today / Ikalawang Bahagi · Ang Biñan Ngayon

## Culture and Daily Life / Kultura at Pang-araw-araw na Buhay

### 1. A market town

Biñan has always been a town of traders. Its public market, on J. Gonzales Street in the Poblacion, is busy day and night; the city calls it the largest public market in Laguna and in the whole CALABARZON region, and retailers from the neighbouring towns come to buy there and sell again at home ([History | City of Biñan](https://www.binan.gov.ph/history/)). A new wet market opened on 15 April 2019, with a *paluto* corner upstairs where shoppers can have the fish or meat they have just bought cooked on the spot ([Smell Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/smell-binan/)). Around it, family businesses – *sari-sari* stores, eateries and bakeries – fill the streets of the old town, while the malls of the last thirty years stand along the national road and the expressway (see chapter X). Each February, during Araw ng Biñan, the *Gawang Biñan* trade fair shows what the city makes ([Araw ng Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/feel-binan/araw-ng-binan/)).

### 2. Hands at work

For most of the twentieth century Biñan was a town of shoemakers. At the height of the trade, in the 1960s, some 500 workshops worked in about 3,000 homes: sandals, step-ins and children’s shoes in De La Paz, men’s shoes in Malaban, and wooden clogs – *bakya* – in Casile and San Jose. Families such as the Almendral and the Dilag built small businesses that sold to department stores and to the wholesale market of Baclaran, and a shoe still appears on the city’s seal ([Sapatos | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/smell-binan/sapatos/)). Cheap imports ruined most workshops from the late 1990s ([Flip-flops, Liliw | Bulatlat, 2007](https://www.bulatlat.com/news/6-51/6-51-liliw.htm)). In Platero, about 450 makers of hats and caps carry on another old trade, celebrated every 17 March since 2008 with a Sombrero Festival ([Sombrero | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/smell-binan/sombrero/)). Each barangay had its craft: horse-drawn *kalesa* in Canlalay and De La Paz, pedicabs in Soro-soro, sampaguita garlands in Bungahan, candles in Santo Domingo ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)). The town remembered its shoemakers on its plaza: the Los Maduros bandstand, given by a local gentlemen’s club in the 1950s or 1960s, is shaped like a wooden clog, or a slipper ([Biñan leads the way to heritage conservation | Daily Tribune, 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)).

### 3. Puto, pospas and pinipig

The city’s most famous product is **Puto Biñan**, a wide, soft rice cake. Its batter, the *galapong*, is made of rice flour, brown sugar, a raising agent and water; it is steamed in a *lansungan* lined with muslin over a fire of rice husks, then topped with grated cheese, salted egg and condensed milk. Local memory credits it to **Petronila “Nila” Samaniego**; vendors sold the cakes to passengers at the railway station, and travellers began to call them “the puto of Biñan”. Her descendant Marvin Endriga still runs the family kitchen, and most puto makers today live in San Vicente ([Puto Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/puto-binan)). The family brand dates its first sale to 1925 ([Tara, biyahe na! Kain na sa Laguna! | GMA News, 2015](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/newstv/poptalk/376327/tara-biyahe-na-kain-na-sa-laguna/story/)), and older makers such as Aling Manay and Aling Juling are still remembered ([Biñan pospas and puto | Philstar, 2015](https://www.philstar.com/starweek-magazine/2015/12/05/1529383/bian-pospas-and-puto)). The morning dish is **pospas**, a porridge of glutinous rice with native chicken and pork innards, coloured yellow with *kasubha* and topped with fried garlic and spring onion. Aling Merta Silva is believed to have pioneered it, perhaps as early as the Japanese occupation, and her helper Antonio “Tonyo” Benjamin carried it on ([Pospas | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/pospas/)). Canlalay is known for **pinipig**, young glutinous rice toasted in a clay pot and pounded flat in a wooden mortar; demand peaks before All Saints’ Day, and one maker, Edna’s, has been at work since 1967 ([Pinipig | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/pinipig/)). Santo Domingo calls itself the capital of *sorbetes*, home-made ice cream ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)). Other favourites of Laguna, such as *espasol* and *kesong puti*, belong to other towns of the province.

### 4. San Isidro, the Queen and the saints

The patron of Biñan is **San Isidro Labrador**, the farmer saint, whose feast falls on 15 May – fitting for a town that began as the “Hacienda de San Isidro Labrador de Biñan” ([Maglalatik | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/fee-binan/maglalatik)). His church, rebuilt around 1970 after the fire of 1968, keeps an old image that many believe to be miraculous, and on 6 May 2022 it became a diocesan shrine ([San Isidro Church to be declared a Diocesan Shrine | OpinYon, 2022](https://opinyon.net/laguna/binan-city-s-san-isidro-church-to-be-declared-a-diocesan-shrine)). Biñan also has a queen. *Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje*, venerated in the chapel of De La Paz, was proclaimed “Honorary Queen of Biñan” by a city resolution in 2018, and each May the men of the city carry her image from her chapel to the church in the *Karakol ng Reyna* ([Puto Latik Festival | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/feel-binan/puto-latik-festival/)). The Chinese-Filipino community holds its own thanksgiving, the *Pistang Intsik*, during the May festival, and each barangay has its fiesta: the Santo Niño in Ganado in January, hats in Platero in March, *puto* and *kakanin* in San Vicente in April, ice cream in Santo Domingo in August, *pinipig* in Canlalay in October ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)). Popular faith has its own stories. According to a pamphlet printed in 1947, the body of Filomena Almarines, a young roadside vendor who had died in 1938 at the age of 25, was found intact when her tomb was opened; a chapel was built and pilgrims came for some years, though the Church never recognized her cult ([“The Cross” | University of the Philippines Library](https://repository.mainlib.upd.edu.ph/omekas/files/original/ac8202e8b5abb8e990ffe85d98415d3798d3cd25.pdf)). The customs of Holy Week – the *pabasa*, the processions, the old images kept by families – have not yet been described in print; they are among the memories this book hopes to gather.

### 5. The Puto Latik Festival

Since 2011 the city has celebrated its rice cake and its dance together in the **Puto Latik Festival** ([Biñan holds online Puto Latik Festival for 2nd straight year | OpinYon, 2021](https://opinyon.net/laguna/binan-holds-online-puto-latik-festival-for-2nd-straight-year)). First held in February, it moved in 2017 to the nine days from 15 to 23 May, around the feast of San Isidro, and it was held online in 2020 and 2021. It opens with the *Karakol ng Reyna* and fills the streets with dancers from every barangay, with floats, with the *Puto Biñan 2.0* contest, in which barangays invent new toppings for the old cake, with the *Pistang Intsik* and, at the end, with the *Santacruzan*. The city reports national tourism awards for the festival in 2018, 2019 and 2022 ([Puto Latik Festival | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/feel-binan/puto-latik-festival/)), and in 2025 it was named the best cultural festival among the country’s cities ([Daily Tribune, 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)). Its heart is the **maglalatik**, a dance in which men with coconut shells strapped to their bodies strike them in rhythm. Biñan tradition holds that it was first danced by the men of Loma and Zapote as an offering to San Isidro during his procession, and that it tells of a fight for the *latik*, the coconut curd, between Moros in red and Christians in blue: the Moros win, but the two sides make peace and the Moros are baptized ([Maglalatik | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/fee-binan/maglalatik)). Other places, such as Cainta in Rizal, also claim the dance.

### 6. Bands, books and football

A city brass band, founded on 5 June 2017 with some 35 musicians, plays the *Serenata* at the dawn masses before Christmas in ten churches, and a Centre for Performing Arts was created the same year ([Biñan City Brass Band | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/bcpa/binan-city-brass-band)). The Sentrong Pangkultura, in the old town hall, houses the city museum and a library affiliated with the National Library. In 2017 the city declared a heritage district around Plaza Rizal and J. Gonzales and V. Ocampo streets, with several old houses and the Catholic cemetery of Canlalay; the Spanish-era dams of Timbao and Soro-soro followed in 2023 ([Daily Tribune, 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/31/bi%C3%B1an-leads-the-way-to-heritage-conservation-efforts-in-laguna)). Biñan now has universities and colleges – Perpetual Help, La Consolacion, the Polytechnic University of the Philippines and a campus of De La Salle among them – a long way from the one-room school where Rizal studied in 1870. Sport has found a home too. A football stadium with an artificial pitch, completed in 2015 in Zapote beside City Hall, became, under an agreement signed on 30 September 2015, the home ground of the women’s national team, the Malditas ([PFF, Biñan sign MOU for new home of Malditas | Dugout, 2015](https://www.dugout.ph/2015/09/pff-binan-sign-mou-for-new-home-of-malditas.html)).

### 7. Customs of 1953

In 1952 and 1953, on the orders of President Elpidio Quirino, the public-school teachers of the whole country wrote down the history and customs of every town and barrio. Their typed reports, the *Historical Data Papers*, are kept by the National Library of the Philippines; for Biñan there survive the report on the town and those on five barrios – Canlalay, De La Paz, Malaban, Platero and San Vicente ([Historical Data Papers: Laguna | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/nav1/bibLaguna.htm)). They explain names. Biñan, they say, comes from the *banian*, a tree that flourished there when the Spaniards came, from *binyagan*, “the place where the early Spaniards baptized the natives”, or from “Viñiang”, a nickname for Virginia ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)). Malaban means “many battles”, after the fights of the conquest ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio of Malaban, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b3/home.htm)); Platero recalls the goldsmiths, the *plateros*, who came from there ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio Platero, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b4/home.htm)); and De La Paz is named after Our Lady of Peace and Good Voyage, or after the shelter it gave the lake boats in stormy weather ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio of De La Paz, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b2/home.htm)).

They also record the customs of daily life. No one should stand in the doorway while a woman is in labour. A child must be baptized quickly, so that it will not fall sick, and given a godparent of good character, for it will inherit that character. In the old days a suitor served in the house of the girl he courted: “He carries water for her, he helps her father in the field, he gathers wood for her.” Before the bride leaves for church, silver coins are placed at her waist for prosperity; at a burial, the children and grandchildren of the dead are carried one by one over the body, “so that they will not be visited by the soul of the one departed”. People still told the time by the shadows, the sun and the moon, the stars and “the eyes of the cat” ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio Platero, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b4/home.htm)). They played *tubigan*, *patintero* and *sungka*, sang *kundiman*, “La Paloma” and “Bituin Marikit”, and in Canlalay still told the stories of Maria Makiling, Maria Banahaw and Maria Sinukuan ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio of De La Paz, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b2/home.htm)) ([Historical Data Papers: Canlalay, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b1/home.htm)).

### 1. Isang bayan ng pamilihan

Mula pa noon, bayan na ng mga mangangalakal ang Biñan. Abala araw at gabi ang pampublikong pamilihan nito sa Kalye J. Gonzales sa Poblacion; tinatawag ito ng lungsod na pinakamalaking pampublikong pamilihan sa Laguna at sa buong rehiyon ng CALABARZON, at dumarayo rito ang mga tindero mula sa mga karatig na bayan upang mamili at muling magtinda sa kanilang lugar. Binuksan noong Abril 15, 2019 ang isang bagong palengke ng sariwang paninda, na may sulok na *paluto* sa itaas kung saan maaaring ipaluto ng mamimili ang isda o karneng kabibili lamang niya. Sa paligid nito, pinupuno ng mga negosyong pampamilya – mga *sari-sari store*, karinderya at panaderya – ang mga kalye ng lumang bayan, habang nakatayo naman sa tabi ng pambansang daan at ng expressway ang mga mall ng nakaraang tatlumpung taon (tingnan ang kabanata X). Tuwing Pebrero, sa Araw ng Biñan, ipinakikita ng trade fair na *Gawang Biñan* ang mga produktong gawa sa lungsod.

### 2. Mga kamay na gumagawa

Sa halos buong ikadalawampung siglo, bayan ng mga sapatero ang Biñan. Sa kasagsagan ng hanapbuhay na ito, noong dekada 1960, mga 500 talyer ang kumikilos sa humigit-kumulang 3,000 tahanan: sandalyas, *step-in* at sapatos pambata sa De La Paz, sapatos panlalaki sa Malaban, at *bakya* sa Casile at San Jose. Nagtayo ang mga pamilyang tulad ng Almendral at Dilag ng maliliit na negosyong nagbebenta sa mga department store at sa pakyawang pamilihan ng Baclaran, at may sapatos pa rin sa sagisag ng lungsod. Pinabagsak ng murang inangkat na produkto ang karamihan sa mga talyer mula sa huling bahagi ng dekada 1990. Sa Platero, mga 450 gumagawa ng sumbrero at gorra ang nagpapatuloy sa isa pang lumang hanapbuhay, na ipinagdiriwang tuwing Marso 17 mula noong 2008 sa pamamagitan ng Sombrero Festival. May sariling gawain ang bawat barangay: *kalesa* sa Canlalay at De La Paz, *padyak* sa Soro-soro, kuwintas ng sampaguita sa Bungahan, kandila sa Santo Domingo. Inalala ng bayan ang mga sapatero nito sa plaza: hugis-bakya o tsinelas ang kiyosko ng Los Maduros, na handog ng isang samahan ng mga ginoo noong dekada 1950 o 1960.

### 3. Puto, pospas at pinipig

Ang pinakatanyag na produkto ng lungsod ay ang **Puto Biñan**, isang malapad at malambot na kakanin. Ang masa nito, ang *galapong*, ay gawa sa harina ng bigas, asukal na pula, pampaalsa at tubig; pinasisingawan ito sa *lansungan* na may sapin na katsa sa ibabaw ng apoy ng ipa, saka nilalagyan ng ginadgad na keso, itlog na maalat at gatas na kondensada. Ayon sa alaala ng bayan, si **Petronila “Nila” Samaniego** ang lumikha nito; ipinagbibili ng mga tindero ang puto sa mga pasahero sa istasyon ng tren, at nagsimulang tawagin ito ng mga manlalakbay na “ang puto ng Biñan”. Ang kanyang inapong si Marvin Endriga pa rin ang nangangasiwa sa kusina ng pamilya, at karamihan sa mga gumagawa ng puto ngayon ay nakatira sa San Vicente. Ayon sa tatak ng pamilya, unang naipagbili ang kanilang puto noong 1925, at inaalala pa rin ang mga matatandang gumagawa nito gaya nina Aling Manay at Aling Juling. Ang pagkain sa umaga ay ang **pospas**, isang lugaw na malagkit na may manok na tagalog at lamang-loob ng baboy, na pinadilaw ng *kasubha* at nilagyan ng pritong bawang at dahon ng sibuyas. Pinaniniwalaang si Aling Merta Silva ang nagpasimula nito, marahil noong panahon pa ng Hapon, at ipinagpatuloy ito ng kanyang katulong na si Antonio “Tonyo” Benjamin. Kilala ang Canlalay sa **pinipig**, batang malagkit na sinangag sa palayok at binayo hanggang pumipi sa lusong; pinakamataas ang pangangailangan dito bago mag-Undas, at mula pa noong 1967 ay gumagawa nito ang isang gumagawa, ang Edna’s. Tinatawag naman ng Santo Domingo ang sarili nito na kabisera ng *sorbetes*, ang lutong-bahay na ice cream. Ang ibang paborito ng Laguna, gaya ng *espasol* at *kesong puti*, ay galing sa ibang bayan ng lalawigan.

### 4. Si San Isidro, ang Reyna at ang mga santo

Ang patron ng Biñan ay si **San Isidro Labrador**, ang santo ng mga magsasaka, na ang kapistahan ay tuwing Mayo 15 – angkop sa isang bayang nagsimula bilang “Hacienda de San Isidro Labrador de Biñan”. Ang kanyang simbahan, na muling itinayo noong bandang 1970 matapos ang sunog noong 1968, ay may lumang imaheng pinaniniwalaan ng marami na mapaghimala, at noong Mayo 6, 2022, naging dambanang pandiyosesis ito. May reyna rin ang Biñan. Ang *Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje*, na pinipintakasi sa kapilya ng De La Paz, ay hinirang na “Pandangal na Reyna ng Biñan” sa isang resolusyon ng lungsod noong 2018, at tuwing Mayo ay pinapasan ng mga kalalakihan ng lungsod ang kanyang imahen mula sa kanyang kapilya hanggang sa simbahan sa *Karakol ng Reyna*. May sariling pasasalamat ang pamayanang Tsino-Pilipino, ang *Pistang Intsik*, sa panahon ng pista ng Mayo, at may kapistahan ang bawat barangay: ang Santo Niño sa Ganado tuwing Enero, ang sumbrero sa Platero tuwing Marso, ang *puto* at *kakanin* sa San Vicente tuwing Abril, ang sorbetes sa Santo Domingo tuwing Agosto, at ang *pinipig* sa Canlalay tuwing Oktubre. May sariling mga kuwento ang pananampalataya ng bayan. Ayon sa isang polyetong inilimbag noong 1947, natagpuang buo ang bangkay ni Filomena Almarines, isang batang tindera sa tabing-daan na namatay noong 1938 sa edad na 25, nang buksan ang kanyang libingan; nagtayo ng kapilya at dinayo ito ng mga peregrino sa loob ng ilang taon, bagama’t hindi kailanman kinilala ng Simbahan ang pamimintuho sa kanya. Ang mga kaugalian tuwing Mahal na Araw – ang *pabasa*, ang mga prusisyon, ang mga lumang imaheng iniingatan ng mga pamilya – ay hindi pa naisusulat; kabilang ang mga ito sa mga alaalang nais tipunin ng aklat na ito.

### 5. Ang Puto Latik Festival

Mula noong 2011, sabay na ipinagdiriwang ng lungsod ang kakanin at ang sayaw nito sa **Puto Latik Festival**. Unang idinaos noong Pebrero, inilipat ito noong 2017 sa siyam na araw mula Mayo 15 hanggang 23, sa paligid ng kapistahan ni San Isidro, at idinaos ito online noong 2020 at 2021. Nagsisimula ito sa *Karakol ng Reyna* at pinupuno nito ang mga kalye ng mga mananayaw mula sa bawat barangay, ng mga karosa, ng paligsahang *Puto Biñan 2.0* kung saan umiimbento ang mga barangay ng mga bagong palaman sa lumang kakanin, ng *Pistang Intsik* at, sa huli, ng *Santacruzan*. Ayon sa lungsod, nagkamit ang pista ng mga pambansang gantimpala sa turismo noong 2018, 2019 at 2022, at noong 2025, hinirang itong pinakamahusay na pistang pangkultura sa mga lungsod ng bansa. Ang puso nito ay ang **maglalatik**, isang sayaw kung saan pinapalo ng mga kalalakihan nang may indayog ang mga bao ng niyog na nakatali sa kanilang katawan. Ayon sa tradisyon ng Biñan, unang isinayaw ito ng mga kalalakihan ng Loma at Zapote bilang handog kay San Isidro sa kanyang prusisyon, at isinasalaysay nito ang labanan para sa *latik* sa pagitan ng mga Morong nakapula at mga Kristiyanong nakaasul: nanalo ang mga Moro, ngunit nagkasundo ang dalawang panig at bininyagan ang mga Moro. Inaangkin din ng ibang lugar ang sayaw na ito, gaya ng Cainta sa Rizal.

### 6. Banda, aklat at futbol

Isang banda ng lungsod, na itinatag noong Hunyo 5, 2017 na may mga 35 musikero, ang tumutugtog ng *Serenata* sa mga misa de gallo bago mag-Pasko sa sampung simbahan, at itinatag din nang taong iyon ang isang Sentro para sa Sining ng Pagtatanghal. Nasa Sentrong Pangkultura, sa lumang munisipyo, ang museo ng lungsod at isang aklatang kaanib ng Pambansang Aklatan. Noong 2017, idineklara ng lungsod ang isang pook-pamana sa paligid ng Plaza Rizal at ng mga kalye J. Gonzales at V. Ocampo, kasama ang ilang lumang bahay at ang sementeryong Katoliko ng Canlalay; sumunod noong 2023 ang mga prinsa ng Timbao at Soro-soro mula pa noong panahon ng Espanyol. May mga unibersidad at kolehiyo na ngayon ang Biñan – kabilang ang Perpetual Help, La Consolacion, ang Polytechnic University of the Philippines at isang kampus ng De La Salle – malayo na sa iisang silid na paaralang pinag-aralan ni Rizal noong 1870. Nagkaroon din ng tahanan ang palakasan. Isang istadyum ng futbol na may artipisyal na damuhan, na natapos noong 2015 sa Zapote katabi ng Bulwagang Panlungsod, ang naging tahanan ng pambansang koponan ng kababaihan, ang Malditas, sa bisa ng kasunduang nilagdaan noong Setyembre 30, 2015.

### 7. Mga kaugalian noong 1953

Noong 1952 at 1953, sa utos ni Pangulong Elpidio Quirino, isinulat ng mga guro ng mga pampublikong paaralan sa buong bansa ang kasaysayan at mga kaugalian ng bawat bayan at baryo. Iniingatan ng Pambansang Aklatan ng Pilipinas ang kanilang mga ulat na makinilyado, ang *Historical Data Papers*; para sa Biñan, natitira pa ang ulat tungkol sa bayan at ang tungkol sa limang baryo – Canlalay, De La Paz, Malaban, Platero at San Vicente. Ipinaliliwanag ng mga ito ang mga pangalan. Ayon sa kanila, nagmula ang Biñan sa *banian*, isang punong yumayabong doon nang dumating ang mga Espanyol, sa *binyagan*, “ang lugar kung saan bininyagan ng mga unang Espanyol ang mga katutubo”, o sa “Viñiang”, palayaw ng Virginia. “Maraming labanan” ang kahulugan ng Malaban, dahil sa mga labanan noong pananakop; ipinaaalala ng Platero ang mga panday-ginto, ang mga *platero*, na nagmula roon; at ipinangalan ang De La Paz sa Birhen ng Kapayapaan at Mabuting Paglalakbay, o sa kanlungang ibinibigay nito sa mga bangka ng lawa kapag masama ang panahon.

Itinala rin ng mga ito ang mga kaugalian sa araw-araw na buhay. Walang dapat tumayo sa pintuan habang nanganganak ang isang babae. Dapat binyagan agad ang sanggol upang hindi ito magkasakit, at bigyan ng ninong o ninang na mabuti ang ugali, dahil mamanahin ng bata ang ugaling iyon. Noong araw, naglilingkod ang manliligaw sa bahay ng dalagang nililigawan niya: “Iniigiban niya ito ng tubig, tinutulungan niya ang ama nito sa bukid, ikinukuha niya ito ng kahoy.” Bago umalis ang nobya patungong simbahan, nilalagyan ng mga baryang pilak ang kanyang baywang para sa kasaganaan; sa libing, isa-isang itinatawid sa ibabaw ng bangkay ang mga anak at apo ng namatay, “upang hindi sila dalawin ng kaluluwa ng yumao.” Sinusukat pa ng mga tao ang oras sa pamamagitan ng mga anino, ng araw at buwan, ng mga bituin at ng “mga mata ng pusa.” Naglalaro sila ng *tubigan*, *patintero* at *sungka*, umaawit ng *kundiman*, ng “La Paloma” at ng “Bituin Marikit”, at sa Canlalay, ikinukuwento pa nila ang mga alamat nina Maria Makiling, Maria Banahaw at Maria Sinukuan.

**Did You Know?** **A bandstand shaped like a clog.** The Los Maduros bandstand on Plaza Rizal was given by a gentlemen’s club in the 1950s or 1960s. Its shape recalls a *bakya*, the wooden clog, or a slipper – a reminder of the time when some 500 shoe and slipper workshops worked from about 3,000 homes of Biñan ([Sapatos | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/smell-binan/sapatos/)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Isang kiyoskong hugis-bakya.** Handog ng isang samahan ng mga ginoo noong dekada 1950 o 1960 ang kiyosko ng Los Maduros sa Plaza Rizal. Ipinaaalala ng hugis nito ang *bakya* o tsinelas – tanda ng panahong mga 500 talyer ng sapatos at tsinelas ang kumikilos sa humigit-kumulang 3,000 tahanan sa Biñan.

**Did You Know?** **Puto named by train passengers.** Petronila “Nila” Samaniego made her rice cakes at home, and her family brand dates its first sale to 1925. Vendors sold them to passengers at Biñan’s railway station, and the travellers gave them the name of the town: Puto Biñan ([Puto Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/puto-binan)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Ang putong pinangalanan ng mga pasahero ng tren.** Sa bahay ginagawa ni Petronila “Nila” Samaniego ang kanyang puto, at ayon sa tatak ng pamilya, unang naipagbili ito noong 1925. Ipinagbibili ito ng mga tindero sa mga pasahero sa istasyon ng tren ng Biñan, at ibinigay ng mga manlalakbay ang pangalan ng bayan: Puto Biñan.

**Did You Know?** **Pounding rice to the guitar.** In Canlalay, *pinipig* was made by hand: the toasted rice was pounded in a big wooden mortar while neighbours sang *kundiman* to a guitar, and the pestles kept time with the music ([Pinipig | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/taste-binan/pinipig/)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Pagbabayo sa saliw ng gitara.** Sa Canlalay, mano-manong ginagawa ang *pinipig*: binabayo sa malaking lusong na kahoy ang sinangag na malagkit habang umaawit ng *kundiman* ang magkakapitbahay sa saliw ng gitara, at sumusunod sa tugtog ang indayog ng mga halo.

**Did You Know?** **“Go to Canlay.”** The history of Canlalay written in 1953 tells how the barrio got its name. In the beginning there were only seven houses and a single guava tree, which belonged to Aling Maria. One day, as she was looking for her pig, her little girl came running and calling “Nay! Nay! Canlay!”, pointing at the animal. The neighbours began to call the girl Canlay, and whoever wanted guavas would say: “Go to Canlay” ([Historical Data Papers: Canlalay, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b1/home.htm)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **“Pumunta ka kay Canlay.”** Ikinukuwento ng kasaysayan ng Canlalay na isinulat noong 1953 kung paano nakuha ng baryo ang pangalan nito. Noong una, pitong bahay lamang ang naroon at iisang puno ng bayabas, na pag-aari ni Aling Maria. Isang araw, habang hinahanap niya ang kanyang baboy, patakbong dumating ang kanyang batang anak na babae, sumisigaw ng “Nay! Nay! Canlay!” habang itinuturo ang hayop. Mula noon, “Canlay” na ang tawag ng mga kapitbahay sa bata, at ang sinumang nangangailangan ng bayabas ay nagsasabing: “Pumunta ka kay Canlay.”

**Did You Know?** **Sayings of De La Paz.** In 1953 the teachers of De La Paz wrote down the sayings of their barrio in Tagalog. “Ang taong masalita, kaunti ang magagawa”: “He who talks much can do very little”, as they translated it. “Ang bayaning masugatan, nag-iibayo ang tapang”: a wounded hero grows twice as brave. “Walang matimtimang birhen sa matiyagang manalangin”: no virgin stays unmoved by one who prays with patience. They also noted two riddles, without the answers: “Isang butil na palay, sikip sa buong bahay” – one grain of rice that fills the whole house – and “Ako ay nagtanim ng dayap sa gitna ng dagat, marami ang humanap, iisa ang nagkapalad” – I planted a lime tree in the middle of the sea; many looked for it, only one was lucky. The first is usually answered *ilaw*, a lamp ([History and Cultural Life of the Barrio of De La Paz, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/b2/home.htm)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Mga kasabihan ng De La Paz.** Noong 1953, isinulat ng mga guro ng De La Paz sa Tagalog ang mga kasabihan ng kanilang baryo: “Ang taong masalita, kaunti ang magagawa”; “Ang bayaning masugatan, nag-iibayo ang tapang”; “Walang matimtimang birhen sa matiyagang manalangin.” Itinala rin nila ang dalawang bugtong, nang walang sagot: “Isang butil na palay, sikip sa buong bahay”, at “Ako ay nagtanim ng dayap sa gitna ng dagat, marami ang humanap, iisa ang nagkapalad.” Karaniwang *ilaw* ang isinasagot sa una.

## Getting Around / Paglalakbay at Transportasyon

### 1. The lake and the old road

For three centuries Biñan looked to the water. *Cascos* and *bancas* carried rice, sugar and cloth across Laguna de Bay and down the Pasig River to Manila, and in December 1870 the steamer *Talim* called at Biñan to take the young Rizal home to Calamba (see chapter IV). Around 1900 an English writer noted that “light draught steamers… ply daily to Binan, Calamba, and Santa Cruz” ([Sawyer, The Inhabitants of the Philippines, 1900 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/inhabitantsofphi00sawy_0)), and an American colonel found that “Binan can be approached from the lake about as easily as Calamba” ([Annual Reports of the War Department, 1900 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/annualreportswa50deptgoog)). On land, paved *calzadas* linked the town to its neighbours, and the Manila South Road – the “Route 1” of the armies of 1941–1945 – became the national road, today numbered N1. No passenger boat leaves Biñan any more. The lake belongs to its fishers, who race their boats off Malaban every 31 July in the *Bangkarera sa Lawa* ([Bangkarera sa Lawa | City of Biñan](https://www.binan.gov.ph/bangkarera-sa-lawa/)); a Coast Guard sub-station registers their boats ([City of Biñan, 2026](https://www.binan.gov.ph/?p=9023)), and a 325-metre lakeshore promenade opened in De La Paz in March 2024 ([Biñan Lakeshore | City of Biñan, 2024](https://www.binan.gov.ph/?p=7055)).

### 2. The railway

The Manila Railroad reached Biñan on 2 September 1908. For more than a century its station on General Malvar Street, in San Vicente, linked the town to Manila and to the south, with a second stop at Golden City 1; in 1972 robbers attacked the Bicol Express as it left the platform (see chapter VII). On 24 April 2019 the first train designed and built in the Philippines, the Hybrid Electric Train of the Department of Science and Technology, was inaugurated at Biñan station after a first run from Alabang ([DOST just inaugurated a 100% locally made train | GMA News, 2019](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/scitech/technology/692256/dost-just-inaugurated-a-100-locally-made-train/story)). Four years later, on 2 July 2023, all trains between Alabang and Calamba stopped and the station closed, to make way for the **North–South Commuter Railway** ([PNR to suspend Alabang–Calamba trips | Philstar, 2023](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2023/06/03/2271165/pnr-suspend-alabang-calamba-trips)). The new electric line, built on a viaduct with loans from the Asian Development Bank and Japan, will have an elevated station in Biñan, between Pacita and Santa Rosa; the works began on 3 July 2023 and are due to end around 2028 ([P73B South Commuter Railway breaks ground | PortCalls, 2023](https://portcalls.com/p73b-south-commuter-railway-breaks-ground/)). The families who lived along the old track have been moved to new housing at The Meadows, in Langkiwa, since February 2026 ([DOTr turns over first housing units to NSCR families | GMA News, 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/975376/dotr-turns-over-first-housing-units-nscr-families/story)).

### 3. The expressways

The South Luzon Expressway reached Biñan in the late 1970s, when its extension from Alabang to Calamba, begun in 1974, was completed ([Motorway Development Study in the Kingdom of Thailand, Final Report: Motorways in Various Countries, p. 142 | JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/10928083_03.pdf)) ([Presidential Decree No. 1113, 1977 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1977/pd_1113_1977.html)). Two of its exits are in Biñan, at Southwoods (exit 31) and at Mamplasan (exit 36); the Carmona exit (33), between them, lies just across the border in Cavite and leads to Biñan by General Malvar Street. Since 30 October 2019 the **Cavite–Laguna Expressway** (CALAX) has started at the Mamplasan roundabout, beside the SLEX exit; an interchange serving the Laguna Technopark opened in Timbao on 18 August 2020 ([CALAX toll collection, 2020 | CALAX (MPT South)](https://www.calax.com.ph/news-and-events/2020/calax-toll-collection)) ([DPWH inspects CALAX Laguna segment interchanges, 2020 | CALAX (MPT South)](https://www.calax.com.ph/news-and-events/2020/dpwh-inspects-calax-laguna-segment-interchanges-sec-villar-announces-august-18)), and since 2026 the road runs 26 kilometres into Cavite, as far as General Trias ([TRB approves toll collection for CALAX Subsection 3 | CALAX, 2026](https://www.calax.com.ph/news-and-events/2026/trb-approves-toll-collection-calax-subsection-3)). Without traffic, Alabang is less than twenty minutes away by car, and Makati, the international airport and Tagaytay about half an hour each; at rush hour the same trips often take two or three times as long.

### 4. Jeepneys, buses and vans

Most people travel by jeepney and bus. Road transport has long been a local business: in 1953 the townspeople ran three big transport companies, the Biñan Transportation Company, the Laguna Transportation Company and the Sousa Transportation Co. ([History and Cultural Life of the Town of Biñan, c. 1953 | National Library of the Philippines](https://nlpdl.nlp.gov.ph/HD01/p22/cm3/md1/home.htm)). Today jeepneys run along the national road to San Pedro, Alabang and Balibago in Santa Rosa, and up General Malvar Street to Carmona and General Mariano Alvarez in Cavite. The national modernization programme launched in 2017 pushed their owners into cooperatives and brought new, larger vehicles: the modern jeepney line “Biñan – Balibago via Manila South Road” was among the first to run again after the lockdown, on 26 June 2020 ([Modern jeepneys will begin operations today | Top Gear Philippines, 2020](https://www.topgear.com.ph/news/motoring-news/modern-jeepney-june-22-2020-a2578-20200622)). Fares are set by the national regulator: since 28 September 2026 the minimum fare has been ₱14 on a traditional jeepney and ₱17 on a modern one ([Higher PUV fares take effect Monday | SunStar, 2026](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/higher-puv-fares-take-effect-monday-sept-28)). Buses leave from the terminal near the old town centre for Buendia and Ayala in Makati by the expressway; in 2022 that route was shared by JAC Liner and LLI, with 105 buses ([Disrupted routes, disrupted lives | OpinYon, 2022](https://opinyon.net/opinion/disrupted-routes-disrupted-lives-2)). Other buses and vans start at Southwoods, and provincial buses heading south stop on the way.

### 5. Tricycles

For short trips, Biñan rides the tricycle, a motorcycle with a covered sidecar. Since the Local Government Code of 1991, it is the city, not the national government, that grants tricycle franchises and regulates their operation ([Republic Act No. 7160, 1991 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1991/ra_7160_1991.html)). National guidelines, quoted by the Supreme Court in 2000, keep tricycles off the national highways used by heavy vehicles unless there is no other road, allow a common colour for the tricycles of each zone and give each unit an identification number; a permit lasts three years ([LTO v. City of Butuan, 2000 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2000/jan2000/gr_131512_2000.html)). In Biñan the permits are issued by the Biñan Tricycle Franchising and Regulatory Board. Owners and drivers belong to TODAs – tricycle operators and drivers’ associations – each with its own zone and terminal, grouped in a city federation; in April 2026, 5,489 TODA members received a fuel subsidy from the city ([City of Biñan, 2026](https://www.binan.gov.ph/?p=8756)). The first electric tricycles arrived in 2017 ([Biñan receives 2 e-trikes | City of Biñan, 2017](https://www.binan.gov.ph/binan-receives-2-e-trikes/)), and a 2023 ordinance lets e-trikes cross the main roads but not ride along them ([Biñan City’s e-bike ordinance | OpinYon, 2023](https://opinyon.net/laguna/binan-city-s-e-bike-ordinance-safety-over-convenience)).

The map will change again. The North–South Commuter Railway should bring trains back around 2028, and a raised road, the **Laguna Lakeshore Road Network**, is to be built along the shore from Taguig to Calamba, with financing approved in 2024 by the Asian Development Bank and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank; its San Pedro–Cabuyao section, 12.23 kilometres long, will have an interchange for San Pedro and Biñan ([ADB supports the Laguna Lakeshore project | ADB, 2024](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-supports-regional-connectivity-resilient-infrastructure-laguna-lakeshore-project)) ([Laguna Lakeshore Road Network Phase 1 | AIIB](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2024/approved/Philippines-Laguna-Lakeshore-Road-Network-Phase-1-Project.html)). The lake, Biñan’s oldest highway, will carry traffic again – on a road built along its edge.

### 1. Ang lawa at ang lumang daan

Sa loob ng tatlong siglo, nakaharap sa tubig ang Biñan. Nagdadala ang mga *kasko* at *bangka* ng palay, asukal at tela patawid ng Laguna de Bay at pababa ng Ilog Pasig hanggang Maynila, at noong Disyembre 1870, dumaan sa Biñan ang barkong de-singaw na *Talim* upang iuwi sa Calamba ang batang si Rizal (tingnan ang kabanata IV). Noong mga 1900, isinulat ng isang manunulat na Ingles na “may mga magagaang barkong de-singaw na araw-araw na naglalayag patungong Biñan, Calamba at Santa Cruz”, at natuklasan ng isang koronel na Amerikano na “halos kasindali ng Calamba ang paglapit sa Biñan mula sa lawa”. Sa lupa, iniugnay ng mga *calzada* ang bayan sa mga karatig nito, at ang Manila South Road – ang “Route 1” ng mga hukbo noong 1941–1945 – ang naging pambansang daan, na N1 ang bilang ngayon. Wala nang bangkang pampasahero na umaalis sa Biñan. Pag-aari na ng mga mangingisda ang lawa, na nagkakarera ng kanilang mga bangka sa harap ng Malaban tuwing Hulyo 31 sa *Bangkarera sa Lawa*; nagtatala ng kanilang mga bangka ang isang sub-istasyon ng Coast Guard, at binuksan noong Marso 2024 ang isang 325-metrong pasyalan sa baybayin ng De La Paz.

### 2. Ang riles

Umabot sa Biñan ang Manila Railroad noong Setyembre 2, 1908. Sa loob ng mahigit isang siglo, iniugnay ng istasyon nito sa Kalye General Malvar, sa San Vicente, ang bayan sa Maynila at sa timog, at may ikalawang hintuan sa Golden City 1; noong 1972, sinalakay ng mga tulisan ang Bicol Express habang umaalis ito sa plataporma (tingnan ang kabanata VII). Noong Abril 24, 2019, pinasinayaan sa istasyon ng Biñan ang unang tren na dinisenyo at ginawa sa Pilipinas, ang Hybrid Electric Train ng Kagawaran ng Agham at Teknolohiya, matapos ang unang biyahe nito mula Alabang. Makalipas ang apat na taon, noong Hulyo 2, 2023, tumigil ang lahat ng tren sa pagitan ng Alabang at Calamba at isinara ang istasyon upang bigyang-daan ang **North–South Commuter Railway**. Ang bagong linyang de-kuryente, na itinatayo sa isang viaduct sa tulong ng mga pautang ng Asian Development Bank at ng Hapon, ay magkakaroon ng nakataas na istasyon sa Biñan, sa pagitan ng Pacita at Santa Rosa; nagsimula ang mga gawain noong Hulyo 3, 2023 at inaasahang matatapos sa mga 2028. Inilipat mula Pebrero 2026 sa bagong pabahay sa The Meadows, sa Langkiwa, ang mga pamilyang nakatira sa tabi ng lumang riles.

### 3. Ang mga expressway

Umabot sa Biñan ang South Luzon Expressway noong huling bahagi ng dekada 1970, nang matapos ang karugtong nito mula Alabang hanggang Calamba, na sinimulan noong 1974. Dalawa sa mga labasan nito ang nasa Biñan, sa Southwoods (labasan 31) at sa Mamplasan (labasan 36); ang labasan ng Carmona (33), sa pagitan ng dalawa, ay nasa kabila lamang ng hangganan, sa Cavite, at patungo ito sa Biñan sa pamamagitan ng Kalye General Malvar. Mula noong Oktubre 30, 2019, nagsisimula sa rotonda ng Mamplasan, sa tabi ng labasan ng SLEX, ang **Cavite–Laguna Expressway** (CALAX); binuksan noong Agosto 18, 2020 sa Timbao ang isang interchange na naglilingkod sa Laguna Technopark, at mula noong 2026, 26 na kilometro na ang haba ng daan papasok sa Cavite, hanggang General Trias. Kapag walang trapiko, wala pang dalawampung minuto ang Alabang sa kotse, at mga kalahating oras ang Makati, ang paliparang pandaigdig at ang Tagaytay; kapag rush hour, madalas na doble o triple ang tagal ng parehong biyahe.

### 4. Dyip, bus at van

Karamihan sa mga tao ay nagbibiyahe sakay ng dyip at bus. Matagal nang negosyo ng mga taga-Biñan ang transportasyon: noong 1953, tatlong malalaking kompanya ang pinatatakbo nila, ang Biñan Transportation Company, ang Laguna Transportation Company at ang Sousa Transportation Co. Ngayon, bumibiyahe ang mga dyip sa pambansang daan patungong San Pedro, Alabang at Balibago sa Santa Rosa, at paakyat sa Kalye General Malvar patungong Carmona at General Mariano Alvarez sa Cavite. Ang pambansang programa ng modernisasyon na sinimulan noong 2017 ang nagtulak sa mga may-ari na sumapi sa mga kooperatiba at nagdala ng mga bago at mas malalaking sasakyan: kabilang ang linya ng modernong dyip na “Biñan – Balibago via Manila South Road” sa mga unang muling bumiyahe matapos ang lockdown, noong Hunyo 26, 2020. Ang pambansang tagapag-regula ang nagtatakda ng pamasahe: mula Setyembre 28, 2026, ₱14 ang pinakamababang pamasahe sa tradisyonal na dyip at ₱17 sa modernong dyip. Umaalis ang mga bus mula sa terminal malapit sa lumang kabayanan patungong Buendia at Ayala sa Makati sa pamamagitan ng expressway; noong 2022, pinaghatian ang rutang ito ng JAC Liner at LLI, na may 105 bus. May iba pang bus at van na nagsisimula sa Southwoods, at dumaraan din ang mga bus na panlalawigan na patungong timog.

### 5. Ang traysikel

Sa maiikling biyahe, traysikel ang sinasakyan ng Biñan, isang motorsiklong may nakakabit na sidecar na may bubong. Mula nang ipatupad ang Local Government Code ng 1991, ang lungsod, at hindi ang pambansang pamahalaan, ang nagbibigay ng prangkisa ng traysikel at nagreregula sa operasyon nito. Ayon sa mga pambansang alituntunin na sinipi ng Korte Suprema noong 2000, hindi maaaring bumiyahe ang traysikel sa mga pambansang lansangang dinaraanan ng malalaking sasakyan maliban kung walang ibang daan; maaaring bigyan ng iisang kulay ang mga traysikel ng bawat sona, at may numero ng pagkakakilanlan ang bawat isa; tatlong taon ang bisa ng permit. Sa Biñan, ang Biñan Tricycle Franchising and Regulatory Board ang naglalabas ng mga permit. Kasapi ang mga may-ari at tsuper sa mga TODA – mga samahan ng mga operator at tsuper ng traysikel – na may kani-kaniyang sona at terminal, at magkakasama sa isang pederasyon ng lungsod; noong Abril 2026, 5,489 na kasapi ng TODA ang tumanggap ng tulong sa gasolina mula sa lungsod. Dumating ang mga unang de-kuryenteng traysikel noong 2017, at pinahihintulutan ng isang ordinansa ng 2023 ang mga e-trike na tumawid sa malalaking daan ngunit hindi bumiyahe sa kahabaan nito.

Magbabago pang muli ang mapa. Inaasahang ibabalik ng North–South Commuter Railway ang tren sa mga 2028, at itatayo sa baybayin mula Taguig hanggang Calamba ang isang nakataas na daan, ang **Laguna Lakeshore Road Network**, na pinondohan noong 2024 ng Asian Development Bank at ng Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank; ang bahagi nito mula San Pedro hanggang Cabuyao, na may habang 12.23 kilometro, ay magkakaroon ng interchange para sa San Pedro at Biñan. Muling magdadala ng trapiko ang lawa, ang pinakamatandang lansangan ng Biñan – sa isang daang itinayo sa gilid nito.

**Did You Know?** **The first Filipino-made train began its life in Biñan.** On 24 April 2019 the Department of Science and Technology inaugurated its Hybrid Electric Train, designed and built by Filipino engineers, at Biñan station. Four years later, on 2 July 2023, the station closed to make way for the new commuter railway ([GMA News, 2019](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/scitech/technology/692256/dost-just-inaugurated-a-100-locally-made-train/story)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Sa Biñan nagsimula ang buhay ng unang tren na gawang Pilipino.** Noong Abril 24, 2019, pinasinayaan ng Kagawaran ng Agham at Teknolohiya sa istasyon ng Biñan ang Hybrid Electric Train nito, na dinisenyo at ginawa ng mga inhinyerong Pilipino. Makalipas ang apat na taon, noong Hulyo 2, 2023, isinara ang istasyon upang bigyang-daan ang bagong riles pang-commuter.

**Did You Know?** **Steamers to Biñan every day.** Before the railway came in 1908, the lake was Biñan’s highway. In 1870 the steamer *Talim* stopped at Biñan to pick up the young José Rizal; around 1900, steamers sailed daily from Manila to Biñan, Calamba and Santa Cruz. Today no passenger boat leaves the town ([Sawyer 1900 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/inhabitantsofphi00sawy_0)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Araw-araw na barko patungong Biñan.** Bago dumating ang riles noong 1908, ang lawa ang lansangan ng Biñan. Noong 1870, huminto sa Biñan ang barkong de-singaw na *Talim* upang sunduin ang batang si José Rizal; noong mga 1900, araw-araw na naglalayag ang mga barko mula Maynila patungong Biñan, Calamba at Santa Cruz. Ngayon, wala nang bangkang pampasaherong umaalis sa bayan.

**Did You Know?** **Why tricycles avoid the highway.** Tricycles are the only public vehicles licensed by the city itself. National rules keep them off the national highways used by heavy trucks, unless there is no other road, and each TODA serves its own zone ([LTO v. City of Butuan, 2000 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2000/jan2000/gr_131512_2000.html)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Kung bakit iniiwasan ng traysikel ang highway.** Ang traysikel ang tanging pampublikong sasakyang ang lungsod mismo ang nagbibigay ng lisensiya. Ipinagbabawal ng mga pambansang tuntunin ang traysikel sa mga pambansang lansangang dinaraanan ng malalaking trak, maliban kung walang ibang daan, at may sariling sona ang bawat TODA.

## Making a Living / Hanapbuhay

### 1. A factory city on old cane fields

Biñan today lives mainly from manufacturing for export. Roughly half of the 471-hectare Laguna Technopark lies inside the city: more than 230 hectares of it, in the barangays of Biñan, Loma, Timbao and Mamplasan, were proclaimed economic zones between 1997 and 2015 ([Proclamation No. 1052, 1997 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc1997/proc_1052_1997.html)) ([Proclamation No. 1147, 1998 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc1998/proc_1147_1998.html)) ([Laguna Technopark celebrates 30 years | Ayala Land Logistics, 2020](https://www.ayalalandlogistics.com/all-news/laguna-technopark-celebrates-30-years/)), and the Laguna International Industrial Park fills Mamplasan and Ganado. The largest employers make parts of the digital world: the sliders that carry the read-write heads of hard disks at Western Digital, disk-drive motors at Nidec, drives and solid-state memory at Toshiba, whose plant was founded in 1995 ([Toshiba Information Equipment (Philippines) | Toshiba](https://toshiba.semicon-storage.com/ap-en/company/about/omc/tip.html)), and electronic assemblies at Integrated Micro-Electronics, whose world headquarters is in the Technopark, at Amkor and at Cirtek. Car parts come next – Isuzu trucks and gearboxes, Honda parts, wiring harnesses at Furukawa, seat belts and airbags at Joyson – then medical goods, such as the syringes of Terumo ([Terumo to invest $45 million | Philstar, 2010](https://www.philstar.com/business/2010/07/08/590838/terumo-invest-45-million-five-years-expand-syringe-output)) and the medicines of Unilab’s Amherst plant, opened in 2007 ([GMA inaugurates Unilab’s Amherst plant | Philstar, 2007](https://philstar.com/business/2007/03/15/389572/gma-inaugurates-unilabs-p17-b-amherst-plant-laguna)), and bread: Gardenia has baked in the industrial park since the late 1990s and opened a new ₱1-billion plant there in 2016 ([Gardenia opens new P1-B bread plant | Philstar, 2016](https://www.philstar.com/business/2016/01/21/1544938/gardenia-opens-new-p1-b-bread-plant-laguna/amp/)). Not every famous name is in Biñan, however: the Honda and Mitsubishi car plants stand on the Santa Rosa side of the Technopark.

### 2. Market town, mall town

The old trade of the town lives on in the Poblacion, around the public market. The malls came with the subdivisions: Pavilion Mall at the end of the 1990s, Biñan Central Mall in 2011 ([History | City of Biñan](https://www.binan.gov.ph/history/)) and Southwoods Mall, a ₱2-billion building of 58,000 square metres opened in September 2017 beside new office towers ([Southwoods Mall grand opening | City of Biñan, 2017](https://www.binan.gov.ph/southwoods-mall-grand-opening/)). In 2021 the city counted 7,524 registered businesses, up from 7,013 a year before; yet, in the mayor’s words, “our top 100 taxpayers contribute 95 percent of all the taxes” ([Biñan City promotes inclusive economic growth | OpinYon, 2022](https://opinyon.net/laguna/binan-city-promotes-inclusive-economic-growth)). The city’s regular income rose from ₱607 million in 2009 to ₱1.53 billion in 2016 ([Biñan | PhilAtlas, after the Bureau of Local Government Finance](https://www.philatlas.com/luzon/r04a/laguna/binan.html)). In the national competitiveness index, Biñan climbed from 136th place among the country’s component cities in 2014 to 7th in 2024, and it has ranked first for economic dynamism every year since 2022 – but only 40th for infrastructure ([Cities and Municipalities Competitiveness Index | DTI](https://cmci.dti.gov.ph/lgu-profile.php?lgu=Binan&year=2024)).

### 3. The farms that disappeared

In 1979 most of Biñan was still farmland; twenty years later less than a tenth was, according to a study by Southern Luzon State University ([Land conversion: a threat to Laguna’s agri sector? | OpinYon, 2022](https://opinyon.net/laguna/land-conversion-a-threat-to-laguna-s-agri-sector)). The farm census of 2012 counted only 118 farms on 135 hectares in the whole city – about 3 per cent of its land ([Census of Agriculture and Fisheries 2012 | PSA](https://openstat.psa.gov.ph/PXWeb/api/v1/en/DB/2E/AFC/0332E6CNAF1.px)). Pockets of rice survive in Langkiwa, Timbao, Bungahan, Platero, Santo Tomas and Malaban, some of them still watered by the Spanish-era dam of Timbao ([About Biñan | Experience Biñan](https://experiencebinan.com/about-binan/)). The memory of the fields lives on in the *pinipig* still pounded in Canlalay, and in a few new ventures, such as the organic farm of De La Paz where the city trained its first 25 organic farmers in 2017 ([25 Biñanenses graduate in organic farming | City of Biñan](https://www.binan.gov.ph/25-binanenses-graduate-organic-farming/)).

### 4. Pigs before the court

Large livestock left the town in a more dramatic way. In November 2004 the town council ordered the gradual phase-out of every farm with more than ten pigs or five hundred birds, after residents of the new subdivisions had complained of the smell. Two large hog farms, Holiday Hills and Domino Farms, went to court, and the case reached the Supreme Court, which on 10 October 2022 declared the ordinance “NOT UNCONSTITUTIONAL” ([Municipality of Biñan v. Holiday Hills, 2022 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2022/oct2022/gr_200403_2022.html)). In January 2025 the mayor announced that the two farms, in Langkiwa and Santo Tomas, no longer had permits ([Biñan City LGU to finally close 2 erring hog yards | OpinYon, 2025](https://opinyon.net/national/binan-city-lgu-to-finally-close-2-erring-hog-yards)). Meanwhile African swine fever struck the whole province: the number of pigs in Laguna fell from 194,394 on 1 January 2020 to 60,258 a year later, and it was back to 130,206 in 2026 ([Swine inventory by province | PSA](https://openstat.psa.gov.ph/PXWeb/api/v1/en/DB/2E/LP/INV/NEW/0032E4FINS0.px)). These figures include the pigs raised in backyards; how many are still kept in Biñan’s own yards, near the lake or elsewhere, has not been published.

### 5. Life on the lakeshore

Only two barangays, Malaban and De La Paz, touch Laguna de Bay, along about four kilometres of shore, and their people have long lived from fishing and from shoemaking. In 2006 the lake authority registered 26 fish-pen operators on 650 hectares and 76 fish-cage operators on 35 hectares off Biñan – 102 in all, more than in any other town of the province; most pens raised milkfish, *bangus*, and almost all cages carp ([Israel, Fishpen and Fishcage Culture in Laguna de Bay, 2008 | PIDS](https://pidswebs.pids.gov.ph/CDN/PUBLICATIONS/pidspjd08-1fishpen.pdf)). Since 2018 the pens and cages of the whole lake have been limited to 9,200 hectares, with priority for registered local fishers ([LLDA Board Resolution No. 540, 2018 | UP Law Center](https://law.upd.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/LLDA-BR-No-540-Series-of-2018.pdf)). The city swore in 25 deputy fish wardens in 2018 ([Mga bagong deputadong fish warden nanumpa | City of Biñan, 2018](https://www.binan.gov.ph/mga-bagong-deputadong-fish-warden-nanumpa/)). But the fishers face polluted rivers, water hyacinth and floods – and now the Laguna Lakeshore Road Network, whose builders expect economic impacts on the fish pens and fish cages of San Pedro, Biñan, Santa Rosa and Cabuyao ([Laguna Lakeshore Road Network Phase 1 | AIIB](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2024/approved/Philippines-Laguna-Lakeshore-Road-Network-Phase-1-Project.html)).

### 1. Isang lungsod ng pabrika sa dating tubuhan

Pangunahing ikinabubuhay ng Biñan ngayon ang pagmamanupaktura para sa pagluluwas. Halos kalahati ng 471-ektaryang Laguna Technopark ay nasa loob ng lungsod: mahigit 230 ektarya nito, sa mga barangay ng Biñan, Loma, Timbao at Mamplasan, ang ipinahayag na mga sonang pang-ekonomiya mula 1997 hanggang 2015, at sakop naman ng Laguna International Industrial Park ang Mamplasan at Ganado. Ang pinakamalalaking employer ay gumagawa ng mga bahagi ng mundong digital: ang mga *slider* na may dalang read-write head ng mga hard disk sa Western Digital, ang mga motor ng disk drive sa Nidec, ang mga drive at solid-state memory sa Toshiba, na ang pabrika ay itinatag noong 1995, at ang mga elektronikong asembliya sa Integrated Micro-Electronics, na may pandaigdigang punong-tanggapan sa Technopark, sa Amkor at sa Cirtek. Kasunod ang mga piyesa ng sasakyan – mga trak at gearbox ng Isuzu, mga piyesa ng Honda, mga wiring harness sa Furukawa, mga seat belt at airbag sa Joyson – saka ang mga produktong medikal, gaya ng mga heringgilya ng Terumo at ng mga gamot ng planta ng Amherst ng Unilab, na binuksan noong 2007, at ang tinapay: nagluluto ang Gardenia sa industrial park mula pa noong huling bahagi ng dekada 1990, at nagbukas ito roon ng isang bagong planta na nagkakahalaga ng ₱1 bilyon noong 2016. Ngunit hindi lahat ng kilalang pangalan ay nasa Biñan: nasa panig ng Santa Rosa ng Technopark ang mga planta ng kotse ng Honda at Mitsubishi.

### 2. Bayan ng pamilihan, bayan ng mall

Buhay pa rin sa Poblacion, sa paligid ng pampublikong pamilihan, ang lumang kalakalan ng bayan. Dumating ang mga mall kasabay ng mga subdibisyon: ang Pavilion Mall sa pagtatapos ng dekada 1990, ang Biñan Central Mall noong 2011, at ang Southwoods Mall, isang gusaling nagkakahalaga ng ₱2 bilyon at may lawak na 58,000 metro kuwadrado, na binuksan noong Setyembre 2017 sa tabi ng mga bagong tore ng opisina. Noong 2021, 7,524 na rehistradong negosyo ang nabilang ng lungsod, mula sa 7,013 noong nakaraang taon; gayunman, ayon sa alkalde, “ang aming nangungunang 100 nagbabayad ng buwis ang nag-aambag ng 95 porsiyento ng lahat ng buwis”. Tumaas ang regular na kita ng lungsod mula ₱607 milyon noong 2009 hanggang ₱1.53 bilyon noong 2016. Sa pambansang indeks ng kakayahang makipagkompetensiya, umakyat ang Biñan mula sa ika-136 na puwesto sa mga component city ng bansa noong 2014 hanggang ikapito noong 2024, at una ito sa *economic dynamism* taon-taon mula 2022 – ngunit ika-40 lamang sa imprastraktura.

### 3. Ang mga bukid na naglaho

Noong 1979, bukirin pa ang malaking bahagi ng Biñan; makalipas ang dalawampung taon, wala pang ikasampung bahagi nito ang bukid, ayon sa isang pag-aaral ng Southern Luzon State University. Nabilang ng senso ng agrikultura noong 2012 ang 118 sakahan lamang sa 135 ektarya sa buong lungsod – mga 3 porsiyento ng lupain nito. May natitira pang mga palayan sa Langkiwa, Timbao, Bungahan, Platero, Santo Tomas at Malaban, at ang ilan ay pinatutubigan pa ng prinsa ng Timbao mula sa panahon ng Espanyol. Buhay pa ang alaala ng mga bukid sa *pinipig* na binabayo pa rin sa Canlalay, at sa ilang bagong gawain, gaya ng organikong bukid sa De La Paz kung saan sinanay ng lungsod ang una nitong 25 organikong magsasaka noong 2017.

### 4. Mga baboy sa harap ng hukuman

Mas madula ang pag-alis ng malalaking babuyan sa bayan. Noong Nobyembre 2004, iniutos ng konseho ng bayan ang unti-unting pagsasara ng bawat sakahang may mahigit sampung baboy o limandaang ibon, matapos magreklamo sa amoy ang mga residente ng mga bagong subdibisyon. Nagsampa ng kaso ang dalawang malaking babuyan, ang Holiday Hills at ang Domino Farms, at umabot ang usapin sa Korte Suprema, na noong Oktubre 10, 2022 ay nagpasyang “HINDI LABAG SA KONSTITUSYON” ang ordinansa. Noong Enero 2025, ipinahayag ng alkalde na wala nang permit ang dalawang sakahan, na nasa Langkiwa at Santo Tomas. Samantala, tinamaan ng African swine fever ang buong lalawigan: bumagsak ang bilang ng baboy sa Laguna mula 194,394 noong Enero 1, 2020 hanggang 60,258 makalipas ang isang taon, at umabot muli sa 130,206 noong 2026. Kasama sa mga bilang na ito ang mga baboy na inaalagaan sa bakuran; hindi pa nailalathala kung ilan pa ang inaalagaan sa mga bakuran ng Biñan mismo, malapit man sa lawa o saanman.

### 5. Buhay sa baybayin ng lawa

Dalawang barangay lamang, ang Malaban at ang De La Paz, ang nakadikit sa Laguna de Bay, sa kahabaan ng mga apat na kilometrong baybayin, at matagal nang nabubuhay ang kanilang mga tao sa pangingisda at paggawa ng sapatos. Noong 2006, nagtala ang awtoridad ng lawa ng 26 na operator ng baklad sa 650 ektarya at 76 na operator ng hawla ng isda sa 35 ektarya sa harap ng Biñan – 102 lahat, higit kaysa sa alinmang bayan sa lalawigan; bangus ang inaalagaan sa karamihan ng baklad, at karpa naman sa halos lahat ng hawla. Mula noong 2018, limitado na sa 9,200 ektarya ang mga baklad at hawla sa buong lawa, at inuuna ang mga rehistradong lokal na mangingisda. Nanumpa noong 2018 ang 25 deputadong fish warden ng lungsod. Ngunit humaharap ang mga mangingisda sa maruruming ilog, sa water lily at sa mga pagbaha – at ngayon sa Laguna Lakeshore Road Network, na inaasahan ng mga nagtatayo na makaaapekto sa kabuhayan ng mga baklad at hawla ng isda sa San Pedro, Biñan, Santa Rosa at Cabuyao.

**Did You Know?** **Pigs before the Supreme Court.** In 2004 Biñan’s council voted to phase out every farm with more than ten pigs or five hundred birds. Two big hog farms sued, and the case went all the way to the Supreme Court, which upheld the ordinance on 10 October 2022 ([G.R. No. 200403, 2022 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2022/oct2022/gr_200403_2022.html)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Mga baboy sa harap ng Korte Suprema.** Noong 2004, bumoto ang konseho ng Biñan na unti-unting isara ang bawat sakahang may mahigit sampung baboy o limandaang ibon. Nagdemanda ang dalawang malaking babuyan, at umabot ang kaso hanggang sa Korte Suprema, na nagpatibay sa ordinansa noong Oktubre 10, 2022.

**Did You Know?** **Milkfish and carp off Malaban.** In 2006, 102 registered fish-pen and fish-cage operators worked 686 hectares of Laguna de Bay off Biñan – more operators than in any other town of Laguna province ([PIDS 2008](https://pidswebs.pids.gov.ph/CDN/PUBLICATIONS/pidspjd08-1fishpen.pdf)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Bangus at karpa sa harap ng Malaban.** Noong 2006, 102 rehistradong operator ng baklad at hawla ng isda ang gumagamit ng 686 na ektarya ng Laguna de Bay sa harap ng Biñan – mas marami kaysa sa alinmang bayan sa lalawigan ng Laguna.

**Did You Know?** **From 87 per cent farmland to 3 per cent.** In 1979, 86.9 per cent of Biñan was farmland. By 1999 it was 9 per cent, and the 2012 farm census found just 118 farms on 135 hectares – about 3 per cent of the city ([OpinYon, 2022](https://opinyon.net/laguna/land-conversion-a-threat-to-laguna-s-agri-sector)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Mula 87 porsiyentong bukirin hanggang 3 porsiyento.** Noong 1979, 86.9 porsiyento ng Biñan ang bukirin. Pagsapit ng 1999, 9 na porsiyento na lamang ito, at noong 2012, 118 sakahan lamang sa 135 ektarya ang nabilang ng senso ng agrikultura – mga 3 porsiyento ng lungsod.

## Who Owns the Land? / Kanino ang Lupa?

### 1. One owner, then three thousand

For two and a half centuries, almost all the land of Biñan had a single owner: the Dominican College of Santo Tomás, whose estate covered some 3,474 hectares, most of it irrigated rice land with a little sugarcane ([Report of the Philippine Commission, 1904 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/1904-vol-xi-p-1-report-of-the-united-states-philippine-c)). After the American government bought the friar estates in 1903, its surveyors measured every field of the “Biñan Estate” between January 1906 and February 1907 – a survey still cited in land titles today ([Proclamation No. 1143, 2006 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2006/proc_1143_2006.html)). When the land went on sale in May 1910, 2,500 of its 3,200 parcels were sold in eight days, and the director of lands told the United States Congress that “nearly all of the purchasers were former tenants” ([Administration of Philippine Lands, 1911 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/afr0955.0001.001.umich.edu)). Only a few dozen people leased more than 16 hectares; among them were members of the Gana, Yatco and Ocampo families and an American settler, Frank J. Ferguson, with about 142 hectares.

### 2. Rice, tenants and sugar

For most of the twentieth century the rice fields of the lowlands stayed in the hands of local owners and were tilled by sharecroppers, the *kasamá*, while the drier uplands to the west became sugarcane fields. Court records keep traces of this world: a 4.5-hectare rice field in Zapote worked by a sharecropper in the 1960s ([Jacinto v. Court of Appeals, 1978 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1978/dec1978/gr_33567_1978.html)), or the rice land of Luis Alberto in Langkiwa, whose workers were found in 2001 to be paid labourers rather than tenants ([Bawalan v. Alberto, Court of Appeals, 2001 | DAR](http://www.lis.dar.gov.ph/documents/1958)). The land reform laws of 1963, 1972 and 1988 reached Biñan, but the disputes they raised show how hard it was for tillers to become owners. In Loma, the tenants of some 37 hectares won: in 2000 the Supreme Court refused to overturn their recognition as legitimate beneficiaries of agrarian reform ([Greenfield Realty v. Cardama, 2000 | DAR](http://www.lis.dar.gov.ph/documents/4007)). In Langkiwa and Timbao, 43.7 hectares owned by Gonzalo Puyat & Sons were placed under agrarian reform in 1998, then defended by their owner as industrial land; in 2012 the Supreme Court sent the question back to the Department of Agrarian Reform ([Gonzalo Puyat & Sons v. Alcaide, 2012 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2012/feb2012/gr_167952_2012.html)).

### 3. From cane fields to factories and townships

Within one generation the uplands became one of the country’s great industrial belts. In March 1989 surveyors redrew the old lots into large blocks ([Proclamation No. 1143, 2006 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2006/proc_1143_2006.html)), and the Laguna Technopark opened the next year; the Laguna International Industrial Park followed in 1992, then Southwoods City on the border with Carmona and, after 2015, large planned communities around Malamig. No public register lists the largest landowners of Biñan. Among the largest documented holdings are those of a handful of developers – Ayala Land, Megaworld, Samsung and Solid, Filinvest, Federal Land and ArthaLand – and of De La Salle University, whose 50-hectare campus includes an economic zone proclaimed in 2025 ([Global-Estate Resorts, Annual Report 2020](https://geri.com.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/GERI-2020-Annual-Report-April-26-2021.pdf)) ([Broadfield | Ayala Land](https://ayalaland.com/estates/broadfield)) ([Meadowcrest | Federal Land](https://federalland.ph/?p=8911)) ([Proclamation No. 985, 2025 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_985_2025.html)). Inside the industrial parks, the lots are sold on to hundreds of companies.

### 4. The land nobody can buy

The State still owns two strips of Biñan. By law, the bed of Laguna de Bay includes every piece of shore at or below 12.5 metres on the lake gauge: a band of public land along Malaban and De La Paz ([Presidential Decree No. 813, 1975 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1975/pd_813_1975.html)). The other is the railway, now being rebuilt for the North–South Commuter Railway; the families who lived along it are being moved to The Meadows in Langkiwa, where 2,030 homes are planned ([DOTr turns over first housing units to NSCR families | GMA News, 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/975376/dotr-turns-over-first-housing-units-nscr-families/story)). Meanwhile the price of land has soared: about ₱164 per hectare around 1909 ([Administration of Philippine Lands, 1911 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/afr0955.0001.001.umich.edu)), ₱30 per square metre for a rice field in Langkiwa in 1989 ([Delos Santos v. De Mata, Court of Appeals, 1997 | DAR](http://www.lis.dar.gov.ph/documents/2675)), and about ₱3,800 per square metre in 2016, when a developer paid ₱307 million for 8.1 hectares ([ArthaLand acquires Laguna lot for P307 M | Philstar, 2016](https://philstar.com/business/2016/11/03/1638994/arthaland-acquires-laguna-lot-p307-m)). The land that some three thousand tenant families bought in 1910 for less than two centavos a square metre has become some of the most valuable in the province.

### 1. Isang may-ari, saka tatlong libo

Sa loob ng dalawa’t kalahating siglo, halos lahat ng lupain ng Biñan ay may iisang may-ari: ang Kolehiyo ng Santo Tomás ng mga Dominiko, na ang asyenda ay sumasakop sa mga 3,474 na ektarya, karamihan ay palayang may patubig at kaunting tubuhan. Matapos bilhin ng pamahalaang Amerikano ang mga asyenda ng mga prayle noong 1903, sinukat ng mga agrimensor nito ang bawat bukid ng “Biñan Estate” mula Enero 1906 hanggang Pebrero 1907 – isang pagsukat na binabanggit pa rin sa mga titulo ng lupa ngayon. Nang ipagbili ang lupa noong Mayo 1910, 2,500 sa 3,200 lote nito ang naipagbili sa loob ng walong araw, at sinabi ng direktor ng lupa sa Kongreso ng Estados Unidos na “halos lahat ng bumili ay dating mga umuupa sa lupa”. Iilang dosenang tao lamang ang umupa ng mahigit 16 na ektarya; kabilang sa kanila ang ilang kasapi ng mga pamilyang Gana, Yatco at Ocampo at isang Amerikanong nanirahan dito, si Frank J. Ferguson, na may mga 142 ektarya.

### 2. Palay, kasama at tubo

Sa halos buong ikadalawampung siglo, nanatili sa kamay ng mga lokal na may-ari ang mga palayan sa kapatagan at sinasaka ng mga *kasamá*, habang naging tubuhan naman ang mas tuyong kabundukan sa kanluran. May bakas ng mundong ito sa mga talaan ng hukuman: isang palayang may 4.5 ektarya sa Zapote na sinasaka ng isang kasama noong dekada 1960, o ang palayan ni Luis Alberto sa Langkiwa, na ang mga manggagawa ay itinuring noong 2001 na mga bayarang trabahador at hindi mga kasama. Umabot sa Biñan ang mga batas sa repormang agraryo ng 1963, 1972 at 1988, ngunit ipinakikita ng mga usaping idinulot nito kung gaano kahirap para sa magsasaka ang maging may-ari. Sa Loma, nagwagi ang mga kasama sa mga 37 ektarya: noong 2000, tumanggi ang Korte Suprema na baligtarin ang pagkilala sa kanila bilang mga lehitimong benepisyaryo ng repormang agraryo. Sa Langkiwa at Timbao, isinailalim sa repormang agraryo noong 1998 ang 43.7 ektaryang pag-aari ng Gonzalo Puyat & Sons, na ipinagtanggol naman ng may-ari bilang lupang pang-industriya; noong 2012, ibinalik ng Korte Suprema ang usapin sa Kagawaran ng Repormang Agraryo.

### 3. Mula sa tubuhan hanggang sa pabrika at bagong bayan

Sa loob ng isang henerasyon, naging isa sa malalaking sinturon ng industriya sa bansa ang kabundukan. Noong Marso 1989, muling iginuhit ng mga agrimensor ang mga lumang lote bilang malalaking bloke, at binuksan ang Laguna Technopark nang sumunod na taon; sumunod ang Laguna International Industrial Park noong 1992, saka ang Southwoods City sa hangganan ng Carmona at, matapos ang 2015, ang malalaking planadong pamayanan sa paligid ng Malamig. Walang pampublikong talaan na naglilista ng pinakamalalaking may-ari ng lupa sa Biñan. Kabilang sa pinakamalalaking naitalang lupain ang sa iilang developer – Ayala Land, Megaworld, Samsung at Solid, Filinvest, Federal Land at ArthaLand – at ang sa De La Salle University, na ang 50-ektaryang kampus ay may sonang pang-ekonomiya na ipinahayag noong 2025. ### 4. Ang lupang hindi mabibili

May dalawang piraso pa rin ng Biñan na pag-aari ng Estado. Ayon sa batas, kasama sa ilalim ng Laguna de Bay ang bawat bahagi ng baybayin na nasa 12.5 metro o mas mababa sa panukat ng lawa: isang guhit ng pampublikong lupa sa kahabaan ng Malaban at De La Paz. Ang isa pa ay ang riles, na muling itinatayo ngayon para sa North–South Commuter Railway; inililipat sa The Meadows sa Langkiwa, kung saan 2,030 tahanan ang nakaplano, ang mga pamilyang nakatira sa tabi nito. Samantala, lubhang tumaas ang presyo ng lupa: mga ₱164 bawat ektarya noong mga 1909, ₱30 bawat metro kuwadrado para sa isang palayan sa Langkiwa noong 1989, at mga ₱3,800 bawat metro kuwadrado noong 2016, nang magbayad ang isang developer ng ₱307 milyon para sa 8.1 ektarya. Ang lupang binili noong 1910 ng mga tatlong libong pamilya ng kasama sa halagang wala pang dalawang sentimo bawat metro kuwadrado ay isa na ngayon sa pinakamahahalaga sa lalawigan.

**Did You Know?** **Your land title still remembers the friars.** Even the newest factory and mall titles in Biñan describe their lots as portions of the “Biñan Estate”, measured by an “original survey, January 1906 – February 1907”: the survey made by the American government right after it bought the Dominicans’ hacienda ([Proclamation No. 1603, 2008 | Lawphil](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2008/proc_1603_2008.html)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Naaalala pa ng titulo ng iyong lupa ang mga prayle.** Kahit ang pinakabagong titulo ng mga pabrika at mall sa Biñan ay naglalarawan sa kanilang lote bilang bahagi ng “Biñan Estate”, na sinukat sa isang “original survey, January 1906 – February 1907”: ang pagsukat na ginawa ng pamahalaang Amerikano matapos nitong bilhin ang asyenda ng mga Dominiko.

**Did You Know?** **The American who vanished with 1,600 hectares.** One of the first leases on the old estate went to an American, A. F. Thayer, for 4,035 acres – but the director of lands reported in 1911 that Thayer “proved to be either a myth or a fraud … he has absconded” ([Administration of Philippine Lands, 1911 | Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/afr0955.0001.001.umich.edu)).

**Alam Mo Ba?** **Ang Amerikanong naglaho kasama ang 1,600 ektarya.** Isa sa mga unang upa sa lumang asyenda ang napunta sa isang Amerikano, si A. F. Thayer, para sa 4,035 akre – ngunit iniulat ng direktor ng lupa noong 1911 na si Thayer ay “isa palang alamat o manloloko … at tumakas na siya”.
