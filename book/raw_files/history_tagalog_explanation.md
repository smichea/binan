# History of Biñan City / Kasaysayan ng Lungsod ng Biñan

Explanation of tagalog text for english only speakers

---
## Geography / Heograpiya

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. Between the lake and the plateau**

> *Ang Biñán ay sumasakop sa isang makitid na lupain na humigit-kumulang 43.5 kilometro kuwadrado sa kanlurang pampang ng Lawa ng Laguna.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sumasakop** – *occupies, covers* (root: *sakop* = scope, territory)
- **makitid** – *narrow* (root: *kitid*)
- **lupain** – *land, territory* (root: *lupa* = soil, land)
- **humigit-kumulang** – *more or less, approximately* (from *higit* = more and *kulang* = less)
- **pampang** – *shore, bank*; **baybayin** – *coast, shoreline*
- **dahan-dahang tumataas** – *rises gently* (*dahan-dahan* = slowly; root of the verb: *taas* = height)
- **talampas** – *plateau*
- **hangganan** – *boundary* (root: *hangga* = limit)
- **maaliwalas** – *clear, bright (weather)*
- **abot-tanaw** – *horizon* (literally "reach of sight": *abot* + *tanaw*)
- **gilid** – *edge, rim*; **nagtatago** – *hides* (root: *tago*)

**Grammar:**
- *Sumasakop* = *-um-* + repeated syllable + *sakop*: ongoing actor focus.
- *Humigit-kumulang* is a fixed pair of *-um-* verbs used as an adverb: "going over, going under" = "about".
- *Kanlurang pampang* – the linker *-ng* joins the adjective *kanluran* (western) to the noun.

**Translation:**
> Biñán occupies a narrow strip of land of about 43.5 square kilometres on the western shore of Laguna de Bay, some 35 kilometres south of Manila. From the lakeshore, barely 2 metres above sea level, the land rises gently westward toward the plateau of Carmona and Silang; the Poblacion stands at about 12 metres. On a clear day, volcanoes close the horizon: Mount Makiling, Mount Banahaw behind it, and to the southwest the Tagaytay ridge, the rim of the caldera that hides Taal Volcano, only 39 km away.

---

**2. Two plates**

> *Ang tanawing ito ay hinubog ng isa sa pinakamagulong bahagi ng mundo.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **tanawin** – *landscape, view* (root: *tanaw* = to look at from afar)
- **hinubog** – *was shaped, was moulded* (root: *hubog* = shape)
- **pinakamagulo** – *most turbulent, most restless* (root: *gulo* = disorder)
- **kapuluan** – *archipelago* (root: *pulo* = island)
- **naiipit** – *is squeezed, is caught* (root: *ipit* = pinch)
- **nagkakalapit** – *come closer to each other* (root: *lapit* = near)
- **sumusuot** – *slips under, goes under* (root: *suot* = to slip into)
- **pahalang** – *horizontal, sideways*
- **sinasalo** – *is caught, is absorbed* (root: *salo* = to catch)
- **banggaan** – *collision* (root: *bangga* = to bump)

**Grammar:**
- *Hinubog* = *-in-* + *hubog*: object focus, completed – "the landscape was shaped by…". The doer comes after *ng*.
- *Pinaka-* + *magulo* = superlative "most restless".
- *Naiipit* = *na-* + *i* + *ipit*: an involuntary, ongoing state – "is (being) caught".
- *Nagkakalapit* = *nag-ka-* + repeated syllable + *lapit*: a reciprocal action in progress, "getting closer to each other".

**Translation:**
> This landscape was shaped by one of the most restless parts of the world. The Philippine archipelago is made of crustal pieces caught between two great tectonic plates, the Philippine Sea Plate to the east and the Sunda (Eurasian) Plate to the west, which come closer to each other by several centimetres a year. Each plate slips under the islands in its own deep trench, while the sideways part of the movement is absorbed by the Philippine Fault. Because of this collision, the country is on the Pacific "Ring of Fire".

---

**3. Born of fire**

> *Ang gitnang bahagi mismo ng lawa ay isang kaldera ng bulkan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sinturon** – *belt*
- **nababanat** – *is stretched* (root: *banat* = to stretch)
- **umaakyat** – *rises, climbs* (root: *akyat*)
- **tangway** – *peninsula*
- **pagsabog** – *eruption, explosion* (root: *sabog* = to burst)
- **nagbabagang agos** – *glowing flow* (root: *baga* = ember)
- **patong** – *layer*
- **kinatatayuan** – *the place where (something) stands* (root: *tayo* = to stand)
- **namamatay** – *dies out* (root: *patay*)
- **bukal** – *spring (of water)*; **mainit** – *hot*
- **pumutok** – *erupted, burst* (root: *putok*)
- **sinuspinde** – *were suspended* (from Spanish *suspender*)

**Grammar:**
- *Kinatatayuan* = *ka-* + *-in-* + repeated syllable + *tayo* + *-an*: a noun of place, "the very spot where it stands".
- *Hindi pa namamatay ang apoy* – "the fire has not yet died out": *hindi pa* + ongoing aspect = "not yet".
- *Umulan ng abo* – "it rained ash": the weather verb *umulan* can take *ng* + what falls.

**Translation:**
> Biñán is at the northern end of the Macolod Corridor, a belt of volcanoes where the crust is stretched and magma rises easily. The central part of the lake itself is a volcanic caldera, left by great eruptions between about two million and thirty thousand years ago. The ash and glowing flows of those eruptions built the layers of tuff on which western Biñán stands. The fire has not yet died out: hot springs still bubble at Los Baños and Calamba, and when Taal erupted on 12 January 2020, ash rained on Biñán and classes were suspended.

---

**4. The fault**

> *May fault din sa ilalim ng lungsod.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sa ilalim ng** – *under, beneath*
- **dumaraan** – *passes through* (root: *daan* = way)
- **magdulot** – *to cause, to bring about* (root: *dulot*)
- **lakas** – *strength, magnitude*
- **niyanig** – *was shaken* (root: *yanig* = tremor)
- **mapanira** – *destructive* (root: *sira* = broken)
- **naghahanda** – *is preparing* (root: *handa* = ready)
- **ginanap** – *was held* (root: *ganap* = to take place)

**Grammar:**
- *Niyanig* = *-in-* + *yanig*; before a *y*, the infix often appears as *ni-*: "was shaken".
- *Mapanira* = *mapang-* + *sira* (s → n): an adjective describing a tendency, "destructive".
- *Ginanap* = *-in-* + *ganap*: object focus, completed – "the drill was held".

**Translation:**
> There is also a fault beneath the city. The West Valley Fault, about 100 km long, passes through Biñán in the barangays of Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) and Biñán. It can cause an earthquake of magnitude 7.2 and moves roughly every 400 to 600 years; its last strong movement was in 1658. The region has been shaken many times since: according to the old catalogue of earthquakes, on 20 July 1880 an earthquake "of destructive violence" struck "the towns around Lake Bay, especially to its south and west". Today most earthquakes felt in Biñán are weak, but the city prepares for the "Big One": on 28 September 2016 it hosted the national earthquake drill.

---

**5. The lake**

> *Iisa lamang ang labasan nito, ang Ilog Pasig.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **napakababaw** – *very shallow* (root: *babaw*)
- **lalim** – *depth*
- **palanggana** – *basin* (literally "washbasin")
- **labasan** – *outlet, exit* (root: *labas* = outside)
- **tag-araw** – *dry season*; **tag-ulan** – *rainy season*
- **pabalik** – *backward, returning* (root: *balik*)
- **pinalulubog** – *makes (something) sink* (root: *lubog*)
- **luminaw** – *became clear* (root: *linaw*)
- **lansangan** – *road, street*
- **hapag-kainan** – *dining table* (here: *pantry, source of food*)
- **huli** – *catch (of fish)*
- **baklad** – *fish pen*; **kulungan** – *cage*
- **binabantayan** – *monitored, watched* (root: *bantay* = guard)

**Grammar:**
- *Iisa* = repeated *isa*: "only one".
- *Napaka-* + adjective = "very": *napakababaw* = "very shallow".
- *Pinalulubog* = causative *pa-* + *lubog* in the ongoing aspect with *-in-*: "is made to sink".

**Translation:**
> Laguna de Bay is the largest lake in the Philippines but very shallow. Twenty-one rivers flow into it, and it has only one outlet, the Pasig River. In the dry season, when the lake falls to sea level, the Pasig flows backward and seawater enters the lake; the salt makes the mud sink, and the water turns clear for a few months. For centuries the lake was Biñán's other road and its pantry: in the early 1960s, 95 per cent of the catch was three native fish – ayungin, biyang puti and kanduli – now replaced by tilapia and bangus raised in pens and cages. The Biñán River is one of the lake's monitored tributaries – and it often lacks oxygen because of the dirty water it carries.

---

**6. When the lake was the sea**

> *Mga 6,600 hanggang 4,700 taon na ang nakararaan, bahagi ng dagat ang lawa.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sa paglipas ng panahon** – *over time* (root: *lipas* = to pass)
- **panahon ng yelo** – *ice age* (*yelo* = ice)
- **tuyong lupa** – *dry land*
- **natunaw** – *melted* (root: *tunaw*)
- **lumampas** – *went beyond* (root: *lampas*)
- **hinukay** – *was dug, was drilled* (root: *hukay* = to dig)
- **maalat** – *salty*; **bahagyang maalat** – *slightly salty, brackish*; **tubig-tabang** – *fresh water*
- **umatras** – *retreated* (root: *atras*)
- **iniangat** – *was raised* (root: *angat* = lift)

**Grammar:**
- *Ang nakararaan* – "(the time) that has passed": *naka-* + repeated syllable + *raan* (*daan*, with d → r). Used in "… taon na ang nakararaan" = "… years ago".
- *Iniangat ang lupa … ng paggalaw ng fault* – object focus: the land (*ang lupa*) was raised by (*ng*) the movement of the fault.

**Translation:**
> The shape of this shore has changed over time. In the ice ages, the sea rose and fell by more than a hundred metres; 20,000 years ago it was about 120 metres lower than today, but Luzon remained an island. When the ice melted, the sea came back and went beyond where it is now. A mud core drilled in the West Bay shows that about 6,600 to 4,700 years ago the lake was part of the sea, a salty lagoon connected to Manila Bay. It became brackish about 4,100 years ago and fresh about 3,000 years ago, when the sea retreated and the land between Muntinlupa and Parañaque was raised by the movement of the West Valley Fault itself.

---

**7. Seasons and typhoons**

> *Dumaraan ang mga bagyo sa rehiyon mula Hunyo hanggang Disyembre.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **habagat** – *southwest monsoon*
- **bagyo** – *typhoon*
- **pinakamatindi** – *the most severe* (root: *tindi* = intensity)
- **sa alaala ng mga tao** – *in living memory* (*alaala* = memory)
- **nagpaangat** – *caused to rise* (root: *angat*)
- **panukat** – *gauge, measuring instrument* (root: *sukat* = measure)
- **nagpalubog** – *submerged* (root: *lubog*)
- **humupa** – *to subside* (root: *hupa*)
- **naranasan** – *experienced* (root: *danas*, d → r)

**Grammar:**
- *Nagpa-* + root = causative actor focus, completed: *nagpaangat* (made rise), *nagpalubog* (made sink).
- *Naranasan* = *na-* + *danas* + *-an* (d → r): "was experienced".

**Translation:**
> Today Biñán's climate is tropical with two clear seasons: the dry season from November to April and the wet season from May to October, when the habagat brings about 90 per cent of the year's rain. At Sangley Point the normal yearly rainfall is about 2,080 mm. The most severe typhoon in living memory was Ondoy in September 2009, which raised the lake to about 14 metres on its gauge and submerged 192 lakeshore barangays for weeks. Lakeshore towns experienced it again in 2020 with Ulysses and in October 2024 with Kristine.

**8. A changing climate**

> *Nagbabago rin ang klima.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nagbabago** – *is changing* (root: *bago* = new)
- **pag-init** – *warming* (root: *init* = heat)
- **naitala** – *was recorded* (root: *tala* = record, note)
- **rekonstruksiyon** – *reconstruction*
- **inaasahan** – *is expected* (root: *asa* = hope, expectation)
- **ramdam** – *felt*
- **lumulubog** – *is sinking* (root: *lubog*)
- **pagsipsip** – *the sucking up, pumping* (root: *sipsip* = to suck)
- **panganib** – *danger, threat*
- **nagpapatagal** – *makes (something) last longer* (root: *tagal* = duration)

**Grammar:**
- *Naitala* = *na-* + *i-* + *tala*: completed, object focus with the idea of something achieved – "was (able to be) recorded".
- *Nagpapatagal* = *nag-* + *pa* + *pa* + *tagal*: ongoing causative – "keeps making longer".

**Translation:**
> The climate is changing too. PAGASA measured a warming of about 0.65 °C across the Philippines from 1951 to 2010, and 2024 was the hottest year recorded in the country. NASA's climate reconstruction for Biñán's own location shows the same: the average temperature in 2015–2024 was about 0.4 °C higher than in 1981–1990, and 2024 was the warmest year of the series. PAGASA expects about 2 °C more warming by mid-century. The heat is already felt: on 15 April 2025 the heat index reached 50 °C at Los Baños. And the water is rising: around Manila Bay, the sea rose by about a metre relative to the land from 1964 to 2002, mainly because the ground is sinking as too much groundwater is pumped out – a threat that also makes the lakeshore floods last longer.

---

**9. Fertile land, growing city**

> *Pinataba ng abo ng bulkan, ng banlik ng ilog at ng patag na lupa ang Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **pinataba** – *made fertile* (root: *taba* = fat)
- **banlik** – *silt*
- **patag** – *flat, level*
- **luad** – *clay*
- **palayan** – *rice field* (root: *palay* = rice plant)
- **tubuhan** – *sugarcane field* (root: *tubo* = sugarcane)
- **pinatutubigan** – *is irrigated* (root: *tubig* = water)
- **riles** – *railway*

**Grammar:**
- *Palay* + *-an* → *palayan*, *tubo* + *-an* → *tubuhan* (o → u): the suffix *-an* makes "a field of…".
- *Pinatutubigan* = *pa-* + *tubig* + *-an* with *-in-* and reduplication: "is (regularly) given water", i.e. irrigated.

**Translation:**
> Volcanic ash, river silt and flat land made Biñán fertile: its soils, formed on tuff and volcanic ash, are deep and rich in clay. For centuries this land fed rice fields and sugarcane fields watered by canals – the *patubigan* that gave its name to Barangay Tubigan. In the last fifty years the expressways, the railway and the industrial parks have turned these fields into a city, and all 24 barangays are now urban. The next chapters tell how people came to live on this shore, between the fire, the fault and the lake.

## Prehistory / Sinaunang Panahon

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. A long walk**

> *Ang kuwento ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng Lawa ng Laguna ay nagsisimula sa malayong Aprika.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nagsisimula** – *begins* (root: *simula* = beginning)
- **isinilang** – *was born* (root: *silang* = birth)
- **sinaunang tao** – *ancient people, early humans* (*sinauna* = ancient, from *una* = first)
- **kasangkapan** – *tool, implement*
- **lakarin** – *to walk across, to reach on foot* (root: *lakad* = walk)
- **nadudugtong** – *is joined, gets connected* (root: *dugtong*)
- **di-nakikitang hangganan** – *invisible boundary* (*di* = not; *nakikita* = can be seen)
- **kailanman ay hindi** – *never*
- **tawirin** – *to cross* (root: *tawid*, d → r)
- **balsa** – *raft*; **nang di-sinasadya** – *by accident*; **nang sinasadya** – *on purpose* (root: *sadya* = intention)
- **walang nakaaalam** – *nobody knows* (root: *alam* = know)

**Grammar:**
- *Nagsisimula* = *nag-* + repeated syllable *si* + *simula*: ongoing actor focus.
- *Isinilang* = *i-* + *-in-* + *silang*: object focus, completed – "was born".
- *Lakarin* and *tawirin* are object-focus infinitives in *-in*. When the root ends in *d*, it becomes *r* before the suffix: *lakad → lakarin*, *tawid → tawirin*.
- *Nakaaalam* = *naka-* + repeated syllable *a* + *alam*: "one who knows" – with *wala* (none): "no one knows".

**Translation:**
> The story of the first people on the shores of Laguna de Bay begins in faraway Africa, where the human family was born. About 1.8 million years ago, early humans had already left Africa: their bones and tools were found at Dmanisi in Georgia. *Homo erectus* reached Java 1.5 to 1.3 million years ago. Java, Sumatra and Borneo could then be reached on foot, but further east, beyond the Wallace Line, the islands were always separated from the continent by deep sea. Luzon was never joined to any continent. To reach it, early humans had to cross the sea – by accident on rafts of vegetation, or on purpose; nobody knows.

---

**2. The first visitors of Luzon**

> *Ngunit dumating sila.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nakatagpo** – *found, came upon* (root: *tagpo* = meeting)
- **kalansay** – *skeleton*
- **nawalang uri** – *extinct species* (*nawala* = lost; *uri* = kind, species)
- **bakas** – *trace, mark*; **hiwa** – *cut*
- **binasag** – *was broken, was smashed* (root: *basag*)
- **utak** – *marrow* (also: *brain*)
- **baluktot** – *bent, curved*
- **pinangalanan** – *was named* (root: *ngalan* = name)
- **pagtataya** – *estimate, assessment* (root: *taya* = estimate)
- **nag-iisa** – *alone* (root: *isa* = one)
- **umunlad** – *developed, evolved* (root: *unlad* = progress)

**Grammar:**
- *Pinangalanan* = *-in-* + *pangalan* + *-an*: object focus, completed – "(they) named it".
- *Hindi bababa sa* – "not lower than" = "at least" (*baba* = low, with reduplication for the future/contemplated aspect).
- *Nag-iisa* = *nag-* + repeated *i* + *isa*: "being one", i.e. alone.

**Translation:**
> But they came. At Rizal, Kalinga, in the Cagayan Valley, archaeologists found 57 stone tools and the skeleton of an extinct rhinoceros whose bones bear cut marks and were smashed to reach the marrow. They are about 709,000 years old – the oldest known trace of humans in the Philippines. Much later, in Callao Cave, researchers found the teeth and bones of a small human with curved finger and toe bones; in 2019 they named it a new species, *Homo luzonensis*. It lived at least 50,000 years ago, perhaps more than 130,000 years ago. Like *Homo floresiensis*, it evolved alone on an island for a very long time.

---

**3. Our species arrives**

> *Ang sarili nating uri, ang Homo sapiens, ay umalis sa Aprika nang mas huli.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sarili nating uri** – *our own species* (*sarili* = self; *natin* = our, inclusive)
- **umalis** – *left, departed* (root: *alis*)
- **nagpinta** – *painted* (root: *pinta*)
- **baboy-ramo** – *wild pig*
- **buto ng binti** – *shin bone* (*binti* = leg, calf)
- **palugit** – *margin, allowance*
- **bungo** – *skull*
- **bagong dating** – *newcomers*
- **henetika** – *genetics*
- **alon ng pandarayuhan** – *wave of migration* (*alon* = wave; root of *pandarayuhan*: *dayo* = stranger, to migrate)
- **ninuno** – *ancestor*; **inapo** – *descendant*

**Grammar:**
- *Nating* = *natin* + linker *-ng*: "our (inclusive)" placed before the noun: *sarili nating uri*.
- *Pandarayuhan* = *pang-* + repeated *da* + *dayo* + *-han* (d → r): the noun for the whole process of migrating.

**Translation:**
> Our own species, *Homo sapiens*, left Africa later, perhaps 70,000 to 50,000 years ago, following the coasts of Asia. On Sulawesi, people painted a wild pig on a cave wall at least 45,500 years ago. In the Philippines, the oldest bone of a modern human is a shin bone from Tabon Cave in Palawan, about 47,000 years old; the famous skull fragment called "Tabon Man" is younger, about 16,500 years. The newcomers met older humans: the Ayta Magbukon of Bataan carry more Denisovan DNA than any other people. Genetics shows at least five waves of migration over the last 50,000 years, beginning with the ancestors of the Agta, Ayta and Dumagat. Their descendants, the Dumagat and Remontado, still live in the Sierra Madre east of the lake.

---

**4. A different land**

> *Hindi ganito ang lupaing nakilala ng mga taong ito.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **ganito** – *like this*
- **nakilala** – *knew, got to know* (root: *kilala* = known)
- **pagtabang** – *the becoming fresh (of water)* (root: *tabang* = tasteless, fresh)

**Grammar:**
- *Hindi ganito ang lupaing nakilala nila* – "The land they knew was not like this": the predicate *hindi ganito* comes first, as is usual in Tagalog.

**Translation:**
> The land these people knew was not like this. In the last ice age, about 20,000 years ago, the sea was about 120 metres lower; when the ice melted, the sea came back, and from about 6,600 to 4,700 years ago the lake in front of Biñán was part of the sea, a salt lagoon connected to Manila Bay. It became the freshwater lake we know only about 3,000 years ago.

---

**5. The seafarers**

> *Wikang Austronesyo ang Tagalog.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **manlalayag** – *seafarer, sailor* (root: *layag* = sail)
- **palayok** – *clay pot*; **mapula** – *reddish*
- **palamuti** – *ornament*
- **bangkang may katig** – *outrigger canoe* (*katig* = outrigger)
- **butil** – *grain*
- **itinanim** – *was planted* (root: *tanim*)
- **tinipon** – *was gathered* (root: *tipon*)
- **lambat** – *net* (here: network)
- **kamag-anak** – *relative*

**Grammar:**
- *Manlalayag* = *mang-* + repeated syllable + *layag*: the person who does the action, "one who sails". Compare *mangangalakal* (trader).
- *Itinanim* (i- + -in- + tanim) and *tinipon* (-in- + tipon) are completed object-focus forms: "was planted", "was gathered".

**Translation:**
> About the time the lake turned fresh, new people arrived by sea. In the Cagayan Valley they left reddish pottery like that of eastern Taiwan and ornaments of Taiwanese jade. They knew the outrigger canoe and perhaps rice: the oldest rice grain found in the Philippines, at Andarayan, is about 3,400 years old, although we do not know whether it was planted or only gathered. Some scholars, following Solheim, prefer to see a network of sea trade. Either way, Tagalog is an Austronesian language, a distant cousin of Malay, Javanese, Malagasy and Hawaiian.

**6. Traces around the lake**

> *May mga bakas ng sinaunang panahong ito na natagpuan sa paligid ng lawa.*

**Translation:**

On a tuff cliff in Binangonan, the painter Carlos "Botong" Francisco reported in 1965 more than a hundred figures carved in the rock: the Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs, declared a National Cultural Treasure in 1973. In 2013, in Barangay Puypuy, Bay, a family dug up hundreds of pieces of earthen jars in their yard; archaeologists of the University of the Philippines identified them as burial jars perhaps 2,800 years old. Far away in Palawan, the Manunggul Jar shows on its lid two figures in a boat carrying a soul to the afterlife.

**7. And in Biñán?**

> *Wala pang natatagpuan o nahuhukay na sinaunang pook sa Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **wala pa** – *not yet (any)*

**Grammar:**
- *Wala pang natatagpuan* – "nothing has been found yet": *wala* + *pa* + an ongoing-aspect verb gives the sense "not yet".

**Translation:**
> And in Biñán? No ancient site has yet been found or excavated in Biñán, nor anywhere on the western shore of the lake. This does not mean that no one lived here: the old shore lies under metres of river silt and lake mud, part of it was under the sea six thousand years ago, and the city grew quickly over the fields before anyone looked. Any old pottery, stone tool or bone found while digging should be reported to the National Museum, as the National Cultural Heritage Act (Republic Act No. 10066) requires.

**8. Life on the lakeshore before writing**

> *Maaari lamang nating isipin ang buhay ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng lawa.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **maaari lamang** – *can only*
- **isipin** – *to imagine, to think of* (root: *isip* = mind)
- **tagapagtala** – *chronicler, recorder* (root: *tala* = record)
- **gabi, ube, saging** – *taro, yam, banana*
- **haligi** – *post, pillar*
- **tinahi** – *sewn* (root: *tahi*)
- **tarugo** – *wooden peg*
- **pamayanan** – *community*
- **pinararangalan** – *are honoured* (root: *dangal* = honour, d → r)
- **inililibing** – *are buried* (root: *libing*)
- **ibubunyag** – *will reveal* (root: *bunyag*)
- **balang araw** – *one day (in the future)*

**Grammar:**
- *Tagapag-* + *tala* = "one whose role is to record": a chronicler.
- *Pinararangalan* = *pa-* + *dangal* + *-an*, with *-in-* and reduplication (d → r): ongoing object focus, "are (regularly) honoured".

**Translation:**
> We can only imagine the life of the first people of the lakeshore, using what archaeology found nearby and what the first Spanish chroniclers saw a few centuries later: fishing in the lake; gardens of taro, yam and banana, and later rice; houses raised on posts; boats of planks sewn and pegged together, like the balangay of Butuan, the oldest about 1,700 years old – the boat that gave its name to the barangay, the community; spirits and ancestors, the anito, honoured in rituals; and the dead buried in jars. In the Metal Age, from about 500 BCE, iron tools, glass beads and gold came through trade. By about the year 900, the lake was already part of a wider world of trade and writing – as a copper plate found at Lumban would one day reveal.

## Pre-Colonial Era / Panahong Pre-Kolonyal

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. What we know, and how**

> *Sa loob ng anim na siglo bago dumating ang mga Espanyol, walang dokumentong bumabanggit sa Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sa loob ng** – *within, during*
- **siglo** – *century*
- **bumabanggit** – *mentions* (root: *banggit*)
- **paghuhukay** – *excavation, digging* (root: *hukay*)
- **nagmumula** – *comes from* (root: *mula*)
- **pinagsasaluhan** – *shared (among them)* (root: *salo* = to share a meal)
- **pamayanan** – *settlement, community*
- **nakakalat** – *scattered* (root: *kalat*)
- **nakapangkat** – *grouped* (root: *pangkat* = group)
- **pinamumunuan** – *led, ruled* (root: *puno* = head, leader)

**Grammar:**
- *Bumabanggit* = *-um-* + repeated syllable + *banggit*: ongoing actor focus, "(that) mentions".
- *Pinagsasaluhan* = *pinag-* + repeated syllable + *salo* + *-han*: an action done together by many people, ongoing.
- *Nakakalat* and *nakapangkat* = *naka-* + root: a resulting state ("in a scattered state", "in groups").

**Translation:**
> For six centuries before the Spaniards came, no document mentions Biñán and no excavation has yet been made on its soil. What we know of life on this shore comes from its neighbours – Tabuco, Bay, Pila and Lumban – from Chinese and Spanish writers, and from the customs shared by the Tagalog communities of the region. The land of today's Biñán was most likely part of Tabuco. Its people lived in small settlements scattered along the lake and the rivers coming down from the hills: they fished, planted rice and vegetables, gathered fruit and hunted wild pig, and they were grouped in barangays led by datus.

**2. The copper plate of 900**

> *Mula sa lawang ito ang pinakamatandang nakasulat na dokumentong natagpuan sa Pilipinas.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **pinakamatanda** – *the oldest* (root: *tanda* = age)
- **nakasulat** – *written* (root: *sulat*)
- **manggagawa** – *worker* (root: *gawa* = work)
- **buhangin** – *sand*
- **bunganga ng ilog** – *river mouth* (*bunganga* = mouth, opening)
- **platong tanso** – *copper plate*
- **titik** – *letter, script*
- **katibayan ng pagbabayad** – *receipt, proof of payment* (*tibay* = firmness; *bayad* = payment)
- **pinalalaya** – *frees, sets free* (root: *laya*)
- **inapo** – *descendant*
- **nagngangalang** – *named* (root: *ngalan* = name)
- **utang** – *debt*
- **saksi** – *witness*
- **pinagtatalunan** – *is debated* (root: *talo* = to argue)
- **iniingatan** – *is kept, is preserved* (root: *ingat* = care)

**Grammar:**
- *Manggagawa* = *mang-* + repeated syllable + *gawa*: the person who does the work.
- *Pinalalaya* = causative *pa-* + *laya* with *-in-* and reduplication: "is (being) set free".
- *Pinagtatalunan* = *pinag-* + repeated syllable + *talo* + *-an* (o → u): ongoing, "is argued about".

**Translation:**
> The oldest written document found in the Philippines comes from this lake. In the late 1980s, a worker digging sand near the mouth of the Lumbang River found a thin copper plate covered with writing, dated 21 April 900. Written mostly in Old Malay, in the Kawi script of Java, the Laguna Copperplate Inscription is a receipt: on behalf of the chief of Tundun (Tondo), it frees the descendants of a man named Namwaran from a debt of about 865 grams of gold, before witnesses from Pailah, Puliran and Binuangan. Scholars still debate where these places were; none of them is Biñán. But the plate shows that by the year 900 the lake belonged to a world of writing, gold, debts and titles linked to Java and Sumatra.

---

**3. Ma-i and the porcelain trade**

> *Bahagi rin ang lawa ng mga ruta ng kalakalan ng Asya.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **talaan** – *record, annal* (root: *tala*)
- **inilarawan** – *described* (root: *larawan* = picture)
- **nakadaong** – *anchored, moored* (root: *daong* = ship)
- **nagaganap** – *takes place* (root: *ganap*)
- **puting payong** – *white umbrella*
- **ipinagpapalit** – *are exchanged* (root: *palit*)
- **pautang** – *on credit* (root: *utang* = debt)
- **bulak** – *cotton*; **pagkit** – *wax*; **kabibi ng pagong** – *tortoiseshell*; **nganga** – *betel*
- **tingga** – *lead (metal)*; **karayom** – *needle*
- **iminungkahi** – *proposed, suggested* (root: *mungkahi*)
- **kaakit-akit** – *attractive* (root: *akit*)
- **napatutunayan** – *proven* (root: *tunay* = true)
- **pagsusunog ng bangkay** – *cremation* (*sunog* = burn; *bangkay* = corpse)
- **pabigat ng lambat** – *net weight*; **ikiran ng sinulid** – *spindle whorl*

**Grammar:**
- *Ipinagpapalit* = *ipag-* + *-in-* + repeated syllable + *palit*: ongoing object focus with *ipag-*, used for exchanging.
- *Hindi pa napatutunayan* – "not yet proven": *napa-* + reduplication + *tunay* + *-an* = can be shown to be true.

**Translation:**
> The lake was also part of Asia's trade routes. Chinese records mention merchants from a country called Ma-i as early as 971. In 1225, the Chinese official Zhao Rugua described Ma-i: big villages on both banks of a river, Chinese ships anchored before the "official place" where trade took place, local chiefs given white umbrellas, and goods exchanged on credit – cotton, wax, tortoiseshell, betel nut and cloth for porcelain, gold, iron pots, lead, glass beads and needles. Ma-i is usually placed on Mindoro, but Go Bon Juan proposed that it may be Ba-i, the town of Bay – an attractive but unproven idea. What is certain is that at Pinagbayanan, in Pila, archaeologists excavated some 241 graves of the 12th to 14th centuries, with Song and Yuan porcelain, cremations, net weights, spindle whorls and traces of rice farming, cotton weaving and ironworking.

**4. Brunei and Islam**

> *Patungong Brunei at Islam ang pinakamalakas na ugnayan ng mundong Tagalog.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **patungo** – *toward*
- **ugnayan** – *relationship, tie* (root: *ugnay* = connection)
- **nadatnan** – *found (on arrival)* (root: *datna*, from *dating*)
- **nakipag-ugnayan** – *dealt with, had dealings with* (root: *ugnay*)
- **nangaral** – *preached* (root: *aral* = teaching)
- **nakababasa** – *can read* (root: *basa*)
- **pagbubuhos** – *pouring* (root: *buhos*)
- **paglilinis** – *cleansing, ablution* (root: *linis*)

**Grammar:**
- *Nakipag-ugnayan* = *nakipag-* + *ugnay* + *-an*: an action done with someone else, completed – "had dealings (with)".
- *Nakababasa* = *naka-* + repeated syllable + *basa*: ability in the ongoing aspect, "is able to read".

**Translation:**
> In the 16th century, the strongest ties of the Tagalog world ran toward Brunei and Islam. When the Spaniards reached Manila Bay in 1570–71, they found Maynila ruled by Rajah Sulayman and Tondo by Lakandula. A Spanish report of 1572 says that the coastal people did not eat pork "because they had dealings with the Moros of Burney, who preached to them a little of the teaching of Mahoma", and that some who had been to Brunei could read a few words of the Koran. The influence reached the language: the Tagalog dictionary of 1754 says that the word *binyag* came from Brunei, where it meant pouring water from above in a Muslim ablution, before it was used for Christian baptism.

**5. The society of the barangay**

> *“Laging may mga pinuno ang bayang ito, na tinatawag nilang datos.”*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nilagdaan** – *signed* (root: *lagda* = signature)
- **kaugalian** – *custom* (root: *ugali* = habit)
- **pinuno** – *leader, chief*
- **namumuno** – *rules, leads* (root: *puno*)
- **malaya** – *free*
- **sumasagwan** – *paddles, rows* (root: *sagwan* = paddle)
- **ani** – *harvest*
- **ipagbili** – *to sell* (root: *bili*)
- **tubusin** – *to redeem, to buy back* (root: *tubos*)
- **palaisdaan** – *fishery* (root: *isda* = fish)
- **takdang hangganan** – *fixed boundaries*
- **takal** – *a measure of grain*
- **ari-arian** – *property*
- **bigay-kaya** – *dowry* (literally "a gift according to one's means")
- **makipaghiwalay** – *to separate, to divorce* (root: *hiwalay*)

**Grammar:**
- *Nilagdaan* = *-in-* + *lagda* + *-an*; before *l*, the infix becomes *ni-*: "(he) signed".

**Translation:**
> The best description of Tagalog society was written on this lake: the Franciscan Juan de Plasencia signed his *Customs of the Tagalogs* at Nagcarlán, Laguna, on 21 October 1589. "This people always had chiefs, whom they call datos," he wrote; each ruled "sometimes up to a hundred houses, sometimes fewer than thirty", a group "called in Tagalog a barangay". Below the datu were the free maharlika, who went to war with him and rowed for him; the aliping namamahay, who had their own houses and gave him part of their harvest; and the aliping sa gigilid, who lived in his house and could be sold. One could become a slave through war or debt, and buy one's freedom back with gold. Some chiefs owned fisheries with fixed boundaries and parts of rivers for markets; at Pila, free men paid their datu a hundred measures of rice a year. Women kept their own property, received a dowry and could divorce. Settlements could be fortified: in 1571 Cainta had about a thousand people behind thick bamboo, a wall and small cannons.

**6. Beliefs and the baybayin**

> *Sinasamba ng mga Tagalog si Bathala, “ang lumikha ng lahat ng bagay”.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sinasamba** – *is worshipped* (root: *samba*)
- **lumikha** – *created* (root: *likha*)
- **nagtatakda** – *sets, marks* (root: *takda*)
- **kinakatawan** – *is represented* (root: *katawan* = body)
- **pinangungunahan** – *is led* (root: *una* = first)
- **katayuan** – *rank, status* (root: *tayo* = to stand)
- **ipinagluluksa** – *are mourned* (root: *luksa* = mourning)
- **inihihimlay** – *are laid to rest* (root: *himlay*)
- **kabaong** – *coffin*
- **bukung-bukong** – *ankle*
- **marunong bumasa at sumulat** – *literate, able to read and write*

**Translation:**
> The Tagalogs worshipped Bathala, "the maker of all things", honoured the sun, the moon and the stars that mark the farming year, and the spirits of their ancestors, the anito, represented by images called lic-ha. There were no temples: a house was prepared for each feast, led by a catalonan, often a woman of high rank. The dead were mourned for four days and laid to rest in a boat as a coffin. Men and women wore gold bracelets and anklets; around 1590 the Boxer Codex shows Tagalog couples in baro, saya and putong. Many could read and write in baybayin, which appears beside the Latin alphabet in the Doctrina Christiana of 1593, the first book printed in the Philippines.

**7. The year 1571**

> *Nagbago ang mundong ito noong 1571.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **galera** – *galley*
- **kanyon** – *cannon*
- **nasakop** – *conquered* (root: *sakop*)
- **pagkubkob** – *siege* (root: *kubkob* = to surround)
- **tagapagtanggol** – *defender* (root: *tanggol*)
- **napatay** – *were killed* (root: *patay*)
- **pinatahimik** – *were "pacified", made quiet* (root: *tahimik* = quiet)
- **ipinagkaloob** – *were granted* (root: *kaloob* = gift)
- **tributo** – *tribute*
- **nagtatag** – *founded* (root: *tatag*)

**Grammar:**

**Translation:**
> This world changed in 1571. Legazpi settled in Manila in May. On 15 August 1571, his young captain Juan de Salcedo left with a galley, a hundred soldiers and three cannons against Cainta, which he conquered after a short siege; about four hundred of its defenders, men and women, were killed. The same report says that "the villages around this lake, with about twenty-four or twenty-six thousand men, were pacified" by Salcedo. Soon the lake towns were granted as encomiendas to Spaniards: Pila in November 1571, and in 1591 Don Luis Enríquez held Tabuco, with a thousand tributes – about four thousand people; Biñán was probably among them, although it is not named. From 1578 the Franciscans founded missions all around the lake. According to a local tradition, Salcedo and the missionaries stopped on the site of Biñán, planted a cross and baptized its people, so the place was called Binyagan, the "place of baptism"; the documents of the time confirm Salcedo's journey around the lake in late 1571, but do not mention Biñán.

## Spanish Colonial Period / Panahon ng Kolonyalismong Espanyol

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. From encomienda to town**

> *Bahagi noon ng Tabuco (Cabuyao) ang lupain ng Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **ipinagkaloob** – *were granted* (root: *kaloob* = gift)
- **mangaral** – *to preach* (root: *aral* = teaching)
- **iba-iba** – *various, different*
- **pagsulat** – *writing, spelling* (root: *sulat*)
- **iniuugnay** – *is linked, is associated* (root: *ugnay*)

**Grammar:**
- *Iniuugnay* = *i-* + *-in-* + repeated syllable + *ugnay*: ongoing object focus, "is (being) linked".

**Translation:**
> After Juan de Salcedo's journey around the lake in 1571, the lakeshore towns were granted as encomiendas to Spaniards, and missionaries began to preach among them. The land of Biñán was then part of Tabuco (Cabuyao). Its name is written in various ways in Spanish documents – Biñan, Biñang, Viñan – and a local tradition links it to *binyagan*, "the place of baptism".

---

**2. An estate for the college**

> *Ang kita nito ang tumutustos sa kolehiyo.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **unti-unting binuo** – *was gradually assembled* (root: *buo* = whole)
- **asyenda** – *hacienda, estate*
- **yugto** – *stage, phase*
- **kita** – *income*
- **tumutustos** – *funds, supports financially* (root: *tustos*)
- **kaburulan** – *hills, hill country* (root: *burol* = hill)
- **sakahin** – *to farm* (root: *saka*)
- **pinanirahan** – *settled (people in a place)* (root: *tira* = to live)
- **naglinis** – *cleared* (root: *linis* = clean)
- **namuno** – *led, headed* (root: *puno*)
- **inapo** – *descendant*

**Grammar:**
- *Unti-unting binuo* – the adverb *unti-unti* (little by little) is linked to the verb with *-ng*.
- *Kaburulan* = *ka-* + *burol* + *-an* (o → u): a whole area of hills.

**Translation:**
> From the 1640s the Dominicans of the College of Santo Tomás gradually assembled a great hacienda in Tabuco's territory, in three stages from 1641 to 1677. Its income supported the college. The Hacienda of Biñán reached from the lakeshore to the hills of Cavite and from San Pedro Tunasan to south of Santa Rosa. To farm it, the Dominicans also settled Christian Chinese from the Parián of Manila. One of them, Domingo Lam-co, cleared the land of today's Barangay Tubigan; his son Francisco Mercado led the town in 1783, and among his descendants was José Rizal.

---

**3. The fraud of 1743 and the revolts of 1745**

> *Nagdala rin ng alitan ang asyenda.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **alitan** – *conflict, quarrel*
- **sukatin** – *to measure* (root: *sukat*)
- **kawani ng hukuman** – *court clerk*
- **huwad** – *false, forged*
- **karatig-bayan** – *neighbouring town*
- **nag-alab** – *flared up, caught fire* (root: *alab* = flame)
- **sinalakay** – *attacked* (root: *salakay*)
- **pag-aalsa** – *uprising, revolt* (root: *alsa* = to rise)
- **magsiyasat** – *to investigate* (root: *siyasat*)
- **inagaw** – *seized, usurped* (root: *agaw*)
- **sinang-ayunan** – *approved* (root: *sang-ayon* = agreement)
- **pasya** – *decision*

**Grammar:**
- *Sukatin* = *sukat* + *-in*: object-focus infinitive, used here after *nang* ("when (they were) measured").
- *Sinang-ayunan* = *-in-* + *sang-ayon* + *-an* (o → u): completed object focus, "(the king) approved (them)".

**Translation:**
> The hacienda also brought conflict. In 1743, when the lands of "the hacienda of Biñán, owned by the Dominican fathers" were measured, a court clerk named Juan Monroy used false measures that gave the friars more than half of the lands of the neighbouring town of Silang. The countryside flared up: in 1745 the people of Silang attacked the hacienda's lands, and the revolts spread to Taguig, Parañaque, Bacoor, Imus, San Mateo and much of Bulacan – the largest uprising of Christian Filipinos before 1896. Judge Pedro Calderón Enríquez, sent to investigate, punished the clerk, declared the usurped lands Crown property and returned them to the villages; in 1751 the king approved his decisions. But the friars' estates remained.

---

**4. A town of its own**

> *Samantala, naging ganap na bayan ang Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **samantala** – *meanwhile*
- **ganap na** – *full, complete*
- **isaayos** – *to reorganize, to put in order* (root: *ayos*)
- **inihiwalay** – *was separated* (root: *hiwalay*)
- **kabayanan** – *town centre*
- **kura paroko** – *parish priest*

**Grammar:**
- *Nang muling isaayos* – "when (it) was reorganized again": after *nang* (when), the plain infinitive is often used for a past event.
- *Inihiwalay* = *i-* + *-in-* + *hiwalay*: completed object focus.

**Translation:**
> Meanwhile Biñán became a full town. In 1688, when the province of La Laguna was reorganized, it was separated from Tabuco, and on 4 February 1747 it became a full pueblo with its own town centre and barrios – the date the city still celebrates as its founding anniversary. According to tradition, Father José Monroy (1757) was its first parish priest and Antonio de Santa Rosa its first capitán. In 1791–92 the southern barrio of Santa Rosa was in turn separated to become a new town.

---

**5. The rice of the hacienda**

> *Palay ang yaman ng asyenda.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **palay** – *rice (the plant, unhusked grain)*
- **binhi** – *seed*
- **ani** – *harvest*
- **sanga** – *branch (here: tributary)*
- **bakuran** – *yard, garden* (root: *bakod* = fence)
- **umuupa** – *tenant* (root: *upa* = rent)
- **kaban** – *cavan, a measure of grain*
- **ikasampu** – *one tenth*
- **paalisin** – *to evict* (root: *alis* = leave)
- **magkasunod** – *consecutive* (root: *sunod* = follow)
- **nagpapasaka** – *have (land) farmed by others* (root: *saka*)
- **kasamá** – *sharecropper* (literally "companion")
- **prinsa** – *dam* (from Spanish *presa*)

**Grammar:**
- *Paalisin* = causative *pa-* + *alis* + *-in*: "to make (someone) leave" = to evict.
- *Nagpapasaka* = *nag-* + *pa* + *pa* + *saka*: causative ongoing, "have others do the farming".
- *Ikasampu* = *ika-* + *sampu*: ordinal / fraction "the tenth".

**Translation:**
> Rice was the hacienda's wealth. Around 1850, the geographers Buzeta and Bravo wrote that the Dominicans owned "all the land of Biñán" and that "rice makes its wealth": the harvest was fifty or sixty times the seed thanks to the river coming down from Mount Sungay; sugar cane, fruit trees and vegetables grew in the gardens. Tenants cleared and farmed the land rent-free for three or four years, then paid five cavans of rice for each cabalita of irrigated land – barely a tenth of the harvest – and could not be evicted unless they failed to pay two years running. Many tenants were rich men who had their land farmed by sharecroppers, the kasamá. The hacienda is said to have had a dozen dams; two remain, at Timbao and Soro-soro.

---

**6. A town of mestizos**

> *Pagsapit ng ika-19 na siglo, bayan na ng mga mestizo ang Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **maunlad** – *prosperous* (root: *unlad*)
- **tisa** – *roof tile*
- **karangyaan** – *luxury* (root: *rangya*)
- **halamanan** – *garden* (root: *halaman* = plant)
- **kaluluwa** – *soul*
- **pinaglilingkuran** – *is served* (root: *lingkod* = servant)
- **nakadikit** – *attached, stuck* (root: *dikit*)
- **koro** – *choir loft*
- **sobra** – *surplus*
- **hinabi** – *woven* (root: *habi*)
- **bapor** – *steamboat* (from Spanish *vapor*)
- **tumuloy** – *stayed (as a guest)* (root: *tuloy*)
- **tuluyan** – *prose*

**Grammar:**
- *Pinaglilingkuran* = *pinag-* + repeated syllable + *lingkod* + *-an* (d → r): ongoing object focus, "is (regularly) served".
- *Nakadikit dito* – *naka-* gives a state: "is (in the state of being) attached to it".

**Translation:**
> By the 19th century Biñán was a town of mestizos, prosperous Chinese-Filipino families who owned the finest houses, "not a few of stone and tile, a luxury found in few towns", with fruit gardens. It had 10,948 souls around 1850. Its church, "of good construction", was served by Filipino secular priests, while the house of the Dominican hacienda was attached to it through the choir loft. Good roads led to the neighbouring towns, and surplus rice and cloth woven by the women went to Manila across the lake, by casco and later by steamboat. In 1858–59 the governor of Hong Kong, Sir John Bowring, stayed with Don José Alberto, Rizal's uncle, and noted "an active trade between Biñán and Manila". The town's families included the Mercado, Alonso and Alberto, and it was the birthplace of Modesto de Castro, father of Tagalog prose, of Francisco Mercado, Rizal's father, and of the lawyer Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista.

---

**7. The schoolboy of 1870**

> *Noong 1870, dumating mula Calamba ang isang siyam na taong gulang na batang lalaki.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **siyam na taong gulang** – *nine years old*
- **alaala** – *memoir, memory*
- **nagpatuloy** – *took in, gave lodging* (root: *tuloy* = enter, stay)
- **leeg** – *neck*; **matulis** – *pointed*
- **pakikipag-away** – *fighting with someone* (root: *away* = quarrel)
- **nagpatulong** – *asked for help* (root: *tulong*)
- **tuyo** – *dried fish*
- **pananda** – *marker* (root: *tanda* = sign)
- **aklat-aralin** – *textbook*
- **ginunita** – *recalled* (root: *gunita* = memory)
- **kidlat** – *lightning*; **kampanaryo** – *bell tower*

**Grammar:**
- *Nagpatuloy sa kaniya* – causative *nagpa-*: "let him stay", i.e. lodged him.
- *Pakikipag-away* = *pakikipag-* + *away*: the noun for doing something with another person – "fighting with".

**Translation:**
> In 1870 a nine-year-old boy arrived from Calamba to study Latin: José Rizal. In his memoirs he describes his aunt of over seventy who lodged him, his teacher Justiniano Aquino Cruz, "a tall, thin man with a long neck and a pointed nose", his fight with the teacher's son, the old painter Juancho who let him help with his pictures, and his days: Mass at four in the morning, class until ten and again in the afternoon, rice and two dried fish for breakfast. He left Biñán on the steamer Talim on Saturday, 17 December 1870. The 1948 marker at the site of his school says 1871, and textbooks often say 1869. In *Noli me tangere*, Rizal recalled that "in the year '70, when lightning struck Biñán", it hit the church tower and destroyed the clock and an altar.

**8. The end of Spanish rule**

> *Nagwakas sa rebolusyon ang pamamahala ng Espanya.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nagwakas** – *ended* (root: *wakas* = end)
- **pamamahala** – *rule, administration* (root: *bahala* = in charge)
- **himpilan ng panustos** – *supply base* (*himpil* = station; *tustos* = supplies)
- **iminungkahi** – *proposed* (root: *mungkahi*)
- **sawikain** – *motto, saying*
- **tapat** – *faithful, loyal*
- **isinantabi** – *was set aside, was shelved* (root: *santabi*)
- **sumulat at bumasa** – *wrote and read*
- **kapalaran** – *fate* (root: *palad*)
- **pagpapasyahan** – *will be decided* (root: *pasya*)

**Grammar:**
- *Isinantabi* = *i-* + *-in-* + *santabi*: completed object focus, "was set aside".
- *Pagpapasyahan* = *pag-* + repeated *pa* + *pasya* + *-han*: **future** object focus, "(it) will be decided".

**Translation:**
> Spanish rule ended in revolution. In 1896–97, Biñán and Santa Rosa served as supply bases and temporary hospitals for the Spanish soldiers fighting the revolutionaries in Cavite, and on 19 January 1898 the provincial junta even proposed to make them villas with the motto "Siempre fiel y leal" – "ever faithful and loyal". The request was set aside when Emilio Aguinaldo returned. A few months later, on 12 June 1898, a son of Biñán, Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista, wrote and read the Act of the Proclamation of Independence at Kawit, and by the end of August the whole province of La Laguna was in Filipino hands. The fate of the friar lands would be decided under the Americans.

**Translation:**

## World War II and Japanese Occupation / Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig

---

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation (New Content Only)

---

**1.**  
> *Ang pananakop ng mga Hapones noong Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig ay nagdulot ng matinding hirap at pighati sa mga taga-Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **Hapones** – *Japanese (people)*  
- **Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig** – *Second World War* (*ikalawa* = second, *digmaan* = war, *pandaigdig* = worldwide, from *daigdig* = world)  
- **nagdulot** – *brought about, caused* (root: *dulot* = to bring, to offer)  
- **matindi** – *intense, severe*  
- **hirap** – *hardship*  
- **pighati** – *grief, sorrow*  
- **taga-Biñán** – *people from Biñán* (*taga-* = "from, native of")

**Grammar:**  
- *Nagdulot* = actor focus, completed aspect (*nag- + dulot*). The occupation is the "actor" that caused the hardship.  
- *Ang ... ay ...* is the inverted sentence order, common in formal or written Tagalog: subject first, then *ay*, then the predicate.

**Translation:**  
> The Japanese occupation during World War II brought severe hardship and grief to the people of Biñán.

---

**2.**  
> *Pinasok ng Imperyal na Hukbong Hapones ang Laguna noong 1942 at kontrolado nila ang Biñán sa loob ng tatlong taon.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **pinasok** – *entered, invaded* (root: *pasok* = enter)  
- **Imperyal na Hukbong Hapones** – *Imperial Japanese Army* (*hukbo* = army)  
- **kontrolado** – *controlled, under control* (borrowed from Spanish *controlado*)  
- **sa loob ng** – *within, for the duration of* (*loob* = inside)

**Grammar:**  
- *Pinasok* = object focus, completed (*-in-* infix + *pasok*). The thing entered (*ang Laguna*) is the focus; the actor is marked by *ng* (*ng Imperyal na Hukbong Hapones*).  
- Compare with actor focus *pumasok* ("entered"), seen in the post-war chapter.

**Translation:**  
> The Imperial Japanese Army entered Laguna in 1942 and controlled Biñán for three years.

---

**3.**  
> *Sa panahong ito, ang karaniwang araw-araw na buhay ay napalitan ng takot at kakulangan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **karaniwan** – *ordinary, usual*  
- **araw-araw** – *daily, every day* (doubling *araw* = day)  
- **napalitan** – *was replaced* (root: *palit* = change, exchange)  
- **takot** – *fear*  
- **kakulangan** – *shortage, lack* (root: *kulang* = lacking)

**Grammar:**  
- *Napalitan* = *na- + palit + -an*, completed. The *na-* prefix shows something that happened to the subject, not something done on purpose.  
- *Kakulangan* = *ka- ... -an* turns an adjective (*kulang*) into an abstract noun ("the state of lacking").

**Translation:**  
> During this time, ordinary daily life was replaced by fear and shortage.

---

**4.**  
> *Nagkulang ang suplay ng pagkain dahil kinukuha ng mga mananakop ang ani para sa kanilang hukbo, kaya't kinailangang magtipid at magtago ng mga pamilya.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **nagkulang** – *became insufficient, ran short* (root: *kulang*)  
- **suplay ng pagkain** – *food supply*  
- **kinukuha** – *were taking* (root: *kuha* = take, get)  
- **mananakop** – *occupiers, invaders* (root: *sakop* = to cover, to rule over)  
- **ani** – *harvest*  
- **kaya't** – *so, therefore* (short for *kaya at*)  
- **kinailangan** – *had to, needed to* (root: *kailangan* = need)  
- **magtipid** – *to save, to be thrifty* (root: *tipid*)  
- **magtago** – *to hide, to keep hidden* (root: *tago*)

**Grammar:**  
- *Kinukuha* = object focus, **ongoing** aspect: *-in-* + repeated first syllable (*ku*) + *kuha*. It describes a repeated action in the past ("kept taking").  
- *Mananakop* = *mang- + sakop*, with the first syllable repeated. The *s* changes to *n* (*mang + sakop → manakop*), and this form names the person who does the action: "one who conquers".  
- *Kinailangang magtipid* – the *-ng* linker joins *kinailangan* to the verb that follows ("had to save").

**Translation:**  
> The food supply ran short because the occupiers were taking the harvest for their army, so families had to save and hide food.

---

**5.**  
> *Maraming kabataan ang hindi nakapag-aral at ang pamahalaang bayan ay napasailalim sa mahigpit na utos ng mga Hapones.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **kabataan** – *youth, young people* (root: *bata* = child)  
- **nakapag-aral** – *was able to study* (root: *aral* = study, lesson)  
- **pamahalaang bayan** – *town government*  
- **napasailalim** – *came under, was placed under* (*sa ilalim* = under, beneath)  
- **mahigpit** – *strict, tight*  
- **utos** – *order, command*

**Grammar:**  
- *Nakapag-aral* = *naka- + pag- + aral*. *Naka-* expresses **ability** (completed): "managed to / was able to". With *hindi*: "could not go to school".  
- *Napasailalim* = *na- + pa- + sa ilalim*, built on a whole phrase: "ended up under".

**Translation:**  
> Many young people could not go to school, and the town government came under the strict orders of the Japanese.

---

**6.**  
> *Subalit sa kabila ng panganib, ang diwa ng paglaban ay buhay sa puso ng mga Biñense.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **subalit** – *but, however*  
- **sa kabila ng** – *despite, in spite of* (*kabila* = other side)  
- **panganib** – *danger*  
- **diwa** – *spirit*  
- **paglaban** – *resistance, fighting back* (root: *laban* = fight)  
- **buhay** – *alive* (also: *life*)  
- **puso** – *heart*

**Grammar:**  
- *Paglaban* = *pag-* + root: a noun naming the action ("the act of fighting back").

**Translation:**  
> But despite the danger, the spirit of resistance was alive in the hearts of the Biñenses.

---

**7.**  
> *Palihim na sumuporta ang mga residente sa mga gerilya – ang ilan ay sumapi mismo sa mga pangkat, at ang iba naman ay nagbigay ng pagkain, taguan, o impormasyon sa mga mandirigma sa kabundukan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **palihim** – *secretly* (root: *lihim* = secret)  
- **sumuporta** – *supported* (root: *suporta*, from Spanish *soporte* / English *support*)  
- **gerilya** – *guerrilla fighters*  
- **ang ilan ... ang iba** – *some ... others*  
- **sumapi** – *joined (a group)* (root: *sapi*)  
- **mismo** – *themselves, personally*  
- **pangkat** – *group, unit*  
- **taguan** – *hiding place* (root: *tago* + *-an* = place)  
- **mandirigma** – *warrior, fighter* (root: *digma* = war)  
- **kabundukan** – *mountains, mountainous area* (root: *bundok* = mountain)

**Grammar:**  
- *Sumuporta* and *sumapi* = actor focus, completed, with the **-um-** infix (*s-um-uporta*, *s-um-api*).  
- *Palihim na* – the *pa-* prefix turns *lihim* into an adverb of manner, linked to the verb with *na*.  
- *Ka- ... -an* on a noun of place makes a collective area: *bundok* → *kabundukan* ("the mountains"). Note the vowel change *o → u* when a suffix is added.

**Translation:**  
> The residents secretly supported the guerrillas – some joined the units themselves, while others gave food, hiding places, or information to the fighters in the mountains.

---

**8.**  
> *Naging bahagi ang Biñán ng malawakang kilusang paglaban sa okupasyon.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **bahagi** – *part*  
- **malawakan** – *widespread, large-scale* (root: *lawak* = breadth)  
- **kilusan** – *movement* (root: *kilos* = to move, to act)  
- **okupasyon** – *occupation*

**Translation:**  
> Biñán became part of the widespread resistance movement against the occupation.

---

**9.**  
> *Nang papalapit na ang kalayaan, naging saksi ang Biñán sa mga huling kabanata ng digmaan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **nang** – *when, as* (introduces a time clause)  
- **papalapit** – *approaching, drawing near* (root: *lapit* = near; *pa-* + repeated *pa* + *lapit*)  
- **na** – *already, now*  
- **kalayaan** – *freedom* (root: *laya* = free)  
- **saksi** – *witness*  
- **huli** – *last, final*  
- **kabanata** – *chapter*

**Note:** *Nang papalapit na ang kalayaan* = "as freedom was (already) approaching". The earlier wording *Pagsapit ng pagpalapit ng kalayaan* ("upon the arrival of the approach of freedom") repeated the same idea and was corrected.

**Translation:**  
> As freedom drew near, Biñán witnessed the final chapters of the war.

---

**10.**  
> *Pebrero 3, 1945 nang tuluyang napalaya ang Biñán mula sa pananakop.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **tuluyan** – *completely, for good*  
- **napalaya** – *was freed, was liberated* (root: *laya* = free)  
- **mula sa** – *from*

**Grammar:**  
- *Napalaya* = *na- + pa- + laya*. *Pa-* means "to cause" (*palayain* = to set free); *na-* marks it as an accomplished result: "was (successfully) set free".  
- *[Date] nang ...* – "It was on [date] that ...": a way to put the date first for emphasis.

**Translation:**  
> It was on February 3, 1945 that Biñán was finally liberated from the occupation.

---

**11.**  
> *Sa araw na ito, isang sama-samang pagsalakay ang isinagawa ng mga gerilyang Pilipino – ang kilalang Hunters ROTC – sa pangunguna ni Komandante Marcial Alintanahin.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **pagsalakay** – *attack, assault* (root: *salakay* = attack)  
- **isinagawa** – *was carried out* (root: *sagawa*, from *gawa* = do, make)  
- **kilala** – *well-known, famous*  
- **sa pangunguna ni** – *under the leadership of* (root: *una* = first)  
- **Komandante** – *Commander*

**Grammar:**  
- *Isinagawa* = object focus, completed: *i-* + *-in-* + *sagawa*. The *-in-* infix becomes part of the prefix (*isina-*). The thing done (*pagsalakay*) is the focus.  
- *Pangunguna* = *pang-* + *una*, with the first syllable repeated: "the act of leading".  
- *Ni* is the *ng*-marker used before a person's name.

**Translation:**  
> On this day, a combined assault was carried out by Filipino guerrillas – the famous Hunters ROTC – under the leadership of Commander Marcial Alintanahin.

---

**12.**  
> *Mula sa iba't ibang direksyon sila nagmula: may dumating mula sa Carmona sa kanluran, mula sa San Pedro sa hilaga, at mula sa Santa Rosa sa timog.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **iba't iba** – *various, different* (short for *iba at iba*)  
- **nagmula** – *came from, originated* (root: *mula* = from)  
- **may dumating** – *some arrived* (literally "there were those who arrived")  
- **kanluran** – *west*  
- **hilaga** – *north*  
- **timog** – *south*  
- *(and for completeness:* **silangan** – *east)*

**Translation:**  
> They came from different directions: some arrived from Carmona in the west, from San Pedro in the north, and from Santa Rosa in the south.

---

**13.**  
> *Nagulat at di nakaagapay ang mga natitirang kawal na Hapones sa Biñán, kaya't sa loob lamang ng isang araw ay napasuko sila at ang mga kakamping lokal na kolaborasyonistang kasama nila.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **nagulat** – *were surprised* (root: *gulat* = shock, surprise)  
- **nakaagapay** – *was able to keep up / cope* (root: *agapay* = to keep alongside)  
- **natitira** – *remaining* (root: *tira* = leftover)  
- **kawal** – *soldier*  
- **lamang** – *only*  
- **napasuko** – *were made to surrender* (root: *suko* = surrender)  
- **kakampi** – *ally* (root: *kampi* = side, party)  
- **kolaborasyonista** – *collaborator*  
- **kasama** – *companion, together with*

**Grammar:**  
- *Nagulat* = *na- + gulat* (not *nag-* + *ulat*): an involuntary feeling that happened to them.  
- *Di nakaagapay* = *naka-* of ability, negated: "could not keep up".  
- *Natitira* = *na-* + repeated syllable *ti* + *tira*: ongoing state, "those still remaining".  
- *Napasuko* = *na- + pa- + suko*: "were caused to surrender" (they surrendered because of the attack).

**Translation:**  
> The remaining Japanese soldiers in Biñán were caught by surprise and could not hold out, so within just one day they were forced to surrender, together with the local collaborators who were on their side.

---

**14.**  
> *Matapos ang labanan, nagwagayway ang watawat ng Pilipinas sa Biñán, hudyat ng katapusan ng mapait na yugto ng digmaan para sa bayan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **matapos** – *after* (similar to *pagkatapos*)  
- **labanan** – *battle* (root: *laban*)  
- **nagwagayway** – *waved, fluttered* (root: *wagayway*)  
- **watawat** – *flag*  
- **hudyat** – *signal, sign*  
- **katapusan** – *end* (root: *tapos* = finished)  
- **mapait** – *bitter* (root: *pait*)  
- **yugto** – *period, stage, chapter*

**Translation:**  
> After the battle, the Philippine flag flew over Biñán, a signal of the end of the bitter period of the war for the town.

---

**15.**  
> *Bagama't napakasaklap ng sinapit ng marami, mapalad ang Biñán dahil hindi ito lubusang nawasak noong digmaan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **bagama't** – *although*  
- **napakasaklap** – *extremely tragic* (root: *saklap* = misery, bitterness)  
- **sinapit** – *fate, what one went through* (root: *sapit* = to arrive at, to befall)  
- **mapalad** – *lucky, fortunate* (root: *palad* = palm of the hand; also luck, fate)  
- **lubusan** – *completely, totally*  
- **nawasak** – *was destroyed* (root: *wasak* = ruined)

**Grammar:**  
- *Napaka-* + adjective = "very, extremely" (*napakasaklap* = "so very tragic").  
- *Nawasak* = *na- + wasak*: something that happened to the subject (destroyed, not "destroyed someone").

**Translation:**  
> Although what many people went through was deeply tragic, Biñán was fortunate because it was not completely destroyed during the war.

---

**16.**  
> *Maraming bayan sa Laguna ang naabo ang mga gusali at simbahan sa mga pambobomba, subalit ang simbahan at kabayanan ng Biñán ay nakaligtas sa matinding pinsala.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **naabo** – *was burned to ashes* (root: *abo* = ash)  
- **gusali** – *building*  
- **pambobomba** – *bombing* (root: *bomba* = bomb)  
- **kabayanan** – *town center, poblacion* (root: *bayan* = town)  
- **nakaligtas** – *survived, escaped* (root: *ligtas* = safe)  
- **pinsala** – *damage*

**Grammar:**  
- *Naabo* – Tagalog can make a verb from a noun: *na- + abo* = "turned into ash".  
- *Nakaligtas* = *naka- + ligtas*: "managed to be safe", i.e. survived.  
- *Pambobomba* = *pang- + bomba*, with the first syllable repeated: the noun for the ongoing act ("the bombing").

**Translation:**  
> Many towns in Laguna saw their buildings and churches reduced to ashes by the bombings, but the church and town center of Biñán escaped severe damage.

---

**17.**  
> *Dahil dito, napanatili ang maraming lumang bahay at istruktura sa Biñán na itinayo pa noong panahon ng Espanyol – na isang bihirang pangyayari sa ibang lugar na lubhang nasira ang mga makasaysayang gusali.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **dahil dito** – *because of this*  
- **napanatili** – *were preserved, were kept* (root: *panatili* = to keep, to remain)  
- **luma** – *old*  
- **itinayo** – *was built* (root: *tayo* = to stand, to build)  
- **bihira** – *rare*  
- **pangyayari** – *event, occurrence* (root: *yari* = happen)  
- **lubha** – *greatly, severely*  
- **nasira** – *was damaged* (root: *sira* = broken)  
- **makasaysayan** – *historical* (root: *kasaysayan* = history)

**Grammar:**  
- *Napanatili* = *na- + panatili*: object focus, completed, with the sense of a result that was achieved.  
- *Itinayo* = *i- + -in- + tayo*: object focus, completed – "was built".

**Translation:**  
> Because of this, many old houses and structures in Biñán built during the Spanish period were preserved – something rare in other places where historic buildings were badly damaged.

---

**18.**  
> *Gayunpaman, ang sugat sa pamayanan ay hindi biro: may mga naulila, may mga nasugatan at namatayan, at halos lahat ay dumanas ng gutom at takot.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **gayunpaman** – *nevertheless, however*  
- **sugat** – *wound*  
- **pamayanan** – *community*  
- **hindi biro** – *no joke, serious*  
- **naulila** – *were orphaned* (root: *ulila* = orphan)  
- **nasugatan** – *were wounded* (root: *sugat*)  
- **namatayan** – *lost a loved one, were bereaved* (root: *patay* = dead)  
- **halos lahat** – *almost everyone*  
- **dumanas** – *experienced, went through* (root: *danas* = experience)  
- **gutom** – *hunger*

**Grammar:**  
- *Namatayan* = *nang-* (completed form of *mang-*) + *patay* + *-an*: *ng + p* merges into *m*. The *-an* suffix points to the person affected: "the one who had someone die" (not the one who died).  
- *Nasugatan* = *na- + sugat + -an*: "got wounded".  
- *Dumanas* = *-um-* + *danas*: actor focus, completed.

**Translation:**  
> Nevertheless, the wound to the community was serious: some were orphaned, some were wounded or lost loved ones, and almost everyone suffered hunger and fear.

**19.**  
> *Matapos ang liberasyon, nagsimula ang mahaba at mahirap na pagbangon.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **liberasyon** – *liberation*  
- **nagsimula** – *began* (root: *simula* = beginning)  
- **mahaba** – *long*  
- **mahirap** – *difficult*  
- **pagbangon** – *recovery, rising again* (root: *bangon* = to rise)

**Translation:**  
> After the liberation, the long and difficult recovery began.

**20.**  
> *Nagbalik unti-unti ang mga pamilya mula sa mga pinagtaguan nilang baryo at bundok, upang muling itayo ang mga bahay, linisin ang mga lansangan, at buhayin ang naantalang mga kabuhayan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **nagbalik** – *returned* (root: *balik*)  
- **unti-unti** – *little by little, gradually*  
- **pinagtaguan** – *(places) where they hid* (root: *tago* = hide)  
- **baryo** – *village* (from Spanish *barrio*)  
- **upang** – *in order to*  
- **muli** – *again*  
- **itayo** – *to build (up)* (root: *tayo*)  
- **linisin** – *to clean* (root: *linis* = clean)  
- **lansangan** – *street*  
- **buhayin** – *to revive, to bring back to life* (root: *buhay* = life)  
- **naantala** – *delayed, interrupted* (root: *antala* = delay)  
- **kabuhayan** – *livelihood* (root: *buhay*)

**Grammar:**  
- *Itayo*, *linisin*, *buhayin* = object-focus **infinitives** after *upang* ("in order to"). Two ways of making them: *i-* + root (*itayo*), or root + *-in* (*linisin*, *buhayin*).  
- *Pinagtaguan* = *pinag- + tago + -an*: "the place where (they) hid". The *-an* suffix often points to a place.  
- *Naantalang* = *naantala* + linker *-ng* to describe *kabuhayan*.

**Translation:**  
> Families gradually returned from the villages and mountains where they had hidden, to rebuild their houses, clean the streets, and revive their interrupted livelihoods.

**21.**  
> *Ang diwa ng bayanihan ang nagsilbing sandigan ng mga Biñense sa panahong ito – nagtulungan ang bawat isa upang ibangon ang Biñán mula sa pagkakadapa.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **nagsilbing** – *served as* (root: *silbi* = use, service)  
- **sandigan** – *support, pillar, something to lean on* (root: *sandig* = to lean)  
- **nagtulungan** – *helped one another* (root: *tulong* = help)  
- **bawat isa** – *each one, everyone*  
- **ibangon** – *to raise up, to lift* (root: *bangon*)  
- **pagkakadapa** – *having fallen flat on one's face* (root: *dapa* = lying face-down)

**Grammar:**  
- *Nagtulungan* = *nag- + tulong + -an*: the *nag- ... -an* pattern shows a **reciprocal** action ("helped each other"). Note *tulong* → *tulung-* before the suffix.  
- *Ibangon* = *i- + bangon*: object focus – "to raise (Biñán) up". Compare actor focus *bumangon* = "to get up (oneself)".  
- *Pagkakadapa* = *pagka- + ka- + dapa*: the state of having fallen. Here it is an image: Biñán had been "knocked to the ground".

**Translation:**  
> The spirit of bayanihan served as the support of the Biñenses during this time – everyone helped one another to lift Biñán up from its fall.

**22.**  
> *Nang makamit ng Pilipinas ang kasarinlan noong Hulyo 4, 1946, ang Biñán ay humakbang na rin patungo sa bagong yugto bilang bahagi ng isang malayang bansa, bitbit ang mga aral at alaala ng digmaan.*

**Vocabulary:**  
- **nang** – *when* (with a past event)  
- **makamit** – *to achieve, to attain* (root: *kamit*)  
- **kasarinlan** – *independence* (root: *sarili* = self)  
- **humakbang** – *stepped, took a step* (root: *hakbang* = step)  
- **patungo sa** – *towards*  
- **malaya** – *free* (root: *laya*)  
- **bansa** – *nation, country*  
- **bitbit** – *carrying (in hand)*  
- **aral** – *lessons*  
- **alaala** – *memories*

**Grammar:**  
- *Nang makamit* – after *nang* ("when"), Tagalog often uses the plain *ma-* form instead of the past: "when (it) was attained".  
- *Humakbang* = *-um-* + *hakbang*: actor focus, completed.  
- *Kasarinlan* = *ka- + sarili + -an*: "self-ness", i.e. independence. The root is shortened (*sarili* → *sarinl-*).

**Translation:**  
> When the Philippines achieved independence on July 4, 1946, Biñán also stepped into a new chapter as part of a free nation, carrying with it the lessons and memories of the war.

### Visual Diagram of New Verb Constructions

---

#### 1. **pinasok** (entered, invaded)

```
Root: pasok → enter
+ -in- (after first consonant) → p-in-asok → pinasok → was entered (completed object focus)
Compare: p-um-asok → pumasok → entered (actor focus)
```

---

#### 2. **kinukuha** (were taking)

```
Root: kuha → take
+ repeat first syllable → ku-kuha
+ -in- → k-in-ukuha → kinukuha → is/was being taken (ongoing object focus)
```

---

#### 3. **nakapag-aral** (was able to study)

```
Root: aral → study
+ pag- → pag-aral → studying
+ naka- → nakapag-aral → was able to study (completed, ability)
hindi + nakapag-aral → could not study
```

---

#### 4. **sumuporta** (supported)

```
Root: suporta → support
+ -um- (after first consonant) → s-um-uporta → sumuporta → supported (completed actor focus)
Same pattern: sapi → sumapi, danas → dumanas, hakbang → humakbang
```

---

#### 5. **napalaya** (was liberated)

```
Root: laya → free
+ pa- → palaya → cause to be free
+ na- → napalaya → was (successfully) set free
Same pattern: suko → napasuko (was made to surrender)
```

---

#### 6. **isinagawa** (was carried out)

```
Root: gawa → do, make
+ sa- → sagawa
+ i- → isagawa → to carry out
+ -in- → is-in-agawa → isinagawa → was carried out (completed object focus)
```

---

#### 7. **nakaligtas** (survived)

```
Root: ligtas → safe
+ naka- → nakaligtas → managed to be safe → survived
```

---

#### 8. **namatayan** (lost a loved one)

```
Root: patay → dead
+ nang- ... -an → nang-patay-an
ng + p merges into m → namatayan → one who suffered a death (bereaved)
```

---

#### 9. **nagtulungan** (helped one another)

```
Root: tulong → help
+ -an → tulungan (o → u before suffix)
+ nag- → nagtulungan → helped one another (completed, reciprocal)
```

---

#### 10. **ibangon** (to raise up)

```
Root: bangon → rise
+ i- → ibangon → to raise (something) up (object focus)
Compare: b-um-angon → bumangon → to get up (actor focus)
         pag- + bangon → pagbangon → recovery (noun)
```

## Culture, Daily Life and Commerce / Kultura, Pang-araw-araw na Buhay at Kalakalan

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation (New Content Only)

**1. The market and everyday trade**

> *Mula pa noon, bayan na ng mga mangangalakal ang Biñán.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **mula pa noon** – *since long ago, from way back*
- **mangangalakal** – *traders, merchants* (root: *kalakal* = goods, trade)
- **pampublikong pamilihan** – *public market* (root: *bili* = buy; *pamilihan* = place for buying)
- **madaling-araw** – *dawn, very early morning*
- **puwesto** – *stall*
- **siksikan** – *crowded, packed together* (root: *siksik* = compressed)
- **dyip, traysikel** – *jeepney, tricycle*
- **umusbong** – *sprang up, sprouted* (root: *usbong* = shoot, bud)
- **sambahayan** – *household*
- **tsinelas** – *slippers*
- **nakaugat** – *rooted* (root: *ugat* = root)
- **entablado ng banda** – *bandstand*
- **pospas** – *Biñan’s yellow rice porridge*; **lugaw** – *rice porridge*; **lamang-loob** – *innards*; **kasubha** – *safflower*

**Grammar:**
- *Mangangalakal* = *mang-* + repeated first syllable + *kalakal* (k → ng): the doer of an action, "one who trades". Compare *mananakop* (occupier) in the WWII chapter.
- *Pamilihan* = *pang-* + *bili* + *-han*: the place where an action happens. The same pattern gives *paaralan* (school), *simbahan* (church).
- *Napupuno* = *na-* + repeated syllable + *puno* (full): ongoing state, "is (always) getting filled".
- *Umusbong* = *-um-* + *usbong*. With a root beginning with a vowel, *-um-* goes in front: *um* + *usbong*.
- *Kinukulayan* = *-in-* + repeated syllable + *kulay* + *-an*: ongoing object focus, "is coloured (with)".

**Translation:**
> Biñán has been a town of traders since long ago. Its public market, near the plaza and the old municipal hall, is where daily life begins: from dawn the stalls fill with fruit, vegetables, fish and flowers, while jeepneys and tricycles crowd the streets of the Poblacion. Small family businesses sprang up around it. For generations, households in barangays such as Dela Paz and Malaban made slippers and shoes – a livelihood so rooted in the town’s identity that its bandstand was even built in the shape of a slipper. The market also enriched the food traditions. Every morning someone sells pospas there, a yellow porridge with chicken and innards coloured with safflower; Merta Silva is said to have started it in Biñán during the Japanese period, and Mang Tony’s still serves it in the Poblacion.

---

**2. Puto Biñán**

> *Ang pinakatanyag na produkto ng lungsod ay ang Puto Biñán, isang pinasingawang kakanin na kasinlaki ng pizza.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **pinakatanyag** – *most famous* (root: *tanyag* = famous)
- **pinasingawan** – *steamed* (root: *singaw* = steam)
- **kakanin** – *native rice cake, snack* (root: *kain* = eat)
- **kasinlaki ng** – *as big as* (root: *laki* = size)
- **ginadgad** – *grated* (root: *gadgad*)
- **itlog na maalat** – *salted egg*
- **maybahay** – *housewife*
- **galapong** – *rice-flour batter*; **lansungan** – *steamer*; **katsa** – *muslin cloth*; **pugon** – *oven*; **ipa** – *rice husk*
- **inilako** – *was peddled* (root: *lako* = to peddle)
- **tagapagmana** – *heir* (root: *mana* = inheritance)

**Grammar:**
- *Pinaka-* + adjective = superlative: *tanyag* (famous) → *pinakatanyag* (most famous).
- *Kasin-* + adjective = equality: *kasinlaki ng pizza* = "as big as a pizza".
- *Pinasingawan* = *pa-* + *singaw* + *-an* with *-in-*: "was caused to be steamed". *Pinaaapoy* (is fired) follows the same causative pattern in the ongoing aspect.
- *Tagapag-* + root names a role: *tagapagmana* = "the one who inherits".

**Translation:**
> The city’s most famous product is Puto Biñán, a steamed rice cake as big as a pizza, soft and moist, topped with grated cheese, bits of salted egg and a little condensed milk. According to the town’s story, it was created almost a century ago by a housewife, Petronila “Aling Nila” Samaniego. She tried out a batter of rice flour, brown sugar and baking powder, poured it onto a steamer covered with muslin, and steamed it in an oven fired with rice husks. Her puto was first peddled to passengers at the old Biñán railway station in Barangay Santo Niño; other puto makers followed, and her recipe is still cooked today in the family’s original bakery, now managed by her heir.

**3. The Puto Latik Festival and the maglalatik**

> *Mula noong 2011, sabay na ipinagdiriwang ng lungsod ang kakanin at ang sayaw nito sa Puto Latik Festival.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sabay** – *together, at the same time*
- **ipinagdiriwang** – *is celebrated* (root: *diwang* = feast)
- **maglalatik** – *the latik dance* (root: *latik* = sweet coconut curd)
- **isinayaw** – *was danced* (root: *sayaw* = dance)
- **handog** – *offering*
- **bao ng niyog** – *coconut shell*
- **dibdib, balakang, hita** – *chest, hips, thighs*
- **kunwaring labanan** – *mock battle* (*kunwari* = pretend)
- **latak** – *residue, dregs*; **gata** – *coconut milk*
- **pagkakasundo** – *reconciliation* (root: *sundo*, *magkasundo* = to agree)
- **karosa** – *float, carriage*
- **ginawaran** – *was awarded* (root: *gawad* = award)
- **hinirang** – *was named, was chosen* (root: *hirang*)

**Grammar:**
- *Maglalatik* = *mag-* + repeated first syllable + *latik*: a noun for the person (or here the dance) associated with an activity, "the latik-fighter".
- *Isinayaw* = *i-* + *-in-* + *sayaw*: object focus, completed ("was danced").
- *Dating idinaraos tuwing Pebrero* – "formerly held every February": *dati* + linker *-ng*; *idinaraos* shows the reduplicated ongoing aspect of *idaos* (to hold an event), with *d → r* between vowels.
- *Ginawaran* = *-in-* + *gawad* + *-an* (d → r): "was given an award".

**Translation:**
> Since 2011, the city has celebrated its rice cake and its dance together in the Puto Latik Festival. Its name joins puto and the maglalatik, a folk dance said to have been first danced by the men of Loma and Zapote as an offering to San Isidro Labrador in his procession. In this dance, men with halved coconut shells tied to their chests, hips and thighs stage a mock battle over latik, the sweet curd of boiled coconut milk: Moros in red trousers against Christians in blue, in a fight that ends in reconciliation. Formerly held every February, the festival was moved to the feast of San Isidro on 15 May, and now lasts nine days, from 15 to 23 May. It begins with the procession of the image of Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje, followed by the inter-barangay street-dance contest, the float parade and the Puto Biñán cooking contest, and ends with the Pistang Intsik and the Santacruzan. It has been given the Pearl Awards of the Department of Tourism and the tourism officers several times – in 2018, 2019 and 2022 – and was named the best cultural festival in the city category in 2025.

**4. The rhythm of the year**

> *May sariling indayog ang bawat araw.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **minamarkahan** – *is marked* (root: *marka* = mark)
- **pagdiriwang** – *celebration* (root: *diwang*)
- **indayog** – *rhythm, sway*
- **pamamasyal** – *strolling, going for a walk* (root: *pasyal*)

**Grammar:**
- *Minamarkahan* = *-in-* + repeated syllable + *marka* + *-han*: ongoing object focus.
- *Pamamasyal* = *pang-* + repeated syllable + *pasyal* (p → m): the noun for an ongoing activity.

**Translation:**
> In this way, the year in Biñán is marked by celebrations – the Araw ng Biñán in the first days of February and the fiesta of San Isidro in May – and each day has its own rhythm: Mass at the church of San Isidro Labrador, the market in the morning, and strolling on the plaza in the evening.
