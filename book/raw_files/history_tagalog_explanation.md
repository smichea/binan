# The Book of Biñan / Ang Aklat ng Biñan

Explanation of tagalog text for english only speakers

---
## Introduction / Panimula

This companion follows the Tagalog edition of the book, *Ang Aklat ng Biñan*, section by section; the English edition, *The Book of Biñan*, has the same numbered sections. For each numbered section, it quotes the first Tagalog sentence, lists the new words with their roots, explains one or two points of grammar and gives a translation. At the end of each chapter, a table lists the verbs with their root, aspect and focus. Read a section in the book, then here, then read the Tagalog again aloud.

### Tagalog grammar in one page

**Markers.** A small word before each noun shows its role in the sentence:

- *ang* (*si* before a name) marks the topic, the noun the sentence is about;
- *ng* (*ni* before a name) marks the actor or the object that is not the topic, and possession;
- *sa* (*kay* before a name) marks place, direction and the receiver.

**Focus.** The verb changes its form to show which noun is the topic (*ang*):

- actor focus (*-um-*, *mag-*, *ma-*): *Bumili si Nila ng bigas.* – Nila bought rice.
- object focus (*-in-*, *i-*): *Binili ni Nila ang bigas.* – The rice was bought by Nila.
- locative focus (*-an*): *Binilhan ni Nila ng bigas ang tindahan.* – Nila bought rice from the shop.

**Aspect.** Each verb shows whether the action is completed, ongoing or yet to come:

| Affix and root | Completed | Ongoing | Future | Infinitive |
|:---------------|:----------|:--------|:-------|:-----------|
| -um- + bili (buy) | bumili | bumibili | bibili | bumili |
| mag- + luto (cook) | nagluto | nagluluto | magluluto | magluto |
| -in- + bili | binili | binibili | bibilhin | bilhin |
| i- + sulat (write) | isinulat | isinusulat | isusulat | isulat |

**Repeated syllables.** Repeating the first syllable of the root marks the ongoing and the future (*nagluluto*, *bibili*), and it also makes adjectives plural (*malalaking bahay*, “big houses”).

**Linkers.** A word and the word that describes it are joined by *na*, or by *-ng* after a vowel or *n*: *malaking bahay*, “a big house”; *bahay na bato*, “a stone house”.

**Word order.** The verb usually comes first: *Dumating ang tren*, “The train arrived”. Formal writing often puts the topic first with *ay*: *Ang tren ay dumating*.

**Other affixes in this book:**

- *pa-* makes a verb causative: *pinabagsak*, “caused to fall, brought down”;
- *ma-* / *na-* expresses ability or an action that just happens: *nasakop*, “was occupied”; *mabibili*, “can be bought”;
- *maka-* / *naka-* expresses ability, and with colours or clothes, “wearing”: *nakapula*, “in red”;
- *pang-* makes adjectives meaning “for”: *pambata*, “for children”;
- *ka-* … *-an* makes abstract or collective nouns: *kasaysayan*, “history”; *kabundukan*, “uplands”.

## Geography / Heograpiya

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. Between the lake and the plateau · Sa pagitan ng lawa at ng talampas**
> *Ang Biñan ay sumasakop sa isang makitid na lupain na mga 40 hanggang 44 kilometro kuwadrado sa kanlurang pampang ng Lawa ng Laguna.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sumasakop** – *occupies, covers* (root: *sakop* = scope, territory)
- **makitid** – *narrow* (root: *kitid*)
- **lupain** – *land, territory* (root: *lupa* = soil, land)
- **pampang** – *shore, bank*; **baybayin** – *coast, shoreline*
- **dahan-dahang tumataas** – *rises gently* (*dahan-dahan* = slowly; root of the verb: *taas* = height)
- **talampas** – *plateau*
- **hangganan** – *boundary* (root: *hangga* = limit)
- **maaliwalas** – *clear, bright (weather)*
- **abot-tanaw** – *horizon* (literally "reach of sight": *abot* + *tanaw*)
- **gilid** – *edge, rim*; **nagtatago** – *hides* (root: *tago*)

**Grammar:**
- *Sumasakop* = *-um-* + repeated syllable + *sakop*: ongoing actor focus.
- *Humigit-kumulang* is a fixed pair of *-um-* verbs used as an adverb: "going over, going under" = "about".
- *Kanlurang pampang* – the linker *-ng* joins the adjective *kanluran* (western) to the noun.

**Translation:**
> Biñan occupies a narrow strip of land of some 40 to 44 square kilometres (43.5 according to the city, 40.27 according to the national statistics of 2013) on the western shore of Laguna de Bay, some 35 kilometres south of Manila. From the lakeshore, barely 2 metres above sea level, the land rises gently westward toward the plateau of Carmona and Silang; the Poblacion stands at about 12 metres. On a clear day, volcanoes close the horizon: Mount Makiling, Mount Banahaw behind it, and to the southwest the Tagaytay ridge, the rim of the caldera that hides Taal Volcano, only 38 km away.

---

**2. Two plates · Dalawang tectonic plate**
> *Ang tanawing ito ay hinubog ng isa sa pinakamagulong bahagi ng mundo.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **tanawin** – *landscape, view* (root: *tanaw* = to look at from afar)
- **hinubog** – *was shaped, was moulded* (root: *hubog* = shape)
- **pinakamagulo** – *most turbulent, most restless* (root: *gulo* = disorder)
- **kapuluan** – *archipelago* (root: *pulo* = island)
- **naiipit** – *is squeezed, is caught* (root: *ipit* = pinch)
- **nagkakalapit** – *come closer to each other* (root: *lapit* = near)
- **sumusuot** – *slips under, goes under* (root: *suot* = to slip into)
- **pahalang** – *horizontal, sideways*
- **sinasalo** – *is caught, is absorbed* (root: *salo* = to catch)
- **banggaan** – *collision* (root: *bangga* = to bump)

**Grammar:**
- *Hinubog* = *-in-* + *hubog*: object focus, completed – "the landscape was shaped by…". The doer comes after *ng*.
- *Pinaka-* + *magulo* = superlative "most restless".
- *Naiipit* = *na-* + *i* + *ipit*: an involuntary, ongoing state – "is (being) caught".
- *Nagkakalapit* = *nag-ka-* + repeated syllable + *lapit*: a reciprocal action in progress, "getting closer to each other".

**Translation:**
> This landscape was shaped by one of the most restless parts of the world. The Philippine archipelago is made of crustal pieces caught between two great tectonic plates, the Philippine Sea Plate to the east and the Sunda (Eurasian) Plate to the west, which come closer to each other by several centimetres a year. Each plate slips under the islands in its own deep trench, while the sideways part of the movement is absorbed by the Philippine Fault. Because of this collision, the country is on the Pacific "Ring of Fire".

---

**3. Born of fire · Isinilang sa apoy**
> *Ang gitnang bahagi mismo ng lawa ay isang kaldera ng bulkan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sinturon** – *belt*
- **nababanat** – *is stretched* (root: *banat* = to stretch)
- **umaakyat** – *rises, climbs* (root: *akyat*)
- **tangway** – *peninsula*
- **pagsabog** – *eruption, explosion* (root: *sabog* = to burst)
- **nagbabagang agos** – *glowing flow* (root: *baga* = ember)
- **patong** – *layer*
- **kinatatayuan** – *the place where (something) stands* (root: *tayo* = to stand)
- **namamatay** – *dies out* (root: *patay*)
- **bukal** – *spring (of water)*; **mainit** – *hot*
- **pumutok** – *erupted, burst* (root: *putok*)
- **sinuspinde** – *were suspended* (from Spanish *suspender*)

**Grammar:**
- *Kinatatayuan* = *ka-* + *-in-* + repeated syllable + *tayo* + *-an*: a noun of place, "the very spot where it stands".
- *Hindi pa namamatay ang apoy* – "the fire has not yet died out": *hindi pa* + ongoing aspect = "not yet".
- *Umulan ng abo* – "it rained ash": the weather verb *umulan* can take *ng* + what falls.

**Translation:**
> Biñan is at the northern end of the Macolod Corridor, a belt of volcanoes where the crust is stretched and magma rises easily. The central part of the lake itself is a volcanic caldera, left by great eruptions between about two million and thirty thousand years ago. The ash and glowing flows of those eruptions built the layers of tuff on which western Biñan stands. The fire has not yet died out: hot springs still bubble at Los Baños and Calamba, and when Taal erupted on 12 January 2020, ash rained on Biñan and classes were suspended.

---

**4. The fault · Ang fault**
> *May fault din sa ilalim ng lungsod.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sa ilalim ng** – *under, beneath*
- **dumaraan** – *passes through* (root: *daan* = way)
- **magdulot** – *to cause, to bring about* (root: *dulot*)
- **lakas** – *strength, magnitude*
- **niyanig** – *was shaken* (root: *yanig* = tremor)
- **mapanira** – *destructive* (root: *sira* = broken)
- **naghahanda** – *is preparing* (root: *handa* = ready)
- **ginanap** – *was held* (root: *ganap* = to take place)

**Grammar:**
- *Niyanig* = *-in-* + *yanig*; before a *y*, the infix often appears as *ni-*: "was shaken".
- *Mapanira* = *mapang-* + *sira* (s → n): an adjective describing a tendency, "destructive".
- *Ginanap* = *-in-* + *ganap*: object focus, completed – "the drill was held".

**Translation:**
> There is also a fault beneath the city. The West Valley Fault, about 100 km long, passes through Biñan in the barangays of Malamig, San Francisco (Halang) and Biñan. It can cause an earthquake of magnitude 7.2 and moves roughly every 400 to 600 years; its last strong movement was in 1658. The region has been shaken many times since: according to the old catalogue of earthquakes, on 20 July 1880 an earthquake "of destructive violence" struck "the towns around Lake Bay, especially to its south and west". Today most earthquakes felt in Biñan are weak, but the city prepares for the "Big One": on 28 September 2016 it hosted the national earthquake drill.

---

**5. The lake · Ang lawa**
> *Iisa lamang ang labasan nito, ang Ilog Pasig.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **napakababaw** – *very shallow* (root: *babaw*)
- **lalim** – *depth*
- **palanggana** – *basin* (literally "washbasin")
- **labasan** – *outlet, exit* (root: *labas* = outside)
- **tag-araw** – *dry season*; **tag-ulan** – *rainy season*
- **pabalik** – *backward, returning* (root: *balik*)
- **pinalulubog** – *makes (something) sink* (root: *lubog*)
- **luminaw** – *became clear* (root: *linaw*)
- **lansangan** – *road, street*
- **hapag-kainan** – *dining table* (here: *pantry, source of food*)
- **huli** – *catch (of fish)*
- **baklad** – *fish pen*; **kulungan** – *cage*
- **binabantayan** – *monitored, watched* (root: *bantay* = guard)

**Grammar:**
- *Iisa* = repeated *isa*: "only one".
- *Napaka-* + adjective = "very": *napakababaw* = "very shallow".
- *Pinalulubog* = causative *pa-* + *lubog* in the ongoing aspect with *-in-*: "is made to sink".

**Translation:**
> Laguna de Bay is the largest lake in the Philippines but very shallow. Twenty-one rivers flow into it, and it has only one outlet, the Pasig River. In the dry season, when the lake falls to sea level, the Pasig flows backward and seawater enters the lake; the salt makes the mud sink, and the water turns clear for a few months. For centuries the lake was Biñan's other road and its pantry: in the early 1960s, 95 per cent of the catch was three native fish – ayungin, biyang puti and kanduli – now replaced by tilapia and bangus raised in pens and cages. The Biñan River is one of the lake's monitored tributaries – and it often lacks oxygen because of the dirty water it carries.

---

**6. When the lake was the sea · Noong dagat pa ang lawa**
> *Mga 6,600 hanggang 4,700 taon na ang nakararaan, bahagi ng dagat ang lawa.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sa paglipas ng panahon** – *over time* (root: *lipas* = to pass)
- **panahon ng yelo** – *ice age* (*yelo* = ice)
- **tuyong lupa** – *dry land*
- **natunaw** – *melted* (root: *tunaw*)
- **lumampas** – *went beyond* (root: *lampas*)
- **hinukay** – *was dug, was drilled* (root: *hukay* = to dig)
- **maalat** – *salty*; **bahagyang maalat** – *slightly salty, brackish*; **tubig-tabang** – *fresh water*
- **umatras** – *retreated* (root: *atras*)
- **iniangat** – *was raised* (root: *angat* = lift)

**Grammar:**
- *Ang nakararaan* – "(the time) that has passed": *naka-* + repeated syllable + *raan* (*daan*, with d → r). Used in "… taon na ang nakararaan" = "… years ago".
- *Iniangat ang lupa … ng paggalaw ng fault* – object focus: the land (*ang lupa*) was raised by (*ng*) the movement of the fault.

**Translation:**
> The shape of this shore has changed over time. In the ice ages, the sea rose and fell by more than a hundred metres; 20,000 years ago it was about 120 metres lower than today, but Luzon remained an island. When the ice melted, the sea came back and went beyond where it is now. A mud core drilled in the West Bay shows that about 6,600 to 4,700 years ago the lake was part of the sea, a salty lagoon connected to Manila Bay. It became brackish about 4,100 years ago and fresh about 3,000 years ago, when the sea retreated and the land between Muntinlupa and Parañaque was raised by the movement of the West Valley Fault itself.

---

**7. Seasons and typhoons · Mga panahon at bagyo**
> *Dumaraan ang mga bagyo sa rehiyon mula Hunyo hanggang Disyembre.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **habagat** – *southwest monsoon*
- **bagyo** – *typhoon*
- **pinakamatindi** – *the most severe* (root: *tindi* = intensity)
- **sa alaala ng mga tao** – *in living memory* (*alaala* = memory)
- **nagpaangat** – *caused to rise* (root: *angat*)
- **panukat** – *gauge, measuring instrument* (root: *sukat* = measure)
- **nagpalubog** – *submerged* (root: *lubog*)
- **humupa** – *to subside* (root: *hupa*)
- **naranasan** – *experienced* (root: *danas*, d → r)

**Grammar:**
- *Nagpa-* + root = causative actor focus, completed: *nagpaangat* (made rise), *nagpalubog* (made sink).
- *Naranasan* = *na-* + *danas* + *-an* (d → r): "was experienced".

**Translation:**
> Today Biñan's climate is tropical with two clear seasons: the dry season from November to April and the wet season from May to October, when the habagat brings about 90 per cent of the year's rain. At Sangley Point the normal yearly rainfall is about 2,080 mm. The most severe typhoon in living memory was Ondoy in September 2009, which raised the lake to about 14 metres on its gauge and submerged 192 lakeshore barangays for weeks. Lakeshore towns experienced it again in 2020 with Ulysses and in October 2024 with Kristine.

---

**8. A changing climate · Nagbabagong klima**
> *Nagbabago rin ang klima.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nagbabago** – *is changing* (root: *bago* = new)
- **pag-init** – *warming* (root: *init* = heat)
- **naitala** – *was recorded* (root: *tala* = record, note)
- **rekonstruksiyon** – *reconstruction*
- **inaasahan** – *is expected* (root: *asa* = hope, expectation)
- **ramdam** – *felt*
- **lumulubog** – *is sinking* (root: *lubog*)
- **pagsipsip** – *the sucking up, pumping* (root: *sipsip* = to suck)
- **panganib** – *danger, threat*
- **nagpapatagal** – *makes (something) last longer* (root: *tagal* = duration)

**Grammar:**
- *Naitala* = *na-* + *i-* + *tala*: completed, object focus with the idea of something achieved – "was (able to be) recorded".
- *Nagpapatagal* = *nag-* + *pa* + *pa* + *tagal*: ongoing causative – "keeps making longer".

**Translation:**
> The climate is changing too. PAGASA measured a warming of about 0.65 °C across the Philippines from 1951 to 2010, and 2024 was the hottest year recorded in the country. NASA's climate reconstruction for Biñan's own location shows the same: the average temperature in 2015–2024 was about 0.4 °C higher than in 1981–1990, and 2024 was the warmest year of the series. PAGASA expects about 2 °C more warming by mid-century. The heat is already felt: on 15 April 2025 the heat index reached 50 °C at Los Baños. And the water is rising: around Manila Bay, the sea rose by about a metre relative to the land from 1964 to 2002, mainly because the ground is sinking as too much groundwater is pumped out – a threat that also makes the lakeshore floods last longer.

---

**9. Fertile land, growing city · Matabang lupa, lumalaking lungsod**
> *Pinataba ng abo ng bulkan, ng banlik ng ilog at ng patag na lupa ang Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **pinataba** – *made fertile* (root: *taba* = fat)
- **banlik** – *silt*
- **patag** – *flat, level*
- **luad** – *clay*
- **palayan** – *rice field* (root: *palay* = rice plant)
- **tubuhan** – *sugarcane field* (root: *tubo* = sugarcane)
- **pinatutubigan** – *is irrigated* (root: *tubig* = water)
- **riles** – *railway*

**Grammar:**
- *Palay* + *-an* → *palayan*, *tubo* + *-an* → *tubuhan* (o → u): the suffix *-an* makes "a field of…".
- *Pinatutubigan* = *pa-* + *tubig* + *-an* with *-in-* and reduplication: "is (regularly) given water", i.e. irrigated.

**Translation:**
> Volcanic ash, river silt and flat land made Biñan fertile: the soils of this part of the Cavite–Laguna plateau, formed on tuff, are mostly deep clays and clay loams, and the plain near the lake is almost level. For centuries this land fed rice fields and sugarcane fields watered by canals – the *patubigan* that gave its name to Barangay Tubigan. In the last fifty years the expressways, the railway and the industrial parks have turned these fields into a city, and all 24 barangays are now urban. The next chapters tell how people came to live on this shore, between the fire, the fault and the lake.

## Prehistory / Sinaunang Panahon

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. A long walk · Isang mahabang paglalakbay**
> *Ang kuwento ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng Lawa ng Laguna ay nagsisimula sa malayong Aprika.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nagsisimula** – *begins* (root: *simula* = beginning)
- **isinilang** – *was born* (root: *silang* = birth)
- **sinaunang tao** – *ancient people, early humans* (*sinauna* = ancient, from *una* = first)
- **kasangkapan** – *tool, implement*
- **lakarin** – *to walk across, to reach on foot* (root: *lakad* = walk)
- **nadudugtong** – *is joined, gets connected* (root: *dugtong*)
- **di-nakikitang hangganan** – *invisible boundary* (*di* = not; *nakikita* = can be seen)
- **kailanman ay hindi** – *never*
- **tawirin** – *to cross* (root: *tawid*, d → r)
- **balsa** – *raft*; **nang di-sinasadya** – *by accident*; **nang sinasadya** – *on purpose* (root: *sadya* = intention)
- **walang nakaaalam** – *nobody knows* (root: *alam* = know)

**Grammar:**
- *Nagsisimula* = *nag-* + repeated syllable *si* + *simula*: ongoing actor focus.
- *Isinilang* = *i-* + *-in-* + *silang*: object focus, completed – "was born".
- *Lakarin* and *tawirin* are object-focus infinitives in *-in*. When the root ends in *d*, it becomes *r* before the suffix: *lakad → lakarin*, *tawid → tawirin*.
- *Nakaaalam* = *naka-* + repeated syllable *a* + *alam*: "one who knows" – with *wala* (none): "no one knows".

**Translation:**
> The story of the first people on the shores of Laguna de Bay begins in faraway Africa, where the human family was born. About 1.8 million years ago, early humans had already left Africa: their bones and tools were found at Dmanisi in Georgia. *Homo erectus* reached Java about 1.3 million years ago, and no earlier than 1.5 million years ago. Java, Sumatra and Borneo could then be reached on foot, but further east, beyond the Wallace Line, the islands were always separated from the continent by deep sea. Luzon was never joined to any continent. To reach it, early humans had to cross the sea – by accident on rafts of vegetation, or on purpose; nobody knows.

---

**2. The first visitors of Luzon · Ang mga unang dumalaw sa Luzon**
> *Ngunit dumating sila.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nakatagpo** – *found, came upon* (root: *tagpo* = meeting)
- **kalansay** – *skeleton*
- **nawalang uri** – *extinct species* (*nawala* = lost; *uri* = kind, species)
- **bakas** – *trace, mark*; **hiwa** – *cut*
- **binasag** – *was broken, was smashed* (root: *basag*)
- **utak** – *marrow* (also: *brain*)
- **baluktot** – *bent, curved*
- **pinangalanan** – *was named* (root: *ngalan* = name)
- **pagtataya** – *estimate, assessment* (root: *taya* = estimate)
- **nag-iisa** – *alone* (root: *isa* = one)
- **umunlad** – *developed, evolved* (root: *unlad* = progress)

**Grammar:**
- *Pinangalanan* = *-in-* + *pangalan* + *-an*: object focus, completed – "(they) named it".
- *Hindi bababa sa* – "not lower than" = "at least" (*baba* = low, with reduplication for the future/contemplated aspect).
- *Nag-iisa* = *nag-* + repeated *i* + *isa*: "being one", i.e. alone.

**Translation:**
> But they came. At Rizal, Kalinga, in the Cagayan Valley, archaeologists found 57 stone tools and the skeleton of an extinct rhinoceros whose bones bear cut marks and were smashed to reach the marrow. They are about 709,000 years old – the oldest known trace of humans in the Philippines. Much later, in Callao Cave, researchers found the teeth and bones of a small human with curved finger and toe bones; in 2019 they named it a new species, *Homo luzonensis*. It lived at least 50,000 years ago, perhaps more than 130,000 years ago. Like *Homo floresiensis*, it evolved alone on an island for a very long time.

---

**3. Our species arrives · Dumating ang ating uri**
> *Ang sarili nating uri, ang Homo sapiens, ay umalis sa Aprika nang mas huli.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sarili nating uri** – *our own species* (*sarili* = self; *natin* = our, inclusive)
- **umalis** – *left, departed* (root: *alis*)
- **nagpinta** – *painted* (root: *pinta*)
- **baboy-ramo** – *wild pig*
- **buto ng binti** – *shin bone* (*binti* = leg, calf)
- **palugit** – *margin, allowance*
- **bungo** – *skull*
- **bagong dating** – *newcomers*
- **henetika** – *genetics*
- **alon ng pandarayuhan** – *wave of migration* (*alon* = wave; root of *pandarayuhan*: *dayo* = stranger, to migrate)
- **ninuno** – *ancestor*; **inapo** – *descendant*

**Grammar:**
- *Nating* = *natin* + linker *-ng*: "our (inclusive)" placed before the noun: *sarili nating uri*.
- *Pandarayuhan* = *pang-* + repeated *da* + *dayo* + *-han* (d → r): the noun for the whole process of migrating.

**Translation:**
> Our own species, *Homo sapiens*, left Africa later, perhaps 70,000 to 50,000 years ago, following the coasts of Asia. On Sulawesi, people painted a wild pig on a cave wall at least 45,500 years ago. In the Philippines, the oldest bone of a modern human is a shin bone from Tabon Cave in Palawan, about 47,000 years old; the famous skull fragment called "Tabon Man" is younger, about 16,500 years. The newcomers met older humans: the Ayta Magbukon of Bataan carry more Denisovan DNA than any other people. Genetics shows at least five waves of migration over the last 50,000 years, beginning with the ancestors of the Agta, Ayta and Dumagat. Their descendants, the Dumagat and Remontado, still live in the Sierra Madre east of the lake.

**4. A different land · Isang ibang lupain**
> *Hindi ganito ang lupaing nakilala ng mga taong ito.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **ganito** – *like this*
- **nakilala** – *knew, got to know* (root: *kilala* = known)
- **pagtabang** – *the becoming fresh (of water)* (root: *tabang* = tasteless, fresh)

**Grammar:**
- *Hindi ganito ang lupaing nakilala nila* – "The land they knew was not like this": the predicate *hindi ganito* comes first, as is usual in Tagalog.

**Translation:**
> The land these people knew was not like this. In the last ice age, about 20,000 years ago, the sea was about 120 metres lower; when the ice melted, the sea came back, and from about 6,600 to 4,700 years ago the lake in front of Biñan was part of the sea, a salt lagoon connected to Manila Bay. It became the freshwater lake we know only about 3,000 years ago.

**5. The seafarers · Ang mga manlalayag**
> *Wikang Austronesyo ang Tagalog.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **manlalayag** – *seafarer, sailor* (root: *layag* = sail)
- **palayok** – *clay pot*; **mapula** – *reddish*
- **palamuti** – *ornament*
- **bangkang may katig** – *outrigger canoe* (*katig* = outrigger)
- **butil** – *grain*
- **itinanim** – *was planted* (root: *tanim*)
- **tinipon** – *was gathered* (root: *tipon*)
- **lambat** – *net* (here: network)
- **kamag-anak** – *relative*

**Grammar:**
- *Manlalayag* = *mang-* + repeated syllable + *layag*: the person who does the action, "one who sails". Compare *mangangalakal* (trader).
- *Itinanim* (i- + -in- + tanim) and *tinipon* (-in- + tipon) are completed object-focus forms: "was planted", "was gathered".

**Translation:**
> About the time the lake turned fresh, new people arrived by sea. Between about 4,500 and 3,500 years ago, seafarers speaking Austronesian languages came down from Taiwan, through Batanes, to northern Luzon. In the Cagayan Valley they left reddish pottery like that of eastern Taiwan and ornaments of Taiwanese jade. They knew the outrigger canoe and perhaps rice: the oldest rice grain found in the Philippines, at Andarayan, is about 3,400 years old, although we do not know whether it was planted or only gathered. Some scholars, following Solheim, prefer to see a network of sea trade. Either way, Tagalog is an Austronesian language, a distant cousin of Malay, Javanese, Malagasy and Hawaiian.

**6. Traces around the lake · Mga bakas sa paligid ng lawa**
> *May mga bakas ng sinaunang panahong ito na natagpuan sa paligid ng lawa.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **natagpuan** – *were found* (root: *tagpo*)
- **tinatayang** – *estimated* (root: *taya*)
- **talim** – *blade*
- **bangin** – *cliff*
- **iniulat** – *was reported* (root: *ulat* = report)
- **inukit** – *was carved* (root: *ukit* = carving)
- **idineklara** – *was declared* (from Spanish *declarar*)
- **Pambansang Yamang Pangkultura** – *National Cultural Treasure*
- **nakahukay** – *dug up (by chance)* (root: *hukay*)
- **tapayan** – *large earthen jar*; **tapayang libingan** – *burial jar*
- **kabilang buhay** – *afterlife* (literally "the other life")

**Grammar:**
- *Nakahukay* = *naka-* + *hukay*: the actor managed to dig something up, here by chance.
- *Inukit* = *-in-* + *ukit*; with a root starting with a vowel, *-in-* goes in front: *in + ukit*.

**Translation:**
> Traces of this ancient time have been found around the lake. In the valleys of Baras, in Rizal, stone tools are estimated from their shape to be 50,000 to 20,000 years old, and small blades of obsidian and chert come from Baras, Tanay and Pililla. On a tuff cliff in Binangonan, the painter Carlos "Botong" Francisco documented in 1965 more than a hundred figures carved in the rock (179 according to the National Museum): the Angono–Binangonan Petroglyphs, declared a National Cultural Treasure in 1973; museum scientists have suggested a date around 3000 BCE, but their age cannot be established. In 2013, in Barangay Puypuy, Bay, a family dug up hundreds of pieces of earthen jars in their yard; archaeologists of the University of the Philippines identified them as burial jars perhaps 2,800 years old. Far away in Palawan, the Manunggul Jar shows on its lid two figures in a boat carrying a soul to the afterlife.

**7. And in Biñan? · At sa Biñan?**
> *Wala pang natatagpuan o nahuhukay na sinaunang pook sa Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **wala pa** – *not yet (any)*
- **pook** – *place, site*
- **nangangahulugan** – *means* (root: *kahulugan* = meaning)
- **banlik** – *silt*
- **iulat** – *to report*
- **Pambansang Museo** – *National Museum*
- **Batas Republika** – *Republic Act*

**Grammar:**
- *Wala pang natatagpuan* – "nothing has been found yet": *wala* + *pa* + an ongoing-aspect verb gives the sense "not yet".
- *Dapat iulat* – "must be reported": *dapat* (should, must) + the object-focus infinitive *iulat*.

**Translation:**
> And in Biñan? No ancient site has yet been found or excavated in Biñan, nor anywhere on the western shore of the lake. This does not mean that no one lived here: the old shore lies under metres of river silt and lake mud, part of it was under the sea six thousand years ago, and the city grew quickly over the fields before anyone looked. Any old pottery, stone tool or bone found while digging should be reported to the National Museum, as the National Cultural Heritage Act (Republic Act No. 10066) requires.

**8. Life on the lakeshore before writing · Buhay sa baybayin ng lawa bago ang pagsulat**
> *Maaari lamang nating isipin ang buhay ng mga unang tao sa baybayin ng lawa.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **maaari lamang** – *can only*
- **isipin** – *to imagine, to think of* (root: *isip* = mind)
- **tagapagtala** – *chronicler, recorder* (root: *tala* = record)
- **gabi, ube, saging** – *taro, yam, banana*
- **haligi** – *post, pillar*
- **tinahi** – *sewn* (root: *tahi*)
- **tarugo** – *wooden peg*
- **pamayanan** – *community*
- **pinararangalan** – *are honoured* (root: *dangal* = honour, d → r)
- **inililibing** – *are buried* (root: *libing*)
- **ibubunyag** – *will reveal* (root: *bunyag*)
- **balang araw** – *one day (in the future)*

**Grammar:**
- *Tagapag-* + *tala* = "one whose role is to record": a chronicler.
- *Pinararangalan* = *pa-* + *dangal* + *-an*, with *-in-* and reduplication (d → r): ongoing object focus, "are (regularly) honoured".
- *Ibubunyag* = *i-* + repeated *bu* + *bunyag*: **future** (contemplated) aspect – "will reveal".

**Translation:**
> We can only imagine the life of the first people of the lakeshore, using what archaeology found nearby and what the first Spanish chroniclers saw a few centuries later: fishing in the lake; gardens of taro, yam and banana, and later rice; houses raised on posts; boats of planks sewn and pegged together, like the balangay of Butuan, the oldest about 1,700 years old – the boat that gave its name to the barangay, the community; spirits and ancestors, the anito, honoured in rituals; and the dead buried in jars. In the Metal Age, from about 500 BCE, iron tools, glass beads and gold came through trade. By about the year 900, the lake was already part of a wider world of trade and writing – as a copper plate found at Lumban would one day reveal.

## Pre-Colonial Era / Panahong Pre-Kolonyal

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. What we know, and how · Ang ating nalalaman, at kung paano**
> *Sa loob ng anim na siglo bago dumating ang mga Espanyol, walang dokumentong bumabanggit sa Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sa loob ng** – *within, during*
- **siglo** – *century*
- **bumabanggit** – *mentions* (root: *banggit*)
- **paghuhukay** – *excavation, digging* (root: *hukay*)
- **nagmumula** – *comes from* (root: *mula*)
- **pinagsasaluhan** – *shared (among them)* (root: *salo* = to share a meal)
- **pamayanan** – *settlement, community*
- **nakakalat** – *scattered* (root: *kalat*)
- **nakapangkat** – *grouped* (root: *pangkat* = group)
- **pinamumunuan** – *led, ruled* (root: *puno* = head, leader)

**Grammar:**
- *Bumabanggit* = *-um-* + repeated syllable + *banggit*: ongoing actor focus, "(that) mentions".
- *Pinagsasaluhan* = *pinag-* + repeated syllable + *salo* + *-han*: an action done together by many people, ongoing.
- *Nakakalat* and *nakapangkat* = *naka-* + root: a resulting state ("in a scattered state", "in groups").

**Translation:**
> For six centuries before the Spaniards came, no document mentions Biñan and no excavation has yet been made on its soil. What we know of life on this shore comes from its neighbours – Tabuco, Bay, Pila and Lumban – from Chinese and Spanish writers, and from the customs shared by the Tagalog communities of the region. The land of today's Biñan was most likely part of Tabuco. Its people lived in small settlements scattered along the lake and the rivers coming down from the hills: they fished, planted rice and vegetables, gathered fruit and hunted wild pig, and they were grouped in barangays led by datus.

**2. The copper plate of 900 · Ang platong tanso ng 900**
> *Mula sa lawang ito ang pinakamatandang nakasulat na dokumentong natagpuan sa Pilipinas.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **pinakamatanda** – *the oldest* (root: *tanda* = age)
- **nakasulat** – *written* (root: *sulat*)
- **manggagawa** – *worker* (root: *gawa* = work)
- **buhangin** – *sand*
- **bunganga ng ilog** – *river mouth* (*bunganga* = mouth, opening)
- **platong tanso** – *copper plate*
- **titik** – *letter, script*
- **katibayan ng pagbabayad** – *receipt, proof of payment* (*tibay* = firmness; *bayad* = payment)
- **pinalalaya** – *frees, sets free* (root: *laya*)
- **inapo** – *descendant*
- **nagngangalang** – *named* (root: *ngalan* = name)
- **utang** – *debt*
- **saksi** – *witness*
- **pinagtatalunan** – *is debated* (root: *talo* = to argue)
- **iniingatan** – *is kept, is preserved* (root: *ingat* = care)

**Grammar:**
- *Manggagawa* = *mang-* + repeated syllable + *gawa*: the person who does the work.
- *Pinalalaya* = causative *pa-* + *laya* with *-in-* and reduplication: "is (being) set free".
- *Pinagtatalunan* = *pinag-* + repeated syllable + *talo* + *-an* (o → u): ongoing, "is argued about".

**Translation:**
> The oldest written document found in the Philippines comes from this lake. In the late 1980s, a worker digging sand near the mouth of the Lumbang River found a thin copper plate covered with writing, dated 21 April 900. Written mostly in Old Malay, in the Kawi script of Java, the Laguna Copperplate Inscription is a receipt: on behalf of the chief of Tundun (Tondo), it frees the descendants of a man named Namwaran from a debt of one kati and eight suwarna (weights of gold), before witnesses from Pailah, Puliran and Binuangan. Scholars still debate where these places were; none of them is Biñan. But the plate shows that by the year 900 the lake belonged to a world of writing, gold, debts and titles linked to Java and Sumatra.

**3. Ma-i and the porcelain trade · Ang Ma-i at ang kalakalan ng porselana**
> *Bahagi rin ang lawa ng mga ruta ng kalakalan ng Asya.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **talaan** – *record, annal* (root: *tala*)
- **inilarawan** – *described* (root: *larawan* = picture)
- **nakadaong** – *anchored, moored* (root: *daong* = ship)
- **nagaganap** – *takes place* (root: *ganap*)
- **puting payong** – *white umbrella*
- **ipinagpapalit** – *are exchanged* (root: *palit*)
- **pautang** – *on credit* (root: *utang* = debt)
- **bulak** – *cotton*; **pagkit** – *wax*; **kabibi ng pagong** – *tortoiseshell*; **nganga** – *betel*
- **tingga** – *lead (metal)*; **karayom** – *needle*
- **iminungkahi** – *proposed, suggested* (root: *mungkahi*)
- **kaakit-akit** – *attractive* (root: *akit*)
- **napatutunayan** – *proven* (root: *tunay* = true)
- **pagsusunog ng bangkay** – *cremation* (*sunog* = burn; *bangkay* = corpse)
- **pabigat ng lambat** – *net weight*; **ikiran ng sinulid** – *spindle whorl*

**Grammar:**
- *Ipinagpapalit* = *ipag-* + *-in-* + repeated syllable + *palit*: ongoing object focus with *ipag-*, used for exchanging.
- *Hindi pa napatutunayan* – "not yet proven": *napa-* + reduplication + *tunay* + *-an* = can be shown to be true.

**Translation:**
> The lake was also part of Asia's trade routes. Chinese records mention merchants from a country called Ma-i as early as 982. In 1225, the Chinese official Zhao Rugua described Ma-i: big villages on both banks of a river, Chinese ships anchored before the "official place" where trade took place, local chiefs given white umbrellas, and goods exchanged on credit – cotton, wax, tortoiseshell, betel nut and cloth for porcelain, gold, iron pots, lead, glass beads and needles. Ma-i is usually placed on Mindoro, but Go Bon Juan proposed that it may be Ba-i, the town of Bay – an attractive but unproven idea. What is certain is that at Pinagbayanan, in Pila, archaeologists excavated some 241 graves of the 12th to 14th centuries, with Song and Yuan porcelain, cremations, net weights, spindle whorls and traces of rice farming, cotton weaving and ironworking.

**4. Brunei and Islam · Ang Brunei at ang Islam**
> *Patungong Brunei at Islam ang pinakamalakas na ugnayan ng mundong Tagalog.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **patungo** – *toward*
- **ugnayan** – *relationship, tie* (root: *ugnay* = connection)
- **nadatnan** – *found (on arrival)* (root: *datna*, from *dating*)
- **nakipag-ugnayan** – *dealt with, had dealings with* (root: *ugnay*)
- **nangaral** – *preached* (root: *aral* = teaching)
- **nakababasa** – *can read* (root: *basa*)
- **pagbubuhos** – *pouring* (root: *buhos*)
- **paglilinis** – *cleansing, ablution* (root: *linis*)

A Spanish report of 1572 says that the coastal people did not eat pork "because they had dealings with the Moros of Burney, who preached to them a little of the teaching of Mahoma", and that some who had been to Brunei could read a few words of the Koran. The influence reached the language: the Tagalog dictionary of 1754 says that the word *binyag* came from Brunei, where it meant pouring water from above in a Muslim ablution, before it was used for Christian baptism.

**5. The society of the barangay · Ang lipunan ng barangay**
> *“Laging may mga pinuno ang bayang ito, na tinatawag nilang datos.”*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nilagdaan** – *signed* (root: *lagda* = signature)
- **kaugalian** – *custom* (root: *ugali* = habit)
- **pinuno** – *leader, chief*
- **namumuno** – *rules, leads* (root: *puno*)
- **malaya** – *free*
- **sumasagwan** – *paddles, rows* (root: *sagwan* = paddle)
- **ani** – *harvest*
- **ipagbili** – *to sell* (root: *bili*)
- **tubusin** – *to redeem, to buy back* (root: *tubos*)
- **palaisdaan** – *fishery* (root: *isda* = fish)
- **takdang hangganan** – *fixed boundaries*
- **takal** – *a measure of grain*
- **ari-arian** – *property*
- **bigay-kaya** – *dowry* (literally "a gift according to one's means")

**Grammar:**
- *Nilagdaan* = *-in-* + *lagda* + *-an*; before *l*, the infix becomes *ni-*: "(he) signed".
- *Tubusin* = *tubos* + *-in* (o → u): object-focus infinitive, "to redeem".

**Translation:**
> The best description of Tagalog society was written on this lake: the Franciscan Juan de Plasencia signed his *Customs of the Tagalogs* at Nagcarlán, Laguna, on 21 October 1589. "This people always had chiefs, whom they call datos," he wrote; each ruled "sometimes up to a hundred houses, sometimes fewer than thirty", a group "called in Tagalog a barangay". Below the datu were the free maharlika, who went to war with him and rowed for him; the aliping namamahay, who had their own houses and gave him part of their harvest; and the aliping sa gigilid, who lived in his house and could be sold. One could become a slave through war or debt, and buy one's freedom back with gold. Some chiefs owned fisheries with fixed boundaries and parts of rivers for markets; at Pila, free men paid their datu a hundred measures of rice a year. Women kept their own property, received a dowry and could divorce. Settlements could be fortified: in 1571 Cainta had about a thousand people behind thick bamboo, a wall and small cannons.

**6. Beliefs and the baybayin · Mga paniniwala at ang baybayin**
> *Sinasamba ng mga Tagalog si Bathala, “ang lumikha ng lahat ng bagay”.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sinasamba** – *is worshipped* (root: *samba*)
- **lumikha** – *created* (root: *likha*)
- **nagtatakda** – *sets, marks* (root: *takda*)
- **kinakatawan** – *is represented* (root: *katawan* = body)
- **pinangungunahan** – *is led* (root: *una* = first)
- **katayuan** – *rank, status* (root: *tayo* = to stand)
- **ipinagluluksa** – *are mourned* (root: *luksa* = mourning)

- **marunong bumasa at sumulat** – *literate, able to read and write*

**Grammar:**

**Translation:**
> The Tagalogs worshipped Bathala, "the maker of all things", honoured the sun, the moon and the stars that mark the farming year, and the spirits of their ancestors, the anito, represented by images called lic-ha. There were no temples: a house was prepared for each feast, led by a catalonan, often a woman of high rank. The dead were mourned for four days and laid to rest in a boat as a coffin. Men and women wore gold bracelets and anklets; around 1590 the Boxer Codex shows Tagalog couples in baro, saya and putong. Many could read and write in baybayin, which appears beside the Latin alphabet in the Doctrina Christiana of 1593, the first book printed in the Philippines.

**Grammar:**
- *Pinatahimik* = causative *pa-* + *tahimik* with *-in-*: "were made quiet". The Spanish word *pacificar* is often translated this way.
- *Ipinagkaloob* = *ipag-* + *-in-* + *kaloob*: completed object focus, "were given (as a grant)".
- *Tagapagtanggol* = *tagapag-* + *tanggol*: the one whose role is to defend.

**Translation:**
> This world changed in 1571. Legazpi settled in Manila in May. From 1578 the Franciscans founded missions all around the lake. According to a local tradition, Salcedo and the missionaries stopped on the site of Biñan, planted a cross and baptized its people, so the place was called Binyagan, the "place of baptism"; the documents of the time confirm Salcedo's journey around the lake in late 1571, but do not mention Biñan.

## Spanish Colonial Period / Panahon ng Kolonyalismong Espanyol

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. From encomienda to town · Mula encomienda hanggang bayan**
> *Bahagi noon ng Tabuco (Cabuyao) ang lupain ng Biñan.*

**Grammar:**

The land of Biñan was then part of Tabuco (Cabuyao). Its name is written in various ways in Spanish documents – Biñan, Biñang, Viñan – and a local tradition links it to *binyagan*, "the place of baptism".

**2. An estate for the college · Isang asyenda para sa kolehiyo**

**Translation:**

## World War II and Japanese Occupation / Ikalawang Digmaang Pandaigdig

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. The invasion · Ang paglusob**
> *Mula sa timog-silangan dumating ang digmaan sa Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **timog-silangan** – *south-east* (*timog* = south, *silangan* = east)
- **dumating** – *arrived* (root: *dating*)
- **salakayin** – *to attack* (root: *salakay*)
- **binomba** – *bombed* (from Spanish/English *bomba*)
- **lumunsad** – *landed, disembarked* (root: *lunsad*)
- **baybayin** – *coast, shore*
- **tumawid** – *to cross* (root: *tawid*)
- **pakanluran / pahilaga** – *westward / northward* (*pa-* + direction)
- **umatras** – *retreated* (root: *atras*)
- **unahan** – *front, vanguard* (root: *una* = first)
- **kabisera** – *capital city*
- **nasakop** – *was conquered, occupied* (root: *sakop*)
- **nakapangalan** – *named after* (root: *pangalan* = name)
- **mabihag** – *to be captured* (root: *bihag* = captive)
- **Martsa ng Kamatayan** – *Death March*

**Grammar:**
- *Mula sa timog-silangan dumating ang digmaan* – the place phrase is moved to the front for emphasis, without *ay*.
- *Nasakop ang Biñan nang walang labanan* – *na-* + *sakop*: a completed, non-volitional object focus ("ended up occupied"). *Nang walang* = "without".

**Translation:**
> War came to Biñan from the south-east. On 8 December 1941, a few hours after Pearl Harbor was attacked, Japanese planes bombed Luzon. On 24 December the Japanese 16th Division landed at Lamon Bay, on the Pacific coast of Tayabas, with orders to cross the mountains westward along Route 1, then go north along the shore of Laguna de Bay to Manila. The Filipino and American soldiers of the South Luzon Force retreated around the lake toward Bataan. By 1 January 1942 the Japanese vanguard was on Manila Bay, and on 2 January it entered the capital from the south. The road it took – Route 1, through Calamba, Biñan and Muntinlupa – crosses the Poblacion: Biñan was occupied without a fight in the first days of 1942. On Bataan, Brigadier General Mateo Capinpin, whose name a street of Biñan now bears, led the 21st Division of the Philippine Army until he was captured by the Japanese on 5 April 1942, four days before the surrender and the Death March that followed.

**2. Life under occupation · Ang buhay sa ilalim ng pananakop**
> *Isang administrasyong militar ng Hapon ang namahala mula Enero 1942.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **namahala** – *governed, took charge* (root: *bahala*)
- **sinundan** – *was followed* (root: *sunod*)
- **nag-iisa** – *single, only* (root: *isa* = one)
- **malaya sa pangalan lamang** – *free in name only*
- **binuksan** – *was opened* (root: *bukas*)
- **kumaunti** – *became fewer* (root: *kaunti* = few)
- **salaysay** – *account, narrative*
- **tinipon** – *gathered, collected* (root: *tipon*)
- **humirap** – *became hard* (root: *hirap*)
- **suliranin** – *problem*
- **kaguluhan** – *disorder, insecurity* (root: *gulo*)
- **nabigo** – *failed* (root: *bigo*)
- **salaping pang-okupasyon** – *occupation money*
- **maglimbag** – *to print* (root: *limbag*)

**Grammar:**
- *Isang administrasyong militar … ang namahala* – an *ang*-inversion that stresses **who** governed: "It was a military administration that governed."
- *Kumaunti* and *humirap* – *-um-* added to an adjective root gives a verb of change: "became few", "became hard".

**Translation:**
> A Japanese military administration governed from January 1942. It was followed by a single party, the KALIBAPI (December 1942), and on 14 October 1943 by a Republic under José P. Laurel that was free in name only. Schools were reopened under Japanese control and taught Nippongo, but fewer children attended. According to accounts gathered in Laguna, the first two years were "almost ordinary" in towns with few Japanese soldiers; in 1944 life became truly hard. Food was the heaviest problem. The army bought or took the rice, and the Laurel government replaced the former rice agency with the BIBA (Bigasang Bayan), but "almost all the plans failed" because of disorder, the lack of fuel and vehicles, and the needs of the Japanese army. Prices rose sharply, and the value of the occupation money – nicknamed "Mickey Mouse money" – fell so far that notes of 500 and 1,000 pesos had to be printed.

**Translation:**

Gustavo Ingles, a Hunters-ROTC officer captured in June 1943, was tortured at Fort Santiago and then imprisoned at New Bilibid Prison in Muntinlupa, only a few kilometres north of Biñan; on 24 June 1944 a guerrilla raid freed him. As the Americans drew near, the occupiers became even crueller. In December 1944 the Makapili was founded, a pro-Japanese militia whose members fought alongside the Japanese in Rizal, Laguna and Bulacan.
>
> In Biñan itself the Japanese had a garrison, where suspects were imprisoned. The teachers who wrote the histories of the barrios in 1953 remembered that in August 1942 a secret organization, the "Biñan Unit", was founded in De La Paz, and a month later it was joined to the McKinley Brigade of the Fil-American Irregular Troops, under the American colonel Hugh Straughn. In July 1943 the Kempeitai arrested one of its captains, Enrique Alcabasa, and took him to the San Pedro garrison, while many of its members were imprisoned in the Biñan garrison and released for lack of evidence. In August Alcabasa was arrested again in his house in De La Paz, the headquarters of the organization; he was cruelly tortured, and the next day he was killed. The young mayor, the lawyer Rogelio Limaco, elected in 1940, saved the men of Platero who had been taken to the garrison. At dawn on 24 October 1944 it was his turn to be seized by the Japanese, and he was never seen again: "he became a hero and was killed by the Japanese while doing his duty," says the history of Platero. The city remembers him every 16 September, the day of his birth in 1913.

**4. The resistance · Ang paglaban**
> *Gayunman, lumakas ang paglaban.*

**Vocabulary:**

- **paglaban** – *resistance, fighting back* (root: *laban*)
- **samahan** – *organization, group* (root: *sama* = together)
- **kumilos** – *acted, operated* (root: *kilos*)
- **magkakahiwalay** – *separate from one another* (root: *hiwalay*)
- **kadete** – *cadet*
- **pinamumunuan** – *is led* (root: *puno* = leader, head)
- **burol** – *hill*
- **mandirigma** – *fighter, warrior* (root: *digma*)
- **humigit-kumulang** – *approximately* (literally "more or less")
- **taga-kalapit na bayan** – *from a neighbouring town*
- **napatay** – *was killed* (root: *patay*)
- **sinasalakay** – *attacking* (root: *salakay*)

**Grammar:**
- *Magkakahiwalay* – *magka-* + repeated syllable + root: a plural reciprocal state ("separate from each other").
- *Humigit-kumulang* – two *-um-* verbs (*higit* "more", *kulang* "less") fused into an adverb.
- *Habang sinasalakay* – *habang* ("while") + ongoing object-focus verb.

**Translation:**
> Nevertheless, the resistance grew stronger. In Laguna, four large guerrilla organizations operated almost separately: the Hunters-ROTC, founded by cadets of the Philippine Military Academy and the ROTC; Marking's Fil-Americans; the President Quezon's Own Guerrillas (PQOG) around San Pablo; and the Fil-American Irregular Troops (FAIT). With them fought the Hukbalahap, led by communists, and the Chinese Wha-Chi. In December 1944 the 47th Division of the Hunters-ROTC was in Manila, Cavite and Batangas, and its 48th Regiment was in Cavite, just across the hills from Biñan. Its 45th Regiment in Laguna had only 80 armed fighters – but about 5,000 "home guards", civilians who provided food, messages and information. One of the best-known leaders of the region came from a neighbouring town: Abelardo "Kapitan Remo" Remoquillo of San Pedro, who was killed in 1945 while attacking a Japanese garrison at Bay.

**5. The liberation of 3 February 1945 · Ang paglaya noong Pebrero 3, 1945**
> *Nang araw ding iyon, bago pa makarating ang sinumang sundalong Amerikano, pinalaya ng mga Pilipino ang Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **nang araw ding iyon** – *on that very same day* (*din* → *ding* + linker)
- **makarating** – *to be able to arrive, reach* (root: *dating*)
- **sinuman** – *anyone*
- **beterano** – *veteran*
- **mananalaysay** – *historian* (root: *salaysay*)
- **nagpasya** – *decided* (root: *pasya*)
- **nagmartsa** – *marched* (from Spanish *marcha*)
- **hanay** – *column, line*
- **harapan** – *head-on, frontal* (root: *harap* = front)
- **isinara** – *closed off* (root: *sara*)
- **hadlangan** – *to block, to prevent* (root: *hadlang*)
- **pagtakas** – *escape* (root: *takas*)
- **nalipol** – *was wiped out* (root: *lipol*)
- **bumagsak** – *fell* (root: *bagsak*)
- **nagpapatunay** – *proves, confirms* (root: *tunay* = true)
- **medyor** – *major (military rank)*
- **hepe** – *chief* (from Spanish *jefe*)
- **sertipiko** – *certificate*
- **iginawad** – *was awarded* (root: *gawad* = award)
- **anibersaryo** – *anniversary*

**Grammar:**
- *Pinalaya ng mga Pilipino ang Biñan* – object focus: Biñan (*ang*) is what was freed; the Filipinos are the actors (*ng*). The sentence puts the stress on the town itself.
- *Bago pa makarating* – *bago* ("before") + *pa* ("even, still") + abilitative *maka-*: "before (anyone) could even arrive".
- *Upang hadlangan* – *upang* ("in order to") + infinitive in *-an*.

**Translation:**
> In January 1945 the Americans returned to Luzon. On 31 January the 11th Airborne Division landed at Nasugbu, Batangas, and on 3 February its paratroopers dropped on Tagaytay Ridge. On that very day, before any American soldier could arrive, Filipinos freed Biñan. According to the Hunters-ROTC veteran and historian Proculo Mojica, de Ocampo came down from Carmona and decided to take Biñan, and on 3 February he marched on the town in three columns. The first column attacked the garrison head-on – about 30 Japanese soldiers reinforced by about 50 Makapili. The second closed the roads from San Pedro to the north, and the third entered from the south to block any escape toward Santa Rosa. "In one day, the whole garrison was wiped out and Biñan fell to the Hunters." The attackers were the 48th Regiment, 47th Division of the Hunters-ROTC under Colonel Emmanuel de Ocampo. A local account names Marcial Alintanahin as the leader of the attack. Plaques now in the city museum prove that he was a major of the Hunters-ROTC, leader of the "Blue Lake Battalion" of that same 48th Regiment and a former chief of police, and that he became the "military mayor" of Biñan after the liberation; they do not say what he did on 3 February. On 5 February the guerrillas came back from San Pedro to "Free Biñan" before attacking Santa Rosa – which is why the town long celebrated its liberation on that date: a certificate now in the city museum was awarded "on the 37th Anniversary of the Liberation Day of Biñan, this 5th of February" 1982.

> *Pagkatapos, naging himpilan sa likuran ang Biñan ng pinakatanyag na pagliligtas sa digmaan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **himpilan sa likuran** – *rear base* (*himpil* = to stop, station; *likuran* = rear)
- **pinakatanyag** – *most famous* (*pinaka-* + *tanyag*)

- **pampang** – *shore, bank*
- **nakakulong** – *imprisoned, confined* (root: *kulong*)
- **nanghihina** – *growing weak* (root: *hina*)
- **sakay ng bangka** – *aboard a boat*
- **lalapagan** – *landing place* (root: *lapag*)
- **naglayag** – *sailed* (root: *layag* = sail)
- **makipag-ugnayan** – *to coordinate, to get in touch* (root: *ugnay*)

**Grammar:**
- *Pinaka-* forms the superlative: *tanyag* "famous" → *pinakatanyag* "most famous".

**Translation:**
> Afterwards, Biñan became a rear base for the most famous rescue of the war. At the College of Agriculture of the University of the Philippines in Los Baños, on the other shore of the lake, 2,147 civilians were held, most of them Americans and many already weak from hunger. On 20 February four American engineers crossed Laguna de Bay aboard a banca from Biñan to choose a landing place. Gustavo Ingles – the very man freed from New Bilibid – also sailed from Biñan to coordinate with the guerrilla leaders on the far shore. On 21 February a battalion of paratroopers marched south on Highway 1, the road that passes through Biñan, to Mamatid in Cabuyao, where the amphibious tractors were waiting. At dawn on 23 February the paratroopers dropped beside the camp, the guerrillas attacked the guards, and the amtracs carried the prisoners across the lake to safety. The Japanese vented their revenge on those left behind. In the barrios of Los Baños, soldiers killed more than two hundred civilians in the weeks after the raid. Calamba suffered even more: American war crimes investigators estimated that about 1,500 people were massacred from 12 to 15 February 1945, and according to the evidence heard at the trial of General Yamashita, more than 2,500 died on 12 February alone.

**Translation:**

"Only a few of the townspeople were killed during the Occupation," its teachers wrote in 1953; "the Japanese no longer had the time to burn the houses or kill the people." The old houses of stone and wood around the plaza – the homes of the Alberto, Ocampo, Gonzales and other families – survived the war, which is why Biñan can still be proud of its heritage houses today. Life returned quickly. On 15 May 1945, while the war was still going on in northern Luzon, Manila's Free Philippines published a short notice: "BIÑAN FIESTA TODAY".

**8. From liberation to independence · Mula sa paglaya hanggang sa kasarinlan**
> *Natapos ang digmaan noong Setyembre 1945.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **natapos** – *ended* (root: *tapos*)

**Translation:**

On 4 July 1946 the Philippines became independent, and the Philippine War Damage Commission began paying for houses, crops and public buildings destroyed in the war. The memory of those years is still alive in Biñan. Every 3 February the city celebrates Biñan Liberation Day at the guerrilla memorial, and in 2025, for the eightieth anniversary, the guerrillas' march from Carmona was re-enacted.

## Post-War Reconstruction and Contemporary Era / Pagbangon at Makabagong Panahon

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. Rebuilding · Muling pagtatayo**
> *Pagkatapos ng digmaan, ginugol ang mga unang taon ng kapayapaan sa muling pagtatayo.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **ginugol** – *was spent (time, effort)* (root: *gugol*)
- **kapayapaan** – *peace* (root: *payapa* = calm)
- **muling pagtatayo** – *rebuilding* (*muli* = again; *tayo* = to build, stand up)
- **ari-arian** – *property* (root: *ari* = to own)
- **binili** – *was bought* (root: *bili*)
- **kinumpuni** – *was repaired* (root: *kumpuni*)
- **nilagyan** – *was provided with, had something added* (root: *lagay*)
- **kamalig** – *granary, storehouse*
- **luklukan** – *seat (of power)* (root: *luklok* = to seat)
- **bakal** – *iron, steel*
- **nag-abuloy** – *donor, the one who donated* (root: *abuloy* = donation)
- **inilantad** – *was unveiled* (root: *lantad* = exposed)
- **pananda** – *marker* (root: *tanda* = sign)
- **kinatayuan** – *site, where something stood* (root: *tayo*)
- **ginawang** – *was made into* (root: *gawa*)

**Grammar:**
- *Ginugol ang mga unang taon … sa muling pagtatayo* – object focus with *-in-*: the years (*ang*) are what was spent.
- *Ang lumang casa hacienda …, na binili ng bayan noong 1910 …, ay kinumpuni* – a long subject in the *ang … ay …* order: the relative clause with *na* tells which building, and *ay* introduces what happened to it.

**Translation:**
> After the war, the first years of peace were spent rebuilding. That same year a steel bridge, the Evangelista Bridge, named after its donor, replaced the old hanging (colgante) bridge of the Poblacion, and on 30 December a marker dedicated to Rizal was unveiled on the site of his school; since 1946 two colleges had also opened, Grinhar and Santa Catalina. In the 1950s the old casa hacienda of the Dominicans on the plaza, which the town had bought in 1910 for its public school, was repaired, given a portico and made into the municipal hall, once the school had moved to a new building in Santo Domingo. In this way the friars' granary of the eighteenth century became the seat of the town government for more than half a century.

- *Dinala ng halalang iyon si Bayani Alonte* – object focus: Alonte (*si*) is what the election "brought".
- *Pinawalang-bisa* – a compound root (*walang-bisa*, "without validity") with causative *pa-* and *-in-*: "was made void".
- *Pinakamayayamang bayan* – superlative plus plural adjective (*mayayaman*, repeated syllable for plural).

**Translation:**
> That election brought Bayani "Arthur" Alonte, mayor from 1988 to 1998, and with him came the factories. Its extensions reached barangay Biñan in 1997 and Loma and Timbao in 1998, and in 1992 Samsung and Solid Corporation opened the Laguna International Industrial Park in Mamplasan and Ganado. Subdivisions followed for workers and for families leaving Metro Manila: Biñan had 134,553 people in 1990 and 201,186 in 2000. Politics also had a scandal: in December 1997 a Manila court convicted Mayor Alonte of rape, but in March 1998 the Supreme Court annulled the verdict because due process had not been followed, and sent the case back for a new trial. Under his successor, Hermis Perez (1998–2007), Biñan became one of the richest towns in the country: by the end of his terms it was, in the city's own words, "the richest municipality in the Philippines", and according to government statistics for 2008 it ranked first in income among all the municipalities of the country.

## Culture and Daily Life / Kultura at Pang-araw-araw na Buhay

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. A market town · Isang bayan ng pamilihan**

> *Mula pa noon, bayan na ng mga mangangalakal ang Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **mangangalakal** – *trader* (root: *kalakal* = goods)
- **abala** – *busy*
- **pamilihan** – *market* (root: *bili* = buy)
- **dumarayo** – *come from afar* (root: *dayo*)
- **mamili** – *to shop* (root: *bili*)
- **sariwa** – *fresh*
- **ipaluto** – *to have something cooked* (root: *luto*)
- **kabibili** – *just bought* (root: *bili*)
- **pinupuno** – *fill* (root: *puno* = full)
- **karinderya** – *small eatery*

**Grammar:**
- *Kabibili lamang niya* – *ka-* + repeated first syllable + root marks an action just completed: “that he or she has just bought”.
- *Ipaluto* – *i-* (object focus) + causative *pa-* + *luto*: “to have (it) cooked” by someone else.

**Translation:**
> Biñan has always been a town of traders. Its public market, on J. Gonzales Street in the Poblacion, is busy day and night; the city calls it the largest public market in Laguna and in the whole CALABARZON region, and retailers from the neighbouring towns come to buy there and sell again at home. A new wet market opened on 15 April 2019, with a *paluto* corner upstairs where shoppers can have the fish or meat they have just bought cooked on the spot. Around it, family businesses – *sari-sari* stores, eateries and bakeries – fill the streets of the old town, while the malls of the last thirty years stand along the national road and the expressway (see chapter X). Each February, during Araw ng Biñan, the *Gawang Biñan* trade fair shows what the city makes.

**2. Hands at work · Mga kamay na gumagawa**

> *Sa halos buong ikadalawampung siglo, bayan ng mga sapatero ang Biñan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sapatero** – *shoemaker* (from Spanish *zapatero*)
- **kasagsagan** – *peak, height* (root: *sagsag*)
- **talyer** – *workshop*
- **kumikilos** – *operate, move* (root: *kilos*)
- **tahanan** – *home* (root: *tahan*)
- **pambata / panlalaki** – *for children / for men*
- **pakyawan** – *wholesale* (root: *pakyaw*)
- **sagisag** – *seal, emblem*
- **pinabagsak** – *brought down, ruined* (root: *bagsak* = fall)
- **inangkat** – *imported* (root: *angkat*)
- **gorra** – *cap*
- **handog** – *gift, offering*

**Grammar:**
- *Pambata*, *panlalaki* – the prefix *pang-* makes adjectives meaning “for”: *pang-* + *bata* → *pambata* (*ng* becomes *m* before *b*); *pang-* + *lalaki* → *panlalaki* (*ng* becomes *n* before *l*).
- *Pinabagsak ng murang inangkat na produkto ang karamihan sa mga talyer* – causative object focus (*pa-* + *-in-*): the workshops (*ang*) were brought down by cheap imports (*ng*).

**Translation:**
> For most of the twentieth century Biñan was a town of shoemakers. At the height of the trade, in the 1960s, some 500 workshops worked in about 3,000 homes: sandals, step-ins and children’s shoes in De La Paz, men’s shoes in Malaban, and wooden clogs – *bakya* – in Casile and San Jose. Families such as the Almendral and the Dilag built small businesses that sold to department stores and to the wholesale market of Baclaran, and a shoe still appears on the city’s seal. Cheap imports ruined most workshops from the late 1990s. In Platero, about 450 makers of hats and caps carry on another old trade, celebrated every 17 March since 2008 with a Sombrero Festival. Each barangay had its craft: horse-drawn *kalesa* in Canlalay and De La Paz, pedicabs in Soro-soro, sampaguita garlands in Bungahan, candles in Santo Domingo. The town remembered its shoemakers on its plaza: the Los Maduros bandstand, given by a local gentlemen’s club in the 1950s or 1960s, is shaped like a wooden clog, or a slipper.

**3. Puto, pospas and pinipig · Puto, pospas at pinipig**

> *Ang pinakatanyag na produkto ng lungsod ay ang **Puto Biñan**, isang malapad at malambot na kakanin.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **kakanin** – *rice cake, native delicacy*
- **masa** – *batter, dough*
- **pampaalsa** – *raising agent* (root: *alsa* = rise)
- **pinasisingawan** – *is steamed* (root: *singaw* = steam)
- **ipa** – *rice husk*
- **ginadgad** – *grated* (root: *gadgad*)
- **inapo** – *descendant*
- **lugaw** – *porridge*
- **malagkit** – *glutinous rice*
- **lamang-loob** – *innards*
- **pinadilaw** – *coloured yellow* (root: *dilaw*)
- **binayo** – *pounded* (root: *bayo*)
- **lusong** – *wooden mortar*
- **Undas** – *All Saints’ Day*

**Grammar:**
- *Pinasisingawan* – *pa-* + *-in-* + repeated syllable + *-an*: an ongoing causative verb in locative focus, “(the batter) is being steamed”.
- *Binayo hanggang pumipi* – *hanggang* (“until”) + an *-um-* verb: “pounded until it becomes flat”.

**Translation:**
> The city’s most famous product is **Puto Biñan**, a wide, soft rice cake. Its batter, the *galapong*, is made of rice flour, brown sugar, a raising agent and water; it is steamed in a *lansungan* lined with muslin over a fire of rice husks, then topped with grated cheese, salted egg and condensed milk. Local memory credits it to **Petronila “Nila” Samaniego**; vendors sold the cakes to passengers at the railway station, and travellers began to call them “the puto of Biñan”. Her descendant Marvin Endriga still runs the family kitchen, and most puto makers today live in San Vicente. The family brand dates its first sale to 1925, and older makers such as Aling Manay and Aling Juling are still remembered. The morning dish is **pospas**, a porridge of glutinous rice with native chicken and pork innards, coloured yellow with *kasubha* and topped with fried garlic and spring onion. Aling Merta Silva is believed to have pioneered it, perhaps as early as the Japanese occupation, and her helper Antonio “Tonyo” Benjamin carried it on. Canlalay is known for **pinipig**, young glutinous rice toasted in a clay pot and pounded flat in a wooden mortar; demand peaks before All Saints’ Day, and one maker, Edna’s, has been at work since 1967. Santo Domingo calls itself the capital of *sorbetes*, home-made ice cream. Other favourites of Laguna, such as *espasol* and *kesong puti*, belong to other towns of the province.

**4. San Isidro, the Queen and the saints · Si San Isidro, ang Reyna at ang mga santo**

> *Ang patron ng Biñan ay si **San Isidro Labrador**, ang santo ng mga magsasaka, na ang kapistahan ay tuwing Mayo 15 – angkop sa isang bayang nagsimula bilang “Hacienda de San Isidro Labrador de Biñan”.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **kapistahan** – *feast day* (root: *pista*)
- **angkop** – *fitting, suitable*
- **mapaghimala** – *miraculous* (root: *himala* = miracle)
- **dambanang pandiyosesis** – *diocesan shrine* (*dambana* = altar, shrine)
- **pinipintakasi** – *is venerated as patron* (root: *pintakasi*)
- **hinirang** – *was proclaimed, chosen* (root: *hirang*)
- **pandangal** – *honorary* (root: *dangal* = honour)
- **pinapasan** – *carry on the shoulders* (root: *pasan*)
- **pasasalamat** – *thanksgiving* (root: *salamat*)
- **pananampalataya** – *faith*
- **polyeto** – *pamphlet*
- **libingan** – *grave* (root: *libing* = burial)
- **pamimintuho** – *devotion*
- **Mahal na Araw** – *Holy Week*

**Grammar:**
- *Pandangal* – *pang-* + *dangal*: *ng* becomes *n* before *d*, as in *pandiyosesis* (“diocesan”).

**Translation:**
> The patron of Biñan is **San Isidro Labrador**, the farmer saint, whose feast falls on 15 May – fitting for a town that began as the “Hacienda de San Isidro Labrador de Biñan”. His church, rebuilt around 1970 after the fire of 1968, keeps an old image that many believe to be miraculous, and on 6 May 2022 it became a diocesan shrine. Biñan also has a queen. *Nuestra Señora de la Paz y Buen Viaje*, venerated in the chapel of De La Paz, was proclaimed “Honorary Queen of Biñan” by a city resolution in 2018, and each May the men of the city carry her image from her chapel to the church in the *Karakol ng Reyna*. The Chinese-Filipino community holds its own thanksgiving, the *Pistang Intsik*, during the May festival, and each barangay has its fiesta: the Santo Niño in Ganado in January, hats in Platero in March, *puto* and *kakanin* in San Vicente in April, ice cream in Santo Domingo in August, *pinipig* in Canlalay in October. Popular faith has its own stories. According to a pamphlet printed in 1947, the body of Filomena Almarines, a young roadside vendor who had died in 1938 at the age of 25, was found intact when her tomb was opened; a chapel was built and pilgrims came for some years, though the Church never recognized her cult. The customs of Holy Week – the *pabasa*, the processions, the old images kept by families – have not yet been described in print; they are among the memories this book hopes to gather.

**5. The Puto Latik Festival · Ang Puto Latik Festival**

> *Mula noong 2011, sabay na ipinagdiriwang ng lungsod ang kakanin at ang sayaw nito sa **Puto Latik Festival**.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **sabay** – *together, at the same time*
- **idinaos** – *was held* (root: *daos*)

- **karosa** – *float, carriage*

- **palaman** – *filling, topping*
- **nagkamit** – *won, obtained* (root: *kamit*)
- **gantimpala** – *award, prize*
- **indayog** – *rhythm, sway*
- **bao** – *coconut shell*
- **nakatali** – *tied* (root: *tali*)
- **isinayaw** – *was danced* (root: *sayaw*)
- **nagkasundo** – *made peace, agreed* (root: *sundo*)
- **inaangkin** – *claims* (root: *angkin*)

**Grammar:**
- *Nakapula*, *nakaasul* – *naka-* + a colour means “wearing” that colour: *mga Morong nakapula* = “Moros in red”.
- *Isinayaw*, *bininyagan* – *i-* + *-in-* (object focus) and *-in-* + *-an* (locative focus): “was danced”, “were baptized”.

**Translation:**
> Since 2011 the city has celebrated its rice cake and its dance together in the **Puto Latik Festival**. First held in February, it moved in 2017 to the nine days from 15 to 23 May, around the feast of San Isidro, and it was held online in 2020 and 2021. It opens with the *Karakol ng Reyna* and fills the streets with dancers from every barangay, with floats, with the *Puto Biñan 2.0* contest, in which barangays invent new toppings for the old cake, with the *Pistang Intsik* and, at the end, with the *Santacruzan*. The city reports national tourism awards for the festival in 2018, 2019 and 2022, and in 2025 it was named the best cultural festival among the country’s cities. Its heart is the **maglalatik**, a dance in which men with coconut shells strapped to their bodies strike them in rhythm. Biñan tradition holds that it was first danced by the men of Loma and Zapote as an offering to San Isidro during his procession, and that it tells of a fight for the *latik*, the coconut curd, between Moros in red and Christians in blue: the Moros win, but the two sides make peace and the Moros are baptized. Other places, such as Cainta in Rizal, also claim the dance.

**7. Customs of 1953 · Mga kaugalian noong 1953**

In 2021 the city counted 7,524 registered businesses, up from 7,013 a year before; yet, in the mayor’s words, “our top 100 taxpayers contribute 95 percent of all the taxes”. The city’s regular income rose from ₱607 million in 2009 to ₱1.53 billion in 2016. In the national competitiveness index, Biñan climbed from 136th place among the country’s component cities in 2014 to 7th in 2024, and it has ranked first for economic dynamism every year since 2022 – but only 40th for infrastructure.

## Who Owns the Land? / Kanino ang Lupa?

### Vocabulary and Grammar Explanation

**1. One owner, then three thousand · Isang may-ari, saka tatlong libo**

> *Sa loob ng dalawa’t kalahating siglo, halos lahat ng lupain ng Biñan ay may iisang may-ari: ang Kolehiyo ng Santo Tomás ng mga Dominiko, na ang asyenda ay sumasakop sa mga 3,474 na ektarya, karamihan ay palayang may patubig at kaunting tubuhan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **dalawa’t kalahati** – *two and a half*
- **may-ari** – *owner* (root: *ari* = own)
- **sumasakop** – *covers* (root: *sakop*)
- **patubig** – *irrigation* (root: *tubig*)
- **agrimensor** – *land surveyor*
- **sinukat** – *measured* (root: *sukat*)
- **titulo** – *land title*
- **binabanggit** – *is mentioned* (root: *banggit*)
- **lote** – *lot, parcel*
- **umuupa** – *renter, tenant* (root: *upa* = rent)
- **iilan** – *only a few*
- **nanirahan** – *settled* (root: *tira*)

**Grammar:**
- *Iilang dosenang tao lamang* – *iilan* (“only a few”) + linker + *dosena* (“dozen”) + linker: “only a few dozen people”.
- *Na binabanggit pa rin* – an ongoing object-focus verb with *pa rin* (“still”): “which is still cited”.

**Translation:**
> For two and a half centuries, almost all the land of Biñan had a single owner: the Dominican College of Santo Tomás, whose estate covered some 3,474 hectares, most of it irrigated rice land with a little sugarcane. After the American government bought the friar estates in 1903, its surveyors measured every field of the “Biñan Estate” between January 1906 and February 1907 – a survey still cited in land titles today. When the land went on sale in May 1910, 2,500 of its 3,200 parcels were sold in eight days, and the director of lands told the United States Congress that “nearly all of the purchasers were former tenants”. Only a few dozen people leased more than 16 hectares; among them were members of the Gana, Yatco and Ocampo families and an American settler, Frank J. Ferguson, with about 142 hectares.

**2. Rice, tenants and sugar · Palay, kasama at tubo**

> *Sa halos buong ikadalawampung siglo, nanatili sa kamay ng mga lokal na may-ari ang mga palayan sa kapatagan at sinasaka ng mga *kasamá*, habang naging tubuhan naman ang mas tuyong kabundukan sa kanluran.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **kapatagan** – *lowlands* (root: *patag* = flat)
- **sinasaka** – *is farmed* (root: *saka*)
- **kasamá** – *sharecropper*
- **kabundukan** – *uplands* (root: *bundok* = mountain)
- **bakas** – *trace*
- **talaan** – *record* (root: *tala*)
- **bayarang trabahador** – *paid labourer*
- **repormang agraryo** – *agrarian reform*
- **idinulot** – *caused* (root: *dulot*)
- **nagwagi** – *won* (root: *wagi*)
- **tumanggi** – *refused* (root: *tanggi*)
- **baligtarin** – *to overturn* (root: *baligtad*)
- **isinailalim** – *was placed under* (root: *ilalim*)
- **ipinagtanggol** – *was defended* (root: *tanggol*)

**Grammar:**
- *Kung gaano kahirap* – *gaano* + *ka-* + adjective: “how hard (it was)”.
- *Na ipinagtanggol naman ng may-ari* – *naman* marks a contrast: “which the owner, for his part, defended”.

**Translation:**
> For most of the twentieth century the rice fields of the lowlands stayed in the hands of local owners and were tilled by sharecroppers, the *kasamá*, while the drier uplands to the west became sugarcane fields. Court records keep traces of this world: a 4.5-hectare rice field in Zapote worked by a sharecropper in the 1960s, or the rice land of Luis Alberto in Langkiwa, whose workers were found in 2001 to be paid labourers rather than tenants. The land reform laws of 1963, 1972 and 1988 reached Biñan, but the disputes they raised show how hard it was for tillers to become owners. In Loma, the tenants of some 37 hectares won: in 2000 the Supreme Court refused to overturn their recognition as legitimate beneficiaries of agrarian reform. In Langkiwa and Timbao, 43.7 hectares owned by Gonzalo Puyat & Sons were placed under agrarian reform in 1998, then defended by their owner as industrial land; in 2012 the Supreme Court sent the question back to the Department of Agrarian Reform.

**3. From cane fields to factories and townships · Mula sa tubuhan hanggang sa pabrika at bagong bayan**

> *Sa loob ng isang henerasyon, naging isa sa malalaking sinturon ng industriya sa bansa ang kabundukan.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **henerasyon** – *generation*
- **sinturon ng industriya** – *industrial belt*
- **iginuhit** – *drew* (root: *guhit* = line)
- **bloke** – *block*

- **naitala** – *recorded* (root: *tala*)

**Grammar:**
- *Muling iginuhit … bilang malalaking bloke* – *bilang* means “as”: “redrawn as large blocks”.
- *Daan-daang kompanya* – doubling *daan* (“hundred”) gives “hundreds of companies”.

**Translation:**
> Within one generation the uplands became one of the country’s great industrial belts. No public register lists the largest landowners of Biñan. Among the largest documented holdings are those of a handful of developers – Ayala Land, Megaworld, Samsung and Solid, Filinvest, Federal Land and ArthaLand – and of De La Salle University, whose 50-hectare campus includes an economic zone proclaimed in 2025. Inside the industrial parks, the lots are sold on to hundreds of companies.

**4. The land nobody can buy · Ang lupang hindi mabibili**

> *May dalawang piraso pa rin ng Biñan na pag-aari ng Estado.*

**Vocabulary:**
- **mabibili** – *can be bought* (root: *bili*)
- **Estado** – *the State*
- **ilalim** – *bottom, bed*
- **panukat** – *gauge, measuring device* (root: *sukat*)

- **lubhang tumaas** – *rose greatly*
- **metro kuwadrado** – *square metre*

**Grammar:**

**Translation:**
> The State still owns two strips of Biñan. By law, the bed of Laguna de Bay includes every piece of shore at or below 12.5 metres on the lake gauge: a band of public land along Malaban and De La Paz. The other is the railway, now being rebuilt for the North–South Commuter Railway; the families who lived along it are being moved to The Meadows in Langkiwa, where 2,030 homes are planned. Meanwhile the price of land has soared: about ₱164 per hectare around 1909, ₱30 per square metre for a rice field in Langkiwa in 1989, and about ₱3,800 per square metre in 2016, when a developer paid ₱307 million for 8.1 hectares. The land that some three thousand tenant families bought in 1910 for less than two centavos a square metre has become some of the most valuable in the province.
